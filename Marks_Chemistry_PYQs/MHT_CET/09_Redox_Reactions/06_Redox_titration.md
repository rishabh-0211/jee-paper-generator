### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify colour change when NaCl solution is titrated against $\text{AgNO}_3$ solution using fluorescein indicator.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Colourless to pale yellow
- **(B)** Reddish pink to pale yellow
- **(C)** Pale yellow to reddish pink
- **(D)** Colourless to reddish pink

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Pale yellow to reddish pink**

**Detailed Solution:**

The titration of $\text{NaCl}$ against $\text{AgNO}_3$ using fluorescein indicator is an example of Fajan's method (precipitation titration).

Fluorescein acts as an adsorption indicator. In the aqueous solution, it exists as the fluoresceinate anion, which imparts a pale yellow (or yellowish-green) colour to the solution.

During the titration, $\text{AgCl}$ precipitates. Before the equivalence point, the $\text{AgCl}$ particles adsorb excess $\text{Cl}^-$ ions, acquiring a negative charge that repels the fluoresceinate anions.

Just after the equivalence point, the addition of excess $\text{AgNO}_3$ causes the $\text{AgCl}$ particles to adsorb $\text{Ag}^+$ ions, acquiring a positive charge.

The positively charged precipitate now strongly adsorbs the fluoresceinate anions. This adsorption deforms the electron cloud of the indicator, resulting in a distinct colour change to reddish pink on the surface of the precipitate.

Thus, the colour change observed at the end point is from pale yellow to reddish pink.

Answer: Pale yellow to reddish pink

### Q2. MHT CET 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

How many gram-equivalents of $\mathrm{NaOH}$ are required to neutralise $25 \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$ of a decinormal $\mathrm{HCl}$ solution?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.00125$
- **(B)** $0.0025$
- **(C)** $0.0050$
- **(D)** $0.025$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.0025$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For acid-base reactions, g-equivalent of acid $=$ g-equivalent of base
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\mathrm{HCl}) &(\mathrm{NaOH}) \\
\therefore \text { g-equivalent of acid } &=\left(25 \times 10^{-3}\right) \mathrm{dm}^{3} \times \frac{1}{10} \\
=& 25 \times 10^{-4} \\
\text { or } &=0.0025
\end{aligned}
$$
$=\mathrm{g}$ -equivalent of $\mathrm{NaOH}$

</details>
