### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which among $\text{F}_2$, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{Br}_2$ and $\text{I}_2$ exhibit disproportionation reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{F}_2$ and $\text{Cl}_2$
- **(B)** $\text{Cl}_2, \text{Br}_2$ and $\text{I}_2$
- **(C)** Only $\text{I}_2$
- **(D)** $\text{F}_2, \text{Cl}_2$ and $\text{Br}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{Cl}_2, \text{Br}_2$ and $\text{I}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Fluorine is the most electronegative element and does not exhibit any positive oxidation state. Hence, it can only be reduced from $0$ to $-1$ oxidation state and cannot undergo disproportionation.

Chlorine, bromine, and iodine have vacant d-orbitals and can exhibit positive oxidation states (such as $+1, +3, +5, +7$) along with the $-1$ oxidation state. Therefore, they undergo disproportionation reactions in alkaline medium.

For example, with cold and dilute alkali:
$\text{X}_2 + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{X}^- + \text{OX}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (where $\text{X} = \text{Cl}, \text{Br}, \text{I}$)

Thus, $\text{Cl}_2$, $\text{Br}_2$, and $\text{I}_2$ exhibit disproportionation reactions.

Answer: $\text{Cl}_2, \text{Br}_2$ and $\text{I}_2$

</details>
