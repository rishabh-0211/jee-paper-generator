### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is the strongest reducing agent?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{Ag}^+$
- **(B)** $\text{Li}$
- **(C)** $\text{F}_2$
- **(D)** $\text{Au}^+$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{Li}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

A reducing agent is a substance that loses electrons and undergoes oxidation.

The strength of a reducing agent is inversely proportional to its standard reduction potential.

Lithium ($\text{Li}$) has the lowest standard reduction potential ($E^\circ_{\text{Li}^+/\text{Li}} = -3.05\text{ V}$) among all elements in the electrochemical series.

Therefore, $\text{Li}$ has the highest tendency to lose electrons and is the strongest reducing agent.

$\text{F}_2$ has the highest standard reduction potential and is the strongest oxidizing agent.

$\text{Ag}^+$ and $\text{Au}^+$ are also oxidizing agents as they tend to gain electrons to form their respective metals.

Answer: $\text{Li}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Find the number of moles of electrons required for reduction of $3n$ moles of $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ to $\text{Zn}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6n$
- **(B)** $4n$
- **(C)** $3n$
- **(D)** $2n$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $6n$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The reduction reaction of zinc ions is given by:

$\text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^{-} \rightarrow \text{Zn}$

From the balanced half-reaction, $1$ mole of $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ requires $2$ moles of electrons for complete reduction.

Therefore, the number of moles of electrons required to reduce $3n$ moles of $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ is:

$3n \times 2 = 6n$

Answer: $6n$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify change in oxidation state of oxidising agent in following redox reaction.
$3\text{H}_3\text{AsO}_{3(aq)} + \text{BrO}_{3(aq)}^- \longrightarrow \text{Br}_{(aq)}^- + 3\text{H}_3\text{AsO}_{4(aq)}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-1\ \text{to}\ +5$
- **(B)** $+5\ \text{to}\ -1$
- **(C)** $+3\ \text{to}\ +5$
- **(D)** $+5\ \text{to}\ +3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $+5\ \text{to}\ -1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given reaction is $3\text{H}_3\text{AsO}_{3(aq)} + \text{BrO}_{3(aq)}^- \longrightarrow \text{Br}_{(aq)}^- + 3\text{H}_3\text{AsO}_{4(aq)}$

Let the oxidation state of As in $\text{H}_3\text{AsO}_3$ be $x$.
$3(+1) + x + 3(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = +3$

Let the oxidation state of Br in $\text{BrO}_3^-$ be $y$.
$y + 3(-2) = -1 \Rightarrow y = +5$

Let the oxidation state of As in $\text{H}_3\text{AsO}_4$ be $z$.
$3(+1) + z + 4(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow z = +5$

The oxidation state of Br in $\text{Br}^-$ is $-1$.

The oxidation state of As increases from $+3$ to $+5$, so $\text{H}_3\text{AsO}_3$ undergoes oxidation and acts as a reducing agent.

The oxidation state of Br decreases from $+5$ to $-1$, so $\text{BrO}_3^-$ undergoes reduction and acts as an oxidising agent.

The change in oxidation state of the oxidising agent is $+5$ to $-1$.

Answer: $+5\ \text{to}\ -1$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following reactions is NOT an example of redox reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{SO}_2 \longrightarrow 4\text{H}^+ + \text{SO}_4^{2-} + 2\text{Cl}^-$
- **(B)** $\text{Cu}^{2+} + \text{Zn} \longrightarrow \text{Cu} + \text{Zn}^{2+}$
- **(C)** $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$
- **(D)** $\text{HCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{HCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$**

**Detailed Solution:**

A redox reaction involves a change in the oxidation states of the elements involved.

In the reaction $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{SO}_2 \longrightarrow 4\text{H}^+ + \text{SO}_4^{2-} + 2\text{Cl}^-$, the oxidation state of $\text{Cl}$ changes from $0$ to $-1$ and $\text{S}$ changes from $+4$ to $+6$. This is a redox reaction.

In the reaction $\text{Cu}^{2+} + \text{Zn} \longrightarrow \text{Cu} + \text{Zn}^{2+}$, the oxidation state of $\text{Cu}$ changes from $+2$ to $0$ and $\text{Zn}$ changes from $0$ to $+2$. This is a redox reaction.

In the reaction $2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$, the oxidation state of $\text{H}$ changes from $0$ to $+1$ and $\text{O}$ changes from $0$ to $-2$. This is a redox reaction.

In the reaction $\text{HCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$, the oxidation state of $\text{H}$ remains $+1$, $\text{Cl}$ remains $-1$, and $\text{O}$ remains $-2$. Since there is no change in the oxidation state of any element, this is an acid-base reaction and not a redox reaction.

Answer: $\text{HCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{H}_3\text{O}^+ + \text{Cl}^-$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following species acts as an weakest reducing agent?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Li}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{F}_2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{F}^{-}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{F}^{-}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

A reducing agent is a substance that donates electrons and undergoes oxidation.

The strength of a reducing agent is inversely proportional to its standard reduction potential ($E^{\circ}$). A lower (more negative) standard reduction potential indicates a stronger reducing agent, while a higher (more positive) standard reduction potential indicates a weaker reducing agent.

In the electrochemical series, fluorine ($\mathrm{F}_2$) has the highest standard reduction potential ($E^{\circ} = +2.87$ V), making it the strongest oxidizing agent. Consequently, its reduced form, the fluoride ion ($\mathrm{F}^{-}$), has the least tendency to lose electrons and undergo oxidation.

Therefore, $\mathrm{F}^{-}$ acts as the weakest reducing agent among the given species. (Note that $\mathrm{Li}$ is the strongest reducing agent, while $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$ and $\mathrm{F}_2$ act as oxidizing agents).

Answer: $\mathrm{F}^{-}$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a reaction, $\mathrm{NH}_4 \mathrm{NO}_2 \rightarrow \mathrm{~N}_2+2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$. Which from following phenomena is true regarding nitrogen?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Oxidised
- **(B)** Reduced
- **(C)** Oxidised as well as reduced
- **(D)** Neither oxidised nor reduced

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Oxidised as well as reduced**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given reaction is $\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{NO}_2 \rightarrow \mathrm{N}_2 + 2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$.

Ammonium nitrite ($\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{NO}_2$) consists of $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ and $\mathrm{NO}_2^-$ ions.

Let the oxidation state of nitrogen in $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ be $x$.
$x + 4(+1) = +1 \Rightarrow x = -3$

Let the oxidation state of nitrogen in $\mathrm{NO}_2^-$ be $y$.
$y + 2(-2) = -1 \Rightarrow y = +3$

In the product $\mathrm{N}_2$, the oxidation state of nitrogen is $0$.

The nitrogen atom in $\mathrm{NH}_4^+$ changes its oxidation state from $-3$ to $0$, which is an increase in oxidation state, hence it is oxidised.

The nitrogen atom in $\mathrm{NO}_2^-$ changes its oxidation state from $+3$ to $0$, which is a decrease in oxidation state, hence it is reduced.

Therefore, nitrogen is oxidised as well as reduced.

Answer: Oxidised as well as reduced

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

Identify correct statement for the reaction.
$\mathrm{I}_2+\mathrm{KClO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{ICl}+\mathrm{KIO}_3$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{I}_2$ is an oxidising agent
- **(B)** $\mathrm{KClO}_3$ is Reducing agent
- **(C)** Oxidation number of 'Cl' decreases by 6
- **(D)** Oxidation number of I increases by 2

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Oxidation number of 'Cl' decreases by 6**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let us determine the oxidation numbers of the elements in the reactants and products.

In the reactant $\mathrm{I}_2$, the oxidation number of $\mathrm{I}$ is $0$.

In $\mathrm{KClO}_3$, let the oxidation number of $\mathrm{Cl}$ be $x$.
$+1 + x + 3(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = +5$

In the product $\mathrm{ICl}$, since $\mathrm{Cl}$ is more electronegative than $\mathrm{I}$, the oxidation number of $\mathrm{Cl}$ is $-1$ and that of $\mathrm{I}$ is $+1$.

In $\mathrm{KIO}_3$, let the oxidation number of $\mathrm{I}$ be $y$.
$+1 + y + 3(-2) = 0 \Rightarrow y = +5$

The oxidation number of $\mathrm{Cl}$ changes from $+5$ in $\mathrm{KClO}_3$ to $-1$ in $\mathrm{ICl}$. Thus, the oxidation number of $\mathrm{Cl}$ decreases by $6$.

Since the oxidation number of $\mathrm{Cl}$ decreases, $\mathrm{KClO}_3$ undergoes reduction and acts as an oxidising agent.

The oxidation number of $\mathrm{I}$ increases from $0$ to $+1$ and $+5$. Thus, $\mathrm{I}_2$ undergoes oxidation and acts as a reducing agent.

Therefore, the only correct statement is that the oxidation number of $\mathrm{Cl}$ decreases by $6$.

Answer: Oxidation number of 'Cl' decreases by 6
