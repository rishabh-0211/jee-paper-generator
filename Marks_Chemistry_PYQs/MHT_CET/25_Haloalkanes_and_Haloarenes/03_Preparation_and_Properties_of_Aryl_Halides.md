### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the product obtained when chlorobenzene is treated with acetyl chloride in presence of anhy. $\text{AlCl}_3$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Mixture of 2-Chloroacetophenone and 4-chloroacetophenone.
- **(B)** Mixture of 2-Chlorotoluene and 4-Chlorotoluene
- **(C)** Mixture of 1, 2-Dichlorobenzene and 1, 4-Dichlorobenzene
- **(D)** Acetophenon and $\text{Cl}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Mixture of 2-Chloroacetophenone and 4-chloroacetophenone.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The reaction of chlorobenzene with acetyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous $AlCl_3$ is a Friedel-Crafts acylation reaction.

The electrophile generated in this reaction is the acylium ion ($CH_3C^{+}O$).

The chlorine atom in chlorobenzene is an ortho-para directing group due to its +R resonance effect, directing the incoming electrophile to the ortho and para positions.

Therefore, the electrophilic attack yields a mixture of 2-chloroacetophenone and 4-chloroacetophenone.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Why does Chlorobenzene exhibit very low reactivity towards nucleophilic substitution reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Formation of phenyl cation is highly stable.
- **(B)** It contains a strong C-Cl bond with partial double-bond character.
- **(C)** It easily forms $SN^1$ intermediate.
- **(D)** The C-Cl bond in chlorobenzene is very weak.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) It contains a strong C-Cl bond with partial double-bond character.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In chlorobenzene, the lone pair of electrons on the chlorine atom is in conjugation with the $\pi$-electrons of the benzene ring. This resonance effect imparts a partial double-bond character to the C-Cl bond.

Due to this partial double-bond character, the C-Cl bond becomes stronger and shorter than a typical C-Cl single bond in alkyl halides. Consequently, it is difficult to break this bond, making chlorobenzene highly unreactive towards nucleophilic substitution reactions.

Additionally, the phenyl cation that would be formed in an $S_N1$ mechanism is highly unstable, further preventing nucleophilic substitution.

Answer: It contains a strong C-Cl bond with partial double-bond character.

</details>
