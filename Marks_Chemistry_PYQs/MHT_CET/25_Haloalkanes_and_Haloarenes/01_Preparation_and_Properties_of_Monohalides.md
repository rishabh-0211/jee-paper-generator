### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The reactivity order of halides for dehydrohalogenation is.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{R-F} > \text{R-Cl} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-I}$
- **(B)** $\text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl} > \text{R-F}$
- **(C)** $\text{R-I} > \text{R-Cl} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-F}$
- **(D)** $\text{R-F} > \text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl} > \text{R-F}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The reactivity of alkyl halides towards dehydrohalogenation depends on the carbon-halogen bond strength and the leaving group ability of the halide ion.

As we move down the halogen group from fluorine to iodine, the size of the halogen atom increases, which increases the carbon-halogen bond length and decreases the bond dissociation energy. The order of bond strength is $\text{C-F} > \text{C-Cl} > \text{C-Br} > \text{C-I}$.

Weaker bonds break more easily, making the iodide ion ($\text{I}^{-}$) the best leaving group and the fluoride ion ($\text{F}^{-}$) the poorest leaving group among the halides.

Therefore, the reactivity order of alkyl halides for dehydrohalogenation is $\text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl} > \text{R-F}$.

Answer: $\text{R-I} > \text{R-Br} > \text{R-Cl} > \text{R-F}$

</details>

Which of the following statements is NOT correct regarding $SN^2$ mechanism?

- **(D)** There is a inversion of configuration.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The transition state is stable.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In the $S_{N}2$ mechanism, the reaction is a single-step concerted process where bond breaking and bond formation occur simultaneously.

Fittig reaction involves the reaction of aryl halides with sodium in dry ether to form diaryls.

Wurtz reaction involves the reaction of alkyl halides with sodium in dry ether to form higher alkanes.

Therefore, Swarts reaction is the correct method for the preparation of alkyl fluorides.

Answer: Swartz reaction

</details>

**Detailed Solution:**

The false statement about SN1 (Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular) reaction mechanism is: (D) It is a single-step mechanism.
The SN1 reaction mechanism is a two-step process. In the first step, the leaving group departs, generating a carbocation intermediate. In the second step, the nucleophile attacks the carbocation, leading to the formation of the substitution product.
The correct statement regarding SN1 reaction mechanism is:
(A) Racemization takes place if the reaction is carried out at a chiral carbon in an optically active substance. (B) Intermediate formed during the reaction is a carbocation. This is true as the leaving group departure creates a carbocation intermediate that is stabilized by the surrounding electrondonating groups or the solvent.
(C) Concentration of nucleophile does not affect the rate of the reaction. This is generally true for $\mathrm{SN} 1$ reactions because the rate-determining step is the formation of the carbocation intermediate, which does not involve the nucleophile. The rate is primarily dependent on the stability of the carbocation and the leaving group ability.
