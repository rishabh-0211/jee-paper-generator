### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which is the correct decreasing order for ionic character of halides, where M is a monovalent metal?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{MF} > \text{MCl} > \text{MBr} > \text{MI}$
- **(B)** $\text{MI} > \text{MBr} > \text{MCl} > \text{MF}$
- **(C)** $\text{MI} > \text{MCl} > \text{MBr} > \text{MF}$
- **(D)** $\text{MF} > \text{MBr} > \text{MCl} > \text{MI}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{MF} > \text{MCl} > \text{MBr} > \text{MI}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Fajan's rules, the covalent character of a bond increases with the size of the anion, assuming the cation remains the same.

The size of the halide ions increases down the group: $\text{F}^- < \text{Cl}^- < \text{Br}^- < \text{I}^-$.

As the size of the anion increases, its polarizability increases, which leads to an increase in the covalent character of the metal halide.

Therefore, the covalent character increases in the order: $\text{MF} < \text{MCl} < \text{MBr} < \text{MI}$.

Consequently, the ionic character follows the reverse order, decreasing as: $\text{MF} > \text{MCl} > \text{MBr} > \text{MI}$.

Alternatively, based on electronegativity, fluorine is the most electronegative halogen. The electronegativity difference between the metal and the halogen is maximum for $\text{MF}$ and minimum for $\text{MI}$. Hence, $\text{MF}$ has the highest ionic character.

Answer: $\text{MF} > \text{MCl} > \text{MBr} > \text{MI}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following metal halides have more covalent character?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{SnCl}_2$
- **(B)** $\text{PbCl}_4$
- **(C)** $\text{SbCl}_3$
- **(D)** $\text{PbCl}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{PbCl}_4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Fajan's rules, the covalent character of a metal halide increases with an increase in the positive charge on the central metal cation. A higher charge on the cation results in greater polarizing power, which increases the covalent character of the bond.

The oxidation states of the central metal cations in the given options are:
$\text{SnCl}_2$: $+2$
$\text{PbCl}_4$: $+4$
$\text{SbCl}_3$: $+3$
$\text{PbCl}_2$: $+2$

Since $\text{Pb}$ in $\text{PbCl}_4$ has the highest oxidation state of $+4$, the $\text{Pb}^{4+}$ ion has the maximum polarizing power. Thus, $\text{PbCl}_4$ has the most covalent character.

Answer: $\text{PbCl}_4$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following compounds has maximum covalent character?

**Options:**

- **(A)** LiCl
- **(B)** LiI
- **(C)** NaCl
- **(D)** NaI

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) LiI**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Fajan's rules, the covalent character of an ionic compound increases with a decrease in the size of the cation and an increase in the size of the anion.

Among the given cations ($\text{Li}^{+}$ and $\text{Na}^{+}$), $\text{Li}^{+}$ has a smaller size, which gives it a higher polarizing power.

Among the given anions ($\text{Cl}^{-}$ and $\text{I}^{-}$), $\text{I}^{-}$ has a larger size, which makes it more polarizable.

Therefore, the combination of the smallest cation ($\text{Li}^{+}$) and the largest anion ($\text{I}^{-}$) results in the maximum covalent character. Thus, $\text{LiI}$ has the maximum covalent character.

Answer: LiI

</details>
