### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following compounds exhibits the highest boiling point?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Propane.
- **(B)** Dimethyl ether.
- **(C)** Methyl chloride.
- **(D)** Methyl cynide.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Methyl cynide.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The boiling point of a compound depends on the strength of its intermolecular forces.

Propane ($CH_3CH_2CH_3$) is a non-polar molecule and exhibits only weak London dispersion forces, resulting in the lowest boiling point.

Dimethyl ether ($CH_3OCH_3$) and methyl chloride ($CH_3Cl$) are polar molecules and exhibit dipole-dipole interactions, giving them higher boiling points than propane.

Methyl cyanide ($CH_3CN$) is highly polar due to the strongly electronegative nitrogen atom and the $C \equiv N$ triple bond, which creates a very large dipole moment. This leads to exceptionally strong dipole-dipole interactions between its molecules.

Therefore, methyl cyanide has the highest boiling point among the given compounds.

Answer: Methyl cynide.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the set of molecules which have similar dipole moment from the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{H}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{BF}_3, \text{CCl}_4$
- **(B)** $\text{H}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{NF}_3, \text{CH}_4$
- **(C)** $\text{H}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{NH}_3, \text{CHCl}_3$
- **(D)** $\text{HBr}, \text{CO}_2, \text{NF}_3, \text{CHCl}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{H}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{BF}_3, \text{CCl}_4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dipole moment of a molecule depends on its geometry and the polarity of its bonds.

$\text{H}_2$ is a homonuclear diatomic molecule, so its dipole moment is zero.

$\text{CO}_2$ has a linear geometry ($\text{O=C=O}$) where the bond dipoles cancel each other out, resulting in a net dipole moment of zero.

$\text{BF}_3$ has a symmetrical trigonal planar geometry, so the three $\text{B-F}$ bond dipoles cancel each other out, giving a net dipole moment of zero.

$\text{CCl}_4$ has a symmetrical tetrahedral geometry, so the four $\text{C-Cl}$ bond dipoles cancel each other out, resulting in a net dipole moment of zero.

Since all the molecules in the first set ($\text{H}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{BF}_3, \text{CCl}_4$) have a net dipole moment of zero, they have similar dipole moments.

In the other options, molecules like $\text{NF}_3$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{CHCl}_3$, and $\text{HBr}$ have asymmetric geometries or different atoms attached to the central atom, leading to non-zero dipole moments.

Answer: $\text{H}_2, \text{CO}_2, \text{BF}_3, \text{CCl}_4$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify a molecule having highest dipole moment from following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{NH}_3$
- **(B)** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$
- **(C)** $\text{CHCl}_3$
- **(D)** $\text{BeF}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{H}_2\text{O}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dipole moment of a molecule depends on its geometry and the electronegativity difference between its constituent atoms.

For $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, the oxygen atom is highly electronegative and possesses two lone pairs. The molecule has a bent structure, and the bond dipoles along with the lone pair dipoles reinforce each other, resulting in a high dipole moment of approximately $1.85\text{ D}$.

For $\text{NH}_3$, the nitrogen atom has one lone pair and the molecule is trigonal pyramidal. Its dipole moment is approximately $1.47\text{ D}$.

For $\text{CHCl}_3$, the molecule has a tetrahedral geometry but is asymmetric. Its dipole moment is approximately $1.04\text{ D}$.

For $\text{BeF}_2$, the molecule is linear and symmetric. The two $\text{Be-F}$ bond dipoles are equal and opposite, cancelling each other out and resulting in a dipole moment of $0\text{ D}$.

Thus, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has the highest dipole moment among the given molecules.

Answer: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

Which of the following order of dipole moment for stated molecules is correct?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{BF}_3 > \text{NF}_3 > \text{NH}_3$
- **(B)** $\text{NF}_3 > \text{BF}_3 > \text{NH}_3$
- **(C)** $\text{NH}_3 > \text{BF}_3 > \text{NF}_3$
- **(D)** $\text{NH}_3 > \text{NF}_3 > \text{BF}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{NH}_3 > \text{NF}_3 > \text{BF}_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\text{BF}_3$ has a symmetrical trigonal planar geometry, so its net dipole moment is zero.

In $\text{NH}_3$, the N-H bond dipoles and the lone pair dipole are in the same direction, which add up to give a high dipole moment.

In $\text{NF}_3$, the N-F bond dipoles and the lone pair dipole are in opposite directions, which partially cancel each other, resulting in a lower dipole moment than $\text{NH}_3$.

Therefore, the correct order of dipole moment is $\text{NH}_3 > \text{NF}_3 > \text{BF}_3$.

Answer: $\text{NH}_3 > \text{NF}_3 > \text{BF}_3$

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is more polar?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{S}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{NH}_3$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{NF}_3$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{CHCl}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{NH}_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The polarity of a molecule is determined by its net dipole moment.

The dipole moments of the given molecules are approximately:
$\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S} \approx 0.95$ D
$\mathrm{NH}_3 \approx 1.47$ D
$\mathrm{NF}_3 \approx 0.23$ D
$\mathrm{CHCl}_3 \approx 1.04$ D

In $\mathrm{NH}_3$, the orbital dipole due to the lone pair is in the same direction as the resultant dipole moment of the three N-H bonds, leading to a high net dipole moment. In $\mathrm{NF}_3$, the orbital dipole opposes the resultant of the N-F bonds, resulting in a low dipole moment.

Thus, $\mathrm{NH}_3$ has the highest dipole moment and is the most polar among the given options.

Answer: $\mathrm{NH}_3$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following molecules has zero dipole moment?

**Options:**

- **(A)** HF
- **(B)** $\mathrm{NH}_3$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{BF}_3$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{CHCl}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{BF}_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In $\mathrm{HF}$, there is an electronegativity difference between hydrogen and fluorine, resulting in a non-zero dipole moment.

In $\mathrm{NH}_3$, the molecule has a trigonal pyramidal geometry with a lone pair on the nitrogen atom. The bond dipoles and the lone pair dipole do not cancel out, resulting in a net non-zero dipole moment.

In $\mathrm{BF}_3$, the molecule has a symmetrical trigonal planar geometry. The three $\mathrm{B}-\mathrm{F}$ bonds are identical and arranged at an angle of $120^{\circ}$ to each other. The vector sum of the three bond dipoles is zero, making the net dipole moment zero.

In $\mathrm{CHCl}_3$, the molecule has a tetrahedral geometry but the $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{H}$ and $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{Cl}$ bonds have different polarities. The asymmetry prevents the bond dipoles from cancelling out, resulting in a non-zero dipole moment.

Answer: $\mathrm{BF}_3$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following molecules exhibits lowest dipole moment?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{~F}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{Cl}$
- **(C)** $\quad \mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{Br}$
- **(D)** $\quad \mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{I}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\quad \mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{I}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dipole moment of a molecule is given by the product of the magnitude of partial charge and the internuclear distance ($\mu = q \times d$).

For methyl halides ($\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{X}$), as we move down the halogen group from $\mathrm{Cl}$ to $\mathrm{I}$, the electronegativity of the halogen decreases significantly. This results in a smaller partial charge separation ($q$) between the carbon and halogen atoms.

Although the bond length ($d$) increases down the group, the decrease in the magnitude of charge ($q$) is more dominant for $\mathrm{Br}$ and $\mathrm{I}$.

The experimental dipole moments of the given molecules are approximately:
$\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{Cl} \approx 1.87 \text{ D}$
$\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{F} \approx 1.85 \text{ D}$
$\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{Br} \approx 1.81 \text{ D}$
$\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{I} \approx 1.62 \text{ D}$

Thus, $\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{I}$ has the lowest dipole moment.

Answer: $\quad \mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{I}$

</details>

### Q14. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If 'Q' is the magnitude of charge and 'r' is the distance between the centres of positive and negative charges then dipole moment $(\mu)$ is given by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mu=\mathrm{Q}+\mathrm{r}$
- **(B)** $\mu=Q \times r$
- **(C)** $\mu=\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{\mathrm{r}}$
- **(D)** $\mu=Q-r$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mu=Q \times r$**

**Detailed Solution:**

If ' $Q$ ' is the magnitude of charge and ' $r$ ' is the distance between the centres of positive and negative charges then dipole moment $(\mu)$ is given by $\boldsymbol{\mu}=\mathbf{Q} \times \mathbf{r}$.
Dipole moment $(\mu)$ is the product of the magnitude of the charge $(Q)$ and the distance between the centres of positive and negative charge (r).
$\therefore \mu=Q \times r$

</details>
