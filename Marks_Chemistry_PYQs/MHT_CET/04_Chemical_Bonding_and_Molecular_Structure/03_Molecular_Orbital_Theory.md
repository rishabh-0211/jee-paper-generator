### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the proportionality between (I) Bond length and size of atom (II) Bond length and multiplicity of bond respectively?

**Options:**

- **(A)** (I)- direct (II)- inverse
- **(B)** (I)- direct (II)- direct
- **(C)** (I)-inverse (II)-direct
- **(D)** (I)-inverse (II)-inverse

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) (I)- direct (II)- inverse**

**Detailed Solution:**

Bond length is the equilibrium distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms in a molecule.

As the size of the bonded atoms increases, the distance between their nuclei increases. Thus, bond length is directly proportional to the size of the atom.

Bond multiplicity refers to the number of bonds between two atoms (single, double, triple). As bond multiplicity increases, the bond becomes stronger and the atoms are pulled closer together, decreasing the bond length. Thus, bond length is inversely proportional to the multiplicity of the bond.

Hence, (I) is direct and (II) is inverse.

Answer: (I)- direct (II)- inverse

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

Which of the following is correct decreasing order of bond length regarding $\mathrm{N}_2, \mathrm{O}_2$ and $\mathrm{Cl}_2$ ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Cl}_2>\mathrm{O}_2>\mathrm{N}_2$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Cl}_2>\mathrm{N}_2>\mathrm{O}_2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{O}_2>\mathrm{N}_2>\mathrm{Cl}_2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{N}_2>\mathrm{O}_2>\mathrm{Cl}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{Cl}_2>\mathrm{O}_2>\mathrm{N}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Bond length depends on the size of the bonded atoms and the bond order.

Chlorine belongs to the third period, so its atomic radius is much larger than that of nitrogen and oxygen, which belong to the second period. Hence, the $\mathrm{Cl}-\mathrm{Cl}$ single bond in $\mathrm{Cl}_2$ is the longest.

In $\mathrm{N}_2$, the nitrogen atoms are bonded by a triple bond ($\mathrm{N}\equiv\mathrm{N}$, bond order $3$). In $\mathrm{O}_2$, the oxygen atoms are bonded by a double bond ($\mathrm{O}=\mathrm{O}$, bond order $2$).

Since bond length is inversely proportional to bond order, the triple bond in $\mathrm{N}_2$ is shorter than the double bond in $\mathrm{O}_2$.

The experimental bond lengths are approximately $199 \text{ pm}$ for $\mathrm{Cl}_2$, $121 \text{ pm}$ for $\mathrm{O}_2$, and $110 \text{ pm}$ for $\mathrm{N}_2$.

Thus, the correct decreasing order of bond length is $\mathrm{Cl}_2 > \mathrm{O}_2 > \mathrm{N}_2$.

Answer: $\mathrm{Cl}_2>\mathrm{O}_2>\mathrm{N}_2$

### Q18. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the orbital having lowest energy from following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2 p
- **(B)** 3 s
- **(C)** 3 d
- **(D)** $4 p$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 2 p**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the $(n + l)$ rule, the energy of an orbital depends on the sum of the principal quantum number $n$ and the azimuthal quantum number $l$.

For $2p$: $n = 2$, $l = 1 \Rightarrow n + l = 3$
For $3s$: $n = 3$, $l = 0 \Rightarrow n + l = 3$
For $3d$: $n = 3$, $l = 2 \Rightarrow n + l = 5$
For $4p$: $n = 4$, $l = 1 \Rightarrow n + l = 5$

For orbitals with the same $(n + l)$ value, the one with the lower value of $n$ has lower energy. Thus, the $2p$ orbital has lower energy than the $3s$ orbital.

Therefore, the $2p$ orbital has the lowest energy among the given options.

Answer: 2 p

</details>
