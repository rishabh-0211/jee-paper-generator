### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is the correct order of lattice energy?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{AlCl}_3 > \text{CaCl}_2 > \text{NaCl}$
- **(B)** $\text{AlCl}_3 > \text{NaCl} > \text{CaCl}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{CaCl}_2 > \text{AlCl}_3 > \text{NaCl}$
- **(D)** $\text{NaCl} > \text{CaCl}_2 > \text{AlCl}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{AlCl}_3 > \text{CaCl}_2 > \text{NaCl}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Lattice energy is directly proportional to the product of the charges on the ions and inversely proportional to the sum of their ionic radii.

Lattice Energy $\propto \dfrac{|Z^+ Z^-|}{r^+ + r^-}$

In the given compounds, the anion is $\text{Cl}^-$ in all cases. The cations are $\text{Al}^{3+}$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, and $\text{Na}^+$.

The product of charges $|Z^+ Z^-|$ for the compounds are:
$\text{AlCl}_3$: $|(+3)(-1)| = 3$
$\text{CaCl}_2$: $|(+2)(-1)| = 2$
$\text{NaCl}$: $|(+1)(-1)| = 1$

Since the charge factor dominates and the ionic radius also decreases in the order $\text{Na}^+ > \text{Ca}^{2+} > \text{Al}^{3+}$, both factors contribute to increasing lattice energy in the same direction.

Thus, the correct order of lattice energy is $\text{AlCl}_3 > \text{CaCl}_2 > \text{NaCl}$.

Answer: $\text{AlCl}_3 > \text{CaCl}_2 > \text{NaCl}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is favourable condition for the formation of ionic bond ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Low ionization enthalpy of metal and low negative value of electron gain enthalpy of non metal.
- **(B)** Low ionization enthalpy of metal and high negative value of electron gain enthalpy of non metal.
- **(C)** High ionization enthalpy of metal and high negative value of electron gain enthalpy of non metal.
- **(D)** High ionization enthalpy of metal and low negative value of electron gain enthalpy of non metal.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Low ionization enthalpy of metal and high negative value of electron gain enthalpy of non metal.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The formation of an ionic bond involves the transfer of electrons from a metal atom to a non-metal atom.

The metal atom loses an electron to form a cation. This process requires energy, which is the ionization enthalpy. A lower ionization enthalpy means the metal can easily lose its valence electrons, favoring cation formation.

The non-metal atom gains an electron to form an anion. This process releases energy, which is measured as the electron gain enthalpy. A high negative value of electron gain enthalpy indicates that a large amount of energy is released, making the formation of the anion highly favorable.

Therefore, the favorable conditions for the formation of an ionic bond are a low ionization enthalpy of the metal and a high negative value of electron gain enthalpy of the non-metal.

Answer: Low ionization enthalpy of metal and high negative value of electron gain enthalpy of non metal.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following compounds has high lattice enthalpy?

**Options:**

- **(A)** LiCl
- **(B)** NaCl
- **(C)** $\mathrm{BeF}_2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{BeF}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Lattice enthalpy is directly proportional to the product of the charges on the ions and inversely proportional to the internuclear distance (sum of ionic radii).

Lattice enthalpy $\propto \dfrac{q_1 q_2}{r^+ + r^-}$

Among the given options, $\mathrm{Be}^{2+}$ and $\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$ have a $+2$ charge, whereas $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$ and $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$ have a $+1$ charge. Due to the higher charge, $\mathrm{BeF}_2$ and $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ will have significantly higher lattice enthalpies than $\mathrm{LiCl}$ and $\mathrm{NaCl}$.

Comparing $\mathrm{BeF}_2$ and $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$, the ionic radius of $\mathrm{Be}^{2+}$ is much smaller than that of $\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$, and the ionic radius of $\mathrm{F}^{-}$ is smaller than that of $\mathrm{Cl}^{-}$.

Because of the smaller sizes of both the cation and the anion in $\mathrm{BeF}_2$, the internuclear distance is the shortest, leading to the strongest electrostatic forces of attraction.

Therefore, $\mathrm{BeF}_2$ has the highest lattice enthalpy.

Answer: $\mathrm{BeF}_2$

</details>
