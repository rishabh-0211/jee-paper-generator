What is the shape of a molecule if it has two lone pairs and three bonding pairs of electrons?

**Options:**

- **(A)** trigonal planar
- **(B)** tetrahedral

- **(D)** T-shape

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) T-shape**

Total number of electron pairs = $3 + 2 = 5$.

The hybridization of the central atom is $sp^3d$, which corresponds to a trigonal bipyramidal electron geometry.

According to VSEPR theory, lone pairs occupy the equatorial positions to minimize lone pair-lone pair and lone pair-bond pair repulsions. Placing the two lone pairs in the equatorial positions leaves the three bonding pairs in one equatorial and two axial positions.

This arrangement results in a T-shaped molecular geometry (e.g., $ClF_3$).

Answer: T-shape

According to VSEPR theory, the electron pairs in the valence shell of the central atom repel each other to minimize electron-electron repulsion.

Lone pairs (Lp) are localized on the central atom and are attracted by only one nucleus, so they occupy more space and exert greater repulsion.

Bond pairs (Bp) are shared between two atoms and are attracted by two nuclei, so they occupy less space and exert lesser repulsion.

Thus, the correct order of repulsion between electron pairs is $\text{Lp-Lp} > \text{Lp-Bp} > \text{Bp-Bp}$.

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) lone pair-lone pair $>$ lone pair-bond pair $>$ bond pair-bond pair**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a molecule, the correct decreasing order of the repulsive interectlon of electron pairs is, lone pair-lone pair > lone pair-bond pair > bond pair-bond pair.
Two atoms share bond pairs of electrons, whereas the lone pair of electrons is only influenced by the central atom. As a result, the electron cloud of a lone pair takes up more space than that of a bond pair. The lone pair-lone pair repulsion is increased as a result of this.
