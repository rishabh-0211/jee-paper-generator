### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the compound having lowest boiling point.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$
- **(B)** $\text{CH}_2(\text{OH})\text{-CH}_2(\text{OH})$
- **(C)** $\text{CH}_3 - \text{O} - \text{C}_2\text{H}_5$
- **(D)** $\text{C}_3\text{H}_7 - \text{OH}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{CH}_3 - \text{O} - \text{C}_2\text{H}_5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Boiling point depends on the strength of intermolecular forces.

$\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CH}_2(\text{OH})\text{-CH}_2(\text{OH})$, and $\text{C}_3\text{H}_7\text{-OH}$ contain $-\text{OH}$ groups, which can form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

$\text{CH}_3\text{-O-C}_2\text{H}_5$ is an ether and lacks hydrogen attached directly to oxygen. Thus, it cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with itself. Its molecules are held together by weaker dipole-dipole interactions and van der Waals forces.

Due to the weakest intermolecular forces, $\text{CH}_3\text{-O-C}_2\text{H}_5$ has the lowest boiling point among the given compounds.

Answer: $\text{CH}_3 - \text{O} - \text{C}_2\text{H}_5$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What types of intermolecular forces are present in $\text{N}_2$ molecules?

**Options:**

- **(A)** London dispersion forces
- **(B)** Dipole-dipole interactions
- **(C)** Dipole-induced dipole interactions
- **(D)** Hydrogen bonding

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) London dispersion forces**

**Detailed Solution:**

The $\text{N}_2$ molecule is a homonuclear diatomic molecule, which means both nitrogen atoms have the same electronegativity.

As a result, the shared electron pair is equally attracted by both atoms, making the $\text{N}_2$ molecule completely non-polar with a zero dipole moment.

Since there is no permanent dipole, dipole-dipole and dipole-induced dipole interactions are absent.

Hydrogen bonding is also not possible because there are no hydrogen atoms present in the molecule.

The only intermolecular forces that exist between non-polar molecules like $\text{N}_2$ are London dispersion forces, which arise due to instantaneous dipole-induced dipole interactions.

Answer: London dispersion forces

</details>

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen bonding that occurs within a single molecule is known as intramolecular hydrogen bonding.

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which is the correct decreasing order of boiling points for different compounds from following ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{CH}_3\text{Cl} > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CHBr}_3$
- **(B)** $\text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CHBr}_3 > \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$
- **(C)** $\text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CHBr}_3 > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$
- **(D)** $\text{CHBr}_3 > \text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{CHBr}_3 > \text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The boiling point of haloalkanes increases with an increase in the molecular mass and the size of the halogen atom due to the stronger van der Waals forces of attraction.

Comparing the given compounds:
$\text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$ has one chlorine atom.
$\text{CH}_3\text{Br}$ has one bromine atom, which is heavier and larger than chlorine.
$\text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2$ has two bromine atoms.
$\text{CHBr}_3$ has three bromine atoms.

The molecular mass follows the order: $\text{CHBr}_3 > \text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$.

Therefore, the decreasing order of boiling points is $\text{CHBr}_3 > \text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$.

Answer: $\text{CHBr}_3 > \text{CH}_2\text{Br}_2 > \text{CH}_3\text{Br} > \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following gases is least soluble in water under same conditions of temperature and pressure ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{HCl}$
- **(B)** $\text{CO}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{NH}_3$
- **(D)** $\text{O}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{O}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Water is a polar solvent. Gases that are polar, can form hydrogen bonds, or react with water are highly soluble in it.

$\text{HCl}$ and $\text{NH}_3$ are highly soluble due to ionization and hydrogen bonding, respectively.

$\text{CO}_2$ is moderately soluble as it reacts with water to form carbonic acid.

$\text{O}_2$ is a non-polar gas and interacts with water only through weak dipole-induced dipole forces, making it the least soluble among the given gases.

Answer: $\text{O}_2$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from following gases is least soluble in water at same temperature and pressure ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{NH}_3$
- **(B)** $\text{CO}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{HCl}$
- **(D)** $\text{N}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{N}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Solubility of a gas in water depends on its ability to interact with water molecules. Water is a polar solvent.

$\text{NH}_3$ is highly soluble in water due to its ability to form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with water.

$\text{HCl}$ is a polar molecule that ionizes completely in water, making it highly soluble.

$\text{CO}_2$ is moderately soluble because it reacts with water to form carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$).

$\text{N}_2$ is a non-polar diatomic gas that neither reacts with water nor forms hydrogen bonds. Therefore, it has the weakest interaction with water molecules and is the least soluble among the given gases.

Answer: $\text{N}_2$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (5 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following forces is involved in dinitrogen?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Dipole - dipole interaction
- **(B)** Dipole - induced dipole interaction
- **(C)** London dispersion force
- **(D)** Hydrogen bonding

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) London dispersion force**

**Detailed Solution:**

Dinitrogen ($N_2$) is a homonuclear diatomic molecule. Since both atoms have the same electronegativity, the molecule is non-polar and has no permanent dipole moment.

The only intermolecular forces that exist between non-polar molecules are London dispersion forces (also known as induced dipole - induced dipole interactions).

Dipole-dipole interactions require permanent dipoles, dipole-induced dipole interactions require at least one polar molecule, and hydrogen bonding requires a hydrogen atom bonded to a highly electronegative atom (F, O, or N). None of these apply to $N_2$.

Answer: London dispersion force

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from the following molecules does not develop intermolecular hydrogen bonding?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Br}_2$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{NH}_3$
- **(D)** HF

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{Br}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen bonding is a special type of dipole-dipole interaction that occurs when a hydrogen atom is covalently bonded to a highly electronegative atom such as fluorine (F), oxygen (O), or nitrogen (N).

In $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$, $\mathrm{NH}_3$, and HF, the hydrogen atom is bonded to O, N, and F respectively, allowing them to form intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

$\mathrm{Br}_2$ is a non-polar molecule and does not contain any hydrogen atoms. Therefore, it cannot form hydrogen bonds. The only intermolecular forces present in $\mathrm{Br}_2$ are weak London dispersion forces.

Answer: $\mathrm{Br}_2$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following molecule can form hydrogen bonding with itself?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{NH}_3$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{C}_2 \mathrm{H}_6$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{~S}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{CH}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{NH}_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen bonding is a special type of dipole-dipole interaction that occurs when a hydrogen atom is covalently bonded to a highly electronegative atom such as fluorine, oxygen, or nitrogen (F, O, N).

In $\mathrm{NH}_3$, the hydrogen atoms are directly bonded to the highly electronegative nitrogen atom, which also possesses a lone pair of electrons. This allows $\mathrm{NH}_3$ molecules to form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with each other.

In $\mathrm{C}_2 \mathrm{H}_6$ and $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{S}$, the hydrogen atoms are bonded to carbon and sulfur respectively, which are not sufficiently electronegative to form hydrogen bonds.

In $\mathrm{CH}_3-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{CH}_3$ (dimethyl ether), there are no hydrogen atoms directly bonded to the oxygen atom, so it cannot act as a hydrogen bond donor to form hydrogen bonds with itself.

Therefore, only $\mathrm{NH}_3$ can form hydrogen bonding with itself.

Answer: $\mathrm{NH}_3$

</details>

### Q16. MHT CET 2020 (16 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What type of inter molecular force is present between magnesium chloride and water?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Dipole-dipole interaction
- **(B)** Ion-dipole interaction
- **(C)** Dipole-induced dipole interaction
- **(D)** Hydrogen bonding

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Ion-dipole interaction**

**Detailed Solution:**

Ion-dipole interaction
Between magnesium chloride and water, there is an ion-dipole interaction. This contact is caused by polar water molecules interacting with a magnesium ion. The water molecule's oxygen atom contains a tiny negative charge, which attracts the positively charged magnesium ion. These are substantially weaker forces than covalent or ionic bonding.

</details>
