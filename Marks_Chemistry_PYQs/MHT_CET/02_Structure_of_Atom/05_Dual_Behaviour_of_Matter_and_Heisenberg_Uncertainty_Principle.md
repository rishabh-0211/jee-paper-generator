### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the frequency of violet light if its wavelength is $400 \text{ nm}$?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4.0 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$
- **(B)** $5.4 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$
- **(C)** $7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$
- **(D)** $4.7 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The relationship between frequency $\nu$, wavelength $\lambda$, and the speed of light $c$ is given by $\nu = \dfrac{c}{\lambda}$.

Given $\lambda = 400 \text{ nm} = 400 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$ and $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.

Substituting the values:

$\nu = \dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{400 \times 10^{-9}}$

$\nu = \dfrac{3}{4} \times 10^{15}$

$\nu = 7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$

Answer: $7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from following colours has highest energy if wave lengths of violet, blue, yellow and red light are 410 nm, 470 nm, 580 nm and 750 nm respectively ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Blue
- **(B)** Violet
- **(C)** Yellow
- **(D)** Red

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Violet**

**Detailed Solution:**

The energy of a photon is given by the Planck-Einstein relation:

$E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

where $h$ is Planck's constant, $c$ is the speed of light, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength.

From the relation, energy is inversely proportional to the wavelength ($E \propto \dfrac{1}{\lambda}$).

The given wavelengths are:
Violet: $410 \text{ nm}$
Blue: $470 \text{ nm}$
Yellow: $580 \text{ nm}$
Red: $750 \text{ nm}$

Since violet light has the shortest wavelength among the given colours, it has the highest energy.

Answer: Violet

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is uncertainty in velocity of an electron if uncertainty in measurement of position is 50 pm ? $(m_e = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{kg},\ h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\text{Js},\ \pi = 3.142)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.98 \times 10^6\ \text{ms}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $1.16 \times 10^6\ \text{ms}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $2.61 \times 10^6\ \text{ms}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $3.77 \times 10^6\ \text{ms}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.16 \times 10^6\ \text{ms}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Heisenberg's uncertainty principle:

$\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \dfrac{h}{4\pi}$

$\Delta x \cdot m \Delta v = \dfrac{h}{4\pi}$

$\Delta v = \dfrac{h}{4\pi m \Delta x}$

Substituting the given values:

$\Delta v = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.142 \times 9.1 \times 10^{-31} \times 50 \times 10^{-12}}$

$\Delta v = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{5718.44 \times 10^{-43}}$

$\Delta v = 1.1594 \times 10^6 \text{ ms}^{-1} \approx 1.16 \times 10^6 \text{ ms}^{-1}$

Answer: $1.16 \times 10^6\ \text{ms}^{-1}$

</details>

### Q13. MHT CET 2021 (24 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

According to de-Broglie hypothesis if an electron of mass ' $\mathrm{m}$ ' is accelerated by potential difference ' $\mathrm{V}$ ' then associated wavelength is ' $\lambda$ '. When a proton of mass ' $M$ ' is accelerated through potential difference ' $9 \mathrm{~V}$ ' then the wavelength associated with it is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\lambda}{3} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{M}}{\mathrm{m}}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\lambda}{3} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{M}}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\lambda}{6} \sqrt{\frac{m}{M}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\lambda}{6} \sqrt{\frac{M}{m}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{\lambda}{3} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{M}}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Do Broglie relation
$$
\lambda=\frac{h}{\mathrm{P}} ...(1)
$$
Energy conservation,
$$
\mathrm{qV}=\mathrm{K} ...(2)
$$
Energy vs momentum relation
$$
K=\frac{p^2}{2 m} ...(3)
$$
Using equation (1), (2) and (3)
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \lambda=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\sqrt{2 \mathrm{Km}}} \\
& \lambda=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\sqrt{2 \mathrm{qVm}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Now, for proton of mass $M$ that is accelerated through $9 \mathrm{~V}$, the de-broglie wavelength can be written as,
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \lambda_{\mathrm{P}}=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\sqrt{2 \mathrm{q}(9 \mathrm{~V}) \mathrm{M}}} \\
& \therefore \frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\mathrm{p}}}=3 \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{M}}{\mathrm{m}}} \\
& \Rightarrow \lambda_{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{\lambda}{3} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{M}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Q14. MHT CET 2021 (21 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When an electron in a hydrogen atom jumps from the third excited state to the ground state, the de-Broglie wavelength associated with the electron becomes

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{32}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{8}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{16}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The De Broglie wavelength is $\lambda=\frac{h}{p}$, where $h=$ Planck's constant and $p=$ momentum of the electron. Also, momentum $p=m v$ and kinetic energy $E_K=\frac{1}{2} m v^2$ From the above equation, we can relate momentum $p$ and kinetic energy $E_K$ as, $p=\sqrt{2 m E_K}$
Now the ratio of wavelength of hydrogen atom when the atom jumps from third excited state to ground state viz $(n=4$ to $n=1$ )
$$
\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}=\frac{\frac{h}{p_1}}{\frac{h}{p_2}}=\frac{p_2}{p_1}=\sqrt{\frac{E_{K 2}}{E_{K 1}}}
$$

Also, we know $E_{K n}=\frac{-13.6 Z^2}{n^2}$
Using equation (1),
$$
\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}=\frac{\sqrt{1^2}}{\sqrt{4^2}}
$$
So, the ratio of the wavelength of ground state $\{n=1\}$ to $3^{\text {rd }}$ excited state $\{n=4\}$ is $\frac{1}{4}$.

</details>
