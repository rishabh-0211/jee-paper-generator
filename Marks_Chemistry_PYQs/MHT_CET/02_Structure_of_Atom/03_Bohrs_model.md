### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the energy of an electron in a hydrogen atom whose angular momentum is $h/\pi$ ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J
- **(B)** $-5.45 \times 10^{-19}$ J
- **(C)** $-2.42 \times 10^{-19}$ J
- **(D)** $-1.36 \times 10^{-19}$ J

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $-5.45 \times 10^{-19}$ J**

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular momentum of an electron in the $n$-th orbit is given by Bohr's quantization rule:

$L = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

Given that the angular momentum is $\dfrac{h}{\pi}$:

$\dfrac{nh}{2\pi} = \dfrac{h}{\pi}$

$n = 2$

The energy of an electron in the $n$-th orbit of a hydrogen atom is given by:

$E_n = \dfrac{-2.18 \times 10^{-18}}{n^2} \text{ J}$

Substituting $n = 2$:

$E_2 = \dfrac{-2.18 \times 10^{-18}}{2^2} \text{ J}$

$E_2 = \dfrac{-2.18 \times 10^{-18}}{4} \text{ J}$

$E_2 = -0.545 \times 10^{-18} \text{ J} = -5.45 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$

Answer: $-5.45 \times 10^{-19}$ J

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the wavelength of the photon emitted during the transition from the orbit of $n = 4$ to that of $n = 2$ in hydrogen atom?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $434$ nm
- **(B)** $486$ nm
- **(C)** $656$ nm
- **(D)** $410$ nm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $486$ nm**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the Rydberg formula for the hydrogen atom:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$

Given $n_1 = 2$ and $n_2 = 4$:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2} \right) = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{16} \right) = R_H \left( \dfrac{3}{16} \right)$

$\lambda = \dfrac{16}{3 R_H}$

Substituting $R_H = 1.097 \times 10^7 \text{ m}^{-1}$:

$\lambda = \dfrac{16}{3 \times 1.097 \times 10^7} \text{ m}$

$\lambda \approx 4.86 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m} = 486 \text{ nm}$

Alternatively, using the energy difference:

$\Delta E = 13.6 \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2} \right) \text{ eV} = 13.6 \times \dfrac{3}{16} \text{ eV} = 2.55 \text{ eV}$

$\lambda = \dfrac{1242 \text{ eV nm}}{\Delta E} = \dfrac{1242}{2.55} \text{ nm} \approx 486 \text{ nm}$

Answer: $486$ nm

</details>

**Options:**

Answer: $\mathrm{Li}^{+}$

### Q12. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following statements is NOT correct regarding Bohr model?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It failed to account for finer details of hydrogen spectrum.
- **(B)** It is unable to explain atomic spectrum other than hydrogen.
- **(C)** It explains Zeeman effect.
- **(D)** It failed to explain ability of atoms to form molecules by chemical bonds.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) It explains Zeeman effect.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The Bohr model of the atom has several limitations:

1. It failed to account for the finer details (fine structure) of the hydrogen atom spectrum observed using sophisticated spectroscopic techniques.

2. It is unable to explain the spectrum of atoms other than hydrogen (multi-electron atoms).

3. It failed to explain the splitting of spectral lines in the presence of a magnetic field (Zeeman effect) or an electric field (Stark effect).

4. It could not explain the ability of atoms to form molecules by chemical bonds.

Therefore, the statement that it explains the Zeeman effect is incorrect.

Answer: It explains Zeeman effect.

</details>
