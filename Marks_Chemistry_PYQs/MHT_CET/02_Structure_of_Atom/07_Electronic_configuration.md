### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The number of unpaired electrons in $\text{Cu}^{+2}$ and $\text{Fe}^{+3}$ respectively are-

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2$ and $3$
- **(B)** $3$ and $2$
- **(C)** $1$ and $5$
- **(D)** $2$ and $4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1$ and $5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The atomic number of $\text{Cu}$ is $29$. Its electronic configuration is $[\text{Ar}] 3d^{10} 4s^{1}$.

For $\text{Cu}^{+2}$, two electrons are removed (one from $4s$ and one from $3d$), giving the configuration $[\text{Ar}] 3d^{9}$. This configuration has $1$ unpaired electron.

The atomic number of $\text{Fe}$ is $26$. Its electronic configuration is $[\text{Ar}] 3d^{6} 4s^{2}$.

For $\text{Fe}^{+3}$, three electrons are removed (two from $4s$ and one from $3d$), giving the configuration $[\text{Ar}] 3d^{5}$. This configuration has $5$ unpaired electrons.

Thus, the number of unpaired electrons in $\text{Cu}^{+2}$ and $\text{Fe}^{+3}$ are $1$ and $5$ respectively.

Answer: $1$ and $5$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the number of unpaired electrons present in ground state of Silicon and Chromium, respectively ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2 and 4
- **(B)** 2 and 6
- **(C)** 0 and 6
- **(D)** 4 and 5

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 2 and 6**

**Detailed Solution:**

The atomic number of Silicon (Si) is $14$.

The electronic configuration of Si is $1s^{2} 2s^{2} 2p^{6} 3s^{2} 3p^{2}$.

In the $3p$ subshell, there are $2$ electrons which occupy separate orbitals according to Hund's rule. Thus, Si has $2$ unpaired electrons.

The atomic number of Chromium (Cr) is $24$.

The electronic configuration of Cr is $[Ar] 3d^{5} 4s^{1}$.

In the $3d$ subshell, there are $5$ unpaired electrons and in the $4s$ subshell, there is $1$ unpaired electron. Thus, Cr has $6$ unpaired electrons.

The number of unpaired electrons in Si and Cr are $2$ and $6$, respectively.

Answer: 2 and 6

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the observed electronic configuration of Cu ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^9\ 4s^2$
- **(B)** $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^{10}\ 4s^1$
- **(C)** $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^5\ 4s^2$
- **(D)** $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^6\ 4s^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^{10}\ 4s^1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The atomic number of copper (Cu) is $29$.

According to the Aufbau principle, the expected electronic configuration is $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^9\ 4s^2$.

However, a completely filled $d$-subshell ($3d^{10}$) provides extra stability due to symmetrical electron distribution and higher exchange energy.

Therefore, one electron from the $4s$ orbital is transferred to the $3d$ orbital, resulting in the observed electronic configuration of $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^{10}\ 4s^1$.

Answer: $[\text{Ar}]\ 3d^{10}\ 4s^1$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

What is the atomic number of an element having $ns^1$ electronic configuration and belonging to 3d transition series?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Only 24
- **(B)** Only 25
- **(C)** Only 29
- **(D)** 24 and 29

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 24 and 29**

**Detailed Solution:**

The 3d transition series belongs to the 4th period, so $n=4$.

The general electronic configuration of the 3d transition series is $[Ar] 3d^{1-10} 4s^{1-2}$.

Elements with an $ns^1$ (i.e., $4s^1$) configuration in this series are Chromium (Cr) and Copper (Cu) due to the extra stability of half-filled and fully-filled d-subshells.

The electronic configuration of Cr ($Z=24$) is $[Ar] 3d^5 4s^1$.

The electronic configuration of Cu ($Z=29$) is $[Ar] 3d^{10} 4s^1$.

Thus, the atomic numbers are 24 and 29.

Answer: 24 and 29

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The electronic configurations of elements are as-
$A = 1s^2 \ 2s^2 \ 2p^6 \ 3s^2 \ 3p^6 \ 4s^2$
$B = 1s^2 \ 2s^2 \ 2p^6 \ 3s^2 \ 3p^5$
Which of the following is formula of ionic compound that could be formed between these two elements A and B?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{A}_2\text{B}$
- **(B)** $\text{AB}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{AB}_5$
- **(D)** $\text{A}_5\text{B}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{AB}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Element A has the electronic configuration $1s^2 \ 2s^2 \ 2p^6 \ 3s^2 \ 3p^6 \ 4s^2$. It has $2$ valence electrons in its outermost shell, so it loses $2$ electrons to achieve a stable noble gas configuration, forming the $\text{A}^{2+}$ ion.

Element B has the electronic configuration $1s^2 \ 2s^2 \ 2p^6 \ 3s^2 \ 3p^5$. It has $7$ valence electrons in its outermost shell, so it gains $1$ electron to achieve a stable noble gas configuration, forming the $\text{B}^{-}$ ion.

To form a neutral ionic compound, one $\text{A}^{2+}$ ion combines with two $\text{B}^{-}$ ions.

The formula of the compound is $\text{AB}_2$.

Answer: $\text{AB}_2$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What are the total number of electrons present in s and p orbitals respectively in the argon?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $12$ and $6$
- **(B)** $8$ and $12$
- **(C)** $6$ and $12$
- **(D)** $18$ and $6$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $6$ and $12$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The atomic number of argon (Ar) is $18$.

The electronic configuration of argon is $1s^{2} 2s^{2} 2p^{6} 3s^{2} 3p^{6}$.

The total number of electrons present in s-orbitals is $2 + 2 + 2 = 6$.

The total number of electrons present in p-orbitals is $6 + 6 = 12$.

Therefore, the number of electrons in s and p orbitals are $6$ and $12$ respectively.

Answer: $6$ and $12$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

"Pairing of electrons in the orbitals belonging to the same subshell does not occur unless each orbital belonging to that subshell has got one electron each." This statement is known as

**Options:**

- **(A)** Pauli's exclusion principle
- **(B)** Hund's rule
- **(C)** Aufbau rule
- **(D)** Heisenberg uncertainty principle

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Hund's rule**

**Detailed Solution:**

The statement "Pairing of electrons in the orbitals belonging to the same subshell does not occur unless each orbital belonging to that subshell has got one electron each" is the definition of Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity.

Pauli's exclusion principle states that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers.

Aufbau principle states that orbitals are filled in order of their increasing energies.

Heisenberg uncertainty principle states that it is impossible to determine simultaneously the exact position and exact momentum of an electron.

Answer: Hund's rule

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following elements contains maximum number of unpaired electrons?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Fluorine
- **(B)** Sodium
- **(C)** Nitrogen
- **(D)** Oxygen

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Nitrogen**

**Detailed Solution:**

The electronic configuration of the given elements are as follows:

Fluorine (Z = 9): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^5$. It has 1 unpaired electron in the 2p subshell.

Sodium (Z = 11): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$. It has 1 unpaired electron in the 3s subshell.

Nitrogen (Z = 7): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^3$. According to Hund's rule, the three electrons in the 2p subshell are singly occupied, so it has 3 unpaired electrons.

Oxygen (Z = 8): $1s^2 2s^2 2p^4$. It has 2 unpaired electrons in the 2p subshell.

Nitrogen contains the maximum number of unpaired electrons (3).

Answer: Nitrogen

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

$"$ No two electrons in an atom can have the identical set of four quantum numbers." The statement is known as

**Options:**

- **(A)** Pauli's exclusion principle.
- **(B)** Hund's rule.
- **(C)** Aufbau rule.
- **(D)** Heisenberg uncertainty principle.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Pauli's exclusion principle.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The statement given is the exact definition of Pauli's exclusion principle, which states that no two electrons in an atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers ($n$, $l$, $m$, and $s$).

Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity states that electron pairing in degenerate orbitals does not take place until each orbital is singly occupied.

The Aufbau principle states that in the ground state of an atom, electrons fill atomic orbitals in the increasing order of orbital energy level.

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle states that it is impossible to determine simultaneously the exact position and exact momentum of a microscopic particle like an electron.

Answer: Pauli's exclusion principle.

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following rules states that it is impossible to determine simultaneously the exact position and exact momentum of an electron?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Aufbau's principle
- **(B)** de Broglie's hypothesis
- **(C)** Heisenberg uncertainty principle
- **(D)** Pauli's exclusion principle

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Heisenberg uncertainty principle**

**Detailed Solution:**

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle states that it is impossible to determine simultaneously, the exact position and exact momentum (or velocity) of an electron.

Mathematically, it is given by $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \dfrac{h}{4\pi}$, where $\Delta x$ is the uncertainty in position and $\Delta p$ is the uncertainty in momentum.

Answer: Heisenberg uncertainty principle

</details>
