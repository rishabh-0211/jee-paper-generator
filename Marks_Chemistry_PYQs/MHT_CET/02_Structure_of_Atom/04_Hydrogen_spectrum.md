### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate the longest wavelength in the hydrogen emission spectrum of the Balmer series. [$\tilde{v} = 109677\ \text{cm}^{-1}$]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.565 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{cm}$
- **(B)** $3.647 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{cm}$
- **(C)** $4.377 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{cm}$
- **(D)** $1.824 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $6.565 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The Rydberg formula for the wavelength of emitted light is given by:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = \tilde{v} \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$

For the Balmer series, the lower energy level is $n_1 = 2$. The longest wavelength corresponds to the minimum energy transition, which occurs when the electron falls from the nearest higher energy level, $n_2 = 3$.

Substituting the values into the formula:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 109677 \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2} \right)$

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 109677 \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9} \right)$

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 109677 \left( \dfrac{5}{36} \right)$

$\lambda = \dfrac{36}{109677 \times 5}$

$\lambda = \dfrac{36}{548385}$

$\lambda = 6.565 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{cm}$

Answer: $6.565 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{cm}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Calculate the shortest wavelength in hydrogen spectrum emission of Lymen series ($R_H = 109677\,\text{cm}^{-1}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9.117 \times 10^{-6}$ cm
- **(B)** $9.241 \times 10^{-6}$ cm
- **(C)** $9.360 \times 10^{-6}$ cm
- **(D)** $9.482 \times 10^{-6}$ cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $9.117 \times 10^{-6}$ cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the Lyman series, the transition is from $n_2$ to $n_1 = 1$.

The shortest wavelength corresponds to the maximum energy transition, which occurs when $n_2 = \infty$.

Using the Rydberg formula:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$

Substituting $n_1 = 1$ and $n_2 = \infty$:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{1^2} - \dfrac{1}{\infty^2} \right) = R_H$

$\lambda = \dfrac{1}{R_H}$

Given $R_H = 109677 \text{ cm}^{-1}$:

$\lambda = \dfrac{1}{109677} \text{ cm} \approx 9.117 \times 10^{-6} \text{ cm}$

Answer: $9.117 \times 10^{-6}$ cm

</details>

Calculate the frequency in $\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{z}}$ of orange colour light having wave length $600 \mathrm{~nm} .\left[\mathrm{C}=3 \times 10^8 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}\right]$

The wavelength of a spectral line of caesium is $460 \mathrm{~nm}$. What is the frequency of spectral line?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4.5 \times 10^8 \mathrm{~Hz}$
- **(B)** $6.5 \times 10^{14} \mathrm{~Hz}$
- **(C)** $3 \times 10^9 \mathrm{~Hz}$
- **(D)** $5.6 \times 10^{14}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $6.5 \times 10^{14} \mathrm{~Hz}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& v=\frac{\mathrm{c}}{\lambda}\left(1 \mathrm{~nm}=10^{-9} \mathrm{~m}\right) \\
& =\frac{3 \times 10^8}{460 \times 10^{-9}} \\
& =6.5 \times 10^{14} \mathrm{~Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$
