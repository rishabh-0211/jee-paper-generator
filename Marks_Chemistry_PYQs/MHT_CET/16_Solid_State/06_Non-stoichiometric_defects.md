### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the type of defect in brass alloy.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Interstitial impurity defect
- **(B)** Schottky defect
- **(C)** Substitutional impurity defect
- **(D)** Metal deficiency defect

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Substitutional impurity defect**

**Detailed Solution:**

Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc.

In the crystal lattice of brass, zinc atoms replace some of the copper atoms at the regular lattice sites.

This type of defect, where foreign atoms occupy the regular lattice sites of the host atoms, is known as a substitutional impurity defect.

Answer: Substitutional impurity defect

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the defect that develops when the ratio of number of cations to anions become different from that indicated by its chemical formula.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Schottky defect
- **(B)** interstitial defect
- **(C)** substitutional impurity defect
- **(D)** nonstoichiometric defect

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) nonstoichiometric defect**

**Detailed Solution:**

When the ratio of the number of cations to anions becomes different from that indicated by the ideal chemical formula, the stoichiometry of the crystal is disturbed.

Such defects are called nonstoichiometric defects. In these defects, the crystal remains electrically neutral, but the ratio of constituent ions changes due to metal excess or metal deficiency.

Schottky and interstitial defects are stoichiometric defects, where the ratio of cations to anions remains the same as in the chemical formula.

Answer: nonstoichiometric defect

</details>
