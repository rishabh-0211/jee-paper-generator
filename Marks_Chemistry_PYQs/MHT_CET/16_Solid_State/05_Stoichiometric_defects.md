### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statement is correct in the context of defects in a crystal?

**Options:**

- **(A)** AgCl as well as $\text{CaF}_2$ has a schottky defect.
- **(B)** AgCl as well as $\text{CaF}_2$ have anion Frenkel defect.
- **(C)** AgCl has cation Frenkel defect and $\text{CaF}_2$ has anion Frenkel defect.
- **(D)** AgCl has cation Frenkel defect and $\text{CaF}_2$ has schottky defect.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) AgCl has cation Frenkel defect and $\text{CaF}_2$ has anion Frenkel defect.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In $\text{AgCl}$, the size of the $\text{Ag}^{+}$ ion is smaller than that of the $\text{Cl}^{-}$ ion. This allows the $\text{Ag}^{+}$ ions to leave their normal lattice sites and occupy interstitial sites, resulting in a cation Frenkel defect.

In $\text{CaF}_2$, the size of the $\text{F}^{-}$ ion is smaller than that of the $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ion. The $\text{F}^{-}$ ions can leave their normal lattice sites and occupy interstitial sites, resulting in an anion Frenkel defect.

Answer: AgCl has cation Frenkel defect and $\text{CaF}_2$ has anion Frenkel defect.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following salts does NOT exhibit Frenkel defect?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{NaCl}$
- **(B)** $\text{AgCl}$
- **(C)** $\text{CaF}_2$
- **(D)** $\text{ZnS}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{NaCl}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Frenkel defect is exhibited by ionic compounds in which there is a large difference in the size of cations and anions. In such defects, the smaller ion (usually the cation) is dislocated from its normal site to an interstitial site. Examples include $\text{ZnS}$, $\text{AgCl}$, $\text{AgBr}$, and $\text{AgI}$.

$\text{NaCl}$ consists of $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ ions which are of comparable sizes. Therefore, it exhibits Schottky defect rather than Frenkel defect.

$\text{CaF}_2$ exhibits anion Frenkel defect because the $\text{F}^-$ ions can occupy interstitial sites.

Answer: $\text{NaCl}$

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** It arises due to an equal number of actions and anions are missing from their regular poisons in the crystal lattice.
- **(B)** It arises when an ion of an ionic compound is missing from its regular site and occupies an interstitial position.

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

Frenkel defect is a type of point defect in crystalline solids where an ion (usually a cation, due to its smaller size) is displaced from its regular lattice site and occupies an interstitial site.

The statement describing an equal number of cations and anions missing from their regular sites refers to a Schottky defect, which causes a decrease in density.

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What happens in Schottky defect?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The density of crystal increases.
- **(B)** Unequal number of cations and anions are missing from the lattice.
- **(C)** Either cation or anion leaves its normal site and occupies interstitial site.
- **(D)** Equal number of cations and anions are missing from their regular positions in the crystal lattice.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Equal number of cations and anions are missing from their regular positions in the crystal lattice.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a Schottky defect, an equal number of cations and anions are missing from their regular lattice sites to maintain electrical neutrality.

Due to the missing ions, the mass of the crystal decreases while the volume remains the same, leading to a decrease in the density of the crystal.

The defect where an ion leaves its normal site and occupies an interstitial site is known as a Frenkel defect.

Answer: Equal number of cations and anions are missing from their regular positions in the crystal lattice.

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the defect developed when an ion of an ionic compound is missing from its regular site and occupies interstitial position between lattice points

**Options:**

- **(A)** Schottky defect
- **(B)** Frenkel defect
- **(C)** Substitutional impurity defect
- **(D)** Interstitial impurity defect

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Frenkel defect**

**Detailed Solution:**

When an ion of an ionic compound is missing from its regular lattice site and occupies an interstitial position between lattice points, the defect is known as a Frenkel defect.

In this defect, a vacancy is created at the original site and an interstitial defect is created at the new location. It is typically shown by ionic solids where there is a large difference in the size of ions (usually the smaller cation is dislocated).

Answer: Frenkel defect

### Q10. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the type of defect from following in stainless steel.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Substitutional impurity defect
- **(B)** Interstitial impurity defect
- **(C)** Metal excess defect
- **(D)** Frenkel defect

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Interstitial impurity defect**

**Detailed Solution:**

In an impurity defect, foreign atoms are present in the crystal lattice of the host.

When the impurity atoms replace host atoms at regular lattice sites, it is a substitutional impurity defect. When the impurity atoms occupy the interstitial spaces (voids) of the host lattice, it is an interstitial impurity defect.

Steel is iron with a small amount of carbon. The Fe atoms occupy the regular lattice sites, while the much smaller C atoms occupy the interstitial spaces of the Fe lattice.

Therefore, stainless steel is an example of an interstitial impurity defect.

</details>
