### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is an example of ferromagnetic substance?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{NaCl}$.
- **(B)** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$.
- **(C)** $\text{O}_2$.
- **(D)** $\text{Gd}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{Gd}$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic properties of the given substances are as follows:

$\text{NaCl}$ is a diamagnetic substance because all its electrons are paired.

$\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$ (benzene) is also a diamagnetic substance.

$\text{O}_2$ is a paramagnetic substance due to the presence of two unpaired electrons in its anti-bonding molecular orbitals.

$\text{Gd}$ (Gadolinium) is a ferromagnetic substance, along with other metals like iron, cobalt, and nickel.

Answer: $\text{Gd}$.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following dopants is used in germanium to form n-type semiconductor ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** B
- **(B)** In
- **(C)** Ga
- **(D)** P

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) P**

**Detailed Solution:**

Germanium (Ge) is a Group 14 element.

To form an n-type semiconductor, it must be doped with a pentavalent impurity, which is an element from Group 15 of the periodic table.

Among the given options, Boron (B), Indium (In), and Gallium (Ga) are Group 13 elements (trivalent impurities) and will form p-type semiconductors when doped with Germanium.

Phosphorus (P) is a Group 15 element (pentavalent impurity). When Germanium is doped with Phosphorus, it provides an extra electron, resulting in an n-type semiconductor.

Answer: P

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the false statement regarding magnetic properties of substance.
(A) Paramagnetic substances are weakly attracted.
(B) Diamagnetic substances are weakly attracted.
(C) Ferromagnetic substances strongly attracted.
(D) Diamagnetic substance strongly attracted

**Options:**

- **(A)** (A) and (C) only
- **(B)** (A), (B), (C) and (D)
- **(C)** (B) and (D) only
- **(D)** (A) and (B) only

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) (B) and (D) only**

**Detailed Solution:**

Paramagnetic substances are weakly attracted by a magnetic field.

Diamagnetic substances are weakly repelled by a magnetic field.

Ferromagnetic substances are strongly attracted by a magnetic field.

Statements (B) and (D) claim that diamagnetic substances are attracted, which is incorrect.

Therefore, statements (B) and (D) are false.

Answer: (B) and (D) only

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

n-type of semiconductor is formed when

**Options:**

- **(A)** Silicon doped with phosphorous
- **(B)** Silicon doped with boron
- **(C)** Germanium doped with boron
- **(D)** Germanium doped with aluminium

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Silicon doped with phosphorous**

**Detailed Solution:**

An n-type semiconductor is formed when an intrinsic semiconductor (like Silicon or Germanium) is doped with a pentavalent impurity (Group 15 elements such as Phosphorus, Arsenic, or Antimony).

Phosphorus is a pentavalent impurity. When Silicon is doped with Phosphorus, four of its five valence electrons form covalent bonds with the surrounding Silicon atoms, while the fifth electron remains loosely bound and is available for conduction, resulting in an n-type semiconductor.

Boron and Aluminium are trivalent impurities (Group 13 elements). Doping Silicon or Germanium with them creates holes, resulting in a p-type semiconductor.

Answer: Silicon doped with phosphorous

</details>

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>
