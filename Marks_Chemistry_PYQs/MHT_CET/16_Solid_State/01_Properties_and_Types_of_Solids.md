### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following has dipole-induced dipole interaction as inter molecular force?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{NH}_3+\mathrm{C}_6 \mathrm{H}_6$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{NaCl}+\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{CH}_4+\mathrm{C}_2 \mathrm{H}_6$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{HF}+\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{NH}_3+\mathrm{C}_6 \mathrm{H}_6$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Dipole-induced dipole interactions occur between a polar molecule (which has a permanent dipole moment) and a non-polar molecule (which lacks a permanent dipole moment).

$\mathrm{NH}_3$ is a polar molecule, while $\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_6$ (benzene) is a non-polar molecule. Therefore, the interaction between them is dipole-induced dipole.

$\mathrm{NaCl}$ and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$ exhibit ion-dipole interactions.

$\mathrm{CH}_4$ and $\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_6$ are both non-polar, so they exhibit London dispersion forces (induced dipole-induced dipole).

$\mathrm{HF}$ and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$ are both polar and exhibit hydrogen bonding (a strong type of dipole-dipole interaction).

Answer: $\mathrm{NH}_3+\mathrm{C}_6 \mathrm{H}_6$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which among the following forces of attraction is developed between polar and non polar molecules?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Dipole-induced dipole interaction
- **(B)** Ion-dipole interaction
- **(C)** Dipole-dipole interaction
- **(D)** van der Waals forces

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Dipole-induced dipole interaction**

**Detailed Solution:**

Polar molecules have a permanent dipole moment, whereas non-polar molecules do not have a permanent dipole moment.

When a polar molecule approaches a non-polar molecule, the permanent dipole of the polar molecule distorts the electron cloud of the non-polar molecule, inducing a temporary dipole in it.

The attractive force that develops between the permanent dipole and the induced dipole is known as dipole-induced dipole interaction.

Answer: Dipole-induced dipole interaction

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify a zero dimensional nano structure from following

**Options:**

- **(A)** Nanorods
- **(B)** Nanoparticles
- **(C)** Thin films
- **(D)** Fibres

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Nanoparticles**

**Detailed Solution:**

Nanomaterials are classified based on the number of dimensions that are not confined to the nanoscale range (less than $100$ nm).

Zero-dimensional ($0$D) nanostructures have all three dimensions in the nanoscale range. Examples include nanoparticles, quantum dots, and fullerenes.

One-dimensional ($1$D) nanostructures have two dimensions in the nanoscale and one dimension outside it. Examples include nanorods, nanowires, and nanofibres.

Two-dimensional ($2$D) nanostructures have one dimension in the nanoscale and two dimensions outside it. Examples include thin films, nanosheets, and coatings.

Therefore, nanoparticles are zero-dimensional nanostructures.

Answer: Nanoparticles

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from the following statements is NOT true regarding crystalline solid?

**Options:**

- **(A)** There is a regularity and periodicity in arrangement of constituent particles.
- **(B)** It is isotropic.
- **(C)** It melts at definite temperature.
- **(D)** It has different magnitudes for refractive index in every direction.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) It is isotropic.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Crystalline solids have a regular and periodic arrangement of constituent particles.

They have a sharp and definite melting point.

Due to the ordered arrangement of particles, crystalline solids are anisotropic in nature, meaning their physical properties like refractive index, electrical resistance, etc., have different magnitudes when measured along different directions.

Amorphous solids are isotropic. Therefore, the statement that a crystalline solid is isotropic is incorrect.

Answer: It is isotropic.

</details>
