### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calulate $\Delta S_{(total)}$ for following reaction.
$\text{CH}_3\text{OH}_{(l)} + 3/2 \text{O}_{2(g)} \rightarrow \text{CO}_{2(g)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(l)}$, $\Delta H = -726$ kJ mol$^{-1}$, $\Delta S_{(system)} = 42$ JK$^{-1}$
(both at $298$ K)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.478$ kJ K$^{-1}$
- **(B)** $-2.378$ kJ K$^{-1}$
- **(C)** $39.58$ kJ K$^{-1}$
- **(D)** $44.42$ kJ K$^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2.478$ kJ K$^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The total entropy change is given by the sum of the entropy change of the system and the surroundings:

$\Delta S_{(total)} = \Delta S_{(system)} + \Delta S_{(surrounding)}$

The entropy change of the surroundings can be calculated using the enthalpy change of the system:

$\Delta S_{(surrounding)} = -\dfrac{\Delta H_{(system)}}{T}$

Substituting the given values:

$\Delta S_{(surrounding)} = -\dfrac{-726 \times 10^3}{298} = 2436.24 \text{ J K}^{-1}$

Now, calculating the total entropy change:

$\Delta S_{(total)} = 42 + 2436.24 = 2478.24 \text{ J K}^{-1}$

Converting to kilojoules:

$\Delta S_{(total)} = 2.478 \text{ kJ K}^{-1}$

Answer: $2.478$ kJ K$^{-1}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following statements regarding entropy is correct?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It decreases in spontaneous process.
- **(B)** Change in entropy of sublimation is negative.
- **(C)** It measures randomness.
- **(D)** It is path dependent.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) It measures randomness.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Entropy is a thermodynamic state function that measures the degree of randomness or disorder of a system.

For a spontaneous process, the total entropy of the universe increases, meaning $\Delta S_{total} > 0$.

Sublimation involves the conversion of a solid directly into a gas, which leads to an increase in randomness, so $\Delta S_{sublimation}$ is positive.

Since entropy is a state function, its change depends only on the initial and final states of the system and is independent of the path followed.

Therefore, the only correct statement is that entropy measures randomness.

Answer: It measures randomness.

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following processes has $\Delta S$ negative?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Dissolving Sugar in Water
- **(B)** Adsorption of a Gas on a Solid Surface
- **(C)** $2\text{NH}_3(g) \rightarrow \text{N}_2(g) + 3\text{H}_2(g)$
- **(D)** Sublimation of Dry Ice

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Adsorption of a Gas on a Solid Surface**

**Detailed Solution:**

Entropy ($\Delta S$) is a measure of randomness or disorder in a system.

For dissolving sugar in water, solid sugar molecules break apart and mix with water, increasing the randomness. Thus, $\Delta S > 0$.

For the adsorption of a gas on a solid surface, the gas molecules are restricted to the surface of the solid, which decreases their translational degrees of freedom. The randomness of the system decreases, leading to $\Delta S < 0$.

For the reaction $2\text{NH}_3(g) \rightarrow \text{N}_2(g) + 3\text{H}_2(g)$, the number of gaseous moles increases from $2$ to $4$ ($\Delta n_g = 2$). An increase in gaseous moles increases randomness, so $\Delta S > 0$.

For the sublimation of dry ice, solid $\text{CO}_2$ converts into gaseous $\text{CO}_2$. Since gases are more disordered than solids, $\Delta S > 0$.

Therefore, the only process with a negative $\Delta S$ is the adsorption of a gas on a solid surface.

Answer: Adsorption of a Gas on a Solid Surface

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a reversible expansion, one mole of gas is expanded isothermally at 200 K till its volume increases 10 times. Calculate $\Delta S_{\text {total }}$ at equilibrium?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Zero J K$^{-1}$
- **(B)** 19.14 J K$^{-1}$
- **(C)** 38.9 J K$^{-1}$
- **(D)** -19.14 J K$^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Zero J K$^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a reversible process, the system and surroundings are always in thermal and mechanical equilibrium with each other.

The total entropy change of the universe is the sum of the entropy change of the system and the surroundings:
$\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}}$

During a reversible isothermal expansion, the heat absorbed by the system from the surroundings is $q_{\text{rev}}$. The entropy change of the system is:
$\Delta S_{\text{sys}} = \dfrac{q_{\text{rev}}}{T}$

Since the surroundings lose the exact same amount of heat reversibly at the same temperature, the entropy change of the surroundings is:
$\Delta S_{\text{surr}} = -\dfrac{q_{\text{rev}}}{T}$

Therefore, the total entropy change is:
$\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \dfrac{q_{\text{rev}}}{T} - \dfrac{q_{\text{rev}}}{T} = 0$

At equilibrium (or for any reversible process), the total entropy change $\Delta S_{\text{total}}$ is always zero.

Answer: Zero J K$^{-1}$

</details>

Which of the following reactions exhibits decrease in entropy?

- **(B)** $\mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{H}_{(\mathrm{g})}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{CaCO}_{3(\mathrm{~s})} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{CaO}_{(\mathrm{s})}+\mathrm{CO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}$
- **(D)** $2 \mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}+\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{l})}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2 \mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}+\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{l})}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Entropy is a measure of randomness or disorder of a system. The entropy of a substance is highest in the gaseous state, lower in the liquid state, and lowest in the solid state. A reaction exhibits a decrease in entropy if the number of moles of gaseous products is less than the number of moles of gaseous reactants ($\Delta n_g < 0$).

For $2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{2(l)} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{(l)}+\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}$, a liquid decomposes to form a liquid and a gas. $\Delta n_g = 1 - 0 = 1 > 0$. Entropy increases.

For $\mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{H}_{(\mathrm{g})}$, one mole of gas forms two moles of gas. $\Delta n_g = 2 - 1 = 1 > 0$. Entropy increases.

For $\mathrm{CaCO}_{3(\mathrm{~s})} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{CaO}_{(\mathrm{s})}+\mathrm{CO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}$, a solid decomposes to form a solid and a gas. $\Delta n_g = 1 - 0 = 1 > 0$. Entropy increases.

For $2 \mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}+\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{l})}$, three moles of gaseous reactants form two moles of liquid product. $\Delta n_g = 0 - 3 = -3 < 0$. Since gases are converted into a liquid, the randomness decreases significantly, leading to a decrease in entropy.

Answer: $2 \mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}+\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{(\mathrm{l})}$

### Q12. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following equation, relates temperature of a reaction with $\Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S}^{\circ}$ at equilibrium?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\Delta H^{\circ}=\frac{T}{\Delta S^{\circ}}$
- **(B)** $\Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{S}^{\circ}}{\mathrm{T}}$
- **(C)** $\Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}=\mathrm{T} \Delta \mathrm{S}^{\circ}$
- **(D)** $\Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{~T} \Delta \mathrm{~S}^{\circ}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}=\mathrm{T} \Delta \mathrm{S}^{\circ}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

At equilibrium, the standard Gibbs free energy change is zero, i.e., $\Delta G^{\circ} = 0$.

The relation between standard Gibbs free energy change, standard enthalpy change, and standard entropy change is given by the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation:

$\Delta G^{\circ} = \Delta H^{\circ} - T \Delta S^{\circ}$

Substituting $\Delta G^{\circ} = 0$:

$0 = \Delta H^{\circ} - T \Delta S^{\circ}$

$\Delta H^{\circ} = T \Delta S^{\circ}$

Answer: $\Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}=\mathrm{T} \Delta \mathrm{S}^{\circ}$

</details>

$\mathrm{Fe}_{2} \mathrm{O}_{3(\mathrm{~s})}+3 \mathrm{CO}_{(\mathrm{g})} \longrightarrow 2 \mathrm{Fe}_{(\mathrm{s})}+3 \mathrm{CO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \Delta \mathrm{H}^{\circ}=-29 \cdot 8 \mathrm{~kJ}$
and $\Delta \mathrm{S}^{\circ}=15 \mathrm{JK}^{-1}$. What is the value of $\Delta \mathrm{S}_{\text {(total) }}$ at $298 \mathrm{~K}$ ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** 29.8 J
- **(B)** $100 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~J}$
- **(C)** $298 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~J}$
- **(D)** $115 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~J}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $115 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~J}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since the reaction is exothermic, system loses heat to surrounding. Hence the entropy of the surrounding increases.
$\begin{aligned}
\quad \Delta \mathrm{H}_{\text {surr }} &=+29.8 \mathrm{~kJ}=29800 \mathrm{~J} \\
\therefore \Delta \mathrm{S}_{\text {sur }} &=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{H}_{\text {sur }}}{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{29800}{298}=100 \mathrm{JK}^{-1} \\
\therefore \quad \Delta \mathrm{S}_{\text {Total }} &=\Delta \mathrm{S}_{\text {sys }}+\Delta \mathrm{S}_{\text {surr }}=15+100=115 \mathrm{JK}^{-1}
\end{aligned}$
