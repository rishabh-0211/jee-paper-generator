### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

2 moles of an ideal gas expand isothermally and reversibly from $10 \text{ L}$ to $50 \text{ L}$ at $298 \text{ K}$. Calculate the work done during this expansion. ($R = 8.314 \text{ JK}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-79.756 \text{ J}$
- **(B)** $-7.9756 \text{ J}$
- **(C)** $-7975 \text{ kJ}$
- **(D)** $-7.975 \text{ kJ}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $-7.975 \text{ kJ}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work done during an isothermal and reversible expansion of an ideal gas is given by the formula:

$W = -2.303 nRT \log \left( \dfrac{V_2}{V_1} \right)$

Given values are:
$n = 2 \text{ moles}$
$R = 8.314 \text{ J K}^{-1} \text{ mol}^{-1}$
$T = 298 \text{ K}$
$V_1 = 10 \text{ L}$
$V_2 = 50 \text{ L}$

Substituting these values into the formula:

$W = -2.303 \times 2 \times 8.314 \times 298 \times \log \left( \dfrac{50}{10} \right)$

$W = -11411.6 \times \log(5)$

Using $\log(5) \approx 0.699$:

$W = -11411.6 \times 0.699$

$W \approx -7976 \text{ J}$

$W \approx -7.975 \text{ kJ}$

Answer: $-7.975 \text{ kJ}$

</details>

In a process, $\Delta U = 0$ and $q \neq 0$. Then the process must involve

**Detailed Solution:**

Answer: Work done by or on a system exactly equal in magnitude and opposite in sign to a heat transfer.

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following processes uses the total heat absorbed by system to perform work on the surrounding?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Adiabatic
- **(B)** Isothermal
- **(C)** Isochoric
- **(D)** Isobaric

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Isothermal**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the first law of thermodynamics, $\Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W$.

If the total heat absorbed by the system is used entirely to perform work on the surroundings, then $\Delta Q = \Delta W$.

This implies that the change in internal energy of the system is zero, i.e., $\Delta U = 0$.

For an ideal gas, the internal energy depends only on temperature. Since $\Delta U = 0$, the change in temperature $\Delta T = 0$.

A process in which the temperature remains constant is called an isothermal process.

Answer: Isothermal

</details>

### Q13. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following statements is NOT true regarding the isothermal process?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Heat is either added or removed from the system.
- **(B)** The temperature of the system remains constant.
- **(C)** The internal energy of the system remains constant.
- **(D)** No heat enters or leaves the system.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) No heat enters or leaves the system.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In an isothermal process, the temperature of the system remains constant, which implies $\Delta T = 0$.

For an ideal gas, the internal energy depends only on temperature, so the internal energy also remains constant, meaning $\Delta U = 0$.

According to the first law of thermodynamics, $Q = \Delta U + W$. Since $\Delta U = 0$, we get $Q = W$.

This indicates that heat must be added to or removed from the system to balance the work done by or on the gas, maintaining a constant temperature.

The statement that no heat enters or leaves the system describes an adiabatic process, not an isothermal process. Therefore, this statement is incorrect regarding an isothermal process.

Answer: No heat enters or leaves the system.

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following processes exhibits the decrease in internal energy of a system?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Isothermal expansion of an ideal gas.
- **(B)** Isothrmal compression of an ideal gas.
- **(C)** Adiabatic expansion of an ideal gas.
- **(D)** Adiabatic compression of an ideal gas

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Adiabatic expansion of an ideal gas.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the first law of thermodynamics, $\Delta U = q + w$.

For an isothermal process, the temperature remains constant ($\Delta T = 0$). Since the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature, $\Delta U = 0$ for both isothermal expansion and compression.

For an adiabatic process, there is no heat exchange with the surroundings, so $q = 0$. The first law becomes $\Delta U = w$.

During adiabatic expansion, work is done by the gas on the surroundings, meaning $w < 0$. Consequently, $\Delta U < 0$, which indicates a decrease in the internal energy of the system.

During adiabatic compression, work is done on the gas, meaning $w > 0$, leading to an increase in internal energy.

Answer: Adiabatic expansion of an ideal gas.

</details>
