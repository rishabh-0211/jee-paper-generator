### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statements is not correct ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\Delta G^\circ$ is an extensive property.
- **(B)** $E^\circ$ cell is an intensive property.
- **(C)** For a chemical reaction to be spontaneous $\Delta G$ must be $-\text{ve}$.
- **(D)** Electrical work is equal to $\Delta G$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Electrical work is equal to $\Delta G$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Gibbs free energy ($G$) and standard Gibbs free energy change ($\Delta G^\circ$) depend on the quantity of the substance, hence they are extensive properties.

Standard cell potential ($E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$) is independent of the amount of substance, hence it is an intensive property.

For a spontaneous reaction at constant temperature and pressure, the change in Gibbs free energy ($\Delta G$) must be negative ($\Delta G < 0$).

The reversible electrical work done by a galvanic cell is equal to the decrease in its Gibbs free energy. Therefore, electrical work done by the system is equal to $-\Delta G$, not $\Delta G$.

Thus, the statement "Electrical work is equal to $\Delta G$" is incorrect.

Answer: Electrical work is equal to $\Delta G$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a reaction to be spontaneous at all temperatures, the values of enthalpy change and entropy change should be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\Delta H > 0$ and $\Delta S > 0$
- **(B)** $\Delta H < 0$ and $\Delta S > 0$
- **(C)** $\Delta H < 0$ and $\Delta S < 0$
- **(D)** $\Delta H > 0$ and $\Delta S < 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\Delta H < 0$ and $\Delta S > 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a reaction to be spontaneous, the change in Gibbs free energy must be negative, i.e., $\Delta G < 0$.

The relationship between $\Delta G$, enthalpy change $\Delta H$, and entropy change $\Delta S$ is given by the Gibbs equation:

$\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$

Since absolute temperature $T$ is always positive, for $\Delta G$ to be negative at all temperatures, both terms contributing to $\Delta G$ must favor spontaneity. This happens when:

$\Delta H < 0$ (exothermic reaction)
$\Delta S > 0$ (increase in entropy)

Under these conditions, the term $-T\Delta S$ is negative, and since $\Delta H$ is also negative, their sum $\Delta G$ will always be negative regardless of the temperature.

Answer: $\Delta H < 0$ and $\Delta S > 0$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify from following the correct set of thermodynamic conditions for the reaction to be spontaneous below equilibrium temperature.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\Delta \mathrm{H} < 0$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S} < 0$
- **(B)** $\Delta H>0$ and $\Delta S>0$
- **(C)** $\Delta \mathrm{H} < 0$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S}>0$
- **(D)** $\Delta \mathrm{H}>0$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S} < 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\Delta \mathrm{H} < 0$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S} < 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The Gibbs free energy change is given by $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$.

For a reaction to be spontaneous, $\Delta G < 0$.

At equilibrium, $\Delta G = 0$, which gives the equilibrium temperature $T_{eq} = \dfrac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}$.

For the reaction to be spontaneous below $T_{eq}$, we require $\Delta G < 0$ when $T < T_{eq}$.

If $\Delta H < 0$ and $\Delta S < 0$, then $T_{eq}$ is positive. For $T < T_{eq}$, we have $T < \dfrac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}$. Since $\Delta S$ is negative, multiplying both sides by $\Delta S$ reverses the inequality, giving $T\Delta S > \Delta H$, which means $\Delta H - T\Delta S < 0$. Thus, $\Delta G < 0$ and the reaction is spontaneous at temperatures below $T_{eq}$.

If $\Delta H > 0$ and $\Delta S > 0$, the reaction is spontaneous only above $T_{eq}$.

Therefore, the correct conditions are $\Delta H < 0$ and $\Delta S < 0$.

Answer: $\Delta \mathrm{H} < 0$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S} < 0$

</details>

### Q24. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a reaction $\Delta \mathrm{H}=-30 \mathrm{~kJ}$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S}=-45 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}$, at what temperature reaction changes from spontaneous to non spontaneous?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $777 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(B)** $675 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(C)** $666 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(D)** $375 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~K}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $666 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~K}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{array}{l}
\Delta \mathrm{H}=-30 \mathrm{~kJ}, \Delta \mathrm{S}=-45 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}=-0.045 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~K}^{-1} \\
\therefore \mathrm{T}=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{H}}{\Delta \mathrm{S}}=\frac{-30 \mathrm{~kJ}}{-0.045 \mathrm{~kJ} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}}=666.67 \mathrm{~K}
\end{array}$
Since $\Delta \mathrm{H}$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S}$ are both negative, the reaction is spontaneous at low temperature and nonspontaneous above $666.67 \mathrm{~K}$.

</details>
