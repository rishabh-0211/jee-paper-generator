### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The cyclic $\pi$-molecular orbital formed by overlap of p-orbitals must contain,

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(4n + 2)$ p electrons.
- **(B)** $(2n + 2)$ p electrons.
- **(C)** $(4n - 2)$ p electrons.
- **(D)** $(2n - 2)$ p electrons.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $(4n + 2)$ p electrons.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to H\u00fcckel's rule, a cyclic, planar, and completely conjugated system is highly stable and aromatic if it contains $(4n + 2)$ $\pi$-electrons, where $n$ is an integer ($0, 1, 2, \dots$).

These $\pi$-electrons reside in the cyclic $\pi$-molecular orbital formed by the continuous lateral overlap of unhybridized p-orbitals of the atoms in the ring.

Thus, the cyclic $\pi$-molecular orbital must contain $(4n + 2)$ p electrons for the system to exhibit aromatic stability.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following is a non benzenoid aromatic compound?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Aniline
- **(B)** Tropone
- **(C)** Naphthalene
- **(D)** Phenol

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Tropone**

**Detailed Solution:**

Aromatic compounds that do not contain a benzene ring but still exhibit aromaticity are called non-benzenoid aromatic compounds.

Aniline, Naphthalene, and Phenol all contain one or more benzene rings in their structures, making them benzenoid aromatic compounds.

Tropone (2,4,6-cycloheptatrien-1-one) contains a seven-membered ring with three conjugated double bonds and a carbonyl group. It exhibits aromatic character due to the delocalization of $6\pi$ electrons in its dipolar resonance structure, satisfying Huckel's rule. Since it lacks a benzene ring, it is a non-benzenoid aromatic compound.

Answer: Tropone

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following groups exhibits $(+) \mathrm{R}$ effect?

**Options:**

- **(A)** -NHR
- **(B)** -CN
- **(C)** $-\mathrm{NO}_2$
- **(D)** -COOR

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) -NHR**

**Detailed Solution:**

The $(+) \mathrm{R}$ (positive resonance) effect is exhibited by groups that can donate electrons to a conjugated system through resonance. This requires the presence of a lone pair of electrons on the atom directly attached to the conjugated system.

In the $-\mathrm{NHR}$ group, the nitrogen atom has a lone pair of electrons which it can donate, thus showing a $(+) \mathrm{R}$ effect.

The groups $-\mathrm{CN}$, $-\mathrm{NO}_2$, and $-\mathrm{COOR}$ have multiple bonds between atoms of different electronegativities, where the more electronegative atom withdraws electron density. These groups withdraw electrons from the conjugated system and exhibit a $(-) \mathrm{R}$ effect.

Answer: -NHR

</details>
