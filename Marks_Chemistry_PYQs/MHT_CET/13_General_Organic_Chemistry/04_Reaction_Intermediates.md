### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What type of following reactions, the oxidative rancidity is?

**Options:**

- **(A)** addition
- **(B)** substitution
- **(C)** free radical chain reaction
- **(D)** displacement

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) free radical chain reaction**

**Detailed Solution:**

Oxidative rancidity is the process of autoxidation of lipids (fats and oils) exposed to oxygen, resulting in unpleasant odors and flavors.

This autoxidation proceeds via a free radical chain reaction mechanism.

The mechanism involves three main steps: initiation (formation of lipid free radicals), propagation (reaction of lipid radicals with oxygen to form peroxyl radicals, which abstract hydrogen from other lipid molecules), and termination (combination of radicals to form stable, non-radical products).

Therefore, oxidative rancidity is a free radical chain reaction.

Answer: free radical chain reaction

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Among the following, the most stable carbocation is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{CH}_3^+$
- **(B)** $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$
- **(C)** $\text{CH}_3\text{-CH}_2^+$
- **(D)** $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The stability of carbocations increases with the increase in the number of alkyl groups attached to the positively charged carbon atom. This is due to the +I (inductive) effect and hyperconjugation.

The order of stability of alkyl carbocations is $3^{\circ} > 2^{\circ} > 1^{\circ} > \text{methyl}$.

Among the given options, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+$ is a tertiary ($3^{\circ}$) carbocation, which has nine $\alpha$-hydrogen atoms, leading to maximum hyperconjugation structures. Hence, it is the most stable carbocation.

Answer: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C}^+$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from following carbocations is least stable?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(\mathrm{R})_3-\mathrm{C}^{+}$
- **(B)** $\quad(\mathrm{R})_2-\stackrel{+}{\mathrm{C}} \mathrm{H}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{R}-\stackrel{+}{\mathrm{C}} \mathrm{H}_2$
- **(D)** $\quad \mathrm{H}_3 \stackrel{+}{\mathrm{C}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\quad \mathrm{H}_3 \stackrel{+}{\mathrm{C}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The stability of carbocations increases with the number of alkyl groups attached to the positively charged carbon atom due to the +I (inductive) effect and hyperconjugation.

Alkyl groups are electron-donating and help to disperse the positive charge, thereby stabilizing the carbocation.

The general order of stability of alkyl carbocations is: Tertiary $(3^{\circ}) >$ Secondary $(2^{\circ}) >$ Primary $(1^{\circ}) >$ Methyl.

The methyl carbocation, $\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{C}^{+}$, has no alkyl groups attached to the positively charged carbon atom. Therefore, it lacks stabilization from both the +I effect and hyperconjugation, making it the least stable among the given options.

Answer: $\quad \mathrm{H}_3 \stackrel{+}{\mathrm{C}}$

</details>
