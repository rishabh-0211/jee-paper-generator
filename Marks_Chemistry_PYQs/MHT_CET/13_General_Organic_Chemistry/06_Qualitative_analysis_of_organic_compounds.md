### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

How can you identify the presence of halogen in given haloalkane?

**Options:**

- **(A)** On heating with alcohol.
- **(B)** On heating with KOH followed by treatment with dilute $\text{AgNO}_3$.
- **(C)** On heating with AgX.
- **(D)** On heating with KOH and NaOH.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) On heating with KOH followed by treatment with dilute $\text{AgNO}_3$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a haloalkane is heated with aqueous KOH, it undergoes nucleophilic substitution (hydrolysis) to form an alcohol and the corresponding potassium halide.

$R-X + KOH \rightarrow R-OH + KX$

The halide ion ($X^-$) released in the solution can be detected by adding dilute $AgNO_3$ solution (after neutralizing excess KOH with dilute $HNO_3$). This results in the formation of a characteristic precipitate of silver halide ($AgX$).

$KX + AgNO_3 \rightarrow AgX \downarrow + KNO_3$

The color of the precipitate (white for $AgCl$, pale yellow for $AgBr$, and yellow for $AgI$) confirms the presence and type of halogen in the given haloalkane.

Answer: On heating with KOH followed by treatment with dilute $\text{AgNO}_3$.

</details>
