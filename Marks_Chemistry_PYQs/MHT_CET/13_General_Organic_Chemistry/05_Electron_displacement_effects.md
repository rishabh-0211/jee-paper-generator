### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is the most stable cation?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{CH}_3^+$
- **(B)** $\text{CF}_3^+$
- **(C)** $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$
- **(D)** $\text{CF}_3\text{-CH}_2^+$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The stability of carbocations increases with the presence of electron-donating groups (+I effect) and hyperconjugation.

$(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$ is a secondary ($2^\circ$) carbocation. It is highly stabilized by the +I effect of two methyl groups and hyperconjugation involving 6 $\alpha$-hydrogens.

$\text{CH}_3^+$ is a methyl carbocation and lacks stabilization from any alkyl groups.

$\text{CF}_3^+$ and $\text{CF}_3\text{-CH}_2^+$ are highly destabilized due to the strong -I (electron-withdrawing) effect of the highly electronegative fluorine atoms, which intensifies the positive charge on the carbon atom.

Thus, $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$ is the most stable cation among the given options.

Answer: $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CH}^+$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following species exhibits $+ I$ effect?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-\text{COOR}$
- **(B)** $-\text{CN}$
- **(C)** $-\text{Cl}$
- **(D)** $-\text{CH}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $-\text{CH}_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The inductive effect is the polarization of a $\sigma$-bond due to the electron-withdrawing or electron-donating nature of adjacent groups or atoms.

Groups that withdraw electrons more strongly than hydrogen exhibit a $-I$ effect. Examples include $-\text{COOR}$, $-\text{CN}$, and $-\text{Cl}$.

Groups that donate electrons more strongly than hydrogen exhibit a $+I$ effect. Alkyl groups are electron-donating in nature. Therefore, $-\text{CH}_3$ exhibits a $+I$ effect.

Answer: $-\text{CH}_3$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following effects involves the delocalization of pi electrons in a molecule?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Inductive effect
- **(B)** Resonance effect
- **(C)** Electromeric effect
- **(D)** Hyperconjugation

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Resonance effect**

**Detailed Solution:**

The resonance effect involves the delocalization of $\pi$ electrons or lone pairs of electrons in a conjugated system, which leads to the stabilization of the molecule.

The inductive effect involves the polarization of $\sigma$ bonds due to differences in electronegativity.

The electromeric effect is a temporary effect involving the complete transfer of $\pi$ electrons to one of the bonded atoms in the presence of an attacking reagent.

Hyperconjugation involves the delocalization of $\sigma$ electrons (typically from a C-H bond) into an adjacent empty p-orbital or $\pi$ system.

Answer: Resonance effect

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statements regarding different electronic effects in organic reactions is TRUE ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Hyperconjugation is a temporary electronic effect.
- **(B)** -CN group show positive resonance effect.
- **(C)** The effect of hyperconjugation is usually stronger than the inductive effect.
- **(D)** The resonance hybrid is less stable than any of the resonance structures.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The effect of hyperconjugation is usually stronger than the inductive effect.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hyperconjugation is a permanent electronic effect involving the delocalization of $\sigma$ electrons.

The $-CN$ group is an electron-withdrawing group and shows a negative resonance effect ($-R$ or $-M$).

The resonance hybrid is always more stable than any of its contributing resonance structures due to resonance energy.

Hyperconjugation involves the actual delocalization of electrons, whereas the inductive effect only involves the polarization of $\sigma$ bonds. Therefore, the effect of hyperconjugation is usually stronger than the inductive effect.

Answer: The effect of hyperconjugation is usually stronger than the inductive effect.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

Find the number of hyperconjugation structures in the isopropyl carbocation.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $6$
- **(C)** $9$
- **(D)** $12$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $6$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The structure of the isopropyl carbocation is $(CH_3)_2CH^+$.

The carbon atom bearing the positive charge is attached to two methyl groups.

The number of $\alpha$-hydrogens is the total number of hydrogen atoms attached to the $\alpha$-carbons.

Here, there are two $\alpha$-carbons, each having $3$ hydrogen atoms. Thus, the total number of $\alpha$-hydrogens is $3 + 3 = 6$.

The number of hyperconjugation structures is equal to the number of $\alpha$-hydrogens.

Therefore, the number of hyperconjugation structures is $6$.

Answer: $6$

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is correct decreasing order of acidic strength for the compounds listed below?
I: p-chlorophenol, II: p-Cresol, III: p-nitrophenol, IV: p-methoxyphenol.

**Options:**

- **(A)** II $>$ IV $>$ I $>$ III
- **(B)** I $>$ II $>$ III $>$ IV
- **(C)** III $>$ I $>$ II $>$ IV
- **(D)** IV $>$ III $>$ I $>$ II

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) III $>$ I $>$ II $>$ IV**

**Detailed Solution:**

Acidic strength of phenols is directly proportional to the stability of the phenoxide ion formed after losing a proton.

Electron-withdrawing groups stabilize the phenoxide ion through $-I$ and $-R$ effects, thereby increasing the acidic strength. Electron-donating groups destabilize the phenoxide ion through $+I$, $+H$, and $+R$ effects, thereby decreasing the acidic strength.

In p-nitrophenol (III), the $-NO_2$ group exerts strong $-R$ and $-I$ effects, making it the most acidic.

In p-chlorophenol (I), the $-Cl$ group exerts a $-I$ effect which dominates over its $+R$ effect, making it more acidic than phenol but less acidic than p-nitrophenol.

In p-cresol (II), the $-CH_3$ group exerts $+I$ and hyperconjugation ($+H$) effects, decreasing the acidic strength compared to phenol.

In p-methoxyphenol (IV), the $-OCH_3$ group exerts a strong $+R$ effect which dominates over its $-I$ effect, making it the least acidic.

Thus, the decreasing order of acidic strength is III $>$ I $>$ II $>$ IV.

Answer: III $>$ I $>$ II $>$ IV

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The correct stability order of alkyl substituted alkenes is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR} \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR} \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR} \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The stability of alkenes is directly proportional to the number of alkyl groups attached to the doubly bonded carbon atoms. This is due to the +I effect and hyperconjugation. More alkyl groups provide more $\alpha$-hydrogens, leading to a greater number of hyperconjugative structures and thus greater stability.

Counting the number of alkyl groups (R) attached to the double bond:
$\mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2$ is a tetra-substituted alkene (4 alkyl groups).
$\mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR}$ is a tri-substituted alkene (3 alkyl groups).
$\mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2$ is a di-substituted alkene (2 alkyl groups).

Therefore, the correct stability order is tetra-substituted $\gt$ tri-substituted $\gt$ di-substituted.

$\mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR} \gt \mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2$

Answer: $\mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CR}_2 \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CHR} \gt \mathrm{R}_2 \mathrm{C}=\mathrm{CH}_2$

</details>
