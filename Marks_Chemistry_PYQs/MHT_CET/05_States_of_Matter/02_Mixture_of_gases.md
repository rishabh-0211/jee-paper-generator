### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A mixture of 0.5 mol $\text{N}_2$ gas, 1.0 mol $\text{O}_2$ gas and 1.5 mol $\text{H}_2$ gas exerts a total pressure of 18 bar. Find the partial pressure of $\text{O}_{2(g)}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4.0$ bar
- **(B)** $6.0$ bar
- **(C)** $8.0$ bar
- **(D)** $10.0$ bar

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $6.0$ bar**

**Detailed Solution:**

Total number of moles in the mixture is:

$n_{total} = n_{N_2} + n_{O_2} + n_{H_2}$

$n_{total} = 0.5 + 1.0 + 1.5 = 3.0$ mol

Mole fraction of $\text{O}_2$ is:

$x_{O_2} = \dfrac{n_{O_2}}{n_{total}} = \dfrac{1.0}{3.0} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

According to Dalton's law of partial pressures, the partial pressure of $\text{O}_2$ is:

$P_{O_2} = x_{O_2} \times P_{total}$

$P_{O_2} = \dfrac{1}{3} \times 18 = 6.0$ bar

Answer: $6.0$ bar

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A container contains mixture of He and Ne gases at certain temperature. He is 20 % by mass of mixture. What is the ratio of partial pressure of Helium to Neon ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:2$
- **(B)** $2:1$
- **(C)** $5:4$
- **(D)** $3:2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5:4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the total mass of the mixture be $100$ g.

Mass of He $= 20$ g

Mass of Ne $= 80$ g

Molar mass of He $= 4$ g/mol

Molar mass of Ne $= 20$ g/mol

Number of moles of He, $n_{\text{He}} = \dfrac{20}{4} = 5$

Number of moles of Ne, $n_{\text{Ne}} = \dfrac{80}{20} = 4$

According to Dalton's law of partial pressures, the ratio of partial pressures is equal to the ratio of number of moles.

$\dfrac{P_{\text{He}}}{P_{\text{Ne}}} = \dfrac{n_{\text{He}}}{n_{\text{Ne}}} = \dfrac{5}{4}$

Answer: $5:4$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What ratio by mass of 'Ne' and $\text{CH}_4$ should be mixed so that partial pressure exerted by each gas is same?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:1$
- **(B)** $2:1$
- **(C)** $2:3$
- **(D)** $5:4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $5:4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of Ne be $w_1$ and the mass of $\text{CH}_4$ be $w_2$.

Molar mass of Ne $= 20 \text{ g/mol}$
Molar mass of $\text{CH}_4 = 16 \text{ g/mol}$

Number of moles of Ne, $n_{\text{Ne}} = \dfrac{w_1}{20}$
Number of moles of $\text{CH}_4$, $n_{\text{CH}_4} = \dfrac{w_2}{16}$

According to Dalton's law of partial pressures, the partial pressure of a gas in a mixture is directly proportional to its number of moles. Since the partial pressures of both gases are equal, their number of moles must be equal.

$n_{\text{Ne}} = n_{\text{CH}_4}$

$\dfrac{w_1}{20} = \dfrac{w_2}{16}$

$\dfrac{w_1}{w_2} = \dfrac{20}{16} = \dfrac{5}{4}$

The required ratio by mass is $5:4$.

Answer: $5:4$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Equal masses of helium and oxygen are mixed in an empty container at $25^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. What is the fraction of the total pressure exerted by helium?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{8}{9}$
- **(D)** $\frac{7}{9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{8}{9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of helium and oxygen be $m$.

Molar mass of helium, $M_{\mathrm{He}} = 4 \mathrm{~g/mol}$

Molar mass of oxygen, $M_{\mathrm{O}_2} = 32 \mathrm{~g/mol}$

Number of moles of helium, $n_{\mathrm{He}} = \dfrac{m}{4}$

Number of moles of oxygen, $n_{\mathrm{O}_2} = \dfrac{m}{32}$

Total number of moles, $n_{\mathrm{total}} = n_{\mathrm{He}} + n_{\mathrm{O}_2} = \dfrac{m}{4} + \dfrac{m}{32} = \dfrac{9m}{32}$

According to Dalton's law of partial pressures, the fraction of total pressure exerted by a gas is equal to its mole fraction.

Fraction of pressure exerted by helium $= \dfrac{n_{\mathrm{He}}}{n_{\mathrm{total}}} = \dfrac{\dfrac{m}{4}}{\dfrac{9m}{32}} = \dfrac{32}{36} = \dfrac{8}{9}$

Answer: $\frac{8}{9}$

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A container contains $4 \mathrm{~g}$ $\mathrm{H}_2, 4 \mathrm{~g}$ $\mathrm{He}$ and certain amount of ' Ne ' at a certain temperature. What is the mass of 'Ne' required so that the partial pressure exerted by ' Ne ' is equal to the partial pressure of He ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** 4 g
- **(B)** 8 g
- **(C)** 10 g
- **(D)** 20 g

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 20 g**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Dalton's law of partial pressures, the partial pressure of a gas in a mixture is directly proportional to its number of moles.

Given that the partial pressure of Ne is equal to the partial pressure of He:

$P_{\text{Ne}} = P_{\text{He}}$

$\dfrac{n_{\text{Ne}} RT}{V} = \dfrac{n_{\text{He}} RT}{V}$

$n_{\text{Ne}} = n_{\text{He}}$

Number of moles of He is given by:

$n_{\text{He}} = \dfrac{\text{Mass of He}}{\text{Molar mass of He}} = \dfrac{4}{4} = 1 \text{ mol}$

Therefore, the number of moles of Ne required is also $1 \text{ mol}$.

Mass of Ne = $n_{\text{Ne}} \times \text{Molar mass of Ne}$

Mass of Ne = $1 \times 20 = 20 \text{ g}$

Answer: 20 g

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following gases of same mass and volume exerts highest pressure at constant temperature?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{N}_2$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{O}_2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Cl}_2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{H}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{H}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the ideal gas equation, $PV = nRT$.

The number of moles is given by $n = \dfrac{m}{M}$, where $m$ is the given mass and $M$ is the molar mass of the gas.

Substituting this into the ideal gas equation gives $P = \dfrac{mRT}{MV}$.

Since mass $m$, volume $V$, and temperature $T$ are constant for all the gases, the pressure $P$ is inversely proportional to the molar mass $M$ ($P \propto \dfrac{1}{M}$).

The molar masses of the given gases are:
$\mathrm{H}_2 = 2 \text{ g/mol}$
$\mathrm{N}_2 = 28 \text{ g/mol}$
$\mathrm{O}_2 = 32 \text{ g/mol}$
$\mathrm{Cl}_2 = 71 \text{ g/mol}$

Since $\mathrm{H}_2$ has the lowest molar mass, it will have the highest number of moles for a given mass, and therefore it will exert the highest pressure.

Answer: $\mathrm{H}_2$

</details>

### Q17. MHT CET 2022 (08 Aug Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the mixture of $7 g$ of $N_2$ and $8 g$ of $\mathrm{Ar}$ in a cylinder has the total pressure $27 \mathrm{bar}$. What is the partial pressure of $N_2$ ?
(Atomic mass of $N=14 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}, \mathrm{Ar}=40 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** 18 bar
- **(B)** 12 bar
- **(C)** 15 bar
- **(D)** 9 bar

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

Moles of $N_2=\frac{7}{28}=\frac{1}{4}$
Moles of $\mathrm{Ar}=\frac{8}{40}=\frac{1}{5}$
Partial pressure $=X_{N_2} \times P_T$
Partial pressure of
$\begin{aligned}
N_2 & =\frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{5}} \times 27 \\
& =\frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\frac{9}{20}} \times 27 \\
& =\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{20}{9} \times 27 \\
& =15 \mathrm{bar}
\end{aligned}$
