### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following gases has the highest density under normal conditions?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{O}_2$
- **(B)** $\text{SO}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{CH}_4$
- **(D)** $\text{N}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{SO}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The density of an ideal gas is given by the formula $d = \dfrac{PM}{RT}$, where $P$ is pressure, $M$ is molar mass, $R$ is the universal gas constant, and $T$ is temperature.

Under normal conditions (constant temperature and pressure), the density of a gas is directly proportional to its molar mass ($d \propto M$).

Calculating the molar mass of the given gases:
Molar mass of $\text{O}_2 = 2 \times 16 = 32 \text{ g/mol}$
Molar mass of $\text{SO}_2 = 32 + 2 \times 16 = 64 \text{ g/mol}$
Molar mass of $\text{CH}_4 = 12 + 4 \times 1 = 16 \text{ g/mol}$
Molar mass of $\text{N}_2 = 2 \times 14 = 28 \text{ g/mol}$

Since $\text{SO}_2$ has the highest molar mass, it will have the highest density under normal conditions.

Answer: $\text{SO}_2$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Four vessels of same volume consist equal moles of four gases $\mathrm{H}_2, \mathrm{Cl}_2$, $\mathrm{N}_2$ and $\mathrm{O}_2$ separately at same temperature. The pressure exerted by the gas is maximum for -

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Cl}_2$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{H}_2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{N}_2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{O}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{H}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the ideal gas equation, $P = \dfrac{nRT}{V}$. If the gases behaved ideally, they would all exert the same pressure since $n$, $V$, and $T$ are constant.

However, for real gases, the pressure is given by the van der Waals equation:
$P = \dfrac{nRT}{V - nb} - \dfrac{an^2}{V^2}$

The term $\dfrac{an^2}{V^2}$ represents the reduction in pressure due to intermolecular forces of attraction. The van der Waals constant $a$ is a measure of the strength of these attractive forces.

Among the given gases, $\mathrm{H}_2$ has the smallest size and the weakest intermolecular forces (London dispersion forces). Therefore, it has the minimum value of $a$.

Because the reduction in pressure is minimum for $\mathrm{H}_2$, it exerts the maximum pressure. Alternatively, at room temperature, the compressibility factor $Z = \dfrac{PV}{nRT}$ is greater than $1$ for $\mathrm{H}_2$, whereas $Z < 1$ for $\mathrm{N}_2$, $\mathrm{O}_2$, and $\mathrm{Cl}_2$ at moderate pressures. Since $P = \dfrac{ZnRT}{V}$, the pressure is maximum for $\mathrm{H}_2$.

Answer: $\mathrm{H}_2$

</details>

### Q16. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following equations gives combined relationship of Boyle's law and Charle's law?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{P}_1 \mathrm{~V}_2}{\mathrm{~T}_1}=\frac{\mathrm{P}_2 \mathrm{~V}_1}{\mathrm{~T}_2}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{n}=\frac{\mathrm{RT}}{\mathrm{PV}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1}=\frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{p}=\frac{\mathrm{RT}}{\mathrm{nV}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1}=\frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Boyle's law, the volume of a given mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure at constant temperature:

$V \propto \dfrac{1}{P}$ (at constant $T$ and $n$)

According to Charles's law, the volume of a given mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature at constant pressure:

$V \propto T$ (at constant $P$ and $n$)

Combining both laws, we get:

$V \propto \dfrac{T}{P}$

$\dfrac{PV}{T} = \text{constant}$

For two different states of the same amount of gas, the relationship can be written as:

$\dfrac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$

Answer: $\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1}=\frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$

</details>
