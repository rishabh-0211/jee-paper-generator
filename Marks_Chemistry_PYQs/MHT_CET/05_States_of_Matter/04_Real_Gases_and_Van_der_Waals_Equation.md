### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following gases exhibits positive deviation from ideal behavior at all pressures?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{H}_2$
- **(B)** $\text{O}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{CH}_4$
- **(D)** $\text{CO}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{H}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The compressibility factor $Z$ is given by $Z = \dfrac{PV}{nRT}$. For an ideal gas, $Z = 1$.

For real gases, deviation from ideal behavior is explained by the van der Waals equation: $\left(P + \dfrac{a}{V^2}\right)(V - b) = RT$ for 1 mole of gas.

For exceptionally small molecules like $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{He}$, the intermolecular attractive forces are very weak. Therefore, the van der Waals constant '$a$' is negligibly small, and the term $\dfrac{a}{V^2}$ can be ignored.

The equation simplifies to $P(V - b) = RT$.

$PV - Pb = RT$

$PV = RT + Pb$

Dividing by $RT$, we get:

$Z = \dfrac{PV}{RT} = 1 + \dfrac{Pb}{RT}$

Since $P$, $b$, $R$, and $T$ are all positive quantities, $Z > 1$ at all pressures. This means $\text{H}_2$ exhibits positive deviation from ideal behavior at all pressures.

Other gases like $\text{O}_2$, $\text{CH}_4$, and $\text{CO}_2$ have significant attractive forces (larger '$a$' values), so they initially show negative deviation ($Z < 1$) at low pressures before showing positive deviation at high pressures.

Answer: $\text{H}_2$

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Calculate the compressibility factor of $1$ mole of a certain real gas if it occupies $0.4 \text{ dm}^3$ at $300$ K and $40$ atm. [R $= 0.082$ atm $\text{dm}^3 \text{K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$]

- **(D)** $1.00$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.65$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The compressibility factor $Z$ is given by the formula:

$Z = \dfrac{PV}{nRT}$

Substituting the given values:

$Z = \dfrac{40 \times 0.4}{1 \times 0.082 \times 300}$

$Z = \dfrac{16}{24.6}$

$Z \approx 0.65$

Answer: $0.65$

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If compressibility factor of real gas is 1.05 at STP. What is molar volume of real gas?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $22.40 \mathrm{dm}^3$
- **(B)** $21.33 \mathrm{dm}^3$
- **(C)** $23.52 \mathrm{dm}^3$
- **(D)** $23.05 \mathrm{dm}^3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $23.52 \mathrm{dm}^3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The compressibility factor $Z$ is given by the ratio of the molar volume of a real gas to the molar volume of an ideal gas at the same temperature and pressure.

$Z = \dfrac{V_{\text{real}}}{V_{\text{ideal}}}$

At STP, the molar volume of an ideal gas is $V_{\text{ideal}} = 22.4 \text{ dm}^3$.

Given $Z = 1.05$, we can find the molar volume of the real gas as:

$V_{\text{real}} = Z \times V_{\text{ideal}}$

$V_{\text{real}} = 1.05 \times 22.4 \text{ dm}^3$

$V_{\text{real}} = 23.52 \text{ dm}^3$

Answer: $23.52 \mathrm{dm}^3$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following formula is used to calculate compressibility factor?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Z}=\dfrac{\mathrm{PVT}}{\mathrm{R}}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Z}=\dfrac{\mathrm{nRT}}{\mathrm{PV}}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Z}=\dfrac{\mathrm{PV}}{\mathrm{nRT}}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Z}=\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{PV}}{\mathrm{RT}}\right) \times \mathrm{n}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{Z}=\dfrac{\mathrm{PV}}{\mathrm{nRT}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The compressibility factor $Z$ is defined as the ratio of the actual volume of a gas to the volume of an ideal gas at the same temperature and pressure.

For an ideal gas, $PV_{ideal} = nRT$, so $V_{ideal} = \dfrac{nRT}{P}$.

For a real gas, $Z = \dfrac{V_{real}}{V_{ideal}} = \dfrac{V}{\dfrac{nRT}{P}} = \dfrac{PV}{nRT}$.

Answer: $\mathrm{Z}=\dfrac{\mathrm{PV}}{\mathrm{nRT}}$

</details>
