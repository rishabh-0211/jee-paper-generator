### Q1. MHT CET 2020 (19 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which among the following elements is obtained in pure form by liquation process of refining?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Copper
- **(B)** Tin
- **(C)** Gallium
- **(D)** Silicon

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Tin**

**Detailed Solution:**

Liquation is used for the refining of metals having low melting point and are associated with high melting impurities. For example, Pb, Sn, Sb, Bi and Hg. The impure metal is heated on the sloping hearth of a furnace. The pure metal flows down leaving behind the non-fusible material on the hearth.

### Q4. MHT CET 2020 (16 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Zirconium is refined by

**Options:**

- **(A)** Liquation process
- **(B)** Mond process
- **(C)** Van Arkel method
- **(D)** Electrolytic refining process

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Van Arkel method**

**Detailed Solution:**

Zirconium is refined by van Arkel method.
Zirconium is refined using the van Arkel technique. By heating metal in an evacuated vessel with iodine, oxygen and nitrogen present as impurities are eliminated. On a tungsten filament heated to roughly $1800 \mathrm{~K}$, the metal iodide volatilizes and is degraded. On the filament, pure metal is deposited.
$\begin{array}{l}
\underset{\text { (Impure) }}{\mathrm{Zr}}+2 \mathrm{l}_{2} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Zrl}_{4} \\
\mathrm{Zrl}_{4} \longrightarrow \underset{\text { (Pure) }}{\mathrm{Zr}}+2 \mathrm{l}_{2}
\end{array}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2020 (15 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which following method is used for refining of impure zirconium?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Liquation
- **(B)** Zone refining
- **(C)** Polling
- **(D)** Van Arkel method

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Van Arkel method**

**Detailed Solution:**

Van Arkel method is used for refining $Zr$ and $Ti$. Van Arkel process involves heating the impure metal with Iodine. It forms a volatile complex which on decomposition at high temperatures, gives back the metal but in very pure form.
Refining of $Zr$ and $Ti$ using Van Arkel method
For Zirconium,
$\mathrm{Zr}$ (impure) $+2 \mathrm{l}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{Zrl}_{4}$
$\mathrm{Zrl}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{Zr}$ (pure) $+2 \mathrm{I}_{2}$ (at 1800K)
For Titanium,
Ti(impure) $+2 \mathrm{I}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{Til}_{4}$
$\mathrm{Til}_{4} \rightarrow \mathrm{Ti}($ pure $)+2 \mathrm{I}_{2}($ at $1800 \mathrm{~K})$

</details>
