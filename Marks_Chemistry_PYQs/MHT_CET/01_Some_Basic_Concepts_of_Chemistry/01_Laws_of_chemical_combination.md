### Q1. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following pair of compounds cannot demonstrate law of multiple proportion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{NO}, \mathrm{NO}_2$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{CO}, \mathrm{CO}_2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}, \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_2$
- **(D)** $\quad \mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{~S}, \mathrm{NaF}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\quad \mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{~S}, \mathrm{NaF}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The law of multiple proportions states that when two elements combine to form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other element are in the ratio of small whole numbers.

For a pair of compounds to demonstrate this law, they must be composed of the exact same two elements.

$\mathrm{NO}$ and $\mathrm{NO}_2$ are composed of N and O.

$\mathrm{CO}$ and $\mathrm{CO}_2$ are composed of C and O.

$\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$ and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ are composed of H and O.

$\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{S}$ and $\mathrm{NaF}$ are composed of three different elements: Na, S, and F. Therefore, they cannot demonstrate the law of multiple proportions.

Answer: $\quad \mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{~S}, \mathrm{NaF}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which law is illustrated by compounds $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$ and $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_2$ formed from two different elements, H and O ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Law of Constant proportion.
- **(B)** Law of Conservation of mass.
- **(C)** Law of Multiple proportion.
- **(D)** Avogadro's law.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Law of Multiple proportion.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$, $2$ parts by mass of hydrogen combine with $16$ parts by mass of oxygen.

In $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$, $2$ parts by mass of hydrogen combine with $32$ parts by mass of oxygen.

The masses of oxygen ($16$ and $32$) that combine with a fixed mass of hydrogen ($2$) bear a simple whole number ratio of $16:32$ or $1:2$.

This illustrates the Law of Multiple Proportions.

Answer: Law of Multiple proportion.

</details>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Law of multiple proportions**

**Detailed Solution:**

The combining ratios of hydrogen and oxygen in water and hydrogen peroxide are 1: 8 and 1: 16. It is an example of law of multiple proportions. According to this law, if two elements combine together to form several compounds then weight of one of these elements, which combines with a fixed weight of the other, are in ratio of simple whole numbers.
