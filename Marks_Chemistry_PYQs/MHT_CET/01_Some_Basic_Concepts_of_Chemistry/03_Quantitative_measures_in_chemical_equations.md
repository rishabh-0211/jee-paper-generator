### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the percentage atom economy when formula weight of product is $65$ u and the sum of formula weight of reactant is $130$ u?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $65\%$
- **(B)** $70\%$
- **(C)** $40\%$
- **(D)** $50\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $50\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The percentage atom economy is calculated using the formula:

$\text{Percentage atom economy} = \dfrac{\text{Formula weight of desired product}}{\text{Sum of formula weights of all reactants}} \times 100$

Substituting the given values:

$\text{Percentage atom economy} = \dfrac{65}{130} \times 100 = 50\%$

Answer: $50\%$

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is number of hydrogen molecules needed to synthesize 3.4 g of ammonia by reaction with nitrogen?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.022 \times 10^{23}$
- **(B)** $1.2044 \times 10^{23}$
- **(C)** $1.8066 \times 10^{23}$
- **(D)** $1.8044 \times 10^{24}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.8066 \times 10^{23}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The balanced chemical equation for the synthesis of ammonia is:

$N_2 + 3H_2 \rightarrow 2NH_3$

Molar mass of $NH_3 = 14 + 3 \times 1 = 17 \text{ g/mol}$

Moles of $NH_3$ to be synthesized $= \dfrac{3.4}{17} = 0.2 \text{ mol}$

From the balanced equation, $2 \text{ moles}$ of $NH_3$ require $3 \text{ moles}$ of $H_2$.

Moles of $H_2$ required $= \dfrac{3}{2} \times 0.2 = 0.3 \text{ mol}$

Number of $H_2$ molecules $= 0.3 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 1.8066 \times 10^{23}$

Answer: $1.8066 \times 10^{23}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the approximate mass of the precipitate formed when $50$ mL of $16.9\%$ solution of $\text{AgNO}_3$ is mixed with $50$ mL of $7.45\%$ KCl solution? (Molar mass of $\text{AgNO}_3 = 169$ g/mol, KCl $= 74.5$ g/mol, AgCl $= 143.3$ g/mol)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3.5$ g
- **(B)** $7$ g
- **(C)** $14$ g
- **(D)** $28$ g

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $7$ g**

**Detailed Solution:**

Mass of $\text{AgNO}_3$ in $50$ mL of $16.9\%$ (w/v) solution $= \dfrac{16.9}{100} \times 50 = 8.45$ g

Moles of $\text{AgNO}_3 = \dfrac{8.45}{169} = 0.05$ mol

Mass of KCl in $50$ mL of $7.45\%$ (w/v) solution $= \dfrac{7.45}{100} \times 50 = 3.725$ g

Moles of KCl $= \dfrac{3.725}{74.5} = 0.05$ mol

The balanced chemical equation is:
$\text{AgNO}_3 + \text{KCl} \rightarrow \text{AgCl} \downarrow + \text{KNO}_3$

Since $1$ mole of $\text{AgNO}_3$ reacts with $1$ mole of KCl to give $1$ mole of AgCl, $0.05$ mol of $\text{AgNO}_3$ will react completely with $0.05$ mol of KCl to form $0.05$ mol of AgCl.

Mass of AgCl precipitate $= 0.05 \times 143.3 = 7.165$ g $\approx 7$ g

Answer: $7$ g

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The vapour density of a certain gas is 16. What is the volume occupied by 8 g of gas at STP assuming ideal behaviour?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.80 \mathrm{~dm}^3$
- **(B)** $5.6 \mathrm{~dm}^3$
- **(C)** $11.2 \mathrm{~dm}^3$
- **(D)** $2.24 \mathrm{~dm}^3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $5.6 \mathrm{~dm}^3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Molar mass of the gas $= 2 \times \text{Vapour density}$

Molar mass $= 2 \times 16 = 32 \text{ g/mol}$

Number of moles of the gas $= \dfrac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Molar mass}} = \dfrac{8}{32} = 0.25 \text{ mol}$

Volume of $1 \text{ mole}$ of an ideal gas at STP $= 22.4 \text{ dm}^3$

Volume occupied by $0.25 \text{ mol}$ of the gas $= 0.25 \times 22.4 = 5.6 \text{ dm}^3$

Answer: $5.6 \mathrm{~dm}^3$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the volume of oxygen required for complete combustion of 0.25 mole of methane at S.T.P.?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $22.4 \mathrm{dm}^3$
- **(B)** $5.6 \mathrm{dm}^3$
- **(C)** $11.2 \mathrm{dm}^3$
- **(D)** $\quad 7.46 \mathrm{dm}^3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $11.2 \mathrm{dm}^3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The balanced chemical equation for the combustion of methane is:

$\mathrm{CH_4(g)} + 2\mathrm{O_2(g)} \rightarrow \mathrm{CO_2(g)} + 2\mathrm{H_2O(l)}$

From the stoichiometry of the reaction, $1$ mole of methane requires $2$ moles of oxygen for complete combustion.

Therefore, $0.25$ mole of methane requires $2 \times 0.25 = 0.50$ mole of oxygen.

At S.T.P., the volume occupied by $1$ mole of a gas is $22.4 \mathrm{\, dm^3}$.

Volume of oxygen required $= 0.50 \times 22.4 \mathrm{\, dm^3} = 11.2 \mathrm{\, dm^3}$.

Answer: $11.2 \mathrm{dm}^3$

</details>

- **(A)** $40.50 \mathrm{cal}$
- **(B)** $78.75 \mathrm{cal}$
- **(C)** $95.23 \mathrm{cal}$
- **(D)** $96 \mathrm{cal}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $96 \mathrm{cal}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} \because 1 \mathrm{~L}-\mathrm{atm} &=101.266 \mathrm{~J} \\ \therefore 4 \mathrm{~L}-\mathrm{atm} &=101.266 \times 4 \mathrm{~J} \\ &=405.06 \mathrm{~J} \\ &=\frac{405.06}{4.2} \mathrm{cal} \quad[\because 1 \mathrm{cal}=4.2 \mathrm{~J}] \\ &=96.4 \mathrm{cal} \end{aligned}$
