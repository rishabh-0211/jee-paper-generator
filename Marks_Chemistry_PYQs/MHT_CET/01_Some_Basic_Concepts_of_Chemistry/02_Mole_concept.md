### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following gases has the maximum number of molecules?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3.5$ g $\text{N}_2$
- **(B)** $0.5$ g $\text{H}_2$
- **(C)** $3.2$ g $\text{O}_2$
- **(D)** $4.6$ g $\text{NO}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.5$ g $\text{H}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The number of molecules is directly proportional to the number of moles.

Number of moles $n = \dfrac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Molar mass}}$

For $3.5$ g $\text{N}_2$: $n = \dfrac{3.5}{28} = 0.125$ mol

For $0.5$ g $\text{H}_2$: $n = \dfrac{0.5}{2} = 0.25$ mol

For $3.2$ g $\text{O}_2$: $n = \dfrac{3.2}{32} = 0.1$ mol

For $4.6$ g $\text{NO}_2$: $n = \dfrac{4.6}{46} = 0.1$ mol

Since $0.5$ g $\text{H}_2$ has the maximum number of moles, it contains the maximum number of molecules.

Answer: $0.5$ g $\text{H}_2$

</details>

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

Atoms per molecule = $3$
Total atoms = $1 \times 3 \times N_A = 3 N_A$

For $24$ grams of dioxygen ($O_2$):
Molar mass of $O_2 = 32 \text{ g/mol}$
Moles of $O_2 = \dfrac{24}{32} = 0.75 \text{ mol}$
Atoms per molecule = $2$
Total atoms = $0.75 \times 2 \times N_A = 1.5 N_A$

For $22$ grams of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$):
Molar mass of $CO_2 = 44 \text{ g/mol}$
Moles of $CO_2 = \dfrac{22}{44} = 0.5 \text{ mol}$
Atoms per molecule = $3$
Total atoms = $0.5 \times 3 \times N_A = 1.5 N_A$

For $16$ grams of methane ($CH_4$):
Molar mass of $CH_4 = 16 \text{ g/mol}$
Moles of $CH_4 = \dfrac{16}{16} = 1 \text{ mol}$
Atoms per molecule = $5$
Total atoms = $1 \times 5 \times N_A = 5 N_A$

Methane contains the maximum number of atoms.

Answer: $16$ grams of methane
