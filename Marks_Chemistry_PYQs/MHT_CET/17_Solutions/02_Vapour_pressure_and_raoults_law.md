### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solution is prepared by dissolving $136.8$ g sucrose in $1$ kg of water. Find the vapor pressure of solution at $298$ K. [Molar mass of sucrose = $342$ g $\text{mol}^{-1}$, Vapour pressure of water = $18$ mm Hg]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $17.75\ \text{mm Hg}$
- **(B)** $17.79\ \text{mm Hg}$
- **(C)** $17.95\ \text{mm Hg}$
- **(D)** $17.87\ \text{mm Hg}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $17.87\ \text{mm Hg}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Number of moles of sucrose, $n_2 = \dfrac{136.8}{342} = 0.4$ mol

Number of moles of water, $n_1 = \dfrac{1000}{18} = 55.55$ mol

Mole fraction of sucrose, $x_2 = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1 + n_2} = \dfrac{0.4}{55.55 + 0.4} = \dfrac{0.4}{55.95} = 0.00715$

Relative lowering of vapour pressure is given by:

$\dfrac{P^0 - P_s}{P^0} = x_2$

$P^0 - P_s = P^0 x_2 = 18 \times 0.00715 = 0.1287$ mm Hg

$P_s = 18 - 0.1287 = 17.87$ mm Hg

Answer: $17.87\ \text{mm Hg}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the correct statement from the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** The vapor pressure of solution containing chloroform and acetone is lower than vapor pressure of pure chloroform and acetone at same temperature.
- **(B)** The vapor pressure of aqueous solution containing nonvolatile solute increases with increasing solute concentration.
- **(C)** The vapor pressure of liquid decreases with increase in temperature.
- **(D)** The lowering in vapor pressure in dilute solutions is greater than concentrated solutions at the same temperature.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) The vapor pressure of solution containing chloroform and acetone is lower than vapor pressure of pure chloroform and acetone at same temperature.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A mixture of chloroform and acetone forms a non-ideal solution that shows a negative deviation from Raoult's law. This is due to the formation of intermolecular hydrogen bonds between the chloroform and acetone molecules, which are stronger than the interactions in the pure liquids.

Because of these stronger attractive forces, the escaping tendency of the molecules decreases, leading to a lower vapor pressure for the solution compared to the vapor pressures of pure chloroform and pure acetone at the same temperature. They form a maximum boiling azeotrope.

Analyzing the other options:
The vapor pressure of a solution containing a non-volatile solute decreases (not increases) with increasing solute concentration.
The vapor pressure of a liquid always increases (not decreases) with an increase in temperature.
The lowering of vapor pressure is directly proportional to the mole fraction of the solute ($\Delta P = P^0 x_{solute}$). Therefore, the lowering is greater in concentrated solutions than in dilute solutions.

Answer: The vapor pressure of solution containing chloroform and acetone is lower than vapor pressure of pure chloroform and acetone at same temperature.

</details>

Phenol and aniline show a negative deviation due to strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding between them.

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a binary solution, when a nonvolatile nonelectrolyte solute is dissolved in solvent, the vapour pressure of solvent decreases by 20 % . what is mole fraction of solvent in solution ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.02$
- **(B)** $0.08$
- **(C)** $0.2$
- **(D)** $0.8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $0.8$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the vapour pressure of the pure solvent be $P^0$.

The decrease in vapour pressure is given as $20\%$, so the lowering of vapour pressure is $\Delta P = 0.20 P^0$.

According to Raoult's law, the relative lowering of vapour pressure is equal to the mole fraction of the solute, $X_{\text{solute}}$.

$\dfrac{\Delta P}{P^0} = X_{\text{solute}}$

$X_{\text{solute}} = \dfrac{0.20 P^0}{P^0} = 0.20$

For a binary solution, the sum of mole fractions of solute and solvent is $1$.

$X_{\text{solvent}} = 1 - X_{\text{solute}}$

$X_{\text{solvent}} = 1 - 0.20 = 0.8$

Answer: $0.8$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate the mole fraction of pure liquid $B$ in solution if total vapour pressure of solution, vapour pressure of pure liquid A and vapour pressure of pure liquid B are $500 \mathrm{mmHg}, 400 \mathrm{~mm} \mathrm{Hg}$ and 575 mm Hg respectively at given temperature.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0.43
- **(B)** 0.57
- **(C)** 0.62
- **(D)** 0.38

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 0.57**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Raoult's law, the total vapour pressure of the solution is given by:

$P_T = P_A^0 X_A + P_B^0 X_B$

Since $X_A + X_B = 1$, substituting $X_A = 1 - X_B$ gives:

$P_T = P_A^0 (1 - X_B) + P_B^0 X_B$

$P_T = P_A^0 + (P_B^0 - P_A^0) X_B$

Substituting the given values $P_T = 500 \text{ mmHg}$, $P_A^0 = 400 \text{ mmHg}$, and $P_B^0 = 575 \text{ mmHg}$:

$500 = 400 + (575 - 400) X_B$

$100 = 175 X_B$

$X_B = \dfrac{100}{175} = \dfrac{4}{7} \approx 0.57$

The mole fraction of liquid B in the solution is $0.57$.

Answer: 0.57

</details>

A solution of nonvolatile solute is obtained by dissolving 0.8 g in $0.3 \mathrm{dm}^3$ water has osmotic pressure 0.2 atm at 300 K . Calculate the molar mass of solute.
$\left[\mathrm{R}=0.082 \mathrm{~atm} \mathrm{dm}^3 \mathrm{~K}^{-1} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\quad 300 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $340 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

$\pi = \dfrac{W R T}{M V}$

$M = \dfrac{W R T}{\pi V}$

$M = \dfrac{0.8}{0.2} \times 0.082 \times \dfrac{300}{0.3}$

$M = 4 \times 0.082 \times 1000$

$M = 4 \times 82 = 328 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

Answer: $328 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

### Q16. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the false statement among the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Ideal solution obey Raoult's law over entire range of temperature and concentration.
- **(B)** For ideal solution $\Delta_{\text {mix }} \mathrm{V}=0$.
- **(C)** Non ideal solution do not obey Raoult's law over the entire range of concentration.
- **(D)** Vapour pressure of non ideal solution always lies between vapour pressure of pure components.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Vapour pressure of non ideal solution always lies between vapour pressure of pure components.**

**Detailed Solution:**

An ideal solution obeys Raoult's law over the entire range of concentration.

For an ideal solution, the volume of mixing $\Delta_{\text{mix}} V = 0$ and the enthalpy of mixing $\Delta_{\text{mix}} H = 0$.

A non-ideal solution does not obey Raoult's law over the entire range of concentration.

Non-ideal solutions show either positive or negative deviations from Raoult's law. For solutions showing a large positive deviation, the vapour pressure of the solution can be higher than the vapour pressure of the most volatile pure component. For solutions showing a large negative deviation, the vapour pressure can be lower than the vapour pressure of the least volatile pure component. Thus, the vapour pressure of a non-ideal solution does not always lie between the vapour pressures of the pure components.

Answer: Vapour pressure of non ideal solution always lies between vapour pressure of pure components.

</details>

### Q20. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

Molar mass of solute, $M_2 = 64$ g mol$^{-1}$
Mass of solvent (benzene), $w_1 = 50$ g
Molar mass of solvent, $M_1 = 78$ g mol$^{-1}$
Vapour pressure of pure benzene, $P_1^0 = 640$ mmHg

$P_1 = P_1^0 x_1$

$P_1 = 640 \times \dfrac{800}{839} = \dfrac{512000}{839} \approx 610.25$ mmHg

Answer: 610.25 mm Hg
