### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Find the solubility of organic compound R-X in water at $25^\circ\text{C}$ and at $130$ mmHg, if Henry's law constant for that organic compound is $0.160$ mol L$^{-1}$ bar$^{-1}$ at that temperature.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $.0152$ M
- **(B)** $0.0276$ M
- **(C)** $0.196$ M
- **(D)** $0.118$ M

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.0276$ M**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Henry's law, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid:

$S = K_H \times P$

Given:
Henry's law constant, $K_H = 0.160 \text{ mol L}^{-1} \text{ bar}^{-1}$
Pressure, $P = 130 \text{ mmHg}$

First, we convert the pressure from mmHg to bar. We know that $1 \text{ atm} = 760 \text{ mmHg} = 1.01325 \text{ bar}$.

$P = \dfrac{130}{760} \text{ atm} = \dfrac{130}{760} \times 1.01325 \text{ bar} \approx 0.1733 \text{ bar}$

Now, substituting the values into Henry's law equation:

$S = 0.160 \text{ mol L}^{-1} \text{ bar}^{-1} \times 0.1733 \text{ bar}$

$S \approx 0.0277 \text{ mol L}^{-1} \text{ (or M)}$

The closest given option is $0.0276 \text{ M}$.

Answer: $0.0276$ M

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

The solubility of $\text{N}_2$ gas in water at $25^\circ C$ and $1$ bar is $6.85 \times 10^{-4}$ $\text{mol L}^{-1}$. Calculate the molarity of $\text{N}_2$ gas dissolved in water under atmospheric conditions when the partial pressure of $\text{N}_2$ in atmosphere is $0.50$ bar.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $12.425 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{mol L}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $3.425 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{mol L}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $5.251 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{mol L}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $3.425 \times 10^{-2}\,\text{mol L}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3.425 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{mol L}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Henry's law, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to its partial pressure.

$S = K_H P$

Given the solubility $S_1 = 6.85 \times 10^{-4}$ $\text{mol L}^{-1}$ at partial pressure $P_1 = 1$ $\text{bar}$.

The solubility $S_2$ at partial pressure $P_2 = 0.50$ $\text{bar}$ can be calculated as:

$\dfrac{S_1}{P_1} = \dfrac{S_2}{P_2}$

$S_2 = S_1 \times \dfrac{P_2}{P_1}$

$S_2 = 6.85 \times 10^{-4} \times \dfrac{0.50}{1}$

$S_2 = 3.425 \times 10^{-4}$ $\text{mol L}^{-1}$

Answer: $3.425 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{mol L}^{-1}$

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When the bottle of soft drink is opened, what would take place from the following?
(A) Solubility of dissolved gas decreases.
(B) bottle released internal pressure.
(C) effervences evolved from the bottle.
(D) increase in external pressure.
Identify the correct choice.

**Options:**

- **(A)** (A), (B) and (C) only.
- **(B)** (A), (B) and (D) only.
- **(C)** (A), (B), (C) and (D).
- **(D)** (B), (C) and (D) only.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) (A), (B) and (C) only.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Soft drinks are bottled under high pressure to increase the solubility of $CO_2$ gas in the liquid, according to Henry's law.

When the bottle is opened, the high internal pressure is released to match the atmospheric pressure. Thus, statement (B) is correct.

As the pressure decreases, the solubility of the dissolved gas decreases. Thus, statement (A) is correct.

The excess dissolved gas escapes rapidly in the form of bubbles, causing effervescence. Thus, statement (C) is correct.

The external atmospheric pressure remains constant and does not increase. Thus, statement (D) is incorrect.

Therefore, only (A), (B), and (C) take place.

Answer: (A), (B) and (C) only.

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify a pair of gases from following that does not obey Henry's law.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$
- **(B)** $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{N}_2$
- **(D)** $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{CH}_4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Henry's law is applicable only to those gases which do not react chemically with the solvent and do not undergo dissociation or association in the solution.

Gases like $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$ react with water to form $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$ respectively.

$\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_4^+ + \text{OH}^-$

$\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3 \rightleftharpoons \text{H}^+ + \text{HCO}_3^-$

Due to their chemical reaction with the solvent, their solubility is exceptionally high and they do not obey Henry's law.

Gases like $\text{O}_2$, $\text{N}_2$, and $\text{CH}_4$ do not react with water and thus obey Henry's law.

Answer: $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$

</details>

### Q25. MHT CET 2021 (20 Sep Shift 1)

Henry's law constant for $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{Br}$ is $0.16 \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{~L}^{-1} \mathrm{bar}^{-1}$ at $298 \mathrm{~K}$. What pressure is required to have solubility of $0.08 \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{~L}^{-1}$ ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.24 \mathrm{bar}$
- **(B)** $1.6 \mathrm{bar}$
- **(C)** $0.5 \mathrm{bar}$
- **(D)** $4.0 \mathrm{bar}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.5 \mathrm{bar}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Henry's Law
Solubility of gas $=\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{H}}$. $\mathrm{P}_{\text {gas }}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{P}_{\text {gas }}=\text { Solubility } / \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{H}} \\

& =0.5 \mathrm{bar}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Q28. MHT CET 2020 (12 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The Henry's law constant for oxygen is $1 \cdot 3 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3} \mathrm{~atm}^{-1}$. If partial pressure of oxygen is $0.46$ atmosphere what is the concentration of dissolved oxygen at $25^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and 1 atm pressure?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5.98 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$
- **(B)** $3.53 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$
- **(C)** $5.98 \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$
- **(D)** $2 \cdot 82 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$

$\therefore \mathrm{S}=1.3 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3} \mathrm{~atm}^{-1} \times 0.46 \mathrm{~atm}=5.98 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{3}$
