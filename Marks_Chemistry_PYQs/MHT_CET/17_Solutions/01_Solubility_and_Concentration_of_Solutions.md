What is the mole fraction of ethanol in solution containing $46$ g of ethanol and $180$ g of water?
(Molar mass of ethanol $=46$ g mol$^{-1}$, Molar mass of water $=18$ g mol$^{-1}$)

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statements is NOT true about solubility?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The solubility of gases in water usually decreases with increase of temperature.
- **(B)** The solubility of gases in water usually increases with increase in pressure.
- **(C)** The substances having similar inter-molecular forces are likely to be soluble in each other.
- **(D)** Gases like $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$ obey Henry's law

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Gases like $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$ obey Henry's law**

**Detailed Solution:**

The solubility of gases in liquids is an exothermic process. According to Le Chatelier's principle, solubility decreases with an increase in temperature.

According to Henry's law, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the pressure of the gas. Thus, solubility increases with an increase in pressure.

Substances with similar intermolecular forces dissolve in each other, following the principle of 'like dissolves like'.

Henry's law is applicable only to gases that do not react chemically with the solvent. Gases like $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$ react with water to form $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ and $\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$ respectively. Hence, they do not obey Henry's law.

Answer: Gases like $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{CO}_2$ obey Henry's law

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The solution containing $18 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$ glucose (molar mass 180) in water and another containing $6 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$ of solute A in water boils at same temperature. What is molar mass of A ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $54 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $90 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $120 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $60 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $60 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since both solutions boil at the same temperature, their boiling point elevations ($\Delta T_b$) are equal.

For dilute solutions, $\Delta T_b = K_b \times C$, where $C$ is the molar concentration. Therefore, the molar concentrations of both solutions must be equal.

$C_{\text{glucose}} = C_A$

$\dfrac{W_{\text{glucose}}}{M_{\text{glucose}} \times V} = \dfrac{W_A}{M_A \times V}$

Substituting the given values:

$\dfrac{18}{180} = \dfrac{6}{M_A}$

$0.1 = \dfrac{6}{M_A}$

$M_A = \dfrac{6}{0.1} = 60 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

Answer: $60 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The solubility of $\mathrm{CaCO}_3$ is $7 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$ at $25^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. What is its solubility product at same temperature?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.7 \times 10^{-9}$
- **(B)** $9.0 \times 10^{-9}$
- **(C)** $1.12 \times 10^{-9}$
- **(D)** $4.9 \times 10^{-9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $4.9 \times 10^{-9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dissociation of $\mathrm{CaCO}_3$ is given by:

$\mathrm{CaCO}_3(s) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Ca}^{2+}(aq) + \mathrm{CO}_3^{2-}(aq)$

Let the solubility of $\mathrm{CaCO}_3$ be $S$. Then the concentrations of the ions are:

$[\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}] = S$

$[\mathrm{CO}_3^{2-}] = S$

The solubility product $K_{sp}$ is:

$K_{sp} = [\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}][\mathrm{CO}_3^{2-}] = S \times S = S^2$

Given $S = 7 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~mol} \mathrm{dm}^{-3}$, substituting this value:

$K_{sp} = (7 \times 10^{-5})^2$

$K_{sp} = 49 \times 10^{-10} = 4.9 \times 10^{-9}$

Answer: $4.9 \times 10^{-9}$

</details>

$C_{\text{sucrose}} = \dfrac{34.2}{342} = 0.1 \text{ mol dm}^{-3}$

### Q14. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

Which from following ionic solids exhibits decrease in its solubility in water with increase of temperature?

**Options:**

- **(A)** NaCl
- **(B)** $\mathrm{KNO}_3$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{NaNO}_3$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{SO}_4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{SO}_4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dissolution of anhydrous sodium sulfate ($\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{SO}_4$) in water is an exothermic process ($\Delta H < 0$).

Thus, the solubility of $\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{SO}_4$ decreases with an increase in temperature.

### Q16. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the reason for the solubility of polar solute in polar solvent from the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** solute - solute interactions, solute solvent interactions and solvent - solvent interactions are of similar magnitude.
- **(B)** solute - solute interactions $\gt$ solute solvent interactions $\gt$ solvent - solvent . interactions .
- **(C)** solvent- solvent interactions $\gt$ solute solvent interactions $\gt$ solute - solute interactions.
- **(D)** solute - solvent interactions $\gt$ solute solute interactions $\gt$ solvent - solvent interactions.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) solute - solute interactions, solute solvent interactions and solvent - solvent interactions are of similar magnitude.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the principle of 'like dissolves like', a polar solute dissolves in a polar solvent because the intermolecular forces in both the solute and the solvent are similar in nature and magnitude.

For dissolution to occur, the energy required to overcome solute-solute and solvent-solvent interactions must be compensated by the energy released upon the formation of solute-solvent interactions. This is possible when solute-solute, solvent-solvent, and solute-solvent interactions are of similar magnitude.

Answer: solute - solute interactions, solute solvent interactions and solvent - solvent interactions are of similar magnitude.

</details>

The molar concentration of chloride ions in the resulting solution of $300 \mathrm{~mL}$ of $3.0 \mathrm{M} \mathrm{NaCl}$ and $200 \mathrm{~mL}$ of $4.0 \mathrm{M} \mathrm{BaCl}_{2}$ will be

- **(A)** $1.7 \mathrm{M}$
- **(B)** $1.8 \mathrm{M}$
- **(C)** $5.0 \mathrm{M}$
- **(D)** $3.5 \mathrm{M}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5.0 \mathrm{M}$**

$\underset{4.0 \mathrm{M}}{\mathrm{BaCl}_{2}} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{Ba}^{2+}+\underset{2 \times 4.0 \mathrm{M}}{2 \mathrm{Cl}^{-}}$
$\therefore$ Molar concentration of
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Cl}^{-} &=\frac{M_{1} V_{1}+M_{2} V_{2}}{V_{1}+V_{2}} \\
&=\frac{3.0 \times 300+2 \times 4.0 \times 200}{300+200} \\
&=\frac{900+1600}{500} \\
&=5.0 \mathrm{M}
\end{aligned}
$$

Given, volume of $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ before dilution, $V_{1}=2.5 \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$
$$
=2.5 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{dm}^{3}

volume of $\mathrm{II}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{4}$ after dilution, $V_{2}-0.5 \mathrm{dm}^{3}$
