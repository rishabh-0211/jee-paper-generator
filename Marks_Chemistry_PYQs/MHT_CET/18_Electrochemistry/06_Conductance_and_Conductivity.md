### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The molar conductivities of NaCl, HCl and $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COONa}$ at infinite dilution are $126.45$, $426.16$ and $91$ ohm$^{-1}$cm$^2$. The molar conductivity of $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $201.28$ ohm$^{-1}$cm$^2$
- **(B)** $698.28$ ohm$^{-1}$cm$^2$
- **(C)** $540.48$ ohm$^{-1}$cm$^2$
- **(D)** $390.71$ ohm$^{-1}$cm$^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $390.71$ ohm$^{-1}$cm$^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions, the molar conductivity of an electrolyte at infinite dilution is the sum of the limiting molar conductivities of its constituent ions.

$\Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH}) = \Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COO}^{-}) + \Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{H}^{+})$

This can be obtained by adding and subtracting the appropriate strong electrolytes:

$\Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH}) = \Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COONa}) + \Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{HCl}) - \Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{NaCl})$

Substituting the given values:

$\Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH}) = 91 + 426.16 - 126.45$

$\Lambda^{\circ}_{m}(\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{COOH}) = 517.16 - 126.45 = 390.71 \text{ ohm}^{-1}\text{cm}^2$

Answer: $390.71$ ohm$^{-1}$cm$^2$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The molar conductivity of $0.01\text{M}$ acetic acid at $25^\circ$C is $16 \text{ S m}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$. Find its percent dissociation in the $0.01\text{M}$ solution if molar conductivity of acetic acid at zero concentration is $390 \text{ S m}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1\%$
- **(B)** $2.2\%$
- **(C)** $3\%$
- **(D)** $4.1\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $4.1\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The degree of dissociation $\alpha$ is given by the ratio of molar conductivity at a given concentration to the molar conductivity at infinite dilution (zero concentration).

$\alpha = \dfrac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda_m^\circ}$

Given:
$\Lambda_m = 16 \text{ S m}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$
$\Lambda_m^\circ = 390 \text{ S m}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$

Substituting the values:
$\alpha = \dfrac{16}{390} \approx 0.041$

Percent dissociation $= \alpha \times 100$

Percent dissociation $= 0.041 \times 100 = 4.1\%$

Answer: $4.1\%$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

What is the common unit of molar conductivity?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{S m}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $\text{ohm cm}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ mol}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Molar conductivity ($\Lambda_m$) is defined as the conducting power of all the ions produced by dissolving one mole of an electrolyte in a solution.

Mathematically, it is given by:
$\Lambda_m = \dfrac{\kappa}{C}$

Where $\kappa$ is the conductivity with common units of $\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^{-1}$ (or $\text{S cm}^{-1}$) and $C$ is the concentration expressed in $\text{mol cm}^{-3}$.

Substituting the units, we get:
$\Lambda_m = \dfrac{\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^{-1}}{\text{mol cm}^{-3}} = \text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$

Thus, the common unit of molar conductivity is $\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$.

Answer: $\text{ohm}^{-1} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ mol}^{-1}$

</details>

$\dfrac{\Lambda_{m1}}{\Lambda_{m2}} = \dfrac{0.2}{0.4} = 0.5$

### Q19. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The graphical variation of molar conductivity $(\Lambda)$ against square root of molar concentration $(\sqrt{c})$ of certain electrolyte 'X' is linear with intercept on y axis. Identify X from following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$
- **(B)** $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$
- **(C)** $\text{HCOOH}$
- **(D)** $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the Debye-Hückel-Onsager equation, the variation of molar conductivity $\Lambda_m$ with the square root of concentration $\sqrt{c}$ is given by $\Lambda_m = \Lambda_m^\circ - A\sqrt{c}$.

This linear relationship with a definite y-intercept ($\Lambda_m^\circ$) is observed only for strong electrolytes.
