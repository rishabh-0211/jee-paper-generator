### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following reactions occurs at cathode during recharging of lead accumulator ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{PbO}_{2(s)} + 4\text{H}^+_{(aq.)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Pb}_{(s)} + \text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\ell)}$
- **(B)** $\text{PbO}_{2(s)} + 4\text{H}^+\ 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Pb}^{2+} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\ell)}$
- **(C)** $\text{Pb}_{(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)} \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2e^-$
- **(D)** $\text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Pb}_{(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Pb}_{(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

During the discharging of a lead accumulator, the cell acts as a galvanic cell with the following reactions:
At anode (oxidation): $\text{Pb}_{(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)} \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2e^-$
At cathode (reduction): $\text{PbO}_{2(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)} + 4\text{H}^+_{(aq.)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\ell)}$

During recharging, an external voltage is applied and the cell acts as an electrolytic cell. The reactions are reversed. The electrode that acted as the anode during discharge becomes the cathode (where reduction occurs), and the electrode that acted as the cathode becomes the anode (where oxidation occurs).

At cathode (reduction) during recharging:
$\text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Pb}_{(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)}$

At anode (oxidation) during recharging:
$\text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}_{(\ell)} \rightarrow \text{PbO}_{2(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)} + 4\text{H}^+_{(aq.)} + 2e^-$

Answer: $\text{PbSO}_{4(s)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Pb}_{(s)} + \text{SO}_4^{2-}{}_{(aq.)}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the change in oxidation number of Pb at positive electrode of lead accumulator acting as galvanic cell ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** increases by 1
- **(B)** decreases by 1
- **(C)** increases by 2
- **(D)** decreases by 2

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) decreases by 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a lead accumulator acting as a galvanic cell (discharging), the positive electrode acts as the cathode where reduction takes place.

The reaction at the positive electrode is:
$PbO_2(s) + SO_4^{2-}(aq) + 4H^+(aq) + 2e^- \rightarrow PbSO_4(s) + 2H_2O(l)$

The oxidation state of Pb in $PbO_2$ is $+4$.
The oxidation state of Pb in $PbSO_4$ is $+2$.

The oxidation number of Pb changes from $+4$ to $+2$, which means it decreases by $2$.

Answer: decreases by 2

</details>

- **(D)** Manganese dioxide

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Options:**

- **(C)** The anode of voltaic cells is positive.
- **(D)** The cathode of voltaic cell is negative.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) It converts chemical energy into electrical energy.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A voltaic cell (or galvanic cell) is an electrochemical cell that uses a spontaneous chemical reaction to generate an electric current. Therefore, it converts chemical energy into electrical energy.

In a voltaic cell, oxidation occurs at the anode, making it the negative electrode, while reduction occurs at the cathode, making it the positive electrode. An electrolytic cell, on the other hand, converts electrical energy into chemical energy.

Calculate the amount of electricity required to convert 1.1 mol of $\mathrm{Cr}_2 \mathrm{O}_7^{2-}$ to $\mathrm{Cr}^{+3}$ in acidic medium.

- **(A)** $6.369 \times 10^5 \mathrm{C}$
- **(B)** $1.462 \times 10^5 \mathrm{C}$
- **(C)** $4.839 \times 10^5 \mathrm{C}$
- **(D)** $3.419 \times 10^5 \mathrm{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $6.369 \times 10^5 \mathrm{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The balanced half-reaction for the reduction of dichromate ion in acidic medium is:

$\mathrm{Cr}_2 \mathrm{O}_7^{2-} + 14\mathrm{H}^+ + 6\mathrm{e}^- \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Cr}^{3+} + 7\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$

From the stoichiometry of the reaction, 1 mole of $\mathrm{Cr}_2 \mathrm{O}_7^{2-}$ requires 6 moles of electrons.

Number of moles of electrons required for 1.1 moles of $\mathrm{Cr}_2 \mathrm{O}_7^{2-}$ is $1.1 \times 6 = 6.6$ moles.

The amount of electricity required is given by $Q = nF$, where $F \approx 96500 \mathrm{C}$.

$Q = 6.6 \times 96500 \mathrm{C} = 636900 \mathrm{C} = 6.369 \times 10^5 \mathrm{C}$

Answer: $6.369 \times 10^5 \mathrm{C}$
