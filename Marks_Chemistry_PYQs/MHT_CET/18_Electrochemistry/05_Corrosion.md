### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If a galvanized iron sheet is scratched and the iron surface is exposed to the atmosphere, the iron does not undergo corrosion. This is because:

**Options:**

- **(A)** Zinc forms a protective oxide layer that covers the scratch.
- **(B)** Iron has a higher reduction potential than zinc, making iron the cathode.
- **(C)** Zinc has a lower oxidation potential than iron, acting as a sacrificial anode.
- **(D)** Electrons flow from the iron to the zinc to prevent oxidation.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Iron has a higher reduction potential than zinc, making iron the cathode.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The standard reduction potential of zinc ($E^{\circ}_{\text{Zn}^{2+}/\text{Zn}} = -0.76 \text{ V}$) is lower than that of iron ($E^{\circ}_{\text{Fe}^{2+}/\text{Fe}} = -0.44 \text{ V}$).

Since iron has a higher reduction potential than zinc, it has a greater tendency to get reduced. When the galvanized sheet is scratched and both metals are exposed to the environment, an electrochemical cell is formed.

Zinc, having a higher oxidation potential, acts as the anode and undergoes oxidation preferentially. It supplies electrons to the iron, which acts as the cathode. This prevents the oxidation of iron, thereby protecting it from corrosion. This method of protection is known as cathodic protection.

Answer: Iron has a higher reduction potential than zinc, making iron the cathode.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2018

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

During galvanization of iron, which metal is used for coating the iron surface?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Copper
- **(B)** Zinc
- **(C)** Nickel
- **(D)** Tin

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Zinc**

**Detailed Solution:**

Because zinc gets oxidized first when comes in contact with moisture and hence iron surface is protected from corrosion.

</details>
