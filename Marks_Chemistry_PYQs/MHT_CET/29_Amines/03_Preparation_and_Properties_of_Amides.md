### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following set of conditions is used to convert acetamide into ethyl amine ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{LiAlH}_4$ / Na
- **(B)** $\text{LiAlH}_4$ / ether
- **(C)** Sn / HCl
- **(D)** $\text{NH}_3$(alc.)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{LiAlH}_4$ / ether**

**Detailed Solution:**

Acetamide ($\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2$) is an amide, which can be reduced to the corresponding primary amine, ethyl amine ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2$), using a strong reducing agent.

Lithium aluminium hydride ($\text{LiAlH}_4$) in the presence of ether is the standard reagent for this conversion.

$\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 / \text{ether}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Sn / HCl is typically used to reduce nitro compounds to amines, while alcoholic $\text{NH}_3$ is used for the ammonolysis of alkyl halides.

Answer: $\text{LiAlH}_4$ / ether

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the number of moles of hydrogen atoms needed to obtain one mole ethanamine from acetamide using $\text{LiAl H}_4$ /ether as reducing agent.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $3$
- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The chemical reaction for the reduction of acetamide to ethanamine using $\text{LiAlH}_4$ is:

$\text{CH}_3\text{CONH}_2 + 4[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4/\text{ether}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{NH}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

In this process, the carbonyl oxygen is removed as a water molecule, which consumes two hydrogen atoms. The carbonyl carbon is reduced to a methylene group ($\text{CH}_2$), which consumes another two hydrogen atoms.

Thus, a total of $4$ moles of hydrogen atoms are required to reduce one mole of acetamide to one mole of ethanamine.

Answer: $4$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the loss in molar mass when a primary amine is obtained by Hofmann degradation of amide?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $32 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $14 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $28 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $30 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $28 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The general reaction for Hofmann degradation of an amide is given by:

$R-CO-NH_2 \xrightarrow{Br_2 / NaOH} R-NH_2$

The amide ($R-CO-NH_2$) is converted into a primary amine ($R-NH_2$) with one carbon atom less than the original amide.

The group lost during this reaction is the carbonyl group ($-CO-$).

Molar mass of the carbonyl group ($-CO-$) = Atomic mass of C + Atomic mass of O

Molar mass of $-CO-$ = $12 + 16 = 28 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

Therefore, the loss in molar mass is $28 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$.

Answer: $28 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

How many moles of iodomethane are consumed in the following conversion?
$\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{I}}\left(\mathrm{CH}_3\right)_4 \cdot \mathrm{~N}^{+} \mathrm{I}^{-}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** Four
- **(B)** Three
- **(C)** Two
- **(D)** One

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Three**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given reaction is the exhaustive methylation of methylamine ($\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2$) to form tetramethylammonium iodide ($\left(\mathrm{CH}_3\right)_4\mathrm{N}^{+}\mathrm{I}^{-}$).

The starting material, methylamine, already contains one methyl group attached to the nitrogen atom.

The product, tetramethylammonium iodide, contains four methyl groups attached to the nitrogen atom.

The reaction proceeds in three steps, with each step consuming one mole of iodomethane ($\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{I}$):

Step 1: $\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{NH}_2 + \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{I} \rightarrow (\mathrm{CH}_3)_2\mathrm{NH} + \mathrm{HI}$

Step 2: $(\mathrm{CH}_3)_2\mathrm{NH} + \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{I} \rightarrow (\mathrm{CH}_3)_3\mathrm{N} + \mathrm{HI}$

Step 3: $(\mathrm{CH}_3)_3\mathrm{N} + \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{I} \rightarrow (\mathrm{CH}_3)_4\mathrm{N}^{+}\mathrm{I}^{-}$

Therefore, a total of three moles of iodomethane are consumed to convert one mole of methylamine into tetramethylammonium iodide.

Answer: Three

</details>
