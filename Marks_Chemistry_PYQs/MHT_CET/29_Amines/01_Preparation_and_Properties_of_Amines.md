### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the false statement regarding amide from the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Amide is obtained on reaction of amines with acetyl chlorid.
- **(B)** Amide is more basic than amine
- **(C)** Acylation of amine is a nucleophilic substitution reaction.
- **(D)** The acylation reaction is carried out in presence of base.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Amide is more basic than amine**

**Detailed Solution:**

Amines react with acid chlorides such as acetyl chloride to form amides. This is a nucleophilic acyl substitution reaction where the amine acts as a nucleophile.

The acylation reaction produces HCl as a byproduct. A base, such as pyridine, is typically used to neutralize the acid formed and prevent the unreacted amine from being protonated into a non-nucleophilic salt.

In amines, the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom is localized and readily available for protonation, making them basic.

However, in amides, the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom is delocalized due to resonance with the highly electronegative oxygen atom of the adjacent carbonyl group. This delocalization makes the lone pair significantly less available for protonation.

Therefore, amides are much less basic than amines. The statement that an amide is more basic than an amine is false.

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following has highest $pK_b$ value ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{CCl}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$
- **(B)** $\text{CCl}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{CF}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$
- **(D)** $\text{CF}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{CF}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The basic strength of an amine is inversely proportional to its $pK_b$ value. Thus, the highest $pK_b$ corresponds to the weakest base.

The basicity of amines decreases with the presence of electron-withdrawing groups (-I effect) because they decrease the electron density on the nitrogen atom.

The -I effect of the $\text{CF}_3$ group is stronger than that of the $\text{CCl}_3$ group due to the higher electronegativity of fluorine compared to chlorine.

The inductive effect is distance-dependent and its strength decreases as the distance from the basic center increases.

In $\text{CF}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$, the strongly electron-withdrawing $\text{CF}_3$ group is closest to the $\text{NH}_2$ group (separated by only one carbon atom). This exerts the maximum -I effect on the nitrogen atom.

Therefore, $\text{CF}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$ is the weakest base among the given options and has the highest $pK_b$ value.

Answer: $\text{CF}_3 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{NH}_2$

</details>

What type of amines exhibits diazotization reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Aliphatic primary amine
- **(B)** Aromatic primary amine
- **(C)** Aliphatic secondary amine
- **(D)** Aromatic secondary amine

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Aromatic primary amine**

Aromatic primary amines react with nitrous acid (prepared in situ from $\text{NaNO}_2$ and a mineral acid such as $\text{HCl}$) at low temperatures ($273 - 278 \text{ K}$) to form stable arenediazonium salts.

Secondary and tertiary amines react differently with nitrous acid, forming N-nitrosamines and other products.

Therefore, aromatic primary amines exhibit the diazotization reaction to form stable diazonium salts.

Answer: Aromatic primary amine

</details>

Which of the following compounds is obtained when N,N,N-triethylpropanammonium hydroxide is heated?

**Options:**

- **(B)** Ethene and N,N-diethylpropylamine
- **(C)** Ethene and N-ethylpropylamine
- **(D)** Ethene, ethane and N,ethylpropylamine

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Ethene and N,N-diethylpropylamine**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given compound, N,N,N-triethylpropanammonium hydroxide, is a quaternary ammonium salt with the structure $[CH_3CH_2CH_2N^{+}(CH_2CH_3)_3] OH^{-}$.

When heated, it undergoes Hofmann elimination (an E2 mechanism). The hydroxide ion abstracts a $\beta$-hydrogen to form an alkene, water, and a tertiary amine. According to Hofmann's rule, the less substituted alkene is formed as the major product due to steric factors and the carbanion-like transition state.

The nitrogen atom is attached to ethyl and propyl groups. Elimination from an ethyl group yields ethene, while elimination from the propyl group yields propene. Since ethene is less substituted than propene, the elimination preferentially occurs at one of the ethyl groups.

The reaction is:
$[CH_3CH_2CH_2N^{+}(CH_2CH_3)_3] OH^{-} \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_2=CH_2 + CH_3CH_2CH_2N(CH_2CH_3)_2 + H_2O$

Thus, the major products are ethene and N,N-diethylpropylamine.

Answer: Ethene and N,N-diethylpropylamine

### Q20. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following statements is NOT correct regarding Hofmann's exhaustive alkylation?

**Options:**

- **(A)** In this quaternary ammmonium salts are obtained.
- **(B)** Two moles of alkylhalide when heated with one mole primary amine forms tetraalkyl ammonium halide.
- **(C)** Depending on the quantity of alkyl halides treated with primary amine, the mixture of secondary and tertiary amines obtained.
- **(D)** The reaction is carried out in presence of $\text{NaHCO}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Two moles of alkylhalide when heated with one mole primary amine forms tetraalkyl ammonium halide.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hofmann's exhaustive alkylation is the process of converting an amine into a quaternary ammonium salt by treating it with an excess of alkyl halide.

When a primary amine ($RNH_2$) is heated with an alkyl halide ($RX$), it undergoes successive nucleophilic substitution reactions:
$RNH_2 + RX \rightarrow R_2NH + HX$ (Secondary amine)
$R_2NH + RX \rightarrow R_3N + HX$ (Tertiary amine)
$R_3N + RX \rightarrow R_4N^+X^-$ (Quaternary ammonium salt)

To convert one mole of a primary amine into a tetraalkyl ammonium halide, three moles of alkyl halide are required, not two. Therefore, the statement that two moles of alkyl halide form a tetraalkyl ammonium halide is incorrect.
