Identify a positively charged sol from the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Antimony trisulfide
- **(B)** Eosin
- **(C)** Gum
- **(D)** Hemoglobin

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Hemoglobin**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the classification of colloidal sols based on the nature of charge on the particles:

Positively charged sols include hydrated metallic oxides (e.g., $Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$), basic dyes (e.g., methylene blue), hemoglobin (blood), and certain oxides (e.g., $TiO_2$).

Negatively charged sols include metal sols (e.g., Cu, Ag, Au), metallic sulfides (e.g., $Sb_2S_3$, $As_2S_3$), acid dyes (e.g., eosin, congo red), and naturally occurring macromolecular sols (e.g., starch, gum, gelatin, clay).

Therefore, hemoglobin is a positively charged sol, while antimony trisulfide, eosin, and gum are negatively charged sols.

Answer: Hemoglobin

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following cations has maximum coagulating power to cause precipitation of negatively charged arsenious sulfide ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{Na}^+$
- **(B)** $\text{Zn}^{++}$
- **(C)** $\text{Ca}^{++}$
- **(D)** $\text{Al}^{+++}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{Al}^{+++}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the Hardy-Schulze rule, the coagulating power of an ion is directly proportional to the magnitude of its charge (valency) for an oppositely charged sol.

Arsenious sulfide is a negatively charged sol, so cations are responsible for its coagulation.

The given cations and their charges are:
$\text{Na}^+$ : $+1$
$\text{Zn}^{++}$ : $+2$
$\text{Ca}^{++}$ : $+2$
$\text{Al}^{+++}$ : $+3$

Since $\text{Al}^{+++}$ has the highest positive charge, it has the maximum coagulating power.

Answer: $\text{Al}^{+++}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the term used to describe movement of colloidal particles under applied electric potential without using semipermeable membrane.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Dialysis
- **(B)** Electrophoresis
- **(C)** Electroosmosis
- **(D)** Brownian motion

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Electrophoresis**

**Detailed Solution:**

The movement of colloidal particles under the influence of an applied electric potential is called electrophoresis.

When the movement of colloidal particles is prevented by some suitable means (like a semipermeable membrane), it is observed that the dispersion medium begins to move in an electric field. This phenomenon is termed electroosmosis.

Since the question specifies the movement of colloidal particles without using a semipermeable membrane, the correct term is electrophoresis.

Answer: Electrophoresis

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

Therefore, proteins are an example of a macromolecular colloid.

### Q10. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from following substances is classified as macromolecular colloid?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Soap
- **(B)** Detergent
- **(C)** $\mathrm{S}_8$ sulphur molecules
- **(D)** Nylon

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Nylon**

**Detailed Solution:**

Colloids are classified into three types based on the type of particles of the dispersed phase:

1. Multimolecular colloids: Formed by the aggregation of a large number of atoms or smaller molecules. Example: Sulphur sol ($\mathrm{S}_8$).

2. Macromolecular colloids: Formed by macromolecules (polymers) having large molecular masses. Examples: Starch, cellulose, proteins, nylon, polystyrene, synthetic rubber.

3. Associated colloids (Micelles): Formed by the aggregation of ions in concentrated solutions. Examples: Soaps and synthetic detergents.

Therefore, nylon is classified as a macromolecular colloid.

Answer: Nylon

</details>

### Q17. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Which from following statements is NOT true about lyophilic colloids?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The particles of dispersed phase have greater affinity for the dispersion medium.
- **(B)** These are reversible.
- **(C)** These are self stabilized.
- **(D)** Coagulation occurs even by adding very small amount of electrolytes.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Coagulation occurs even by adding very small amount of electrolytes.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Lyophilic colloids are liquid-loving colloids where the dispersed phase has a strong affinity for the dispersion medium. This strong interaction leads to extensive solvation, making them highly stable and self-stabilized.

Because of their high stability, they are not easily coagulated. A large amount of electrolyte is required to cause their coagulation (a process known as salting out). The statement that coagulation occurs even by adding a very small amount of electrolytes is true for lyophobic colloids, not lyophilic colloids.

They are also reversible in nature, meaning if the dispersion medium is separated from the dispersed phase, the sol can be reconstituted simply by remixing.

Answer: Coagulation occurs even by adding very small amount of electrolytes.

</details>
