### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the role of catalyst in catalytic reaction ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It decreases the enthalpy of reaction.
- **(B)** It increases the enthalpy of reaction.
- **(C)** It decreases the energy of activation of reaction.
- **(D)** It increases the energy of activation of reaction.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) It decreases the energy of activation of reaction.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A catalyst increases the rate of a chemical reaction by providing an alternative reaction pathway that has a lower activation energy.

It does not alter the enthalpy, entropy, or Gibbs free energy of the reaction.

Thus, the primary role of a catalyst is to decrease the energy of activation of the reaction.

Answer: It decreases the energy of activation of reaction.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is an example of heterogenous catalysis?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Oxidation of $\mathrm{SO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}$ in presence of $\mathrm{NO}_{(\mathrm{g})}$.
- **(B)** Decomposition of aqueous $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_2{ }^{-}$in presence of $\mathrm{I}_{\text {(aq) }}^{-}$.
- **(C)** Hydrolysis of sugar in presence of aq. $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{SO}_4$.
- **(D)** Hydrogenation of vegetable oil in presence of $\mathrm{Ni}_{(\mathrm{s})}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Hydrogenation of vegetable oil in presence of $\mathrm{Ni}_{(\mathrm{s})}$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Heterogeneous catalysis is a catalytic process in which the catalyst and the reactants are in different phases.

In the oxidation of $\mathrm{SO}_{2(\mathrm{g})}$ in the presence of $\mathrm{NO}_{(\mathrm{g})}$, both the reactants and the catalyst are in the gaseous phase, making it an example of homogeneous catalysis.

In the decomposition of aqueous $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ in the presence of $\mathrm{I}^{-}_{(\mathrm{aq})}$, both the reactant and the catalyst are in the aqueous phase, which is homogeneous catalysis.

In the hydrolysis of sugar in the presence of aqueous $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4$, the reactants and the catalyst are in the same liquid phase, so it is homogeneous catalysis.

In the hydrogenation of vegetable oil (liquid) with $\mathrm{H}_{2(\mathrm{g})}$ in the presence of $\mathrm{Ni}_{(\mathrm{s})}$, the catalyst is in the solid phase while the reactants are in liquid and gaseous phases. Since the catalyst and reactants are in different phases, this is an example of heterogeneous catalysis.

Answer: Hydrogenation of vegetable oil in presence of $\mathrm{Ni}_{(\mathrm{s})}$.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2021 (24 Sep Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Which of the following is a first step in mechanism of heterogenous catalysis?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Desorption of reaction product from catalyst surface
- **(B)** Adsorption of reactant molecules on catalyst surface to form an intermediate
- **(C)** Occurrence of chemical reaction on catalyst surface to form an intermediate
- **(D)** Diffusion of reactants towards catalyst surface

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Diffusion of reactants towards catalyst surface**

**Detailed Solution:**

The catalytic activity is localised on the surface of the catalyst. The mechanism involves five steps:
(i) Diffusion of reactants to the surface of the catalyst.
(ii) Adsorption of reactant molecules on the surface of the catalyst.
(iii) Occurrence of chemical reaction on the catalyst surface, through formation of an intermediate.
(iv) Desorption of reaction products away from the catalyst surface.
(v) Diffusion of reaction products away from the catalyst surface.

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2020 (12 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is a character of catalyst?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It changes the position of equilibrium.
- **(B)** It increases the rates of both forward and Backward reactions equally in reversible reaction.
- **(C)** It affects the energies of reactants and products of the reaction.
- **(D)** It increases the activation energy of reactants.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) It increases the rates of both forward and Backward reactions equally in reversible reaction.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Reactions can be sped up by the addition of a catalyst, including reversible reactions involving a final equilibrium state. Recall that for a reversible reaction, the equilibrium state is one in which the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. In the presence of a catalyst, both the forward and reverse reaction rates will speed up equally, thereby allowing the system to reach equilibrium faster. However, it is very important to keep in mind that the addition of a catalyst has no effect whatsoever on the final equilibrium position of the reaction. It simply gets it there faster.

</details>
