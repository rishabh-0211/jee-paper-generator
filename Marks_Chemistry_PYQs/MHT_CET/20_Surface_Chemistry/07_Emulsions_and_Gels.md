### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statements is NOT true regarding oil in water emulsion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The oil is dispersed phase and water is dispersion medium.
- **(B)** If water is added, it will be miscible with the emulsion.
- **(C)** The addition of a small amount of an electrolyte has no effect on conducting power.
- **(D)** Water is a continuous phase.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The addition of a small amount of an electrolyte has no effect on conducting power.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In an oil-in-water emulsion, oil is the dispersed phase and water is the dispersion medium, which acts as the continuous phase.

Since water is the continuous phase, the emulsion can be diluted with water, making it miscible.

Because the continuous phase is aqueous, the emulsion can conduct electricity. The addition of a small amount of an electrolyte will increase the conducting power of the emulsion.

Therefore, the statement that the addition of a small amount of an electrolyte has no effect on conducting power is incorrect.

Answer: The addition of a small amount of an electrolyte has no effect on conducting power.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following properties is NOT true regarding emulsion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The droplets in emulsions are often negatively charged and can be precipitated by electrolysis.
- **(B)** Emulsions exhibit Brownian movement and Tyndall effect.
- **(C)** The two liquids in emulsion can not be seperated by heating, freezing etc.
- **(D)** The emulsions are stabilised by addition of third component called emulsifying agent.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The two liquids in emulsion can not be seperated by heating, freezing etc.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Emulsions can be broken into their constituent liquids by physical methods such as heating, freezing, centrifuging, etc. This process is known as demulsification. Therefore, the statement that the two liquids in an emulsion cannot be separated by heating, freezing, etc., is incorrect.

The other statements are true:
1. Emulsions are colloidal systems, so they exhibit the Tyndall effect and Brownian movement.
2. Emulsions are thermodynamically unstable and are stabilised by the addition of a third component called an emulsifying agent.
3. The dispersed droplets in emulsions are often negatively charged and can be precipitated (coagulated) by the addition of electrolytes.

Answer: The two liquids in emulsion can not be seperated by heating, freezing etc.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from following is NOT an example of oil in water emulsion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Paint
- **(B)** Butter
- **(C)** Vanishing cream
- **(D)** Milk

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Butter**

**Detailed Solution:**

Emulsions are broadly classified into two types based on the dispersed phase and dispersion medium:

1. Oil in water (O/W) emulsion: In this type, oil acts as the dispersed phase and water acts as the dispersion medium. Examples include milk, vanishing cream, and paint.

2. Water in oil (W/O) emulsion: In this type, water acts as the dispersed phase and oil acts as the dispersion medium. Examples include butter, cold cream, and cod liver oil.

Since butter is a water in oil (W/O) emulsion, it is not an example of an oil in water emulsion.

Answer: Butter

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is a water-in-oil type of emulsion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Milk
- **(B)** Vanishing Cream
- **(C)** Paint
- **(D)** Butter

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Butter**

**Detailed Solution:**

Emulsions are colloidal systems in which both the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium are liquids.

In a water-in-oil (W/O) emulsion, water is the dispersed phase and oil is the dispersion medium.

Butter is an example of a water-in-oil emulsion, where water is dispersed in fat (oil).

Milk and vanishing cream are examples of oil-in-water (O/W) emulsions, where oil is dispersed in water.

Answer: Butter

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which among the following is an example of emulsion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Foam rubber
- **(B)** Froth
- **(C)** Gelatin
- **(D)** Hair cream

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Hair cream**

**Detailed Solution:**

An emulsion is a colloidal dispersion in which both the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium are liquids.

Foam rubber is a solid foam (gas dispersed in a solid).

Froth is a foam (gas dispersed in a liquid).

Gelatin is a gel (liquid dispersed in a solid).

Hair cream is an emulsion (liquid dispersed in a liquid).

Answer: Hair cream

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2020 (12 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When soap is added to an oily part of cloth, the hydrocarbon part of soap dissolves in

**Options:**

- **(A)** Water, keeping the tail away from the oil
- **(B)** Oil, keeping the head away from the oil
- **(C)** Water, keeping the head away from the oil
- **(D)** Oil, keeping the tail away from the oil

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Oil, keeping the head away from the oil**

**Detailed Solution:**

When soap is added to an oily or greasy part of cloth, the hydrocarbon part of soap dissolves in oil, keeping the head away from the oil. Big molecules of oil and soap break by rubbing into small emulsified oil droplets in water, which are washed away by stream of water.

</details>
