Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of divalent cation of a transition metal with atomic number $25$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.84$ BM
- **(B)** $3.87$ BM
- **(C)** $4.90$ BM
- **(D)** $5.92$ BM

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $5.92$ BM**

**Detailed Solution:**

Atomic number $Z = 25$ corresponds to Manganese (Mn).

The electronic configuration of neutral Mn is $[Ar] 3d^5 4s^2$.

For the divalent cation $Mn^{2+}$, two electrons are removed from the $4s$ orbital, giving the configuration $[Ar] 3d^5$.

The spin-only magnetic moment is given by $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$ BM.

Answer: $5.92$ BM

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The effective magnetic moment ($\mu$) of $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ is measured as $3.87$ Bohr magnetons (BM). Determine the number of unpaired electrons in $\text{Cr}^{3+}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The effective magnetic moment $\mu$ is given by the formula $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}$ BM, where $n$ is the number of unpaired electrons.

Given $\mu = 3.87$ BM, we have:

$\sqrt{n(n+2)} = 3.87$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$n(n+2) \approx 15$

$n^2 + 2n - 15 = 0$

$(n+5)(n-3) = 0$

Since the number of unpaired electrons cannot be negative, $n = 3$.

Alternatively, the atomic number of Cr is $24$ with the electronic configuration $[\text{Ar}] 3d^5 4s^1$.

For the $\text{Cr}^{3+}$ ion, three electrons are removed (one from $4s$ and two from $3d$), resulting in the configuration $[\text{Ar}] 3d^3$.

Thus, there are $3$ unpaired electrons.

Answer: $3$

</details>

### Q112. MHT CET 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which gives $+7$ oxidation state?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Mn}(25)$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Cr}(24)$
- **(C)** $\operatorname{Cu}(29)$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Fe}(26)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{Mn}(25)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For $d$ -block elements, oxidation state $=n s$ electrons $+(n-1) d$
electrons (unpaired)
(a) $\operatorname{Mn}(25)=[\operatorname{Ar}] 3 d^{5} 4 s^{2}$
$$
\text { O. } \mathrm{S} .=5+2=7
$$
(b) $\operatorname{Cr}(24)=[\operatorname{Ar}] 3 d^{5} 4 s^{1}$
O. S. $=5+1=6$
(c) $\operatorname{Cu}(29)=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{10} 4 s^{1}$
O. S. $=0+1=1$
(d) $\mathrm{Fe}(26)=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{6} 4 s^{2}$
O.S. $=+2$ and $+3$
Thus, only Mn exhibits $+7$ oxidation state among the given elements.

$\mathrm{Ce}^{4+}$ is stable. This is because of

**Options:**

### Q117. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following ions is colourless in solution?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{V}^{3+}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Cr}^{3+}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Sc}^{3+}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{Sc}^{3+}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\mathrm{V}(23)=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 \mathrm{~d}^{3}, 4 s^{2}$
$\mathrm{V}^{3+}=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{2}, 4 s^{0} \quad$ (two unpaired electrons)
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\operatorname{Cr}(24)=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{5}, 4 s^{1} \\
\mathrm{Cr}^{3+}=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{3}, 4 s^{0}
\end{array}
$$
(three unpaired electrons)
$$
\operatorname{Co}(27)=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{7}, 4 s^{2}
$$
$$
\mathrm{Co}^{2+}=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 \mathrm{~d}^{7}, 4 s^{0}
$$
(three unpaired electrons)
$$
\mathrm{Sc}(21)=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{7}, 4 s^{2}
$$
$\mathrm{Sc}^{3+}=[\mathrm{Ar}] 3 d^{0}, 4 s^{0} \quad$ (no unpaired electrons)
Thus, in $\mathrm{Sc}^{3+}$ no unpaired $d$ -electron is present. Hence, no $d-d$ transition is possible and it is colourless ion.

</details>
