### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Why is actinoid contraction more as compared to lanthanoide contraction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The shielding effect of electrons present in 4f and 5f orbitals is equal
- **(B)** The azimuthal quantum numbers of 4f and 5f are same
- **(C)** Poor shielding is offered by 5f electrons compared to 4f electrons in actinoids.
- **(D)** 4f orbitals are more diffused as compared to 5f orbitals

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Poor shielding is offered by 5f electrons compared to 4f electrons in actinoids.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Actinoid contraction is greater from element to element than lanthanoid contraction. This is due to the poor shielding effect of $5f$ electrons in actinoids compared to that of $4f$ electrons in lanthanoids.

The $5f$ orbitals are more extended in space (more diffused) than $4f$ orbitals, which leads to poorer shielding of the outer electrons from the increasing nuclear charge. As a result, the effective nuclear charge experienced by the outer electrons increases more rapidly in actinoids, causing a greater contraction in size.

Answer: Poor shielding is offered by 5f electrons compared to 4f electrons in actinoids.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following properties do the lanthanoides and actinoides resemble?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Electronic configuration
- **(B)** Ionisation enthalpy
- **(C)** Formation of complexes
- **(D)** Oxidation state

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Electronic configuration**

**Detailed Solution:**

Lanthanoids and actinoids both belong to the f-block of the periodic table and resemble each other in their general electronic configuration. Both series involve the progressive filling of f-orbitals and have their three outermost shells incompletely filled. Their general electronic configuration is $(n-2)f^{1-14} (n-1)d^{0-1} ns^2$.

For the other properties:
Oxidation state: Actinoids show a wide range of oxidation states (up to $+7$) due to the comparable energies of $5f$, $6d$, and $7s$ orbitals, whereas lanthanoids predominantly show a $+3$ oxidation state.
Formation of complexes: Actinoids have a much greater tendency to form complexes compared to lanthanoids because of their higher charge density and smaller size of highly charged ions.
Ionisation enthalpy: The ionisation enthalpies of early actinoids are lower than those of early lanthanoids because $5f$ electrons are less effectively shielded from the nuclear charge than $4f$ electrons.

Answer: Electronic configuration

</details>

### Q13. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following factors may be regarded as the main cause of lanthanide contraction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Poor shielding of one of 4f electron by another in the sub-shell.
- **(B)** Effective shielding of one of 4f electrons by another in the subshell
- **(C)** Poorer shielding of 5d electrons by 4f electrons
- **(D)** Greater shielding of 5d electrons by 4f electrons

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Poor shielding of one of 4f electron by another in the sub-shell.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Lanthanide contraction refers to the steady decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of lanthanides with an increase in atomic number.

As we move along the lanthanide series, the nuclear charge increases by one unit at each step, and the new electron enters the 4f subshell. The shape of the 4f orbitals is highly diffused, which results in a very poor shielding effect. Consequently, the shielding of one 4f electron by another in the same subshell is inadequate to counterbalance the increased nuclear charge.

Due to this poor shielding, the effective nuclear charge experienced by the outermost electrons increases, pulling them closer to the nucleus and causing a contraction in size.

Answer: Poor shielding of one of 4f electron by another in the sub-shell.

</details>

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Lu**

The given elements belong to the lanthanide series.

As we move from left to right in the lanthanide series (from Cerium to Lutetium), the nuclear charge increases while the new electrons enter the inner 4f subshell. Due to the poor shielding effect of the 4f electrons, the effective nuclear charge increases, leading to a steady decrease in the ionic radii of the elements in their +3 oxidation state. This phenomenon is known as lanthanide contraction.

The order of atomic numbers for the given elements is Nd (60) < Sm (62) < Ho (67) < Lu (71).

Since Lutetium (Lu) is the last element in the lanthanide series, its $+3$ ion ($Lu^{3+}$) experiences the maximum effective nuclear charge among the given options. Therefore, $Lu^{3+}$ has the smallest ionic size.

Answer: Lu

### Q66. MHT CET 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Most basic hydroxide among the following is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Lu}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Eu}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Yb}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Ce}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{Ce}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Due to lanthanide contraction, the size of $M^{3+}$ ions [ie, $\mathrm{Lu}^{3+}, \mathrm{Eu}^{3+}, \mathrm{Yb}^{3+}$ and $\mathrm{Ce}^{3+}$ ] decreases
and thus, the basic strength of their hydroxides decreases.
The order of size of given $M^{3+}$ ions is $\mathrm{Ce}^{3+}>\mathrm{Eu}^{3+}>\mathrm{Yb}^{3+}>\mathrm{Lu}^{3+}$
$\therefore$ The order of basic strength of hydroxides is $\mathrm{Ce}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}>\mathrm{Eu}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}>\mathrm{Yb}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}>\mathrm{Lu}(\mathrm{OH})_{3}$
(most basic)

</details>

### Q67. MHT CET 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The pair, that referred as 'chemical twins' is

**Options:**

- **(A)** Ac, Cf
- **(B)** Hf, Ta
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Tc}$, $\mathrm{Re}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{La}, \mathrm{Ac}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{Tc}$, $\mathrm{Re}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Due to the presence of $f$ -electrons, the nucleus is not screened effectively. Thus, the atomic radius of elements of second transition series (ie, Nb, Mo, Tc etc) is similar to that of corresponding third transition series elements (ie, Ta, W, Re). Hence, Nb-Ta; Mo-W or Tc-Re are called chemical twins.

</details>
