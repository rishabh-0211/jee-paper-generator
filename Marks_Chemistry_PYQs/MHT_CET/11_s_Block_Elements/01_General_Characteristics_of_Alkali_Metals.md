### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which one of the following nitrates will decompose to give $\text{NO}_2$ on heating ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{NaNO}_3$
- **(B)** $\text{KNO}_3$
- **(C)** $\text{RbNO}_3$
- **(D)** $\text{LiNO}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{LiNO}_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Alkali metal nitrates generally decompose on heating to give the corresponding metal nitrite and oxygen gas.

$2\text{MNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{MNO}_2 + \text{O}_2$ (where $\text{M} = \text{Na}, \text{K}, \text{Rb}, \text{Cs}$)

However, lithium is an exception due to its small size and high polarizing power. It exhibits a diagonal relationship with magnesium. Lithium nitrate decomposes on heating to give lithium oxide, nitrogen dioxide, and oxygen.

$4\text{LiNO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\text{Li}_2\text{O} + 4\text{NO}_2 + \text{O}_2$

Therefore, $\text{LiNO}_3$ decomposes to give $\text{NO}_2$ on heating.

Answer: $\text{LiNO}_3$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

Which element from following forms superoxide with oxygen?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Sodium
- **(B)** Potassium
- **(C)** Lithium
- **(D)** Magnesium

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Potassium**

**Detailed Solution:**

Alkali metals react with oxygen to form different types of oxides depending on their size.

Lithium forms mainly monoxide ($Li_{2}O$).

Sodium forms mainly peroxide ($Na_{2}O_{2}$).

Potassium, rubidium, and cesium form mainly superoxides ($MO_{2}$) due to the stabilization of the large superoxide ion by large cations.

Magnesium forms mainly monoxide ($MgO$).

Therefore, potassium forms a superoxide with oxygen.

Answer: Potassium

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

What is the general formula of halide salts of alkali metals if M is a metal atom ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{MX}$
- **(B)** $\text{MX}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{M}_3\text{X}_2$
- **(D)** $\text{M}_2\text{X}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{MX}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Alkali metals belong to Group 1 of the periodic table and have one valence electron. They lose this electron to form a unipositive ion, $M^{+}$.

Halogens belong to Group 17 and have seven valence electrons. They gain one electron to form a uninegative ion, $X^{-}$.

To form a neutral salt, one $M^{+}$ ion combines with one $X^{-}$ ion.

The general formula of halide salts of alkali metals is $MX$.

Answer: $\text{MX}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from the following metal chlorides does not contain water of crystallisation ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{LiCl}$
- **(B)** $\text{MgCl}_2$
- **(C)** $\text{CsCl}$
- **(D)** $\text{BeCl}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{CsCl}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Alkali metal chlorides generally do not form hydrates, with the exception of lithium chloride, which is deliquescent and crystallises as a dihydrate, $\text{LiCl} \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

Alkaline earth metal chlorides such as $\text{MgCl}_2$ and $\text{BeCl}_2$ readily form hydrates. $\text{MgCl}_2$ crystallises as a hexahydrate, $\text{MgCl}_2 \cdot 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and $\text{BeCl}_2$ crystallises as a tetrahydrate, $\text{BeCl}_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$.

Caesium chloride ($\text{CsCl}$) is an alkali metal chloride that does not form hydrates and crystallises as an anhydrous salt.

Therefore, $\text{CsCl}$ does not contain water of crystallisation.

Answer: $\text{CsCl}$

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{K}$ and $\text{Ca}$

- **(C)** $\text{Rb}$ and $\text{Ba}$
- **(D)** $\text{K}$ and $\text{Sr}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{Li}$ and $\text{Be}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Ionization enthalpy decreases down a group in the periodic table due to an increase in atomic size and shielding effect.

Among alkali metals (Group 1), Lithium ($\text{Li}$) is at the top of the group and has the smallest atomic size, hence it has the highest first ionization enthalpy.

Among alkaline earth metals (Group 2), Beryllium ($\text{Be}$) is at the top of the group and has the smallest atomic size, hence it has the highest first ionization enthalpy.

Therefore, the elements with the highest first ionization enthalpy are $\text{Li}$ and $\text{Be}$ respectively.

Answer: $\text{Li}$ and $\text{Be}$

</details>
