### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following reactions is used to prepare diethyl ether from ethyl chloride?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Wurtz reaction
- **(B)** Wurtz- fitting reaction
- **(C)** Williamson's reaction
- **(D)** Reimer- Tiemann reaction

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Williamson's reaction**

**Detailed Solution:**

Williamson's synthesis is a widely used method for the preparation of symmetrical and unsymmetrical ethers.

In this reaction, an alkyl halide reacts with sodium alkoxide to form an ether.

$C_2H_5Cl + C_2H_5ONa \rightarrow C_2H_5-O-C_2H_5 + NaCl$

Thus, diethyl ether is prepared from ethyl chloride by Williamson's reaction.

Answer: Williamson's reaction

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the temperature required for preparation of ethoxyethane from ethanol in the presence of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $113\,K$
- **(B)** $443\,K$
- **(C)** $413\,K$
- **(D)** $213\,K$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $413\,K$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dehydration of ethanol in the presence of concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ gives different products depending on the temperature.

At $443\,\text{K}$, ethanol undergoes intramolecular dehydration (elimination) to form ethene.

At $413\,\text{K}$, ethanol undergoes intermolecular dehydration (nucleophilic substitution) to form ethoxyethane (diethyl ether).

The reaction is:

$2\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4, 413\,\text{K}} \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{O}\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Thus, the required temperature is $413\,\text{K}$.

Answer: $413\,K$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following compounds cannot be prepared by Williamson's synthesis?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OCH}_2\text{CH}_3$
- **(B)** $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{O CH}_2\text{CH}_3$
- **(C)** $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-CH}_2\text{CH}_3$
- **(D)** $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-C(CH}_3)_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-C(CH}_3)_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Williamson's synthesis is an $S_N2$ reaction between an alkoxide ion and an alkyl halide.

For the successful synthesis of an ether, the alkyl halide must be primary or methyl to avoid elimination. If a tertiary alkyl halide is used, elimination dominates over substitution, yielding an alkene instead of an ether.

To prepare di-tert-butyl ether, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-C(CH}_3)_3$, the reaction would require a tert-butyl halide and a tert-butoxide ion. Since the alkyl halide is tertiary and the alkoxide is a strong base, an elimination reaction will occur exclusively to form isobutylene.

Thus, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-C(CH}_3)_3$ cannot be prepared by Williamson's synthesis.

Answer: $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-C(CH}_3)_3$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which among the following does NOT undergo Williamson's synthesis?

- **(A)** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Br}$
- **(B)** <img style="width: 200px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/16_april_shift_2__q75_d1.png" />
- **(C)** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5 - \text{Br}$
- **(D)** $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5 - \text{CH}_2 - \text{Br}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5 - \text{Br}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Williamson's synthesis is a method for preparing ethers by the reaction of an alkyl halide with a sodium alkoxide. This reaction follows an $S_N2$ mechanism.

For an $S_N2$ reaction to occur favorably, the halide should be primary or secondary (primary being the most reactive).

Let us evaluate the given options:
1. $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{Br}$ (Ethyl bromide) is a primary alkyl halide and readily undergoes Williamson's synthesis.
2. $\text{CH}_3-\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)-\text{CH}_2-\text{Br}$ (Isobutyl bromide) is a primary alkyl halide and undergoes Williamson's synthesis.
3. $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{Br}$ (Bromobenzene) is an aryl halide. In aryl halides, the carbon-halogen bond acquires a partial double bond character due to resonance with the benzene ring. The bulky benzene ring also sterically hinders the backside attack required for an $S_N2$ mechanism. Therefore, aryl halides do not undergo Williamson's synthesis.
4. $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{CH}_2-\text{Br}$ (Benzyl bromide) is a primary benzylic halide, which is highly reactive towards $S_N2$ reactions and readily undergoes Williamson's synthesis.

Thus, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5-\text{Br}$ does not undergo Williamson's synthesis.

Answer: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5 - \text{Br}$

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following compounds is obtained by Williamson synthesis ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** only simple ethers
- **(B)** only mixed ethers
- **(C)** simple and mixed ethers.
- **(D)** esters

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) simple and mixed ethers.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Williamson synthesis is a general method for the preparation of ethers. It involves the nucleophilic substitution reaction of an alkyl halide with a sodium alkoxide.

$R-X + R'-ONa \rightarrow R-O-R' + NaX$

When the alkyl groups $R$ and $R'$ are identical, symmetrical or simple ethers are formed. When $R$ and $R'$ are different, unsymmetrical or mixed ethers are formed.

Thus, both simple and mixed ethers can be prepared by this method.

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What type of mechanism is followed by dehydration of alcohol to form ether?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Nucleophilic substitution unimolecular reaction
- **(B)** Nucleophilic substitution bimolecular reaction
- **(C)** Electrophilic substitution reaction
- **(D)** Electrophilic addition reaction

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Nucleophilic substitution bimolecular reaction**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dehydration of alcohols to form ethers is an acid-catalyzed reaction that proceeds via an $S_N2$ mechanism.

Step 1: Protonation of the alcohol to form an oxonium ion.
$R-OH + H^+ \rightleftharpoons R-OH_2^+$

Step 2: Nucleophilic attack of an unprotonated alcohol molecule on the protonated alcohol. This is a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution ($S_N2$) reaction where water acts as the leaving group.
$R-OH + R-OH_2^+ \rightarrow R-O^+(H)-R + H_2O$

Step 3: Loss of a proton to yield the ether.
$R-O^+(H)-R \rightarrow R-O-R + H^+$

Since the rate-determining step involves two molecules (the alcohol acting as a nucleophile and the protonated alcohol acting as the substrate), the mechanism is a nucleophilic substitution bimolecular reaction.

</details>

Which from following is a suitable method for preparation of ether?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Reacting sodium methoxide and tertiary butyl bromide
- **(B)** Reacting sodium tertiarybutoxide and methyl bromide
- **(C)** Reacting methyl alcohol and ethene
- **(D)** Reacting methyl alcohol and methyl bromide

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Reacting sodium tertiarybutoxide and methyl bromide**

**Detailed Solution:**

Williamson ether synthesis is an $S_N2$ reaction between an alkoxide ion and an alkyl halide. For the successful preparation of an ether, the alkyl halide must be primary or methyl, as secondary and tertiary alkyl halides tend to undergo elimination reactions in the presence of strong bases (alkoxides) to form alkenes.

Reacting sodium methoxide with tertiary butyl bromide (a $3^{\circ}$ alkyl halide) will predominantly yield isobutylene (an alkene) due to elimination.

Reacting sodium tertiarybutoxide with methyl bromide (a methyl halide) will successfully yield methyl tert-butyl ether, as methyl halides cannot undergo elimination and are highly reactive towards $S_N2$ substitution.

Therefore, reacting sodium tertiarybutoxide and methyl bromide is the suitable method.

Answer: Reacting sodium tertiarybutoxide and methyl bromide
