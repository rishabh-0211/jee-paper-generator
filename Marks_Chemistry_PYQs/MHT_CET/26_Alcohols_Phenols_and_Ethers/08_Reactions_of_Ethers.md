### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the product obtained in following reaction.

\(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7+\underset{\text { excess }}{\mathrm{HI}} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{Hot}]{\Delta} X+Y\)

**Options:**

- **(A)** \(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7 \mathrm{OH}\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}\)
- **(B)** \(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}\)
- **(C)** \(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7 \mathrm{I}\) and \(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7 \mathrm{OH}\)
- **(D)** \(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7 \mathrm{I}\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}\)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) \(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7 \mathrm{I}\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}\)**

**Detailed Solution:**

The reaction of an ether with excess hot hydroiodic acid ($\mathrm{HI}$) leads to the cleavage of the $\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{O}$ bond on both sides.

First, the ether reacts with one molecule of $\mathrm{HI}$ to form an alkyl iodide and an alcohol:
$\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7 + \mathrm{HI} \rightarrow \mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7\mathrm{I} + \mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7\mathrm{OH}$

Since $\mathrm{HI}$ is present in excess and the reaction mixture is heated, the alcohol formed undergoes further substitution by $\mathrm{HI}$ to yield another molecule of alkyl iodide and water:
$\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7\mathrm{OH} + \mathrm{HI} \rightarrow \mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7\mathrm{I} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$

The overall reaction is:
$\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7 + 2\mathrm{HI} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7\mathrm{I} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$

Thus, the products $X$ and $Y$ are $\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7\mathrm{I}$ and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$.

Answer: \(\mathrm{C}_3 \mathrm{H}_7 \mathrm{I}\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}\)

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify 'X' and 'Y' in the following reaction.
$C_3H_7 - O - C_3H_7 + HI\ (\text{excess}) \xrightarrow[\text{Hot}]{\Delta} X + Y$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $C_3H_7OH$ and $H_2O$
- **(B)** $C_3H_7$ and $H_2O$
- **(C)** $C_3H_7I$ and $C_3H_7OH$
- **(D)** $C_3H_7I$ and $H_2O$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $C_3H_7I$ and $H_2O$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When an ether is treated with excess $HI$ at high temperature, the ether undergoes complete cleavage to form alkyl iodides and water.

The reaction proceeds in two steps. First, one molecule of $HI$ cleaves the ether to form one molecule of alkyl iodide and one molecule of alcohol.

$C_3H_7 - O - C_3H_7 + HI \rightarrow C_3H_7I + C_3H_7OH$

Since $HI$ is present in excess and the reaction mixture is hot, the alcohol formed further reacts with $HI$ to form another molecule of alkyl iodide and water.

$C_3H_7OH + HI \rightarrow C_3H_7I + H_2O$

The overall reaction is:

$C_3H_7 - O - C_3H_7 + 2HI \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2C_3H_7I + H_2O$

Thus, the products $X$ and $Y$ are $C_3H_7I$ and $H_2O$.

Answer: $C_3H_7I$ and $H_2O$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statements regarding ethers is NOT true?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Aryl alkyl ethers have weaker and longer bonds between oxygen and aromatic ring.
- **(B)** The alkoxy group in aryl alkyl ethers exhibits $+R$ effect.
- **(C)** When ethers are exposed to the atmosphere, they form corresponding peroxides.
- **(D)** Ethers dissolve in cold, concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Aryl alkyl ethers have weaker and longer bonds between oxygen and aromatic ring.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In aryl alkyl ethers, the lone pair of electrons on the oxygen atom is in conjugation with the aromatic ring. This resonance imparts a partial double bond character to the carbon-oxygen bond attached to the aromatic ring. As a result, the bond between the oxygen and the aromatic ring is shorter and stronger than a typical single bond.

The alkoxy group ($-OR$) exhibits a $+R$ effect, increasing electron density on the benzene ring.

Ethers react with atmospheric oxygen to form peroxides, which is a well-known property.

Ethers act as Lewis bases and dissolve in cold, concentrated $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ by forming soluble oxonium salts.

Therefore, the statement that aryl alkyl ethers have weaker and longer bonds between oxygen and the aromatic ring is incorrect.

Answer: Aryl alkyl ethers have weaker and longer bonds between oxygen and aromatic ring.

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the product formed when aryl-alkyl ether reacts with cold HI.

**Options:**

- **(A)** alkyl iodide and water
- **(B)** aryl iodide and water
- **(C)** alkyl iodide, aryl iodide and water
- **(D)** phenol and alkyl iodide

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) phenol and alkyl iodide**

**Detailed Solution:**

Aryl-alkyl ethers have the general formula $Ar-O-R$.

When reacted with cold $HI$, the ether oxygen gets protonated to form an oxonium ion, $Ar-\overset{+}{O}(H)-R$.

The $Ar-O$ bond has partial double bond character due to resonance between the lone pair of electrons on the oxygen atom and the aromatic ring. This makes the $Ar-O$ bond stronger and more difficult to cleave than the $O-R$ bond.

Furthermore, nucleophilic substitution ($S_{N}2$) by the iodide ion cannot occur at the $sp^2$ hybridized carbon of the aromatic ring.

Therefore, the iodide ion attacks the alkyl group $R$, cleaving the $O-R$ bond to yield phenol ($Ar-OH$) and an alkyl iodide ($R-I$).

Answer: phenol and alkyl iodide

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the product obtained when tert-Butyl methyl ether is heated with hot HI.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{CH}_3\text{I} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{COH}$
- **(B)** $\text{CH}_3\text{OH} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I}$
- **(C)** $\text{CH}_3\text{OH} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-OH}$
- **(D)** $\text{CH}_3\text{I} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{CH}_3\text{I} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When tert-butyl methyl ether is treated with HI, the ether oxygen is first protonated to form an oxonium ion.

The cleavage of the C-O bond occurs via an $S_N1$ mechanism because the resulting tert-butyl carbocation is highly stable. The iodide ion ($I^-$) then attacks the tert-butyl carbocation to form tert-butyl iodide, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I}$, and methanol, $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$, is released.

Since the reaction is carried out with hot HI (which implies excess HI and elevated temperature), the methanol formed undergoes further nucleophilic substitution with HI to form methyl iodide, $\text{CH}_3\text{I}$, and water.

The overall reaction is:
$(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-O-CH}_3 + 2\text{HI} \xrightarrow{\Delta} (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I} + \text{CH}_3\text{I} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

Therefore, the final products are $\text{CH}_3\text{I}$ and $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I}$.

Answer: $\text{CH}_3\text{I} + (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{C-I}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following ether, on hydrolysis, gives two different products that are successive members of the homologous series?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Methoxymethane
- **(B)** Methoxypropane
- **(C)** Ethoxypropane
- **(D)** 1-Methoxypropane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Ethoxypropane**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrolysis of an ether $R-O-R'$ yields two alcohol molecules, $R-OH$ and $R'-OH$.

For the products to be successive members of a homologous series, they must differ by exactly one $-CH_2-$ unit. This means the alkyl groups $R$ and $R'$ must differ by one carbon atom.

Methoxymethane ($CH_3-O-CH_3$) gives two molecules of methanol, which are identical.

Methoxypropane ($CH_3-O-C_3H_7$) gives methanol and propanol, which differ by two carbon atoms.

Ethoxypropane ($C_2H_5-O-C_3H_7$) gives ethanol ($C_2H_5OH$) and propanol ($C_3H_7OH$). These are successive members of the alcohol homologous series as they differ by one $-CH_2-$ group.

Answer: Ethoxypropane

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following compounds is formed when ether is dissolved in cold concentrated sulphuric acid?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Alkanol
- **(B)** Alkanoic acid
- **(C)** Alkyl hydrogen sulphate
- **(D)** Oxonium salt

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Oxonium salt**

**Detailed Solution:**

Ethers behave as Lewis bases due to the presence of two lone pairs of electrons on the oxygen atom.

When dissolved in cold concentrated sulphuric acid, ethers accept a proton from the acid to form oxonium salts.

$R-O-R + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow [R-O(H)-R]^+ HSO_4^-$

Due to the formation of this oxonium salt, ethers are soluble in cold concentrated sulphuric acid.

Answer: Oxonium salt

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the percentage of p-bromoanisole formed in the bromination of anisole with bromine in acetic acid?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10 \%$
- **(B)** $30 \%$
- **(C)** $60 \%$
- **(D)** $90 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $90 \%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Bromination of anisole with bromine in ethanoic acid (acetic acid) occurs even in the absence of a Lewis acid catalyst due to the strong activating nature of the methoxy group.

The methoxy group is ortho/para directing. Due to steric hindrance at the ortho position, the para isomer (p-bromoanisole) is formed as the major product.

The yield of p-bromoanisole in this reaction is $90 \%$.

Answer: $90 \%$

</details>
