### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Find the number of allotropic forms of Tellurium under normal conditions.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2$
- **(B)** $3$
- **(C)** $6$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Tellurium exists in two allotropic forms under normal conditions: crystalline and amorphous.

Crystalline tellurium has a silvery-white, metallic appearance and consists of parallel helical chains of atoms. Amorphous tellurium is a brownish-black powder.

Thus, the number of allotropic forms of Tellurium is $2$.

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the number of metallic allotrops of polonium?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Polonium exists in two metallic allotropic forms: $\alpha$-polonium and $\beta$-polonium.

$\alpha$-polonium has a simple cubic structure, while $\beta$-polonium has a rhombohedral structure.

The transition from the $\alpha$ form to the $\beta$ form occurs at around $36^{\circ}\text{C}$.

Therefore, the number of metallic allotropes of polonium is $2$.

Answer: $2$

</details>
