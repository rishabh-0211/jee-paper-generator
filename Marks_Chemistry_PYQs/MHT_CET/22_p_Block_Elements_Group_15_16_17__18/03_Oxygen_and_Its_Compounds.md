### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following factors is responsible for the anomalous behavior of oxygen?
(A). small atomic size.
(B). higher electronegativity.
(C). low O - O bond dissociation energy.
(D). absence of d - orbital
(E). presence of vaccant d - orbitals.
Choose the <strong>correct</strong> answer from the options given below:

**Options:**

- **(A)** (A), (B), (C) and (E) only.
- **(B)** (A), (B) and (D) only.
- **(C)** (A), (B), (C) and (D).
- **(D)** (B), (C) and (D) only.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) (A), (B) and (D) only.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The anomalous behaviour of oxygen compared to other members of group 16 is primarily due to the following factors:

1. Small atomic size.
2. High electronegativity.
3. Absence of d-orbitals in its valence shell.

These fundamental factors lead to unique properties such as the ability to form strong $p\pi-p\pi$ multiple bonds, the presence of hydrogen bonding in its compounds (like $H_2O$), and the restriction of its maximum covalency to four.

The low $O-O$ single bond dissociation energy is a consequence of its small size (due to high interelectronic repulsions between lone pairs), rather than a primary cause of its anomalous behaviour.

Therefore, the correct factors are (A), (B), and (D).

Answer: (A), (B) and (D) only.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following reactions exhibits the reducing property of ozone ?

**Options:**

- **(C)** $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{(l)} + \text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}\text{(l)} + 2\text{O}_2\text{(g)}$
- **(D)** $\text{PbS}\text{(s)} + 4\text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow \text{PbSO}_4\text{(s)} + 4\text{O}_2\text{(g)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{(l)} + \text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}\text{(l)} + 2\text{O}_2\text{(g)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In the reaction $\text{NO}\text{(g)} + \text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow \text{NO}_2\text{(g)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)}$, nitrogen is oxidized from $+2$ to $+4$. $\text{O}_3$ acts as an oxidizing agent.

In the reaction $2\text{KI}\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}\text{(l)} + \text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\text{KOH}\text{(aq)} + \text{I}_2\text{(g)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)}$, iodine is oxidized from $-1$ to $0$. $\text{O}_3$ acts as an oxidizing agent.

In the reaction $\text{PbS}\text{(s)} + 4\text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow \text{PbSO}_4\text{(s)} + 4\text{O}_2\text{(g)}$, sulphur is oxidized from $-2$ to $+6$. $\text{O}_3$ acts as an oxidizing agent.

In the reaction $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{(l)} + \text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}\text{(l)} + 2\text{O}_2\text{(g)}$, both hydrogen peroxide and ozone undergo mutual reduction. Ozone reduces $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ to $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and itself gets reduced to $\text{O}_2$. Thus, this reaction exhibits the reducing property of ozone.

Answer: $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2\text{(l)} + \text{O}_3\text{(g)} \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}\text{(l)} + 2\text{O}_2\text{(g)}$

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

What is O-O bond length in ozone ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $178$ pm
- **(B)** $128$ pm
- **(C)** $256$ pm
- **(D)** $483$ pm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $128$ pm**

**Detailed Solution:**

Ozone ($O_3$) is a resonance hybrid of two equivalent structures.

Due to resonance, both O-O bonds in the ozone molecule are identical.

The O-O bond length is intermediate between a typical O-O single bond ($148$ pm) and an O=O double bond ($121$ pm).

The experimentally determined O-O bond length in ozone is $128$ pm.

Answer: $128$ pm

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statements is false about oxygen and sulphur?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Atoms of oxygen and sulphur consist two unpaired electrons in valence shell.
- **(B)** Oxygen and sulphur show $-2,+4$ and +6 oxidation states.
- **(C)** Oxygen is gas while sulphur is solid at room temperature.
- **(D)** Hydride of oxygen is more stable than hydride of sulphur.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Oxygen and sulphur show $-2,+4$ and +6 oxidation states.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The electronic configuration of oxygen is $[	ext{He}] 2s^2 2p^4$ and sulphur is $[	ext{Ne}] 3s^2 3p^4$. Both have two unpaired electrons in their valence $p$-subshell.

Oxygen is a diatomic gas ($O_2$) at room temperature, whereas sulphur is a polyatomic solid ($S_8$).

The thermal stability of group 16 hydrides decreases down the group as the size of the central atom increases and the $E-H$ bond dissociation energy decreases. Thus, $H_2O$ is more stable than $H_2S$.

Oxygen exhibits oxidation states of $-2, -1, +1,$ and $+2$. Due to the absence of vacant d-orbitals in its valence shell, oxygen cannot expand its octet and does not show $+4$ and $+6$ oxidation states. Sulphur exhibits $-2, +2, +4,$ and $+6$ oxidation states. Therefore, the statement that both oxygen and sulphur show $-2, +4,$ and $+6$ oxidation states is false.

Answer: Oxygen and sulphur show $-2,+4$ and +6 oxidation states.

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following statements is correct about $\mathrm{O}_2$ and $\mathrm{O}_3$ molecule?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{O}_2$ and $\mathrm{O}_3$ are paramagnetic
- **(B)** The enthalpy change during the formation of $\mathrm{O}_3$ from $\mathrm{O}_2$ is positive
- **(C)** The entropy change in the formation of $\mathrm{O}_3$ from $\mathrm{O}_2$ is positive
- **(D)** $\mathrm{O}_3$ is more stable than $\mathrm{O}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) The enthalpy change during the formation of $\mathrm{O}_3$ from $\mathrm{O}_2$ is positive**

**Detailed Solution:**

The formation of ozone from oxygen is an endothermic process.

$3\mathrm{O}_2(g) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{O}_3(g)$

The enthalpy change ($\Delta H$) for this reaction is positive ($\Delta H = +142 \text{ kJ/mol}$ of $\mathrm{O}_3$).

$\mathrm{O}_2$ is paramagnetic due to the presence of two unpaired electrons in its antibonding molecular orbitals, while $\mathrm{O}_3$ is diamagnetic.

During the formation of $\mathrm{O}_3$, the number of gaseous moles decreases from $3$ to $2$, meaning the entropy change ($\Delta S$) is negative.

$\mathrm{O}_3$ is thermodynamically less stable than $\mathrm{O}_2$ because its decomposition into $\mathrm{O}_2$ is exothermic and accompanied by an increase in entropy, making the decomposition highly spontaneous.

Answer: The enthalpy change during the formation of $\mathrm{O}_3$ from $\mathrm{O}_2$ is positive

</details>
