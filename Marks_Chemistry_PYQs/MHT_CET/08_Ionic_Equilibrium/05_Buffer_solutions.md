### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate the pH of buffer solution containing $0.02$ M weak acid and $0.04$ M its salt with a strong base.
[$K_a = 7.2 \times 10^{-4}$]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3.14$
- **(B)** $3.44$
- **(C)** $3.76$
- **(D)** $3.96$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3.44$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for an acidic buffer:

$pH = pK_a + \log \left( \dfrac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]} \right)$

Given values are:
$[\text{Acid}] = 0.02$ M
$[\text{Salt}] = 0.04$ M
$K_a = 7.2 \times 10^{-4}$

First, calculate the $pK_a$ of the weak acid:
$pK_a = -\log(K_a) = -\log(7.2 \times 10^{-4})$
$pK_a = 4 - \log(7.2)$
$pK_a = 4 - 0.857 = 3.143$

Now, substitute the values into the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:
$pH = 3.143 + \log \left( \dfrac{0.04}{0.02} \right)$
$pH = 3.143 + \log(2)$
$pH = 3.143 + 0.301 = 3.444$

Rounding to two decimal places, we get $pH = 3.44$.

Answer: $3.44$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

One litre of a buffer solution containing $0.01$ M $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ and $0.1$ M $\text{NH}_4\text{OH}$ has $pK_b$ value of $5$. Find the pH.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9$
- **(B)** $10$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $6$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $10$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Henderson-Hasselbalch equation for a basic buffer:

$pOH = pK_b + \log \left( \dfrac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Base}]} \right)$

Given $[\text{Salt}] = [\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}] = 0.01$ M, $[\text{Base}] = [\text{NH}_4\text{OH}] = 0.1$ M, and $pK_b = 5$.

Substituting the values:

$pOH = 5 + \log \left( \dfrac{0.01}{0.1} \right)$

$pOH = 5 + \log (10^{-1})$

$pOH = 5 - 1 = 4$

The pH of the solution is given by:

$pH = 14 - pOH$

$pH = 14 - 4 = 10$

Answer: $10$

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Find the pH of a solution formed by mixing equal volume of solution of $0.1$ M sodium propionate and $0.1$ molar propionic acid.
(Given The dissociation constant of propionic acid is $1.3 \times 10^{-5}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.45$
- **(B)** $4.89$
- **(C)** $5.98$
- **(D)** $6.89$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4.89$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The mixture of propionic acid and sodium propionate forms an acidic buffer.

Using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:

$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log \left( \dfrac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]} \right)$

Since equal volumes of $0.1$ M solutions are mixed, the concentrations of the salt and the acid in the final mixture are equal.

$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log(1)$

$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a = -\log(K_a)$

$\text{pH} = -\log(1.3 \times 10^{-5})$

$\text{pH} = 5 - \log(1.3)$

$\text{pH} = 5 - 0.1139 = 4.8861 \approx 4.89$

\mathrm{pH} & =\mathrm{pK}_{\mathrm{a}}+\log _{10} \frac{[\text { Salt }]}{[\text { Acid }]}=\mathrm{pK}_{\mathrm{a}}+\log _{10} \frac{1}{1}=\mathrm{pK}_{\mathrm{a}} \\
\mathrm{pK}_{\mathrm{a}} & =-\log _{10} \mathrm{~K}_{\mathrm{a}}=-\log _{10}\left(1.78 \times 10^{-5}\right) \\
& =-\left(\log _{10} 1.78+\log _{10} 10^{-5}\right) \\
& =-(0.25-5)=4.75
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore, $\mathrm{pH}$ of buffer solution $=4.75$

</details>

### Q26. MHT CET 2021 (21 Sep Shift 1)

Which among the following salts turns blue litmus red in its aqueous solution?

- **(B)** $\mathrm{NH}_4 \mathrm{~F}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{COONa}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{CH}_3 \mathrm{COONH}_4$

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{NH}_4 \mathrm{~F}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

<p>To identify the salt that yields an acidic aqueous solution—thus turning blue litmus red—consider the hydrolysis behavior based on the strength of the parent acid and base.</p><p>Option A, $\text{KCN}$, derives from a strong base (KOH) and weak acid (HCN). The cyanide ion hydrolyzes as $\text{CN}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{HCN} + \text{OH}^-$, producing hydroxide ions and resulting in a basic solution.</p><p>Option B, $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$, originates from a strong base (NaOH) and weak acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$). The carbonate ion hydrolyzes via $\text{CO}_3^{2-} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{HCO}_3^- + \text{OH}^-$, also yielding a basic solution.</p><p>Option C, $\text{NaNO}_3$, is formed from a strong base (NaOH) and strong acid ($\text{HNO}_3$). Neither ion hydrolyzes significantly, so the solution remains neutral.</p><p>Option D, $\text{CuCl}_2$, arises from a weak base ($\text{Cu(OH)}_2$) and strong acid (HCl). The copper(II) ion hydrolyzes as $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{Cu(OH)}_2 + 2\text{H}^+$, producing hydrogen ions and creating an acidic solution.</p><p>Consequently, $\text{CuCl}_2$ is the salt that turns blue litmus red.</p><p>The final answer is $\boxed{\text{D}}$</p>
