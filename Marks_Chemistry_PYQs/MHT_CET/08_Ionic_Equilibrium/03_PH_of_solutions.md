<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $5.623 \times 10^{-5}$ M**

Evaluating $10^{0.75}$:

$10^{0.75} = 10^{3/4} = (1000)^{1/4} \approx 5.623$

Therefore, the concentration is:

$[\text{OH}^-] = 5.623 \times 10^{-5}$ M

Answer: $5.623 \times 10^{-5}$ M

What is the pH of a mixture of $40$ ml of $0.1$ M $\text{Ba(HCOO)}_2$ and $20$ ml of $0.1$ M $\text{HCOOH}$ solution?
($pK_a$ for formic acid $= 5$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.8$
- **(B)** $5.6$
- **(C)** $8.4$
- **(D)** $11.2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $5.6$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Millimoles of $\text{Ba(HCOO)}_2 = 40 \times 0.1 = 4$ mmol

Since each mole of $\text{Ba(HCOO)}_2$ provides two moles of formate ions, millimoles of $\text{HCOO}^- = 2 \times 4 = 8$ mmol

The mixture of a weak acid ($\text{HCOOH}$) and its conjugate base ($\text{HCOO}^-$) forms an acidic buffer. The pH of the buffer is given by the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:

$\text{pH} = pK_a + \log \left( \dfrac{[\text{Conjugate Base}]}{[\text{Acid}]} \right)$

$\text{pH} = 5 + \log \left( \dfrac{8}{2} \right)$

$\text{pH} = 5 + \log 4$

$\text{pH} = 5 + 2 \log 2 = 5 + 2(0.3) = 5.6$

Answer: $5.6$

$\alpha = \sqrt{\dfrac{1.8 \times 10^{-5}}{0.01}}$

$\alpha = \sqrt{1.8 \times 10^{-3}}$

$\alpha = \sqrt{18 \times 10^{-4}}$

$\alpha = 4.242 \times 10^{-2}$

$\alpha = 0.04242$
