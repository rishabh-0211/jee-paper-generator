### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is degree of ionization of water at $298$ K?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.8 \times 10^{-6}$
- **(B)** $1.8 \times 10^{-7}$
- **(C)** $1.8 \times 10^{-8}$
- **(D)** $1.8 \times 10^{-9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1.8 \times 10^{-9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

At $298$ K, the ionic product of water is $K_w = 10^{-14}$.

In pure water, the concentration of hydrogen ions is $[H^+] = \sqrt{K_w} = 10^{-7}$ M.

The molarity of pure water is $C = \dfrac{1000}{18} = 55.55$ M.

The degree of ionization $\alpha$ is given by the ratio of the concentration of ionized water to the total concentration of water.

$\alpha = \dfrac{[H^+]}{C} = \dfrac{10^{-7}}{55.55} = 1.8 \times 10^{-9}$

Answer: $1.8 \times 10^{-9}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the $\text{P}^{OH}$ of solution having $[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]\,1\times 10^{-4}$ at $298$ K?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $10$
- **(C)** $1$
- **(D)** $12$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $10$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+] = 1 \times 10^{-4}$ M.

The pH of the solution is calculated as:

$\text{pH} = -\log[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]$

$\text{pH} = -\log(1 \times 10^{-4}) = 4$

At $298$ K, the ionic product of water gives the relationship:

$\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14$

Substituting the value of pH:

$4 + \text{pOH} = 14$

$\text{pOH} = 14 - 4 = 10$

Answer: $10$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the concentration of hydrogen ion in the solution when the concentration of hydroxyl ion is $0.08$ mol / dm$^3$ at $298$ K ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.125$ mol/L
- **(B)** $0.125 \times 10^{-12}$ mol/L
- **(C)** $0.0125$ mol/L
- **(D)** $0.125 \times 10^{-10}$ mol/L

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.125 \times 10^{-12}$ mol/L**

**Detailed Solution:**

At $298$ K, the ionic product of water is $K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}$.

Given $[OH^-] = 0.08$ mol/dm$^3$ = $0.08$ mol/L.

$[H^+] = \dfrac{K_w}{[OH^-]} = \dfrac{1.0 \times 10^{-14}}{0.08}$

$[H^+] = \dfrac{1}{8} \times 10^{-12} = 0.125 \times 10^{-12}$ mol/L.

Answer: $0.125 \times 10^{-12}$ mol/L

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following sentences is true for a basic solution?

**Options:**

- **(A)** For basic solution, $[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+] = [\text{OH}^-]$
- **(B)** In basic solution, the excess of $\text{OH}^-$ ions are present.
- **(C)** In the basic solution, there is excess of $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ ions.
- **(D)** The pOH of a basic solution can be calculated by pOH = $-\log[\text{H}^+]$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) In basic solution, the excess of $\text{OH}^-$ ions are present.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a neutral solution, $[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+] = [\text{OH}^-]$.

In an acidic solution, $[\text{H}_3\text{O}^+] > [\text{OH}^-]$, meaning there is an excess of $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ ions.

In a basic solution, $[\text{OH}^-] > [\text{H}_3\text{O}^+]$, meaning there is an excess of $\text{OH}^-$ ions.

The pOH of any solution is defined as $\text{pOH} = -\log[\text{OH}^-]$.

Thus, the only true statement is that in a basic solution, an excess of $\text{OH}^-$ ions are present.

Answer: In basic solution, the excess of $\text{OH}^-$ ions are present.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A monobasic weak acid dissociates $2 \%$ in its 0.002 M solution.
Calculate the dissociation constant of weak acid.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \times 10^{-9}$
- **(B)** $8 \times 10^{-7}$
- **(C)** $6 \times 10^{-7}$
- **(D)** $4 \times 10^{-6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $8 \times 10^{-7}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $C = 0.002$ M and $\alpha = 2\% = 0.02$.

For a weak monobasic acid, the dissociation constant $K_a$ is given by Ostwald's dilution law:

$K_a = C \alpha^2$

Substituting the given values:

$K_a = 0.002 \times (0.02)^2$

$K_a = 2 \times 10^{-3} \times 4 \times 10^{-4}$

$K_a = 8 \times 10^{-7}$

Answer: $8 \times 10^{-7}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate the value of dissociation constant of weak acid, which dissociates to $0.01 \%$ in its 0.1 M solution?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10^{-3}$
- **(B)** $10^{-4}$
- **(C)** $10^{-5}$
- **(D)** $10^{-9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $10^{-9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given concentration $C = 0.1$ M and percent dissociation $= 0.01 \%$.

The degree of dissociation $\alpha = \dfrac{0.01}{100} = 10^{-4}$.

The dissociation constant $K_a$ for a weak acid is given by Ostwald's dilution law:

$K_a = \dfrac{C \alpha^2}{1 - \alpha}$

Since $\alpha \ll 1$, $1 - \alpha \approx 1$.

$K_a = C \alpha^2$

Substituting the given values:

$K_a = 0.1 \times (10^{-4})^2$

$K_a = 10^{-1} \times 10^{-8} = 10^{-9}$

Answer: $10^{-9}$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The degree of dissociation of a $0.01 \mathrm{M}$ weak acid is $10^{-3}$. Its $\mathrm{pOH}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\underline{5}$
- **(B)** 3
- **(C)** 9
- **(D)** 11

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 9**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\left[\mathrm{H}^{+}\right]=\sqrt{K_{a} \cdot C}=139$
Or $=\sqrt{\alpha^{2} \cdot C^{2}} \quad\left[\therefore K_{a}=\alpha^{2} C\right]$
$$
\begin{aligned}
=& \sqrt{\left(10^{-3}\right)^{2} \cdot(0.01)^{2}} \\
=& 10^{-5} \\
\mathrm{pH}=-\log \left[\mathrm{H}^{+}\right] &=-\log 10^{-5} \\
&=5
\end{aligned}
$$
$\mathrm{pOH}=14-5=9$

</details>
