- **(B)** $\text{I-CH}_2\text{COOH}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{F-CH}_2\text{COOH}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since stronger acids have lower $pK_a$ values, $\text{F-CH}_2\text{COOH}$ has the least $pK_a$ value.

Answer: $\text{F-CH}_2\text{COOH}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following has a higher acidic character than benzoic acid?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Acetic acid
- **(B)** p-Methoxybenzoic acid
- **(C)** p-Bromobenzoic acid
- **(D)** p-Aminobenzoic acid

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) p-Bromobenzoic acid**

**Detailed Solution:**

The acidic strength of carboxylic acids depends on the stability of the conjugate base (carboxylate ion). Electron-withdrawing groups (EWG) stabilize the carboxylate ion and increase acidity, while electron-donating groups (EDG) destabilize it and decrease acidity.

Acetic acid is less acidic than benzoic acid because the methyl group has a $+I$ effect, whereas the $sp^2$ hybridized carbon of the phenyl ring in benzoic acid acts as an electron-withdrawing group.

In p-methoxybenzoic acid, the $-\text{OCH}_3$ group exerts a strong $+M$ effect which dominates over its $-I$ effect, making it an electron-donating group. This decreases the acidic strength compared to benzoic acid.

In p-bromobenzoic acid, the $-\text{Br}$ atom exerts a $-I$ effect that dominates over its $+M$ effect. It acts as an electron-withdrawing group, stabilizing the carboxylate ion and making p-bromobenzoic acid more acidic than benzoic acid.

In p-aminobenzoic acid, the $-\text{NH}_2$ group exerts a strong $+M$ effect, which significantly decreases the acidic strength.

Therefore, p-bromobenzoic acid has a higher acidic character than benzoic acid.

Answer: p-Bromobenzoic acid

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the reason for the highest boiling point of R-COOH as compared to alkanes, ethers, alcohols and aldehydes.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Formation of carboxylate ion
- **(B)** Van der waals force of attraction
- **(C)** Formation of intramolecular hydrogen bonding
- **(D)** Formation of intermolecular hydrogen bonding

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Formation of intermolecular hydrogen bonding**

**Detailed Solution:**

Carboxylic acids have higher boiling points than alkanes, ethers, alcohols, and aldehydes of comparable molecular mass.

This is due to the presence of strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding in carboxylic acids. The highly polarized O-H bond, enhanced by the electron-withdrawing carbonyl group, allows carboxylic acid molecules to form strong hydrogen bonds with each other. Because of this extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding, they often exist as dimers even in the vapor phase or in non-polar solvents, requiring more energy to separate the molecules during boiling.

Answer: Formation of intermolecular hydrogen bonding

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify final products in the following sequence of reactions
$\text{RCOOH} + \text{R'OH} \xrightarrow{\large{\text{H}^+}} \text{intermediate} \xrightarrow{\large{\text{Ni}, \Delta}}^{\large{\text{H}_2}} \text{Products}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{R'COOH} + \text{RCH}_2\text{OH}$
- **(B)** $\text{RCH}_2\text{OH} + \text{R'}-\text{OH}$
- **(C)** $\text{R'CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{R}-\text{OH}$
- **(D)** $\text{R}-\text{OH} + \text{R'}-\text{OH}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{RCH}_2\text{OH} + \text{R'}-\text{OH}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The first step is the Fischer esterification reaction between a carboxylic acid and an alcohol in the presence of an acid catalyst. The reaction produces an ester as the intermediate.

$\text{RCOOH} + \text{R'OH} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{RCOOR'} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

The intermediate formed is the ester $\text{RCOOR'}$.

The second step involves the catalytic hydrogenation (hydrogenolysis) of the ester using hydrogen gas over a nickel catalyst at high temperature. This process cleaves the ester bond, reducing the acyl group ($\text{RCO}-$) to a primary alcohol and releasing the alkoxy group ($-\text{OR'}$) as another alcohol.

$\text{RCOOR'} + 2\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni}, \Delta} \text{RCH}_2\text{OH} + \text{R'OH}$

Thus, the final products are $\text{RCH}_2\text{OH}$ and $\text{R'OH}$.

Answer: $\text{RCH}_2\text{OH} + \text{R'}-\text{OH}$

### Q9. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When will be the acidic character of carboxylic acid higher in following cases ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Lower $K_a$ and $pK_a$ values
- **(B)** Lower $K_a$ value and higher $pK_a$ value
- **(C)** Higher $K_a$ value and lower $pK_a$ value
- **(D)** Higher $K_a$ and $pK_a$ values

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Higher $K_a$ value and lower $pK_a$ value**

**Detailed Solution:**

Acidic strength of a carboxylic acid is directly proportional to its acid dissociation constant $K_a$.

Higher the value of $K_a$, stronger is the acid.

The relationship between $pK_a$ and $K_a$ is given by $pK_a = -\log K_a$.

Thus, a higher value of $K_a$ corresponds to a lower value of $pK_a$.

Therefore, higher acidic character corresponds to a higher $K_a$ value and a lower $pK_a$ value.

Answer: Higher $K_a$ value and lower $pK_a$ value

</details>

When treated with a strong oxidizing agent like $\mathrm{KMnO}_4$ in dilute $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4$ (acidic medium), oxidative cleavage of the carbon-carbon double bond takes place.

The terminal $=\mathrm{CH}_2$ group is oxidized to $\mathrm{CO}_2$ and $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$, while the $\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{CH}=$ group is oxidized to a carboxylic acid group, yielding benzoic acid.

$\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{CH}=\mathrm{CH}_2 \xrightarrow{\mathrm{KMnO}_4 / \mathrm{H}^+} \mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{COOH} + \mathrm{CO}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}$

$\mathrm{C}_2 \mathrm{H}_5 \mathrm{N}\left(\mathrm{CH}_3\right)_2$ is a tertiary amine and lacks intermolecular hydrogen bonding, resulting in the lowest boiling point.

$\left(\mathrm{CH}_3\right)_3 \mathrm{C}-\mathrm{NH}_2$ is a primary amine and forms hydrogen bonds, but these are weaker than those in alcohols and carboxylic acids because nitrogen is less electronegative than oxygen.

$\mathrm{n}-\mathrm{C}_4 \mathrm{H}_9 \mathrm{OH}$ is an alcohol and forms strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

$\mathrm{C}_2 \mathrm{H}_5 \mathrm{COOH}$ is a carboxylic acid. It forms stronger intermolecular hydrogen bonds than alcohols due to the more polarized $\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{H}$ bond and the presence of the carbonyl oxygen, allowing it to exist as a stable dimer. Answer: $\mathrm{C}_2 \mathrm{H}_5 \mathrm{COOH}$
