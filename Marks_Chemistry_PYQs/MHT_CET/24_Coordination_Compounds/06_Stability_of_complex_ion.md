### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Which from following cations forms least stable complex with same ligand?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The stability of a coordination complex depends on the charge-to-size ratio of the central metal cation. Higher the charge-to-size ratio, greater is the stability of the complex.

All the given metal ions have the same charge ($+2$). Therefore, the stability will depend inversely on their ionic radii. The ionic radii of the given cations follow the order:
$\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} < \mathrm{Co}^{2+} < \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} < \mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$

Because $\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$ has the largest ionic radius among the given options, it has the lowest charge-to-size ratio. 

For divalent metal ion complexes, the general stability trend is:
$\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} > \mathrm{Ni}^{2+} > \mathrm{Co}^{2+} > \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} > \mathrm{Mn}^{2+} > \mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$

Thus, $\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$ forms the least stable complex with the same ligand.

Answer: $\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following cations will form most stable complex with same ligand?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the Irving-Williams series, the stability of complexes formed by divalent metal ions of the first transition series generally increases across the period and reaches a maximum at copper.

The order of stability for high-spin octahedral complexes is:
$\mathrm{Mn}^{2+} < \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} < \mathrm{Co}^{2+} < \mathrm{Ni}^{2+} < \mathrm{Cu}^{2+} > \mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$

This trend is due to the decrease in ionic radius and the increase in effective nuclear charge, which leads to a higher charge-to-radius ratio. Additionally, $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ complexes are further stabilized by Jahn-Teller distortion.

Therefore, $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$ forms the most stable complex among the given cations.

Answer: $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the correct decreasing order of stability of complexes formed by divalent metal ions with same ligand.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}>\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}>\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}>\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}>\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}>\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}>\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}>\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}>\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}>\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}>\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The stability of coordination complexes formed by metal ions with the same ligand depends on the charge-to-size ratio of the central metal ion.

Higher the charge-to-size ratio, greater is the stability of the complex.

For divalent metal ions, the charge is the same ($+2$). Thus, the stability is inversely proportional to the ionic radius.

The ionic radii of the given divalent metal ions follow the order: $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} < \mathrm{Mn}^{2+} < \mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$.

Therefore, the charge-to-size ratio follows the order: $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} > \mathrm{Mn}^{2+} > \mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$.

Consequently, the decreasing order of stability of their complexes is $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} > \mathrm{Mn}^{2+} > \mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$.

Answer: $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+}>\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}>\mathrm{Cd}^{2+}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from following is a correct stability order of complex formed by metal ions if the ligand remains same?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Co}^{2+}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Co}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the Irving-Williams series, the stability of complexes formed by divalent metal ions of the first transition series generally increases across the period up to copper.

This is due to the decrease in ionic radius and the increase in effective nuclear charge, which leads to a higher charge-to-size ratio and higher crystal field stabilization energy (CFSE).

The general stability order for the divalent metal ions of the 3d series is $\mathrm{Mn}^{2+} < \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} < \mathrm{Co}^{2+} < \mathrm{Ni}^{2+} < \mathrm{Cu}^{2+} > \mathrm{Zn}^{2+}$.

Therefore, the correct decreasing order of stability for the given ions is $\mathrm{Co}^{2+} > \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} > \mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$.

Answer: $\mathrm{Co}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\gt\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What type of ligand is the oxalate ion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Monodentate
- **(B)** Bidentate
- **(C)** Ambidentate
- **(D)** Hexadentate

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Bidentate**

**Detailed Solution:**

The oxalate ion, $C_2O_4^{2-}$, has two donor oxygen atoms that can coordinate to a central metal atom simultaneously.

Since it forms two coordinate bonds with the metal ion, it is classified as a bidentate ligand.

Answer: Bidentate

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which among the following is a correct decreasing order of thermodynamic stability of the complexes?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left[\mathrm{Co}\left(\mathrm{NH}_3\right)_6\right]^{3+}\gt\left[\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2\right]^{-}\gt\left[\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4\right]^{2-}$
- **(B)** $\left[\mathrm{Co}\left(\mathrm{NH}_3\right)_6\right]^{3+}\gt\left[\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4\right]^{2-}\gt\left[\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2\right]^{-}$
- **(C)** $\left[\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2\right]^{-}\gt\left[\mathrm{Co}\left(\mathrm{NH}_3\right)_6\right]^{3+}\gt\left[\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4\right]^{2-}$
- **(D)** $\left[\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4\right]^{2-}\gt\left[\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2\right]^{--}\gt\left[\mathrm{Co}\left(\mathrm{NH}_3\right)_6\right]^{3+}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\left[\mathrm{Co}\left(\mathrm{NH}_3\right)_6\right]^{3+}\gt\left[\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4\right]^{2-}\gt\left[\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2\right]^{-}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The thermodynamic stability of a coordination complex is directly proportional to its formation or stability constant ($K_f$).

According to standard values:
Stability constant of $[\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6]^{3+} = 5.0 \times 10^{33}$
Stability constant of $[\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4]^{2-} = 2.0 \times 10^{27}$
Stability constant of $[\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2]^{-} = 5.5 \times 10^{18}$

Since a higher value of the stability constant indicates greater thermodynamic stability, the correct decreasing order of stability is:
$[\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6]^{3+} \gt [\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4]^{2-} \gt [\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2]^{-}$

Answer: $\left[\mathrm{Co}\left(\mathrm{NH}_3\right)_6\right]^{3+}\gt\left[\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{CN})_4\right]^{2-}\gt\left[\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{CN})_2\right]^{-}$

</details>
