### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Complex $[\text{Co}(\text{H}_2\text{O})_6]^{2+}$ when dissolved in water forms a pink solution. But when this solution is treated with concentrated hydrochloric acid, it turns deep blue to form another complex $[\text{CoCl}_4]^{2-}$. Identify the main reason for this colour change.

**Options:**

- **(A)** presence of unpaired electrons
- **(B)** charge transfer
- **(C)** nature of ligands attached to the metal ion
- **(D)** change in geometry of new complex formed

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) change in geometry of new complex formed**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial complex $[Co(H_2O)_6]^{2+}$ has a coordination number of 6, which corresponds to an octahedral geometry.

The newly formed complex $[CoCl_4]^{2-}$ has a coordination number of 4. Since $Cl^-$ is a weak field ligand, it forms a high-spin complex with a tetrahedral geometry.

The crystal field splitting energy for a tetrahedral complex ($\Delta_t$) is significantly lower than that of an octahedral complex ($\Delta_o$), related approximately by $\Delta_t = \dfrac{4}{9} \Delta_o$.

This large change in splitting energy alters the wavelength of light absorbed. Furthermore, the tetrahedral complex lacks a center of symmetry, which makes the d-d transitions partially allowed and leads to a much more intense ("deep") colour compared to the centrosymmetric octahedral complex.

Therefore, the distinct change in colour from pale pink to deep blue is primarily due to the change in the geometry of the complex.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the complex having the highest number of unpaired electrons from following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$
- **(B)** $[\text{CoF}_6]^{3-}$
- **(C)** $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$
- **(D)** $[\text{Ni(CN)}_4]^{2-}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $[\text{CoF}_6]^{3-}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In $[\text{Co(NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$, Co is in $+3$ oxidation state with $3d^6$ configuration. Since NH$_3$ acts as a strong field ligand for Co$^{3+}$, pairing of electrons takes place resulting in $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$ configuration. Number of unpaired electrons $= 0$.

In $[\text{CoF}_6]^{3-}$, Co is in $+3$ oxidation state with $3d^6$ configuration. F$^-$ is a weak field ligand, so no pairing occurs. The configuration is $t_{2g}^4 e_g^2$. Number of unpaired electrons $= 4$.

In $[\text{NiCl}_4]^{2-}$, Ni is in $+2$ oxidation state with $3d^8$ configuration. Cl$^-$ is a weak field ligand, forming a tetrahedral complex with $e^4 t_2^4$ configuration. Number of unpaired electrons $= 2$.

In $[\text{Ni(CN)}_4]^{2-}$, Ni is in $+2$ oxidation state with $3d^8$ configuration. CN$^-$ is a strong field ligand, forming a square planar complex where all electrons are paired. Number of unpaired electrons $= 0$.

Thus, $[\text{CoF}_6]^{3-}$ has the highest number of unpaired electrons.

Answer: $[\text{CoF}_6]^{3-}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following comparisons is correct for $\Delta_0$ of the following complexes?
I- $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$
II- $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$
III- $[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$
IV- $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{3+}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{I} < \text{II}$
- **(B)** $\text{I} < \text{III}$
- **(C)** $\text{IV} < \text{II}$
- **(D)** $\text{II} < \text{I}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{I} < \text{II}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The crystal field splitting energy ($\Delta_0$) depends on several factors, including the oxidation state of the central metal ion and the nature of the ligands.

1. Oxidation state of the metal ion: For the same metal and ligand, $\Delta_0$ increases with an increase in the oxidation state of the central metal ion. This is because a higher charge on the metal ion draws the ligands closer, leading to greater repulsion and a larger splitting of the d-orbitals. 
In complex I, Co is in the $+2$ oxidation state: $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+}$.
In complex II, Co is in the $+3$ oxidation state: $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$.
Therefore, $\Delta_0$ of II $>$ I, which means $\text{I} < \text{II}$ is a correct comparison.

2. Nature of the ligand: Strong field ligands cause a larger splitting than weak field ligands. $\text{CN}^-$ is a strong field ligand, whereas $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ is a weak field ligand. 
Thus, $\Delta_0$ of $[\text{Fe(CN)}_6]^{3+}$ (IV) is significantly greater than that of $[\text{Co(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$ (II) and $[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{3+}$ (III). The comparison $\text{IV} < \text{II}$ is incorrect.

3. The comparison $\text{II} < \text{I}$ is incorrect based on the oxidation state rule.

Thus, the most appropriate and direct standard comparison is $\text{I} < \text{II}$.

Answer: $\text{I} < \text{II}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Which from the following statements about $\left[\mathrm{Co}\left(\mathrm{NH}_3\right)_6\right]^{3+}$ complex is NOT correct?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Prior to hybridization $\mathrm{Co}^{3+}$ possesses four unpaired electrons.
- **(B)** This complex has all electrons'paired.
- **(C)** It is a high spin complex.
- **(D)** It is a diamagnetic complex.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) It is a high spin complex.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The oxidation state of cobalt in $[\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}$ is $+3$.

The electronic configuration of $\mathrm{Co}$ is $[\mathrm{Ar}] 3d^7 4s^2$.

The electronic configuration of $\mathrm{Co}^{3+}$ is $[\mathrm{Ar}] 3d^6$. In the free ion, these $6$ electrons are distributed in five $3d$ orbitals as one pair and four unpaired electrons. Thus, prior to hybridization, $\mathrm{Co}^{3+}$ has four unpaired electrons.

In the presence of $\mathrm{NH}_3$, which acts as a strong field ligand for $\mathrm{Co}^{3+}$, the electrons pair up to give a $t_{2g}^6 e_g^0$ configuration.

Since all electrons are paired, the complex is diamagnetic.

Because the pairing of electrons takes place, it forms an inner orbital complex ($d^2sp^3$ hybridization) and is a low spin complex.

Therefore, the statement that it is a high spin complex is incorrect.

Answer: It is a high spin complex.

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which from following coordinate complexes is a heteroleptic complex?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Pentacarbonyliron(0)
- **(B)** Triamminetrinitrocobalt(III)
- **(C)** Tetraamminecopper(II) ion
- **(D)** Tetracyanonickelate(II) ion

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Triamminetrinitrocobalt(III)**

**Detailed Solution:**

A heteroleptic complex is a coordination complex in which the central metal atom or ion is bonded to more than one type of ligand.

Pentacarbonyliron(0) is $[Fe(CO)_5]$, which has only one type of ligand (carbonyl) and is homoleptic.

Triamminetrinitrocobalt(III) is $[Co(NH_3)_3(NO_2)_3]$, which has two different types of ligands (ammine and nitro). Thus, it is a heteroleptic complex.

Tetraamminecopper(II) ion is $[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}$, which has only one type of ligand (ammine) and is homoleptic.

Tetracyanonickelate(II) ion is $[Ni(CN)_4]^{2-}$, which has only one type of ligand (cyano) and is homoleptic.

Answer: Triamminetrinitrocobalt(III)

</details>
