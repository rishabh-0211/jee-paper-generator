### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Sucrose on hydrolysis gives

**Options:**

- **(A)** two glucose units
- **(B)** one glucose unit and one fructose unit
- **(C)** one glucose unit and two fructose units
- **(D)** one unit each of glucose, fructose and sucrose

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) one glucose unit and one fructose unit**

**Detailed Solution:**

Sucrose is a disaccharide with the molecular formula $C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$.

On hydrolysis with dilute acids or the enzyme invertase, it breaks down into equimolar amounts of D-(+)-glucose and D-(-)-fructose.

$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11} + H_2O \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 \text{ (glucose)} + C_6H_{12}O_6 \text{ (fructose)}$

Therefore, sucrose on hydrolysis gives one glucose unit and one fructose unit.

Answer: one glucose unit and one fructose unit

</details>

- **(D)** Glycogen

<details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which from following on hydrolysis forms invert sugar?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Sucrose
- **(B)** Maltose
- **(C)** Starch
- **(D)** Lactose

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Sucrose**

**Detailed Solution:**

Sucrose is a dextrorotatory disaccharide. On hydrolysis with dilute acid or the enzyme invertase, it yields an equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose and D-(-)-fructose.

Since the levorotation of fructose ($-92.4^{\circ}$) is greater than the dextrorotation of glucose ($+52.5^{\circ}$), the resulting mixture is levorotatory. This change in the sign of optical rotation from dextro to levo is known as inversion, and the equimolar mixture of glucose and fructose thus formed is called invert sugar.

Therefore, sucrose on hydrolysis forms invert sugar.

Answer: Sucrose

</details>
