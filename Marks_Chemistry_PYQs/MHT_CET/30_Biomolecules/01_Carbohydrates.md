### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

What is the number of chiral carbon atoms in the open chain structure of glucose and fructose respectively?

**Options:**

- **(A)** 4 carbon atoms in each
- **(B)** 3 carbon atoms in each
- **(C)** 4 carbon atoms in glucose and 3 carbon atoms in fructose
- **(D)** 3 carbon atoms in glucose and 4 carbon atoms in fructose

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 4 carbon atoms in glucose and 3 carbon atoms in fructose**

**Detailed Solution:**

The open chain structure of glucose is an aldohexose with the formula $CHO-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH$. The carbon atoms at positions 2, 3, 4, and 5 are attached to four different groups, making them chiral centers. Thus, glucose has 4 chiral carbon atoms.

The open chain structure of fructose is a ketohexose with the formula $CH_2OH-CO-(CHOH)_3-CH_2OH$. The carbon atoms at positions 3, 4, and 5 are attached to four different groups, making them chiral centers. Thus, fructose has 3 chiral carbon atoms.

Answer: 4 carbon atoms in glucose and 3 carbon atoms in fructose

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is non-reducing sugar?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Lactose
- **(B)** Fructose
- **(C)** Sucrose
- **(D)** Maltose

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Sucrose**

**Detailed Solution:**

Reducing sugars are carbohydrates that have a free aldehyde or ketone group (hemiacetal or hemiketal form) capable of acting as a reducing agent.

Lactose is a disaccharide formed by $\beta$-D-galactose and $\beta$-D-glucose with a $\beta-1,4$-glycosidic linkage. The anomeric carbon of the glucose unit is free, making it a reducing sugar.

Fructose is a monosaccharide containing a ketose group, which can isomerize to an aldose in an alkaline medium, making it a reducing sugar.

Sucrose is a disaccharide formed by $\alpha$-D-glucose and $\beta$-D-fructose. The glycosidic linkage involves the anomeric carbon of glucose (C1) and the anomeric carbon of fructose (C2). Since both anomeric carbons are involved in the bond, there is no free hemiacetal or hemiketal group. Thus, sucrose is a non-reducing sugar.

Maltose is a disaccharide formed by two $\alpha$-D-glucose units with an $\alpha-1,4$-glycosidic linkage. The anomeric carbon of the second glucose unit is free, making it a reducing sugar.

Answer: Sucrose

</details>
