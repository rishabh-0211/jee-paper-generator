### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate $\triangle G^\circ$ for reaction,
$\text{CH}_{4(g)} + \text{H}_{2(g)} \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_{6(g)}$ ($K_p = 2 \times 10^{17}$, $R = 8.314\ \text{JK}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-64.695$ kJ mol$^{-1}$
- **(B)** $-98.716$ kJ mol$^{-1}$
- **(C)** $-44.08$ kJ mol$^{-1}$
- **(D)** $-58.78$ kJ mol$^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $-98.716$ kJ mol$^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The standard Gibbs free energy change is related to the equilibrium constant by the equation:

$\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K_p = -2.303 RT \log K_p$

Assuming standard temperature $T = 298 \text{ K}$ and substituting the given values:

$\Delta G^\circ = -2.303 \times 8.314 \times 298 \times \log(2 \times 10^{17})$

$\Delta G^\circ = -5705.84 \times (\log 2 + 17 \log 10)$

$\Delta G^\circ = -5705.84 \times (0.3010 + 17)$

$\Delta G^\circ = -5705.84 \times 17.3010$

$\Delta G^\circ = -98716 \text{ J mol}^{-1}$

$\Delta G^\circ = -98.716 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$

Answer: $-98.716$ kJ mol$^{-1}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Find $\Delta n$ when 1 mol of each $\text{NH}_{3(g)}$ and $\text{HCl}_{(g)}$, reacts to form solid $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $-2$
- **(C)** $-1$
- **(D)** $-3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $-2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The balanced chemical equation for the reaction is:

$\text{NH}_{3(g)} + \text{HCl}_{(g)} \rightarrow \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}_{(s)}$

The change in the number of moles of gaseous species, $\Delta n$, is given by the difference between the number of moles of gaseous products and gaseous reactants.

Number of moles of gaseous products, $n_P = 0$

Number of moles of gaseous reactants, $n_R = 1 + 1 = 2$

$\Delta n = n_P - n_R = 0 - 2 = -2$

Answer: $-2$

</details>

### Q13. MHT CET 2021 (23 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The $\mathrm{pH}$ of monoacidic weak base is 10.9. Calculate the percent dissociation in $0.02 \mathrm{M}$ solution.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $7.92 \%$
- **(B)** $3.95 \%$
- **(C)** $6.25 \%$
- **(D)** $2.51 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3.95 \%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Monoacidic weak base $\mathrm{BOH}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{BOH}_{(\mathrm{aq})} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{B}_{(\mathrm{aq})}^{+}+\mathrm{OH}_{(\mathrm{aq})}^{-} \\
& \mathrm{C} \quad \mathrm{C} \alpha \quad \mathrm{C} \alpha \\
& \mathrm{C} \alpha \quad- \\
& \mathrm{pH}=10.9 \\
& \mathrm{pH}+\mathrm{pOH}=14 \\
& \mathrm{pOH}=14-10.9=3.1 \\
& \mathrm{pOH}=-\log _{10}\left[\mathrm{OH}^{-}\right] \\
& {\left[\mathrm{OH}^{-}\right]=10^{-\mathrm{pOH}}} \\
& =10^{-3.1}=10^{-4}+0.9 \\
& =10^{-4} \times 10^{0.9} \\
& {\left[\mathrm{OH}^{-}\right]=7.9 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{M}} \\
& {\left[\mathrm{OH}^{-}\right]=\mathrm{C}_\alpha} \\
& \alpha=\frac{7.9 \times 10^{-4}}{0.02} \\
& \alpha=3.95 \times 10^{-2} \\

\end{aligned}
