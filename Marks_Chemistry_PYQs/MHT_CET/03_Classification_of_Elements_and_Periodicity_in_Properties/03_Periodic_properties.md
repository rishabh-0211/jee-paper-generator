### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

Which element in group $17$ has the highest electronegativity?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{I}$
- **(B)** $\text{Br}$
- **(C)** $\text{Cl}$
- **(D)** $\text{F}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{F}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Electronegativity is the tendency of an atom to attract a shared pair of electrons towards itself. In the periodic table, electronegativity decreases down a group due to an increase in atomic size and shielding effect.

Group $17$ elements (halogens) include Fluorine ($\text{F}$), Chlorine ($\text{Cl}$), Bromine ($\text{Br}$), Iodine ($\text{I}$), and Astatine ($\text{At}$). Since Fluorine is at the top of the group, it has the smallest atomic radius and the highest effective nuclear charge among the halogens.

Therefore, Fluorine ($\text{F}$) has the highest electronegativity in group $17$ (and in the entire periodic table).

Answer: $\text{F}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following elements of group $17$ has the highest negative electron gain enthalpy?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Fluorine
- **(B)** Chlorine
- **(C)** Bromine
- **(D)** Iodine

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Chlorine**

**Detailed Solution:**

The electron gain enthalpy generally becomes less negative as we move down a group. However, in group $17$, chlorine has a more negative electron gain enthalpy than fluorine.

This anomaly is due to the very small size of the fluorine atom. The added electron goes into the compact $2p$ subshell of fluorine, where it experiences significant interelectronic repulsion from the existing electrons. In contrast, chlorine has a larger $3p$ subshell, so the interelectronic repulsions are much weaker, and it can more easily accommodate an extra electron.

Therefore, chlorine has the highest negative electron gain enthalpy among all the halogens.

Answer: Chlorine

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify increasing order of acidity for following diprotic acids in aqueous solutions.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{H}_2\text{S} < \text{H}_2\text{Se} < \text{H}_2\text{Te}$
- **(B)** $\text{H}_2\text{Se} < \text{H}_2\text{S} < \text{H}_2\text{Te}$

As we move down the group from Sulphur (S) to Tellurium (Te), the atomic size of the central atom increases.

Therefore, the acidic strength increases down the group.

The increasing order of acidity is $\text{H}_2\text{S} < \text{H}_2\text{Se} < \text{H}_2\text{Te}$.

- **(B)** Size increases then decreases
- **(C)** Size increases
- **(D)** Size decreases

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Size decreases**

**Detailed Solution:**

As we move from left to right across a period, the atomic number increases, meaning the number of protons in the nucleus increases.

The additional electrons are added to the same principal shell, so the shielding effect remains almost constant.

Consequently, the atomic size decreases from left to right in the same period.

Answer: Size decreases

</details>
