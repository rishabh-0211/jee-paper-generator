### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following compounds is used to prepare $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$ by Merck process using aqueous $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{BaSO}_4$
- **(B)** 2-ethylanthraquinol
- **(C)** $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_{2(aq)}$
- **(D)** $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_{2(aq)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In Merck's process, hydrogen peroxide is prepared by adding calculated amounts of sodium peroxide to an ice-cold $20\%$ solution of dilute sulfuric acid.

$\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2$

The solution is cooled to crystallize out sodium sulfate as Glauber's salt ($\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$), which is then filtered off, leaving behind an aqueous solution of hydrogen peroxide.

Thus, $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_2$ is the compound used in this process.

Answer: $\text{Na}_2\text{O}_{2(aq)}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate $\%$ by mass of a $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_2$ solution that is 67.2 volume. (Assume density of solution $= 1 \mathrm{~g/mL}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $13.60 \%$ by mass
- **(B)** $20.40 \%$ by mass
- **(C)** $22.44 \%$ by mass
- **(D)** $17.60 \%$ by mass

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $20.40 \%$ by mass**

**Detailed Solution:**

The volume strength of $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ solution is related to its molarity by the formula:

$\text{Volume strength} = 11.2 \times \text{Molarity}$

Substituting the given volume strength:

$67.2 = 11.2 \times \text{Molarity}$

$\text{Molarity} = \dfrac{67.2}{11.2} = 6 \text{ mol L}^{-1}$

This means $1 \text{ L}$ ($1000 \text{ mL}$) of the solution contains $6 \text{ moles}$ of $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$.

Molar mass of $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2 = 2(1) + 2(16) = 34 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$

Mass of $\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$ in $1 \text{ L}$ of solution $= 6 \times 34 = 204 \text{ g}$

Given the density of the solution is $1 \text{ g mL}^{-1}$, the mass of $1000 \text{ mL}$ of the solution is:

$\text{Mass of solution} = 1000 \text{ mL} \times 1 \text{ g mL}^{-1} = 1000 \text{ g}$

The percentage by mass is calculated as:

$\% \text{ by mass} = \dfrac{\text{Mass of solute}}{\text{Mass of solution}} \times 100$

$\% \text{ by mass} = \dfrac{204}{1000} \times 100 = 20.40 \%$

Answer: $20.40 \%$ by mass

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is used in preparation of hydrogen peroxide by Merck process?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{CaCO}_{3(\mathrm{~s})}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{BaO}_2 \cdot 8 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{CaCl}_{2(\mathrm{~s})}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{aq})}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{aq})}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Merck's process is a laboratory method used for the preparation of hydrogen peroxide.

In this process, a calculated quantity of sodium peroxide ($\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{O}_2$) is gradually added in small proportions to an ice-cold $20\%$ dilute solution of sulfuric acid ($\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4$).

The chemical reaction is given by:
$\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{O}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 \rightarrow \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{SO}_4 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2$

Therefore, sodium peroxide is the reagent used in the preparation of hydrogen peroxide by Merck's process.

Answer: $\mathrm{Na}_2 \mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{aq})}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is not a property of hydrogen peroxide?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It is immiscible in water.
- **(B)** It is pale blue coloured liquid in pure state.
- **(C)** It's strength is explained in volume unit.
- **(D)** It is mild oxidising as well reducing agent.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) It is immiscible in water.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$) is highly soluble in water and is miscible in all proportions due to its ability to form extensive hydrogen bonds with water molecules.

In its pure state, $H_2O_2$ is an almost colourless (very pale blue) liquid.

The strength of $H_2O_2$ is commonly expressed in terms of volume strength (e.g., $10$ volume, $20$ volume).

It acts as both an oxidising and a reducing agent in both acidic and alkaline media.

Therefore, the statement that it is immiscible in water is incorrect.

Answer: It is immiscible in water.

</details>
