### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the role of glycerol in the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It increases the reaction rate.
- **(B)** It reduces the reaction rate.
- **(C)** It increases the enthalpy of the reaction.
- **(D)** It increases the rate of collisions between reactant molecules.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) It reduces the reaction rate.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$) decomposes into water and oxygen gas upon standing or heating.

To prevent or slow down this decomposition, certain substances are added which act as negative catalysts (inhibitors).

Glycerol, along with urea and acetanilide, is commonly used as a stabilizer for hydrogen peroxide. It acts as a negative catalyst and retards the decomposition process.

Therefore, glycerol reduces the reaction rate of the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.

Answer: It reduces the reaction rate.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify role of glycerol in following reaction.
$2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{2(\ell)} \xrightarrow{\text { glycerol }} 2 \mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}_{(\ell)}+\mathrm{O}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** Homogeneous catalyst
- **(B)** Inhibitor
- **(C)** Promoter
- **(D)** Heterogeneous catalyst

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Inhibitor**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen peroxide decomposes into water and oxygen. This decomposition is retarded by the addition of certain substances like glycerol, acetanilide, or phosphoric acid.

These substances decrease the rate of decomposition of hydrogen peroxide and thus act as inhibitors (or negative catalysts).

Therefore, the role of glycerol in the given reaction is that of an inhibitor.

Answer: Inhibitor

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What type of following forces stabilizes the gauche conformation of ethylene glycol?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Dipole-induced dipole
- **(B)** Dipole-dipole
- **(C)** Dispersion forces
- **(D)** Intramolecular hydrogen bonding

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Intramolecular hydrogen bonding**

**Detailed Solution:**

Ethylene glycol is $HO-CH_2-CH_2-OH$.

In the gauche conformation, the dihedral angle between the two hydroxyl ($-OH$) groups is approximately $60^{\circ}$.

This spatial arrangement brings the two $-OH$ groups close enough to form an intramolecular hydrogen bond between the hydrogen atom of one group and the highly electronegative oxygen atom of the other.

This intramolecular hydrogen bonding provides additional stability to the gauche conformation, making it more stable than the anti conformation despite the steric repulsion.

Answer: Intramolecular hydrogen bonding

</details>
