### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify unstable isotope of hydrogen from the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Hydrogen
- **(B)** Deuterium
- **(C)** Tritium
- **(D)** Deuterium and Tritium

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Tritium**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen has three isotopes: Protium ($^{1}H$), Deuterium ($^{2}H$), and Tritium ($^{3}H$).

Protium and Deuterium are stable isotopes.

Tritium is a radioactive and unstable isotope of hydrogen. It decays by emitting low-energy $\beta^{-}$ particles.

Answer: Tritium

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the number of neutrons present in tritium?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $3$
- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Tritium is an isotope of hydrogen with the symbol $^{3}_{1}\text{H}$.

The atomic number ($Z$) of hydrogen is $1$, which means it has $1$ proton.

The mass number ($A$) of tritium is $3$.

The number of neutrons is given by $A - Z$.

Number of neutrons = $3 - 1 = 2$.

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following statements is false regarding hydrogen ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It has two isotopes.
- **(B)** In periodic table hydrogen is placed separately above group 1.
- **(C)** When one electron is lost it forms proton.
- **(D)** It does not exist freely.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) It has two isotopes.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Hydrogen has three isotopes: Protium ($^{1}H$), Deuterium ($^{2}H$), and Tritium ($^{3}H$). Thus, the statement that it has two isotopes is false.

Hydrogen is placed separately in the periodic table due to its anomalous behavior, resembling both alkali metals and halogens.

A hydrogen atom consists of one proton and one electron. When it loses its only electron, it forms an $H^{+}$ ion, which is a bare proton.

Atomic hydrogen is highly reactive and does not exist freely in nature; it exists as a diatomic molecule ($H_2$) or in combined states.

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following statements is NOT correct regarding dihydrogen?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It is colorless and tasteless gas.
- **(B)** It burns with a pale blue flame.
- **(C)** It is a polar water-soluble gas.
- **(D)** It is lighter than air.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) It is a polar water-soluble gas.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Dihydrogen ($H_2$) is a homonuclear diatomic molecule, meaning both atoms have the same electronegativity. Therefore, it is a non-polar molecule.

Since water is a polar solvent, dihydrogen is practically insoluble in water due to the "like dissolves like" principle.

Dihydrogen is a colorless, odorless, and tasteless gas. It is lighter than air and burns with a pale blue flame.

Thus, the statement that it is a polar water-soluble gas is incorrect.

Answer: It is a polar water-soluble gas.

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following methods is used to prepare dihydrogen with purity greater than 99.95% ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Electrolysis of pure water
- **(B)** Action of NaOH on Zinc
- **(C)** From hydrocarbons
- **(D)** Electrolysis of warm $\mathrm{Ba(OH)}_2$ solution

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Electrolysis of warm $\mathrm{Ba(OH)}_2$ solution**

**Detailed Solution:**

High purity dihydrogen (greater than $99.95\%$) is prepared by the electrolysis of warm aqueous barium hydroxide solution between nickel electrodes.

Other methods such as the action of $\mathrm{NaOH}$ on zinc or production from hydrocarbons yield dihydrogen of lower purity.

Answer: Electrolysis of warm $\mathrm{Ba(OH)}_2$ solution

</details>
