### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify radioactive element from the following.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{Fr}$
- **(B)** $\text{K}$
- **(C)** $\text{Rb}$
- **(D)** $\text{Li}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{Fr}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Francium ($\text{Fr}$) is the heaviest alkali metal belonging to group 1 of the periodic table. It is a highly radioactive element. Its most stable isotope, $^{223}\text{Fr}$, has a half-life of only 22 minutes.

Lithium ($\text{Li}$), Potassium ($\text{K}$), and Rubidium ($\text{Rb}$) are not considered primarily radioactive elements, although some of them have naturally occurring radioactive isotopes in trace amounts.

Answer: $\text{Fr}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate the time required to reduce the radioactive substance to $20\%$ if the half life period is $10$ minutes.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $23.23$ minute
- **(B)** $14.41$ minute
- **(C)** $18.52$ minute
- **(D)** $34.4$ minute

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $23.23$ minute**

**Detailed Solution:**

The radioactive decay follows first-order kinetics.

The decay constant $\lambda$ is given by:

$\lambda = \dfrac{0.693}{t_{1/2}} = \dfrac{0.693}{10} = 0.0693 \text{ min}^{-1}$

The time required for the substance to reduce to $20\%$ is calculated using the first-order rate equation:

$t = \dfrac{2.303}{\lambda} \log \left( \dfrac{N_0}{N_t} \right)$

Substituting $N_0 = 100$ and $N_t = 20$:

$t = \dfrac{2.303}{0.0693} \log \left( \dfrac{100}{20} \right)$

$t = \dfrac{2.303}{0.0693} \log 5$

Using $\log 5 = 0.699$:

$t = \dfrac{2.303 \times 0.699}{0.0693} = 23.23 \text{ min}$

Answer: $23.23$ minute

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is order of radioactive decay of a substance ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0$
- **(B)** $1$
- **(C)** $1.5$
- **(D)** $2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Radioactive decay follows first-order kinetics.

The rate of decay is directly proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei present at that instant.

$-\dfrac{dN}{dt} = \lambda N$

Since the rate depends on the first power of the number of nuclei, the order of radioactive decay is $1$.

Answer: $1$

</details>
