### Q1. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The experimentally determined rate law for the following reaction is Rate = k[H₂O₂]. What is the order of the reaction $2H_2O_{2(g)} \longrightarrow 2H_2O_{(l)} + O_{2(g)}$?

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0
- **(B)** 3
- **(C)** 2
- **(D)** 1

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

The order of a reaction is defined as the sum of the powers of the concentration terms in the experimentally determined rate law.

Given the rate law:
$\text{Rate} = k[H_2O_2]$

The power of the concentration term $[H_2O_2]$ is $1$.

Therefore, the order of the reaction is $1$.

Answer: 1

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a reaction $\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B} \rightarrow$ product, if $[\mathrm{A}]$ is doubled keeping [B] constant, the rate of reaction doubles. Calculate the order of reaction with respect to $A$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0$
- **(B)** $1 / 2$
- **(C)** $1$
- **(D)** $2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{array}{ll} & \mathrm{A}+\mathrm{B} \longrightarrow \text { Product } \\ & \text { Rate }^{\mathrm{k}[\mathrm{A}]^x[\mathrm{~B}]^{\mathrm{y}}} \\ & \text { Rate }_1=\mathrm{k}[2 \mathrm{~A}]^x[\mathrm{~B}]^{\mathrm{y}} \\ \therefore \quad & \frac{\text { Rate }_1}{\text { Rate }}=\frac{\mathrm{k} 2^x[\mathrm{~A}]^x[\mathrm{~B}]^y}{\mathrm{k}[\mathrm{A}]^x[\mathrm{~B}]^{\mathrm{y}}}\end{array}$
$\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore & \frac{2 \times \text { Rate }}{\text { Rate }}=2^x \\
\therefore & 2^x=2 \\
\therefore & x=1
\end{array}$
$\therefore \quad$ Order of reaction with respect to $A=1$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Slope of the graph between $\log \frac{[\mathrm{A}]_0}{[\mathrm{~A}]_{\mathrm{t}}}$ (y axis) and time ( $x$ axis) for first order reaction is equal to:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\pm \frac{k}{2.303}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{k}$
- **(C)** $-\mathrm{k}$
- **(D)** $-\frac{2.303}{k}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\pm \frac{k}{2.303}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The integrated rate law for the first order reaction is
$$
\begin{gathered}
k=\frac{2.303}{t} \log _{10} \frac{[\mathrm{A}]_0}{[\mathrm{~A}]_{\mathrm{t}}} \\
\log _{10} \frac{[\mathrm{A}]_0}{[\mathrm{~A}]_{\mathrm{t}}}=\frac{\mathrm{k}}{2.303} \mathrm{t} \\
\downarrow \\
\mathrm{y} \quad \mathrm{m} x
\end{gathered}
$$

The graph of $\log _{10} \frac{[\mathrm{A}]_0}{[\mathrm{~A}]_{\mathrm{t}}}$ versus time $(\mathrm{t})$ is a straight line passing through origin with slope $(\mathrm{m})=+\frac{\mathrm{k}}{2.303}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Calculate the amount of reactant in percent that remains after 60 minutes involved in first order reaction. $\left(\mathrm{k}=0.02303\right.$ minute $\left.^{-1}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $25 \%$
- **(B)** $50 \%$
- **(C)** $75 \%$
- **(D)** $12.5 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $25 \%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a first order reaction,
$\begin{aligned}
& k=\frac{0.693}{t_{1 / 2}} \\
& t_{1 / 2}=\frac{0.693}{0.02303}=30 \mathrm{~min}
\end{aligned}$
Percent of reactant that remains after $t_{1 / 2}=50 \%$. $2 \times \mathrm{t}_{1 / 2}=60 \mathrm{~min}$
Therefore, percent of reactant that remains after $2 \mathrm{t}_{1 / 2}=25 \%$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2021 (23 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A reaction is first order with respective to A and second order with respective to $B$. What is the effect on reaction rate if concentration of B is increased 3 times?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Rate increases 6 times
- **(B)** Rate increase 9 times
- **(C)** Rate increase 2 times
- **(D)** Rate increase 3 times

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Rate increase 9 times**

**Detailed Solution:**

Rate law of reaction,
$$
\mathrm{r}=\mathrm{k}[\mathrm{A}]^{\prime}[\mathrm{B}]^2
$$
If concentration of $\mathrm{B}$ is increased 3 times,
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{B} \rightarrow 3 \mathrm{~B} \\
& \mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{New}}=\mathrm{k}[\mathrm{A}][3 \mathrm{~B}]^2 \\
& \frac{\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{New}}}{\mathrm{r}}=\frac{\mathrm{k}[\mathrm{A}][3 \mathrm{~B}]^2}{\mathrm{k}[\mathrm{A}][\mathrm{B}]^2} \\
& \frac{\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{New}}}{\mathrm{r}}=3^2 \\
& \mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{New}}=9 \mathrm{r}
\end{aligned}
$$
Rate increase 9 times.

</details>
