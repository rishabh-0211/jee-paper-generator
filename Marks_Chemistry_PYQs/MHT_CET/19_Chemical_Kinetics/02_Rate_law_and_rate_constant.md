### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Identify the nature of the graph obtained when rate versus initial concentration is plotted for first order reaction.

**Options:**

- **(A)** A straight line passing through the origine having -ve slope.
- **(B)** A straight line parallel to X axis.
- **(C)** A straight line passing through the origine having +ve slope.
- **(D)** A straight line intercepting X and Y axes.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) A straight line passing through the origine having +ve slope.**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a first order reaction, the rate law is given by:

$\text{Rate} = k[A]$

where $k$ is the rate constant and $[A]$ is the concentration of the reactant.

Comparing this with the equation of a straight line, $y = mx + c$, we get:

$y = \text{Rate}$
$x = [A]$
$m = k$
$c = 0$

Since the rate constant $k$ is always positive, the slope $m$ is positive. The $y$-intercept $c$ is zero, which means the line passes through the origin.

Thus, the plot of rate versus initial concentration for a first order reaction is a straight line passing through the origin with a positive slope.

Answer: A straight line passing through the origine having +ve slope.

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Determine the rate law for a reaction, $2\,\text{NO} + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow 2\,\text{NOBr}$ that occurs in following two steps.
i) $\text{NO} + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{NOBr}_2$ (fast)
ii) $\text{NOBr}_2 + \text{NO} \rightarrow 2\text{NOBr}$ (slow)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $r = k[\text{NOBr}_2][\text{NO}]$
- **(B)** $r = k'[\text{NO}]^2[\text{Br}_2]$
- **(C)** $r = k'[\text{NO}][\text{Br}_2]$
- **(D)** $r = k[\text{NO}][\text{Br}_2]^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $r = k'[\text{NO}]^2[\text{Br}_2]$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rate of the reaction is determined by the slowest step, which is the rate-determining step.

From step (ii), the rate equation is:
$r = k[\text{NOBr}_2][\text{NO}]$

Since $\text{NOBr}_2$ is an intermediate, its concentration must be expressed in terms of the reactants. Assuming step (i) is a fast equilibrium:
$K_{eq} = \dfrac{[\text{NOBr}_2]}{[\text{NO}][\text{Br}_2]}$

$[\text{NOBr}_2] = K_{eq}[\text{NO}][\text{Br}_2]$

Substituting this into the rate equation:
$r = k(K_{eq}[\text{NO}][\text{Br}_2])[\text{NO}]$

$r = k'[\text{NO}]^2[\text{Br}_2]$ where $k' = k K_{eq}$

Answer: $r = k'[\text{NO}]^2[\text{Br}_2]$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If for a reaction, $A + B \rightarrow C + D$, when $[A]$ alone is doubled, the rate gets doubled and when $[B]$ alone is increased by $9$ times, the rate gets tripled. Find the order of reaction.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3/4$
- **(B)** $3/2$
- **(C)** $4/9$
- **(D)** $2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3/2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the rate law for the reaction be $r = k[A]^x[B]^y$.

When $[A]$ alone is doubled, the rate gets doubled:

$2r = k(2[A])^x[B]^y$

Dividing this by the initial rate equation gives $2^x = 2$, which implies $x = 1$.

When $[B]$ alone is increased by $9$ times, the rate gets tripled:

$3r = k[A]^x(9[B])^y$

Dividing this by the initial rate equation gives $9^y = 3$.

Since $9^y = (3^2)^y = 3^{2y}$, we have $3^{2y} = 3^1$, which implies $2y = 1$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

The overall order of the reaction is the sum of the individual orders:

Order $= x + y = 1 + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$

Answer: $3/2$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

Which of the following is a pseudo-first order reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Decomposition of ammonia on platinum metal
- **(B)** Decomposition of $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2(l)$
- **(C)** Decomposition of $\text{N}_2\text{O}_5(g)$
- **(D)** Hydrolysis of methyl acetate

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Hydrolysis of methyl acetate**

**Detailed Solution:**

A pseudo-first order reaction is a bimolecular reaction that appears to follow first-order kinetics because one of the reactants is present in large excess, making its concentration essentially constant throughout the reaction.

The acid-catalyzed hydrolysis of methyl acetate is given by the equation:
$\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+} \text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{CH}_3\text{OH}$

The rate law is expected to be:
$\text{Rate} = k[\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_3][\text{H}_2\text{O}]$

Since water is present in large excess, its concentration remains practically constant. The rate law becomes:
$\text{Rate} = k'[\text{CH}_3\text{COOCH}_3]$ where $k' = k[\text{H}_2\text{O}]$

Thus, it behaves as a first-order reaction and is called a pseudo-first order reaction.

Decomposition of ammonia on platinum metal is a zero-order reaction at high pressure.
Decomposition of $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2(l)$ and $\text{N}_2\text{O}_5(g)$ are true first-order reactions.

Answer: Hydrolysis of methyl acetate

For the reaction $4 \mathrm{NH}_{3}+50_{2} \rightarrow 4 \mathrm{NO}+6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$ if the rate of disappearance of $\mathrm{NH}_{3}$ is $3.6 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{M} / \mathrm{s}$. What is the rate of formation of water?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4.0 \times 10^{4} \mathrm{M} / \mathrm{S}$
- **(B)** $3.6 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{M} / \mathrm{S}$
- **(C)** $6.0 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{M} / \mathrm{S}$
- **(D)** $5.4 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{M} / \mathrm{S}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $5.4 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{M} / \mathrm{S}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$4 \mathrm{NH}_{3}+5 \mathrm{O}_{2} \rightarrow 4 \mathrm{NO}+6 \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}$
Rate of reaction $=-\frac{1}{4} \frac{\mathrm{d}\left[\mathrm{NH}_{3}\right]}{\mathrm{dt}}=-\frac{1}{5} \frac{\mathrm{d}\left[\mathrm{O}_{2}\right]}{\mathrm{dt}}=\frac{1 \mathrm{~d}[\mathrm{NO}]}{4}=\frac{1}{6} \frac{\mathrm{d}\left[\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right]}{\mathrm{dt}}$
$\therefore$ Rate of formation of water, $\frac{\mathrm{d}\left[\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right]}{\mathrm{dt}}=\frac{6}{4} \frac{\mathrm{d}\left[\mathrm{NH}_{3}\right]}{\mathrm{dt}}$
$$
=\frac{6}{4} \times 3.6 \times 10^{-3}=5.4 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{M} / \mathrm{s}
$$

</details>
