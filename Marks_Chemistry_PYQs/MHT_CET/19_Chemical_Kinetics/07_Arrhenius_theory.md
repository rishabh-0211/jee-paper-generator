$0.602 = \dfrac{E_a}{19.147} \left( \dfrac{1}{4800} \right)$

$E_a = 0.602 \times 19.147 \times 4800$

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The equation for the rate constant is $k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$.
A chemical reaction will proceed more rapidly if there is a decrease in

**Options:**

- **(A)** $k$
- **(B)** $A$
- **(C)** $E_a$
- **(D)** $T$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $E_a$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the rate constant $k$.

According to the Arrhenius equation, $k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$.

For the reaction to proceed more rapidly, the rate constant $k$ must increase.

A decrease in the activation energy $E_a$ makes the exponent $-E_a/RT$ less negative, which increases the value of $e^{-E_a/RT}$.

Therefore, a decrease in $E_a$ leads to an increase in $k$, making the reaction proceed more rapidly.

### Q13. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following is true for a reaction as per collision theory?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Every collision between reactants leads to chemical reaction.
- **(B)** It may be expected that rate of reaction is equal to rate of collision.
- **(C)** For gas phase reactions, the number of collisions is far less compared to observed rate.
- **(D)** The colliding molecules do not need proper orientation.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) It may be expected that rate of reaction is equal to rate of collision.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the collision theory of bimolecular reactions, chemical reactions occur as a result of collisions between reactant molecules. Based on this, it may be expected that the rate of reaction is equal to the rate of collision.

However, the other statements are incorrect because:
1. Not every collision leads to a chemical reaction; only effective collisions (with sufficient energy and proper orientation) result in a reaction.
2. For gas-phase reactions, the number of collisions is far greater (not less) compared to the observed rate of reaction.
3. The colliding molecules must have proper orientation to form the products.

Thus, the only true statement among the given options is that it may be expected that the rate of reaction is equal to the rate of collision.

Answer: It may be expected that rate of reaction is equal to rate of collision.

</details>

### Q14. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the Arrhenius plot of logk versus $1 / T$ find the value of intercept on $y$ axis

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\quad \log _{10} \mathrm{~A}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{-\mathrm{Ea}}{\mathrm{R}}$
- **(C)** $\quad \ln k$
- **(D)** $\quad R / E_a$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\quad \log _{10} \mathrm{~A}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The Arrhenius equation is given by $k = A e^{-E_a / RT}$

Taking the logarithm to the base $10$ on both sides, we get:

$\log_{10} k = \log_{10} A - \dfrac{E_a}{2.303 RT}$

This equation is of the form $y = mx + c$, which represents a straight line.

Here, $y = \log_{10} k$ and $x = \dfrac{1}{T}$

The slope of the line is $m = -\dfrac{E_a}{2.303 R}$

The y-intercept is $c = \log_{10} A$

Thus, in the plot of $\log_{10} k$ versus $\dfrac{1}{T}$, the intercept on the y-axis is $\log_{10} A$.

Answer: $\quad \log _{10} \mathrm{~A}$
