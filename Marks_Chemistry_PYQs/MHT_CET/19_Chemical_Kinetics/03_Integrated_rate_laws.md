The half life of a first order reaction is $13.86$ minutes. Calculate the time required for the reactant to be reduced to $20\%$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8.05$ min.
- **(B)** $16.1$ min.
- **(C)** $32.2$ min.
- **(D)** $48.3$ min.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $32.2$ min.**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a first order reaction, the rate constant $k$ is given by:

$k = \dfrac{0.693}{t_{1/2}}$

Substituting the given half-life $t_{1/2} = 13.86$ min:

$k = \dfrac{0.693}{13.86} = 0.05 \text{ min}^{-1}$

The time $t$ required for the reactant to be reduced to $20\%$ of its initial concentration is calculated using the first order integrated rate equation:

$t = \dfrac{2.303}{k} \log \left( \dfrac{[A]_0}{[A]} \right)$

Given that the reactant is reduced to $20\%$, $[A] = 0.20 [A]_0$.

$t = \dfrac{2.303}{0.05} \log \left( \dfrac{100}{20} \right)$

$t = \dfrac{2.303}{0.05} \log 5$

Using $\log 5 = 0.699$:

$t = 46.06 \times 0.699 = 32.19 \text{ min} \approx 32.2 \text{ min}$

Answer: $32.2$ min.

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is the correct relation for half life period of a zero order reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** It is directly proportional to initial concentration of reactants
- **(B)** It is inversely proportional concentrations of reactants
- **(C)** It is directly proportional to the concentration of product.
- **(D)** It is inversely proportional to the square of initial concentration of reactants

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) It is directly proportional to initial concentration of reactants**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a zero order reaction, the integrated rate equation is given by:

$[A] = [A]_0 - kt$

At half life, $t = t_{1/2}$ and the concentration of reactant becomes half of its initial value, so $[A] = \dfrac{[A]_0}{2}$.

Substituting these values into the rate equation:

$\dfrac{[A]_0}{2} = [A]_0 - k t_{1/2}$

$k t_{1/2} = \dfrac{[A]_0}{2}$

$t_{1/2} = \dfrac{[A]_0}{2k}$

Thus, $t_{1/2} \propto [A]_0$.

The half life period of a zero order reaction is directly proportional to the initial concentration of the reactants.

Answer: It is directly proportional to initial concentration of reactants

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8 \alpha$ and $4 \beta$
- **(B)** $8 \alpha$ and $16 \beta$
- **(C)** $4 \alpha$ and $2 \beta$
- **(D)** $6 \alpha$ and $4 \beta$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $6 \alpha$ and $4 \beta$**

**Detailed Solution:**

No. of $\alpha$ particles $=\frac{232-208}{4}=\frac{24}{4}=6 \alpha$
No. of $\beta$ particles $=2 \times 6-(90-82)$
$$
\begin{array}{l}
=12-8 \\
=4 \beta
\end{array}
$$
Hence, $6 \alpha$ and $4 \beta$ particles are emitted.

</details>
