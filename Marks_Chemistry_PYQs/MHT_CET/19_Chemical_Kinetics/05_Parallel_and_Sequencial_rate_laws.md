### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If a reaction occurs in following two steps
i) $2 \mathrm{ClO}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$
ii) $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}+\mathrm{ClO}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{ClO}_3^{-}+\mathrm{Cl}^{-}$
Find the reaction intermediate.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{ClO}^{-}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{ClO}_3^{-}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Cl}^{-}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

A reaction intermediate is a chemical species that is formed in one elementary step and consumed in a subsequent step of a reaction mechanism.

In the given mechanism:
Step i) $2 \mathrm{ClO}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$ (Formation of $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$)
Step ii) $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-} + \mathrm{ClO}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{ClO}_3^{-} + \mathrm{Cl}^{-}$ (Consumption of $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$)

Since $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$ is produced in the first step and consumed in the second step, it does not appear in the overall balanced chemical equation ($3 \mathrm{ClO}^{-} \rightarrow \mathrm{ClO}_3^{-} + \mathrm{Cl}^{-}$).

Therefore, $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$ is the reaction intermediate.

Answer: $\mathrm{ClO}_2^{-}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2022 (07 Aug Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A complex reaction takes place in 2 steps
(i) $\mathrm{NO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}+\mathrm{F}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \rightarrow \mathrm{NO}_2 \mathrm{~F}_{(\mathrm{g})}+\mathrm{F}_{(\mathrm{g})}+$ (slow)
(ii) $\mathrm{F}_{(\mathrm{g})}+\mathrm{NO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})} \rightarrow \mathrm{NO}_2 \mathrm{~F}_{(\mathrm{g})}($ fast $)$
Identify the reaction intermediate?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{F}_{(\mathrm{g})}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{NO}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{F}_{2(\mathrm{~g})}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{NO}_2 \mathrm{~F}_{(\mathrm{g})}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{F}_{(\mathrm{g})}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Reaction intermediates are those substance which are formed in one step and get consumed in the successive step
$\therefore$ In the given mechanism it is $\mathrm{F}(\mathrm{g})$

</details>
