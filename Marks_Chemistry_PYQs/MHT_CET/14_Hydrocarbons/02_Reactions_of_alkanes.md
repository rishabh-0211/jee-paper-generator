### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following reaction steps is known as propagation step

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{Cl-Cl} \rightarrow \text{Cl}^\bullet + \text{Cl}^\bullet$
- **(B)** $\text{CH}_3 + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-Cl} + \text{Cl}^\bullet$
- **(C)** $\text{CH}_3^\bullet + \text{Cl}^\bullet \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-Cl}$
- **(D)** $\text{CH}_3^\bullet + \text{CH}_3^\bullet \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-CH}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\text{CH}_3 + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-Cl} + \text{Cl}^\bullet$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a free radical substitution reaction, the mechanism involves three types of steps:

1. Initiation step: A neutral molecule breaks down to form free radicals.
$\text{Cl-Cl} \rightarrow \text{Cl}^\bullet + \text{Cl}^\bullet$

2. Propagation step: A free radical reacts with a neutral molecule to generate a new free radical and a new neutral molecule. This step keeps the chain reaction going.
$\text{CH}_3^\bullet + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-Cl} + \text{Cl}^\bullet$

3. Termination step: Two free radicals combine to form a neutral molecule, stopping the chain reaction.
$\text{CH}_3^\bullet + \text{Cl}^\bullet \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-Cl}$
$\text{CH}_3^\bullet + \text{CH}_3^\bullet \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-CH}_3$

Therefore, the reaction $\text{CH}_3^\bullet + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{-Cl} + \text{Cl}^\bullet$ represents the propagation step.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When excess of chlorine is used in the chlorination of methane find the major product obtained.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Chloromethane
- **(B)** Dichloromethane
- **(C)** Trichloromethane
- **(D)** Tetrachloromethane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Tetrachloromethane**

**Detailed Solution:**

Chlorination of methane is a free radical substitution reaction.

When an excess of chlorine is used, all the hydrogen atoms of methane are successively replaced by chlorine atoms.

The sequence of reactions is:
$CH_4 + Cl_2 \rightarrow CH_3Cl + HCl$
$CH_3Cl + Cl_2 \rightarrow CH_2Cl_2 + HCl$
$CH_2Cl_2 + Cl_2 \rightarrow CHCl_3 + HCl$
$CHCl_3 + Cl_2 \rightarrow CCl_4 + HCl$

Thus, the final major product obtained is tetrachloromethane ($CCl_4$).

Answer: Tetrachloromethane

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which major product is formed when excess of methane is treated with chlorine in presence of UV light?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Chloromethane
- **(B)** Dichloromethane
- **(C)** Trichloromethane
- **(D)** Tetrachloromethane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Chloromethane**

**Detailed Solution:**

When an excess of methane is treated with chlorine in the presence of UV light, free radical halogenation takes place.

Since methane is present in excess, the chlorine radicals are much more likely to collide with methane molecules rather than with the already formed chloromethane molecules. This minimizes further substitution reactions.

$\text{CH}_4 \text{ (excess)} + \text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{UV light}} \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{HCl}$

Thus, the reaction stops predominantly at the first step, making chloromethane the major product.

Answer: Chloromethane

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following isomers has highest boiling point?

**Options:**

- **(A)** tert-Butylamine
- **(B)** Ethyldimethylamine
- **(C)** Diethylamine
- **(D)** n-Butylamine

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) n-Butylamine**

**Detailed Solution:**

The boiling point of amines depends on the extent of intermolecular hydrogen bonding and van der Waals forces.

Primary amines have two hydrogen atoms attached to the nitrogen atom, allowing for more extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding compared to secondary amines (one hydrogen atom) and tertiary amines (no hydrogen atoms). Therefore, primary amines generally have higher boiling points than secondary and tertiary amines of comparable molecular mass.

Among the given options, n-butylamine and tert-butylamine are primary amines, diethylamine is a secondary amine, and ethyldimethylamine is a tertiary amine.

Between the two primary amines, n-butylamine is a straight-chain molecule, whereas tert-butylamine is highly branched. Branching decreases the surface area of the molecule, which weakens the van der Waals forces and lowers the boiling point.

Thus, n-butylamine has the highest boiling point among the given isomers.

Answer: n-Butylamine

</details>
