### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following hydrocarbons has most acidic H atom?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Methane
- **(B)** Ethene
- **(C)** Ethyne
- **(D)** Ethane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Ethyne**

**Detailed Solution:**

The acidity of a hydrogen atom in a hydrocarbon depends on the hybridization of the carbon atom to which it is attached.

Methane ($CH_4$) and Ethane ($C_2H_6$) have $sp^3$ hybridized carbon atoms with $25\%$ s-character.

Ethene ($C_2H_4$) has $sp^2$ hybridized carbon atoms with $33.3\%$ s-character.

Ethyne ($C_2H_2$) has $sp$ hybridized carbon atoms with $50\%$ s-character.

As the s-character of the hybridized orbital increases, the electronegativity of the carbon atom increases. A more electronegative carbon atom pulls the shared electron pair of the C-H bond closer to itself, making it easier to release the hydrogen as an $H^+$ ion.

Since ethyne has the highest s-character ($50\%$), its hydrogen atoms are the most acidic among the given options.

Answer: Ethyne

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify the product when but-2-yne is treated with $\text{H}_2$ gas in the presence of Lindlar's catalyst.

**Options:**

- **(A)** Cis-But-2-ene
- **(B)** Trans-But-2-ene
- **(C)** Mixture of both
- **(D)** Butane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Cis-But-2-ene**

**Detailed Solution:**

But-2-yne is an alkyne with the structure $CH_3-C \equiv C-CH_3$.

Lindlar's catalyst consists of palladium deposited on calcium carbonate and poisoned with lead acetate or quinoline. It is used for the partial hydrogenation of alkynes to alkenes.

The addition of hydrogen gas in the presence of Lindlar's catalyst proceeds via syn-addition.

Syn-addition of $H_2$ to but-2-yne results in the formation of cis-but-2-ene.

Answer: Cis-But-2-ene

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify 'A' in the following reaction.
$\text { A } \xrightarrow{\text { Lithium amide }} \text { Ethynyl lithium } \xrightarrow{\text { Bromoethane }} \text { But }-1-\text { yne }$

**Options:**

- **(A)** Ethene
- **(B)** Ethyne
- **(C)** But-1-ene
- **(D)** But-2-ene

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Ethyne**

**Detailed Solution:**

The reaction sequence involves the formation of a terminal alkyne salt and its subsequent alkylation.

When ethyne ($\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH}$) reacts with a strong base like lithium amide ($\text{LiNH}_2$), the acidic hydrogen of the terminal alkyne is abstracted to form ethynyl lithium ($\text{HC}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Li}^+$).

$\text{HC}\equiv\text{CH} + \text{LiNH}_2 \rightarrow \text{HC}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Li}^+ + \text{NH}_3$

Ethynyl lithium then acts as a nucleophile and undergoes an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ reaction with bromoethane ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$) to form but-1-yne.

$\text{HC}\equiv\text{C}^-\text{Li}^+ + \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} \rightarrow \text{HC}\equiv\text{C}-\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3 + \text{LiBr}$

Thus, the starting material 'A' is ethyne.

Answer: Ethyne

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Identify product ' $B$ ' in the following sequence of reactions.
$\text { 1,2 Dibromoethane } \xrightarrow[\text { alcohol }]{\mathrm{KOH}} \mathrm{~A} \xrightarrow{\text { sodamide }} \mathrm{B}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** Bromoethane
- **(B)** Bromoethene
- **(C)** Ethene
- **(D)** Ethyne

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Ethyne**

**Detailed Solution:**

1,2-Dibromoethane reacts with alcoholic $\mathrm{KOH}$ to undergo dehydrohalogenation, forming bromoethene (A).

$\mathrm{CH_2Br-CH_2Br} + \mathrm{KOH(alc.)} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH_2=CHBr} + \mathrm{KBr} + \mathrm{H_2O}$

Bromoethene (A) is less reactive towards further elimination. It requires a stronger base like sodamide ($\mathrm{NaNH_2}$) for the second dehydrohalogenation step to form ethyne (B).

$\mathrm{CH_2=CHBr} + \mathrm{NaNH_2} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH \equiv CH} + \mathrm{NaBr} + \mathrm{NH_3}$

Thus, product B is ethyne.

Answer: Ethyne

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following catalysts is used to convert $-\mathrm{C} \equiv \mathrm{C}-\mathrm{to}$<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/py35bdsq/S_c4LOKpSNcJw0T3_KwjAbkT53Z6laqJ2tEqizGFZbM.original.fullsize.png"/>
to form cis-alkene?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\quad \mathrm{ZnCl}_2 / \mathrm{HCl}$
- **(B)** $\quad \mathrm{Pd}-\mathrm{C}$ quinoline
- **(C)** Na / liquid $\mathrm{NH}_3$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Na} / \mathrm{Hg}$ in $\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{O}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\quad \mathrm{Pd}-\mathrm{C}$ quinoline**

**Detailed Solution:**

The conversion of an alkyne ($-\mathrm{C} \equiv \mathrm{C}-$) to a cis-alkene requires a catalyst that allows for the partial reduction and specific syn-addition of hydrogen.

Lindlar's catalyst, which consists of Palladium on Carbon ($\mathrm{Pd}-\mathrm{C}$) or $\mathrm{CaCO}_3$ poisoned with quinoline or lead acetate, is used for this purpose. The poisoning of the catalyst prevents the further reduction of the alkene to an alkane, and the surface chemistry of the metal ensures that both hydrogen atoms are added to the same side of the alkyne (syn-addition), exclusively forming a cis-alkene.

In contrast, using sodium in liquid ammonia ($\mathrm{Na}$ / liquid $\mathrm{NH}_3$) results in a Birch reduction, which proceeds via an anti-addition mechanism to yield a trans-alkene.

Therefore, $\mathrm{Pd}-\mathrm{C}$ quinoline is the correct catalyst.

Answer: $\quad \mathrm{Pd}-\mathrm{C}$ quinoline

</details>
