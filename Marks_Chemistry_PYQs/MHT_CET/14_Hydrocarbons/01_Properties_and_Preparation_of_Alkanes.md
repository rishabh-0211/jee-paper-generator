Which of the following compounds is obtained in the first step of Wolf - Kishner reduction when alkyl aryl ketones are reduced to alkyl benzene?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Acids
- **(B)** Alcohols
- **(C)** Hydrazones
- **(D)** Alkenes

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Hydrazones**

**Detailed Solution:**

Wolff-Kishner reduction involves two main steps.

In the first step, the carbonyl compound (alkyl aryl ketone) reacts with hydrazine ($NH_2NH_2$) to form a hydrazone.

Thus, the compound obtained in the first step is a hydrazone.

Answer: Hydrazones

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which reaction is used to synthesize straight-chain alkylbenzenes?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Etard
- **(B)** Rosenmund reduction
- **(C)** Stephen's reaction
- **(D)** Wolf kishner reduction

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Wolf kishner reduction**

**Detailed Solution:**

Straight-chain alkylbenzenes cannot be prepared directly by Friedel-Crafts alkylation of benzene with primary alkyl halides (longer than ethyl) because the intermediate primary carbocation undergoes rearrangement to form a more stable secondary or tertiary carbocation, yielding branched alkylbenzenes.

To synthesize straight-chain alkylbenzenes, benzene is first subjected to Friedel-Crafts acylation with a straight-chain acyl chloride to form an acylbenzene (a ketone). The carbonyl group of the acylbenzene is then reduced to a methylene group ($-CH_2-$) to give the straight-chain alkylbenzene.

This reduction is achieved using either the Clemmensen reduction ($Zn(Hg)$ and concentrated $HCl$) or the Wolff-Kishner reduction ($NH_2NH_2$, $KOH$, and heat).

Among the given options, the Wolff-Kishner reduction is the correct reaction for this synthesis. The other reactions are used for different purposes:
Etard reaction: Oxidation of toluene to benzaldehyde.
Rosenmund reduction: Reduction of acyl chlorides to aldehydes.
Stephen's reaction: Reduction of nitriles to aldehydes.

Answer: Wolf kishner reduction

</details>

Which of the following hydrocarbons has stronger London dispersion forces ?

- **(D)** neo-pentane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

The straight-chain structure of n-pentane provides a larger surface area for intermolecular contact compared to the compact structure of neo-pentane.

Therefore, n-pentane has the strongest London dispersion forces among the given hydrocarbons.

Which of the following alkyl chlorides on Wurtz reaction gives 2, 2, 5, 5- tetramethylhexane ?

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following alkanes is NOT obtained when a mixture of ethyl iodide and n-propyl iodide is subjected to Wurtz reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** n-Butane
- **(B)** Propane
- **(C)** n-Pentane
- **(D)** n-Hexane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Propane**

**Detailed Solution:**

In the Wurtz reaction, alkyl halides react with sodium in dry ether to form alkanes through the coupling of alkyl radicals.

The given mixture contains ethyl iodide ($C_2H_5I$) and n-propyl iodide ($C_3H_7I$). The free radicals generated are the ethyl radical ($C_2H_5^{\bullet}$) and the n-propyl radical ($C_3H_7^{\bullet}$).

The possible coupling products are:
1. Self-coupling of ethyl radicals:
$C_2H_5^{\bullet} + C_2H_5^{\bullet} \rightarrow C_4H_{10}$ (n-Butane)

2. Cross-coupling of ethyl and n-propyl radicals:
$C_2H_5^{\bullet} + C_3H_7^{\bullet} \rightarrow C_5H_{12}$ (n-Pentane)

3. Self-coupling of n-propyl radicals:
$C_3H_7^{\bullet} + C_3H_7^{\bullet} \rightarrow C_6H_{14}$ (n-Hexane)

Propane ($C_3H_8$) would require the coupling of a methyl radical ($CH_3^{\bullet}$) and an ethyl radical ($C_2H_5^{\bullet}$). Since methyl iodide is not present in the mixture, propane is not obtained as a coupling product.

Answer: Propane

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

When a mixture of two different alkyl halides reacts with metallic sodium in dry ether, the formation of a possible number of alkanes are-

**Options:**

- **(A)** Two
- **(B)** Three
- **(C)** Four
- **(D)** Five

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Three**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the two different alkyl halides be $R-X$ and $R'-X$.

When this mixture reacts with metallic sodium in dry ether (Wurtz reaction), three types of coupling reactions take place.

$R-X + 2Na + X-R \rightarrow R-R + 2NaX$

$R'-X + 2Na + X-R' \rightarrow R'-R' + 2NaX$

$R-X + 2Na + X-R' \rightarrow R-R' + 2NaX$

Thus, a total of three different alkanes are formed.

Answer: Three

</details>

What is the difference in molar mases of third and fourth homologues of alkane series?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $28 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $14 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $15 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $14 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The general formula for the alkane series is $C_n H_{2n+2}$.

The third homologue of the alkane series is propane ($C_3H_8$) and the fourth homologue is butane ($C_4H_{10}$).

Any two successive members of a homologous series differ by a $-CH_2-$ unit.

The molar mass of a $-CH_2-$ unit is $12 + 2 \times 1 = 14 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.

Therefore, the difference in molar masses of the third and fourth homologues is $14 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$.

Answer: $14 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

### Q12. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following compounds has highest boiling point?

**Options:**

- **(A)** n-Hexane
- **(B)** 2-Mathylpentane
- **(C)** 3-Methylpentane
- **(D)** 2,2-Dimethylbutane

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) n-Hexane**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given compounds are isomers of hexane ($C_6H_{14}$).

The boiling point of alkanes depends on the magnitude of intermolecular van der Waals forces, which are directly proportional to the surface area of the molecule.

As the branching in an alkane increases, the molecule becomes more spherical in shape. This decreases the effective surface area of contact between molecules, leading to weaker van der Waals forces and a lower boiling point.

Among the given isomers, n-hexane is a straight-chain alkane with no branching, so it has the maximum surface area and the strongest intermolecular forces. Therefore, n-hexane has the highest boiling point.

Answer: n-Hexane
