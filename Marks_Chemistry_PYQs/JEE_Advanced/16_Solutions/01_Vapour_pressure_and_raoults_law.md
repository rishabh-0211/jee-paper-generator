### Q1. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

<strong>Passage:</strong> Two volatile liquids A and B form an ideal solution. Consider a $5$ molal solution of B in A inside a closed container having a total vapour pressure of $100$ mm Hg at $300$ K. The vapour pressure of pure A at $300$ K is $105$ mm Hg. Assume that A and B behave as ideal gases in the vapour phase.
Given:
The gas constant $R = 0.08$ L atm K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$
Molar mass of A is $50$ g mol$^{-1}$
Molar mass of B is $57$ g mol$^{-1}$
Density of liquid B at $300$ K is $0.5$ g/mL
$1$ atm $= 760$ mm Hg

<strong>Question:</strong> The mole fraction of B in vapour phase which is in equilibrium with this solution is ____.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **0.16**

**Detailed Solution:**

Molality of B in A is $5$ m, which means $5$ moles of B are dissolved in $1$ kg ($1000$ g) of solvent A.

Number of moles of A, $n_A = \dfrac{1000}{50} = 20$ mol

Mole fraction of A in the liquid phase, $x_A = \dfrac{n_A}{n_A + n_B} = \dfrac{20}{20 + 5} = 0.8$

Mole fraction of B in the liquid phase, $x_B = 1 - 0.8 = 0.2$

According to Raoult's law, the partial vapour pressure of A is:

$P_A = P_A^0 x_A = 105 \times 0.8 = 84$ mm Hg

Given total vapour pressure, $P_T = 100$ mm Hg

Partial vapour pressure of B, $P_B = P_T - P_A = 100 - 84 = 16$ mm Hg

Mole fraction of B in the vapour phase, $y_B = \dfrac{P_B}{P_T} = \dfrac{16}{100} = 0.16$

Answer: $0.16$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

<strong>Passage:</strong> Two volatile liquids A and B form an ideal solution. Consider a $5$ molal solution of B in A inside a closed container having a total vapour pressure of $100$ mm Hg at $300$ K. The vapour pressure of pure A at $300$ K is $105$ mm Hg. Assume that A and B behave as ideal gases in the vapour phase.
Given:
The gas constant $R = 0.08$ L atm K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$
Molar mass of A is $50$ g mol$^{-1}$
Molar mass of B is $57$ g mol$^{-1}$
Density of liquid B at $300$ K is $0.5$ g/mL
$1$ atm $= 760$ mm Hg

<strong>Question:</strong> At $300$ K, the ratio of the molar volume of pure B in vapour phase to its molar volume in liquid phase is ____.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **2000**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a $5$ molal solution of B in A, there are $5$ moles of B in $1000$ g of A.

Moles of A $= \dfrac{1000}{50} = 20$ mol.

Mole fraction of B, $x_B = \dfrac{5}{20 + 5} = 0.2$

Mole fraction of A, $x_A = 1 - 0.2 = 0.8$

Using Raoult's law for the total vapour pressure:

$P_T = P_A^\circ x_A + P_B^\circ x_B$

$100 = 105 \times 0.8 + P_B^\circ \times 0.2$

$100 = 84 + 0.2 P_B^\circ$

$0.2 P_B^\circ = 16 \Rightarrow P_B^\circ = 80$ mm Hg

The molar volume of pure B in the liquid phase ($V_{m,l}$) is:

$V_{m,l} = \dfrac{\text{Molar mass of B}}{\text{Density of liquid B}} = \dfrac{57}{0.5} = 114$ mL/mol $= 0.114$ L/mol

Assuming pure B behaves as an ideal gas in the vapour phase, its molar volume ($V_{m,v}$) at its vapour pressure $P_B^\circ$ is:

$V_{m,v} = \dfrac{RT}{P_B^\circ} = \dfrac{0.08 \times 300}{\left(\dfrac{80}{760}\right)} = \dfrac{24 \times 760}{80} = 228$ L/mol

The ratio of the molar volume of pure B in the vapour phase to its molar volume in the liquid phase is:

$\text{Ratio} = \dfrac{V_{m,v}}{V_{m,l}} = \dfrac{228}{0.114} = 2000$

Answer: $2000$

</details>
