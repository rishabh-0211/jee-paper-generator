### Q1. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

In a solvent $\textbf{S}$, a compound $\textbf{B}$ is partially dissociated into $\textbf{C}$ and $\textbf{D}$ as given below:
$\textbf{B} \rightleftharpoons 2\textbf{C} + 2\textbf{D}$
$\textbf{B}$, $\textbf{C}$ and $\textbf{D}$ are non-volatile in nature. The molar mass of $\textbf{B}$ is $10$ times the molar mass of $\textbf{S}$. The standard boiling point and the standard enthalpy of vaporization of $\textbf{S}$ are $400$ K and $10R$ J mol$^{-1}$, respectively ($R$ is the gas constant in J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$). A solution of $\textbf{B}$ in $\textbf{S}$ with an initial concentration of $\textbf{B}$ as $0.25\%$ (mass/mass) has a boiling point of $408$ K at $1$ bar pressure. In this solution, the mole percent of $\textbf{B}$ that has been dissociated is ____.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **33.33**

**Detailed Solution:**

The elevation in boiling point is given by $\Delta T_b = i K_b m$.

The ebullioscopic constant $K_b$ is calculated as:
$K_b = \dfrac{R (T_b^\circ)^2 M_S}{1000 \Delta H_{vap}}$

Substituting the given values ($T_b^\circ = 400$ K, $\Delta H_{vap} = 10R$ J mol$^{-1}$):
$K_b = \dfrac{R (400)^2 M_S}{1000 (10R)} = 16 M_S$

For a dilute solution with $0.25\%$ (mass/mass) concentration, the mass of solute $w_B = 0.25$ g and the mass of solvent $w_S \approx 100$ g.
The molality $m$ is:
$m = \dfrac{w_B \times 1000}{M_B \times w_S} = \dfrac{0.25 \times 1000}{10 M_S \times 100} = \dfrac{0.25}{M_S}$

Given $\Delta T_b = 408 - 400 = 8$ K, we substitute into the boiling point elevation formula:
$8 = i \times (16 M_S) \times \left(\dfrac{0.25}{M_S}\right)$
$8 = 4i \implies i = 2$

The dissociation reaction is $\textbf{B} \rightleftharpoons 2\textbf{C} + 2\textbf{D}$.
The van't Hoff factor $i$ is related to the degree of dissociation $\alpha$ by:
$i = 1 + (n - 1)\alpha$
Here, $n = 4$ (since 1 molecule of $\textbf{B}$ yields 4 particles).
$2 = 1 + (4 - 1)\alpha$
$3\alpha = 1 \implies \alpha = \dfrac{1}{3}$

The mole percent of $\textbf{B}$ that has been dissociated is $\alpha \times 100 = 33.33\%$.

Answer: $33.33$

</details>

**Detailed Solution:**

\(\left(\Delta \mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{b}}\right)_{\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{x}} \frac{\mathrm{w}_2}{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{x}}} \cdot \frac{1}{\mathrm{w}_1} \times 1000 \times \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{b}}\)
\(\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{X}}=\) Molar mass of ' X '
Vessel-II
\(\left(\Delta \mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{b}}\right)_{\mathrm{II}}=\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{Y}} \frac{\mathrm{w}_2}{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Y}}} \cdot \frac{1}{\mathrm{w}_1} \times 1000 \times \mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{b}}\)
\(M_Y=\) Molar mass of ' \(Y\) '
\(\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\left(\Delta T_b\right)_{\mathrm{I}}}{\left(\Delta \mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{b}}\right)_{\mathrm{II}}} \times 100=\frac{\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{x}}}{\mathrm{i}_{\mathrm{Y}}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{Y}}}{\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{x}}} \times 100 \\
& =1.2 \times \frac{100}{80} \times 100 \\
& =150 \%
\end{aligned}\)

### Q12. JEE Advanced 2012 (Paper 2)

- **(C)** 736
- **(D)** 718

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 724**

**Detailed Solution:**

From Raoult's law,

$\frac{p^{\circ}-p}{p^{\circ}}=\frac{\text { No. of moles of solute }}{\text { No. of moles of solvent }+\text { No. of moles of solute }}$

When the concentration of solute is much lower than the concentration of solvent,

$\begin{array}{l}

\frac{p^{\circ}-p}{p^{\circ}}=\frac{\text { No. of moles of solute }}{\text { No. of moles of solvent }} \\

\frac{760-p}{760}=\frac{2.5 / m}{100 / 18}...(i)

\end{array}$

From elevation in boiling point, $\Delta T_{b}=K_{b} \times m$

$\begin{array}{l}

2=0.76 \times m \\

m=\frac{2}{0.76}...(ii)

\end{array}$

From(i) and (ii), $p=724 \mathrm{~mm}$

### Q18. JEE Advanced 2006

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

$75.2 \mathrm{~g}$ of $\mathrm{C}_6 \mathrm{H}_5 \mathrm{OH}$ (phenol) is dissolved in a solvent of $K_f=14$. If the depression in freezing point is $7 \mathrm{~K}$, then find the $\%$ of phenol that dimerises.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **35**

**Detailed Solution:**

Phenol is dimerised as follows
At $t=0$
$$
2 \mathrm{C}_6 \mathrm{H}_5 \mathrm{OH} \rightleftharpoons\left(\mathrm{C}_6 \mathrm{H}_5 \mathrm{OH}\right)_2
$$
At equilibrium
$$
(1-\alpha) \quad \alpha / 2
$$
Total moles after dimerisation $=1-\alpha+\alpha / 2=1-\alpha / 2$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text { Van't Hoff factor (i) } & =\frac{\text { Normal molecular wt. of phenol }}{\text { Abnormal molecular wt. of phenol }}=\frac{94}{m_{a b}} \\
m_{a b} & =\frac{1000 \times K_f \times w}{\Delta T_f \times W}
\end{aligned}
$$
Given that
$$
\begin{aligned}
& K_f=14 \\
& w=72.5 \mathrm{gm} \\
& W=1000 \mathrm{gm} \\
& \therefore \quad m_{a b}=\frac{1000 \times 14 \times 72.5}{7 \times 1000}=145 \\
&
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\therefore \quad i=\frac{94}{145}
$$

$$
\text { (i) }=\frac{\text { No. of particles after dimerisation }}{\text { No. of particles before dimerisation }}=\frac{1-\alpha / 2}{1}
$$
From eq. (i) and (ii)
$$
\begin{aligned}
1-\alpha / 2 & =\frac{94}{145}=0.65 \\
\frac{\alpha}{2} & =1-0.65=0.35
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, $35 \%$ phenol is present in dimeric form.

</details>
