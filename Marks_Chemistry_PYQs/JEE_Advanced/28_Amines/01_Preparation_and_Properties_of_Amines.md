### Q1. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

<strong>Passage:</strong> Consider the following reaction sequence in which $\textbf{J}$, $\textbf{K}$, $\textbf{L}$ and $\textbf{M}$ are the major products.
<img style="width: 600px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/jee_adv/2026/watermarked_images/p2_english__q53_d1.png" />
Given:
Atomic mass (in amu): H : $1$, C : $12$, N : $14$, O : $16$, S : $32$, Br : $80$, Ba : $137$

<strong>Question:</strong> The volume of $1$ M aqueous $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ required to completely neutralize the ammonia evolved from $5.72$ g of $\textbf{L}$ in Kjeldahl's method of nitrogen estimation is ____ mL.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **10**

**Detailed Solution:**

The reaction sequence proceeds as follows:

1. Friedel-Crafts Acylation: Reaction of $m$-xylene (1,3-dimethylbenzene) with chloroacetyl chloride ($\text{ClCH}_2\text{COCl}$) in the presence of anhydrous $\text{AlCl}_3$ occurs at the less sterically hindered activated position (position 4) to give 1-(2,4-dimethylphenyl)-2-chloroethan-1-one.

2. Finkelstein Reaction: Heating with $\text{NaI}$ replaces the chloride with iodide, yielding 1-(2,4-dimethylphenyl)-2-iodoethan-1-one.

3. Williamson Ether Synthesis: Reaction with sodium 3-nitrophenoxide gives compound $\textbf{J}$, which is 1-(2,4-dimethylphenyl)-2-(3-nitrophenoxy)ethan-1-one.

4. Reduction and Bromination: $\text{NaBH}_4$ reduces the ketone group in $\textbf{J}$ to a secondary alcohol without affecting the nitro group. Subsequent reaction with $\text{PBr}_3$ converts the alcohol to a bromide, yielding compound $\textbf{K}$, 1-(1-bromo-2-(3-nitrophenoxy)ethyl)-2,4-dimethylbenzene.
The molecular formula of $\textbf{K}$ is $\text{C}_{16}\text{H}_{16}\text{BrNO}_3$.
Molar mass of $\textbf{K} = (16 \times 12) + (16 \times 1) + 80 + 14 + (3 \times 16) = 192 + 16 + 80 + 14 + 48 = 350 \text{ g/mol}$, which matches the given data.

5. Amination: Reaction of $\textbf{K}$ with excess $\text{NH}_3$ results in nucleophilic substitution of the bromide to form a primary amine, compound $\textbf{L}$, 1-(2,4-dimethylphenyl)-2-(3-nitrophenoxy)ethanamine.
The molecular formula of $\textbf{L}$ is $\text{C}_{16}\text{H}_{18}\text{N}_2\text{O}_3$.
Molar mass of $\textbf{L} = (16 \times 12) + (18 \times 1) + (2 \times 14) + (3 \times 16) = 192 + 18 + 28 + 48 = 286 \text{ g/mol}$.

Moles of $\textbf{L}$ taken = $\dfrac{5.72 \text{ g}}{286 \text{ g/mol}} = 0.02 \text{ mol}$.

In Kjeldahl's method, nitrogen present in nitro ($-\text{NO}_2$) groups is not converted to ammonium sulfate and thus is not estimated. Only the nitrogen from the primary amine ($-\text{NH}_2$) group will evolve as ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$).
Therefore, 1 mole of $\textbf{L}$ yields 1 mole of $\text{NH}_3$.
Moles of $\text{NH}_3$ evolved = $0.02 \text{ mol}$.

The neutralization reaction with sulfuric acid is:
$2\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightarrow (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$

Moles of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ required = $\dfrac{0.02}{2} = 0.01 \text{ mol}$.

Given the molarity of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is $1 \text{ M}$:
Volume of $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 = \dfrac{\text{Moles}}{\text{Molarity}} = \dfrac{0.01 \text{ mol}}{1 \text{ mol/L}} = 0.01 \text{ L} = 10 \text{ mL}$.

Answer: $10$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `multipleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

In the following reaction sequence, $\textbf{P}$, $\textbf{Q}$, $\textbf{S}$ and $\textbf{T}$ are the major products.
<img style="width: 600px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/jee_adv/2026/watermarked_images/p2_english__q44_d1.png" />
The correct statement(s) about $\textbf{P}$, $\textbf{Q}$, $\textbf{S}$ and $\textbf{T}$ is(are)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\textbf{Q}$ on treatment with ethanol generates an aromatic aldehyde.
- **(B)** $\textbf{S}$ gives positive phthalein dye test.
- **(C)** $\textbf{P}$ is a dinitro compound.
- **(D)** $\textbf{T}$ is a coloured compound.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\textbf{T}$ is a coloured compound.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In the first reaction sequence:
Step 1: Aniline reacts with acetic anhydride in the presence of pyridine to undergo acetylation, forming acetanilide.
Step 2: Acetanilide is treated with a nitrating mixture (conc. $\text{HNO}_3$ and conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) at $288 \text{ K}$. The $-\text{NHCOCH}_3$ group is moderately activating and ortho/para directing. Due to steric hindrance, the major product $\textbf{P}$ is $p$-nitroacetanilide.
Step 3: $\textbf{P}$ is hydrolyzed with $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ to yield $p$-nitroaniline.
Step 4: $p$-nitroaniline is diazotized using $\text{NaNO}_2/\text{HCl}$ at $273-278 \text{ K}$ to form the diazonium salt $\textbf{Q}$, which is $p$-nitrobenzenediazonium chloride.

In the second reaction sequence:
Step 1: Cumene (isopropylbenzene) is oxidized by $\text{O}_2$ followed by acid hydrolysis ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$) to produce phenol and acetone. The major aromatic product is phenol.
Step 2: Phenol undergoes the Kolbe-Schmitt reaction with $\text{NaOH}$ and $\text{CO}_2$ to form sodium salicylate.
Step 3: Acidification with $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ yields salicylic acid as the major product $\textbf{S}$.

In the final reaction:
Salicylic acid ($\textbf{S}$) reacts with $p$-nitrobenzenediazonium chloride ($\textbf{Q}$) in an aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ medium. This is an electrophilic aromatic substitution (azo coupling). The phenoxide ion formed is highly activating, and coupling occurs at the para position with respect to the $-\text{OH}$ group (position 5 of salicylic acid). The resulting product $\textbf{T}$ is an azo dye, which is a highly conjugated and coloured compound.

Evaluating the options:
(A) Treatment of $\textbf{Q}$ ($p$-nitrobenzenediazonium chloride) with ethanol reduces the diazonium group, yielding nitrobenzene, not an aromatic aldehyde. (Incorrect)
(B) The phthalein dye test is a characteristic test for phenols. Salicylic acid ($\textbf{S}$) contains a phenolic $-\text{OH}$ group with a free para position (position 5) and gives a positive phthalein dye test when heated with phthalic anhydride and conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. (Correct)
(C) $\textbf{P}$ is $p$-nitroacetanilide, which is a mononitro compound, not a dinitro compound. (Incorrect)
(D) $\textbf{T}$ is an azo dye, which is a coloured compound. (Correct)

Answer: $\textbf{S}$ gives positive phthalein dye test.; $\textbf{T}$ is a coloured compound.

- **(B)** P $\rightarrow$ 1; Q $\longrightarrow$ 2; R $\rightarrow$ 4; S $\longrightarrow$ 5
- **(C)** P $\rightarrow$ 1; Q $\longrightarrow$ 2; R $\rightarrow$ 3; S $\longrightarrow$ 4
- **(D)** P $\rightarrow$ 2; Q $\longrightarrow$ 1; R $\rightarrow$ 3; S $\longrightarrow$ 5

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) P $\rightarrow$ 1; Q $\longrightarrow$ 2; R $\rightarrow$ 4; S $\longrightarrow$ 5**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the reaction in (P), 2-bromo-5-nitrobenzaldehyde oxime reacts with aqueous NaOH. The strong base deprotonates the oxime group. The resulting oximate anion undergoes intramolecular nucleophilic aromatic substitution ($S_NAr$), attacking the ortho-carbon and displacing the bromide ion to form a 1,2-benzisoxazole intermediate. Since it is derived from an aldoxime, this intermediate has a hydrogen atom at the C3 position. In the presence of NaOH, it undergoes base-catalyzed Kemp elimination (ring opening) to form 2-hydroxy-5-nitrobenzonitrile, which corresponds to structure (1). Thus, P $\rightarrow$ 1.

In reaction (Q), the aldoxime is treated with acetic anhydride, which acetylates the oxime group to form an O-acetate. Subsequent treatment with the mild base $Na_2CO_3$ causes the elimination of acetic acid, converting the aldoxime acetate into a nitrile. The bromide is not displaced under these mild conditions. The product is 2-bromo-5-nitrobenzonitrile, which corresponds to structure (2). Thus, Q $\rightarrow$ 2.

For the reaction in (R), 2-bromo-5-nitroacetophenone oxime reacts with aqueous NaOH. Similar to (P), the oximate anion is formed and displaces the ortho-bromide via $S_NAr$ to form a 1,2-benzisoxazole ring. However, because it is derived from a ketoxime, there is a methyl group at the C3 position instead of a hydrogen. Therefore, Kemp elimination cannot occur, and the reaction stops at the stable 3-methyl-5-nitro-1,2-benzisoxazole, which corresponds to structure (4). Thus, R $\rightarrow$ 4.

In reaction (S), the O-acetate of the ketoxime is treated with aqueous $Na_2CO_3$. Since it is a ketoxime derivative, it cannot undergo elimination to form a nitrile. The mild base simply hydrolyzes the acetate group back to the oxime. The base is not strong enough to promote the subsequent $S_NAr$ ring closure. The product is 2-bromo-5-nitroacetophenone oxime, which corresponds to structure (5). Thus, S $\rightarrow$ 5.

Combining these results gives the sequence P $\rightarrow$ 1, Q $\rightarrow$ 2, R $\rightarrow$ 4, S $\rightarrow$ 5.

Answer: P $\rightarrow$ 1; Q $\longrightarrow$ 2; R $\rightarrow$ 4; S $\longrightarrow$ 5

</details>
