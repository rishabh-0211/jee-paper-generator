### Q1. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

$\text{X}^{a+}$ and $\text{Y}^{b+}$ are hydrogen-like species. The wavelength of light absorbed during the transition between the states with principal quantum numbers $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ of $\text{X}^{a+}$ is $\lambda$. The wavelength of light absorbed during the transition between the states with principal quantum numbers $n = 2$ and $n = 4$ of $\text{Y}^{b+}$ is $9\lambda$. The lowest possible value of $(a + b)$ is ____.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **3**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a hydrogen-like species, the wavelength of the absorbed light during a transition from $n_1$ to $n_2$ is given by the Rydberg formula:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R Z^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$

For $\text{X}^{a+}$, the transition is from $n = 1$ to $n = 2$:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R Z_X^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{1^2} - \dfrac{1}{2^2} \right) = \dfrac{3}{4} R Z_X^2 \Rightarrow \lambda = \dfrac{4}{3 R Z_X^2}$

For $\text{Y}^{b+}$, the transition is from $n = 2$ to $n = 4$:

$\dfrac{1}{9\lambda} = R Z_Y^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2} \right) = R Z_Y^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{16} \right) = \dfrac{3}{16} R Z_Y^2 \Rightarrow 9\lambda = \dfrac{16}{3 R Z_Y^2}$

Substituting the expression for $\lambda$ from the first equation into the second:

$9 \left( \dfrac{4}{3 R Z_X^2} \right) = \dfrac{16}{3 R Z_Y^2}$

$\dfrac{12}{Z_X^2} = \dfrac{16}{3 Z_Y^2}$

$36 Z_Y^2 = 16 Z_X^2$

$9 Z_Y^2 = 4 Z_X^2 \Rightarrow 3 Z_Y = 2 Z_X$

$\dfrac{Z_X}{Z_Y} = \dfrac{3}{2}$

For the lowest possible integer values of atomic numbers, we get $Z_X = 3$ and $Z_Y = 2$.

Since $\text{X}^{a+}$ and $\text{Y}^{b+}$ are hydrogen-like species, they must contain exactly $1$ electron. Therefore, the charge on the ion is $(Z - 1)$.

For $\text{X}^{a+}$ ($Z_X = 3$): $a = 3 - 1 = 2$

For $\text{Y}^{b+}$ ($Z_Y = 2$): $b = 2 - 1 = 1$

The lowest possible value of $(a + b)$ is $2 + 1 = 3$.

Answer: $3$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 1)

*Type:* `multipleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The 2s and the 2p orbital energies of hydrogen atom are $E_{2s}(\text{H})$ and $E_{2p}(\text{H})$, respectively. The 2s and the 2p orbital energies of lithium atom are $E_{2s}(\text{Li})$ and $E_{2p}(\text{Li})$, respectively. The correct option(s) about the orbital energies is(are)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $E_{2s}(\text{Li}) < E_{2p}(\text{Li})$
- **(B)** $E_{2s}(\text{H}) = E_{2p}(\text{H})$
- **(C)** $E_{2p}(\text{H}) < E_{2s}(\text{Li})$
- **(D)** $E_{2s}(\text{H}) > E_{2s}(\text{Li})$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $E_{2s}(\text{H}) > E_{2s}(\text{Li})$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For hydrogen atom (a single-electron system), the energy of an orbital depends only on the principal quantum number $n$. Thus, the 2s and 2p orbitals are degenerate.

$E_{2s}(\text{H}) = E_{2p}(\text{H})$

For lithium atom (a multi-electron system), the energy depends on both $n$ and $l$. Due to the penetration effect, the 2s electron penetrates closer to the nucleus and experiences a higher effective nuclear charge than the 2p electron. Thus, the 2s orbital has lower energy than the 2p orbital.

$E_{2s}(\text{Li}) < E_{2p}(\text{Li})$

The energy of an orbital is given by $E \propto -\dfrac{Z_{\text{eff}}^2}{n^2}$.

For hydrogen, $Z_{\text{eff}} = 1$. For the 2s electron in lithium, the nuclear charge is $Z=3$, and it is shielded by the $1s^2$ electrons. The effective nuclear charge $Z_{\text{eff}}$ for the 2s electron in Li is greater than 1 (approximately 1.3).

Since $Z_{\text{eff}}(\text{Li}, 2s) > Z_{\text{eff}}(\text{H}, 2s)$, the energy of the 2s orbital in Li is more negative than that in H.

$E_{2s}(\text{Li}) < E_{2s}(\text{H}) \Rightarrow E_{2s}(\text{H}) > E_{2s}(\text{Li})$

Since $E_{2p}(\text{H}) = E_{2s}(\text{H})$, we also have $E_{2p}(\text{H}) > E_{2s}(\text{Li})$.

Answer: $E_{2s}(\text{Li}) < E_{2p}(\text{Li})$; $E_{2s}(\text{H}) = E_{2p}(\text{H})$; $E_{2s}(\text{H}) > E_{2s}(\text{Li})$

</details>

### Q10. JEE Advanced 2012 (Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The kinetic energy of an electron in the second Bohr orbit of a hydrogen atom is $\left[a_{0}\right.$ is Bohr radius $]$ :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{h^{2}}{4 \pi^{2} m a_{0}^{2}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{h^{2}}{16 \pi^{2} m a_{0}^{2}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{h^{2}}{32 \pi^{2} m a_{0}^{2}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{h^{2}}{64 \pi^{2} m a_{0}^{2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{h^{2}}{32 \pi^{2} m a_{0}^{2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

As per Bohr's postulate,

$\begin{array}{l}

m v r=\frac{n h}{2 \pi} \quad \text { So, } v=\frac{n h}{2 \pi m r} \\

\mathrm{KE}=\frac{1}{2} m v^{2} \quad \text { So, } \mathrm{KE}=\frac{1}{2} m\left(\frac{n h}{2 \pi m r}\right)^{2}

\end{array}$

$\text { Since, } r=\frac{a_{\mathrm{o}} \times n^{2}}{z}$

So, for $2^{\text {nd }}$ Bohr orbit

$\begin{array}{l}

r=\frac{a_{\mathrm{o}} \times 2^{2}}{1}=4 a_{\mathrm{o}} \\

\mathrm{KE}=\frac{1}{2} m\left(\frac{2^{2} h^{2}}{4 \pi^{2} m^{2} \times\left(4 a_{\mathrm{o}}\right)^{2}}\right)=\frac{h^{2}}{32 \pi^{2} m a_{\mathrm{o}}^{2}}

\end{array}$

</details>
