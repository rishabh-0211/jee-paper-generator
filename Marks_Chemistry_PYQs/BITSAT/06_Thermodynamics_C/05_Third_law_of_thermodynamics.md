### Q2. BITSAT 2016

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a particular reversible reaction at temperature $T, \Delta H$ and $\Delta S$ were found to be both +ve. If $T_{e}$ is the temperature at equilibrium, the reaction would be spontaneous when

**Options:**

- **(A)** $T_{e}>T$
- **(B)** $T>T_{e}$
- **(C)** $T_{e}$ is 5 times $T$
- **(D)** $T=T_{e}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $T>T_{e}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

At equilibrium $\Delta G=0$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\text { Hence, } \Delta G=\Delta H-T_{e} \Delta S=0 \\

\therefore \Delta H=T_{e} \Delta S \text { or } T_{e}=\frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}

\end{array}

$$

For a spontaneous reaction $\Delta G$ must be negative which is possible only if $\Delta H-T \Delta S<0$

$\therefore \Delta H<T \triangle S$

or $T>\frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S} ; T_{e}<T$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which law of the thermodynamics helps in calculating the absolute entropies of various substances at different temperatures?

**Options:**

- **(A)** First law
- **(B)** Second law
- **(C)** Third law
- **(D)** Zeroth law

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Third law**

**Detailed Solution:**

The third law helps to calculate the absolute entropies of pure substances at different temperature. The entropy of the substance at different temperature. $\mathrm{T}$ may be calculated by the measurement of heat capacity change

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{T}}-\mathrm{S}_{0}=\Delta \mathrm{S}=\int_{0}^{\mathrm{T}} \frac{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{p}} \mathrm{dt}}{\mathrm{T}} \\

\text { Where } \mathrm{S}_{\mathrm{T}}=\text { Entropy at } \mathrm{T} \mathrm{K}

\end{array}

$$

$\mathrm{S}_{0}=$ Entropy at $0 \mathrm{~K}$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{p}} \cdot \log _{e} \mathrm{~T} \\

=2.303 \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{p}} \mathrm{log} \mathrm{T}

\end{array}

$$

</details>
