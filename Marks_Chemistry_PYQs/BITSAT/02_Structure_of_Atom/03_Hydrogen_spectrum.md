### Q1. BITSAT 2021

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The frequency of radiation emitted when the electron falls from $\mathrm{n}=4$ to $\mathrm{n}=1$ in a hydrogen atom will be (Given : ionization energy of $\mathrm{H}=2.18 \times 10^{-18} \mathrm{~J}$ atom ${ }^{-1}$ and $\mathrm{h}=6.625 \times 10^{-34} \mathrm{Js}$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.54 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $1.03 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $3.08 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $2.00 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3.08 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{v} &=\frac{1}{\mathrm{~h}} \times \mathrm{IE} \times\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{n}_{1}{ }^{2}}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{n}_{2}{ }^{2}}\right] \\ &=\frac{2.18 \times 10^{-18}}{6.625 \times 10^{-34}} \times\left[\frac{1}{1}-\frac{1}{16}\right]=3.08 \times 10^{15} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} \end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2020

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What is the maximum wavelength line in the Lyman series of $\mathrm{He}^{+}$ ion?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 R$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{3 R}$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{1}{3 R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\frac{1}{\lambda}=\mathrm{RZ}^{2}\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{n}_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{n}_{2}^{2}}\right]=\mathrm{R} \times 2^{2}\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{l}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2^{2}}\right]$

$\Rightarrow 3 \mathrm{R} ; \lambda=\frac{1}{3 \mathrm{R}}$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2016

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The first emission line in the atomic spectrum of hydrogen in the Balmer series appears at

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{9 R}{400} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $\frac{7 \mathrm{R}}{144} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $\frac{3 \mathrm{R}}{4} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $\frac{5 \mathrm{R}}{36} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{5 \mathrm{R}}{36} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For Balmer $\mathrm{n}_{1}=2$ and $\mathrm{n}_{2}=3$; $v=R\left(\frac{1}{2^{2}}-\frac{1}{3^{2}}\right)=\frac{5 R}{36} \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The wave number of the limiting line in Lyman series of hydrogen is $109678 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$. The wave number of the limiting line in Balmer series of $\mathrm{He}^{+}$ would be :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $54839 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $109678 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $219356 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $438712 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $109678 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{H}}=109678 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}$

Wave number of the limiting line in Balmer series of $\mathrm{He}^{+}$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

=\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{H}} \cdot \mathrm{Z}^{2}\left[\frac{1}{\mathrm{n}_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{n}_{2}^{2}}\right] \\

=109678 \times(2)^{2}\left[\frac{1}{[2)^{2}}-\frac{1}{\infty}\right] \\

=109678 \mathrm{~cm}^{-1}

\end{array}

$$

</details>
