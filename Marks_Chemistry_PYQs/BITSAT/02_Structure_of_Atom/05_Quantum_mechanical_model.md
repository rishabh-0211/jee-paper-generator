### Q1. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Observe the given boundary surface diagrams of two orbitals I and II and choose the correct option.
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/GYKz3EmQr_n_fr0MOL35Bk2XhGlRzEASRkVOxdpcEBs.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{I}-d_{x^2-y^2}, \mathrm{II}-d_{y z}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{I}-d_{y z}, \mathrm{II}-d_{x^2-y^2}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{I}-d_{x z}, \mathrm{II}-d_{z^2}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{I}-d_{x y}, \mathrm{II}-d_{x z}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{I}-d_{y z}, \mathrm{II}-d_{x^2-y^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In boundary surface diagram (1) the four lobes lie between $y$ and $z$-axis $\left(d_{y z}\right)$ whereas, in boundary surface diagram (2) the four lobes lie on the $x$ and $y$-axis $\left(d_{x^2-y^2}\right)$.

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The probability density plots of $1 s$ and $2 s$ orbitals are given in figure.
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/KW6UlqtmYx8qU0E2Ze00YtyIq-_2FHH_3i0-1RRE1OQ.original.fullsize.png">

The density of dots in a region represents the probability density of finding electrons in the region.
On the basis of above diagram which of the following statements is incorrect?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 s$ and $2 s$ orbitals are spherical in shape.
- **(B)** The probability of finding the electron is maximum near the nucleus.
- **(C)** The probability of finding the electron at a given distance is equal in all directions.
- **(D)** The probability density of electrons for $2 s$ orbital decreases uniformly as distance from the nucleus increases.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) The probability density of electrons for $2 s$ orbital decreases uniformly as distance from the nucleus increases.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The probability density of electrons in $2 s$ orbital first increases then decreases and after that it increases again as distance increases from nucleus.

### Q4. BITSAT 2016

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

An e $^{-}$ has magnetic quantum number as $-3,$ what is its principal quantum number?

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1
- **(B)** 2
- **(C)** 3
- **(D)** 4

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 4**

**Detailed Solution:**

When $m=-3, l=3, \therefore \mathrm{n}=4$.

</details>
