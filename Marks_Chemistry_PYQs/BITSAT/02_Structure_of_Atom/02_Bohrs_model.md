### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The degeneracy of hydrogen atom that has energy equal to $\frac{-R_{\mathrm{H}}}{9}$ is (where, $R_{\mathrm{H}}=$ Rydberg constant)

**Options:**

- **(A)** 6
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** 5
- **(D)** 9

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 9**

**Detailed Solution:**

The energy is given as, $E=-\frac{R_{\mathrm{H}}}{9}...(i)$
We know that,
$\begin{aligned}
E_n & =-\frac{R_{\mathrm{H}}}{n^2} ...(ii)\\
n^2 & =9 \\
n & =3
\end{aligned}$
When $n=3, l=0,1,2$
$(3 s, 3 p, 3 d \text { sub-shells) }$
Orbitals present (i.e., degeneracy),
$3 s=1,3 p=3,3 d=5$
Total number of orbital present $=3+1+5=9$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the radius of the $3 \mathrm{rd}$ Bohr's orbit of hydrogen atom is $r_3$ and the radius of $4^{\text {th }}$ Bohr's orbit is $r_4$. Then:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $r_4=\frac{9}{16} r_3$
- **(B)** $r_4=\frac{16}{9} r_3$
- **(C)** $r_4=\frac{3}{4} r_3$
- **(D)** $r_4=\frac{4}{3} r_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $r_4=\frac{16}{9} r_3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & r=0.529 \times \frac{n^2}{Z} Å \\ & r_3=0.529 \times \frac{3^2}{1} \Rightarrow r_4=0.529 \times \frac{4^2}{1} \\ & \frac{r_4}{r_3}=\frac{4^2}{3^2}=\frac{16}{9} \Rightarrow r_4=\frac{16 r_3}{9}\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The Bohr orbit radius for the hydrogen atom $(n=1)$ is approximately $0.530 Å$. The radius for the first excited state $(n=2)$ orbit is (in $Å$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0.13
- **(B)** 1.06
- **(C)** 4.77
- **(D)** 2.12

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 2.12**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given : Radius of hydrogen atom $=0.530$
$Å$, Number of excited state $(n)=2$ and atomic number of hydrogen atom $(Z)=1$. We know that the Bohr radius
$(r)=\frac{n^2}{Z} \times \text { radius of atom }=\frac{(2)^2}{1} \times 0.530$
$=4 \times 0.530=2.12 Å$

</details>

- **(A)** 0.13

$$

$$

$=4 \times 0.530=2.12 Ã…$
