### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If the de-broglie wavelength of a particle of mass $(m)$ is 100 times its velocity. Then, its value in terms of its mass $(m)$ and plank constant $(h)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{10} \sqrt{\frac{m}{h}}$
- **(B)** $10 \sqrt{\frac{h}{m}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{10} \sqrt{\frac{h}{m}}$
- **(D)** $10 \sqrt{\frac{m}{h}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $10 \sqrt{\frac{h}{m}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the wavelength of given particle $=x$
Velocity $(v)=\frac{x}{100}$
Using wavelength formula
$\begin{aligned}
 \lambda = \frac{h}{m v} \\
\therefore &  x  =\frac{h}{m\left(\frac{x}{100}\right)} \\
x & =\frac{100 h}{m x} \\
x^2 & =100 \frac{h}{m} \\
\Rightarrow \quad x & =10 \sqrt{\frac{h}{m}}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A stream of electrons from a heated filament was passed between two charged plates kept at a potential difference $V$ volt. If 'e' and $m$ are charge and mass of an electron, respectively, then the value of $h / \lambda$ (where $\lambda$ is wavelength associated with electron wave) is given by:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{m \mathrm{e} V}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{2 m \mathrm{e} V}$
- **(C)** $m \mathrm{eV}$
- **(D)** $2 m \mathrm{e} V$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{2 m \mathrm{e} V}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

As electron of charge ' $e$ ' is passed through ' $V$ ' volt, kinetic energy of electron will be eV .
Wavelength of electron wave $(\lambda)=\frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m \cdot \mathrm{KE}}}$
$\lambda=-\frac{h}{\sqrt{2 m \mathrm{eV}}} \quad \therefore \frac{h}{\lambda}=\sqrt{2 m \mathrm{eV}}$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A photon of wavelength $3000 Ã…$ strikes a metal surface. The work function of the metal is 2.13 eV. What is the kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectron?
$\left(\mathrm{h}=6.626 \times 10^{-34} \mathrm{Js}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 4.0 eV
- **(B)** 3.0 eV
- **(C)** 2.0 eV
- **(D)** 1.0 eV

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 2.0 eV**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\lambda=3000 Ã…=3 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{~m}, \phi=2.13 \mathrm{eV}$
K.E. $=h v-h v_0=h v-\phi=\frac{h c}{\lambda}-\phi$
$=\frac{\left(6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3 \times 10^8\right)}{3 \times 10^{-7}}-\left(2.13 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\right)$
$=\frac{1.98 \times 10^{-25}}{3 \times 10^{-7}}-\left(3.408 \times 10^{-19}\right)$
$=\left(6.60 \times 10^{-19}\right)-\left(3.408 \times 10^{-19}\right)$
$=3.192 \times 10^{-19} \mathrm{~J}=2.0 \mathrm{eV}$

</details>

$\begin{aligned} \lambda &=\frac{h}{m v}=\frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 1 \times 10^{3}} \\ &=3.97 \times 10^{-10} \text { meter }=0.397 \text { nanometer } \approx 0.40 \mathrm{~nm} \end{aligned}$
