### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In the chemical reaction $A \rightarrow B$, what is the order of the reaction? Given that, the rate of reaction doubles if the concentration of $A$ is increased four times.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2$
- **(B)** $1.5$
- **(C)** $0.5$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We know, $r_1=k[A]^{\mathrm{n}}$    ....(i)
If conc. of $A$ is increased four times rate of reaction on doubles.
$2 r_1=k[4 A]^{\mathrm{n}}$     ....(ii) 
Divide Eq. (ii) by (i).
$\frac{2 r_i}{r_1}=\frac{k(4 A)^n}{k(A)^n}$
$\Rightarrow \quad 2=(4)^{\mathrm{n}} \Rightarrow 2 \mathrm{n}=(2)^{2 \mathrm{n}} \Rightarrow n=0.5$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In the reaction, $\mathrm{A} \rightarrow$ products, If the concentration of the reactant is doubled rate of the reaction remains unchanged. The order of the reaction with respect to A is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $0.5$
- **(D)** $0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$r_1=k[A]^n$ and $r_2=k[2 A]^n$
$\because \quad \mathrm{r}_1=\mathrm{r}_2 \quad \therefore \mathrm{k}[\mathrm{A}]^{\mathrm{n}}=\mathrm{k}(2[\mathrm{~A}])^{\mathrm{n}}$
or, $\quad 2^n=1$
or, $2^{\mathrm{n}}=2^{\circ} \Rightarrow \mathrm{n}=0$
It is a zero order reaction.

</details>
