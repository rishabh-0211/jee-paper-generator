### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The total number of aromatic compounds/species from the following is
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/08APRS2__q64_d1.jpg" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6$
- **(B)** $4$
- **(C)** $3$
- **(D)** $5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

A species is aromatic if it is cyclic, planar, fully conjugated (continuous cyclic overlap of $p$-orbitals) and contains $(4n + 2)\,\pi$ electrons (Huckel's rule). Let us analyse each species.

$1,4$-Benzoquinone:
The six-membered ring has only $4\pi$ electrons (from the two $C=C$ bonds); the two $C=O$ groups are cross-conjugated and do not contribute to a continuous $(4n + 2)\,\pi$ ring system. Hence, it is non-aromatic.

Tropylium cation:
A planar, cyclic, fully conjugated seven-membered system with three $C=C$ bonds and a positive charge (empty $p$-orbital). It has $6\,\pi$ electrons, satisfying Huckel's rule with $n = 1$. It is aromatic.

Phenanthrene:
A fused tricyclic, planar, fully conjugated hydrocarbon with $14\,\pi$ electrons. It obeys Huckel's rule with $n = 3$. It is aromatic.

$1,2$-Dihydronaphthalene:
It contains an intact benzene ring fused to a partially saturated six-membered ring. Because of the benzene ring, the molecule as a whole is classified as aromatic.

Cyclobutadiene dication:
A planar, four-membered ring with one $C=C$ bond and two positive charges (two empty $p$-orbitals), giving $2\,\pi$ electrons. It satisfies Huckel's rule with $n = 0$. It is aromatic.

Cyclohexadienyl anion (as drawn with a $-CH_2-$ group):
The $sp^3$ $-CH_2-$ carbon breaks the continuous cyclic conjugation, so the ring is not fully conjugated. Hence, it is non-aromatic.

Counting the aromatic species: tropylium cation, phenanthrene, $1,2$-dihydronaphthalene and cyclobutadiene dication give a total of $4$ aromatic species.

Hence, the correct option is $(2)\ 4$.

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

Shown below is the structure of methyl acetate with three different $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ carbon - oxygen bonds.
<img style="width: 200px" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/06APRS2__q63_d1.jpg" />
The correct order of bond lengths of these bonds is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\alpha > \beta > \gamma$
- **(B)** $\alpha < \beta < \gamma$
- **(C)** $\alpha = \beta = \gamma$
- **(D)** $\alpha < \beta = \gamma$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\alpha < \beta < \gamma$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In methyl acetate, the $\alpha$ bond is a $C=O$ double bond, which is the shortest among the three.

The $\beta$ bond is a $C-O$ single bond adjacent to a carbonyl group. Due to resonance ($CH_3-C(=O)-O-CH_3 \leftrightarrow CH_3-C(-O^-)=O^+-CH_3$), the $\beta$ bond acquires partial double bond character, making it shorter than a pure single bond.

The $\gamma$ bond is a pure $C-O$ single bond between an $sp^3$ hybridized carbon and oxygen, with no resonance involvement. Thus, it is the longest bond.

Therefore, the correct order of bond lengths is $\alpha < \beta < \gamma$.

Answer: $\alpha < \beta < \gamma$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

Consider the following molecules/species:
<img style="width:550px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/04APRS2__q61_d1.jpg" />
$(x)$ tropone, $(y)$ acetone, $(z)$ acetate ion
The correct order of carbon – oxygen double bond length is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x>y>z$
- **(B)** $y>z>x$
- **(C)** $z>x>y$
- **(D)** $x>z>y$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $z>x>y$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In acetone $(y)$, the carbon-oxygen bond is a localized double bond with a bond order of approximately $2$.

In the acetate ion $(z)$, there are two equivalent resonance structures that delocalize the negative charge equally over both oxygen atoms. This results in a carbon-oxygen bond order of exactly $1.5$.

In tropone $(x)$, the carbon-oxygen double bond is conjugated with the $\pi$ system of the seven-membered ring. Polarization of the $C=O$ bond leads to a resonance structure with a single $C-O^{-}$ bond and a positive charge on the ring, forming an aromatic tropylium cation. This aromatic stabilization gives the $C=O$ bond significant single-bond character. However, because this resonance structure involves charge separation, its contribution is less than that of the neutral structure. Thus, the bond order in tropone is between $1.5$ and $2$.

Since bond length is inversely proportional to bond order, the carbon-oxygen bond length is longest for the acetate ion (bond order $1.5$), followed by tropone (bond order between $1.5$ and $2$), and shortest for acetone (bond order $2$).

Therefore, the correct order of carbon-oxygen bond length is $z > x > y$.

Answer: $z>x>y$

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/21S1_o_69_4_1.png">**

**Detailed Solution:**

To determine the stability of resonating structures, we follow these rules:
1. Structures with more covalent bonds are generally more stable.
2. Structures where all atoms have complete octets are more stable.
3. Separation of opposite charges decreases stability, while like charges on adjacent atoms highly destabilize the structure.
4. Negative charge on a more electronegative atom and positive charge on a more electropositive atom is preferred.
In structure (4), there are two positive charges ($+$) located on adjacent atoms (the carbon atom and the nitrogen atom of the nitro group).
Electrostatic repulsion between like charges on adjacent atoms makes the structure highly unstable.
In structures (1), (2), and (3), like charges are not on adjacent atoms.
Therefore, structure (4) is the least stable.
