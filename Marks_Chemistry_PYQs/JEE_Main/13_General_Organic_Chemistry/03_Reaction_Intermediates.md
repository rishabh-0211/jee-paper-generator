### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

Given below are two statements:
Statement I: In $O_2 N$-$C_6 H_4$-$CH^{\oplus}$-$C_6 H_4$-$OCH_3$, the carbocation is stabilised by $+R$ effect of $-OCH_3$ group.
Statement II: In $O_2 N$-$C_6 H_4$-$CH^{\ominus}$-$C_6 H_4$-$OCH_3$, the carbanion is stabilised by $-R$ effect of $-NO_2$ group.
In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below:

**Options:**

- **(A)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true
- **(B)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false
- **(C)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false
- **(D)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Both Statement I and Statement II are true**

**Detailed Solution:**

Statement I is true because the $-OCH_3$ group is an electron-donating group. It donates electron density through resonance ($+R$ effect), which delocalizes the positive charge of the carbocation and stabilizes it.

Statement II is true because the $-NO_2$ group is a strong electron-withdrawing group. It withdraws electron density through resonance ($-R$ effect), which delocalizes the negative charge of the carbanion and stabilizes it.

Therefore, both Statement I and Statement II are true.

Answer: Both Statement I and Statement II are true

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2024 (01 Feb Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `3`

<p>Ionic reactions with organic compounds proceed through:</p><p>(A) Homolytic bond cleavage  (B) Heterolytic bond cleavage  (C) Free radical formation</p><p>(D) Primary free radical  (E) Secondary free radical</p><p>Choose the correct answer from the options given below:</p>

**Options:**

- **(A)** (A) only
- **(B)** (C) only
- **(C)** (B) only
- **(D)** (D) and (E) only

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) (B) only**

**Detailed Solution:**

<p>Heterolytic fission, also known as heterolysis, is a type of bond fission in which a covalent bond between two chemical species is broken in an unequal manner, resulting in the bond pair of electrons being retained by one of the chemical species (while the other species does not retain any of the electrons from the bond pair). When a neutrally charged molecule undergoes heterolytic fission, one of the products will have a positive charge whereas the other product will have a negative charge.
Polar reactions occur when two bonded atoms come apart, one taking more of the shared electrons than the other. They involve heterolytic cleavage. The result is two charged species—one cation and one anion.
Hence, organic reactions which proceed through heterolytic bond cleavage are called polar reactions.</p><p>In homolytic reactions, free radicals are formed and these reaction takes place in gaseous phase.</p>

</details>
