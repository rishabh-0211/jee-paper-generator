### Q6. JEE Main 2021 (27 Jul Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

If the Thomson model of the atom was correct, then the result of Rutherford&#39;s gold foil experiment would have been:

**Options:**

- **(A)** All of the <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><mi mathvariant="normal">&#945;</mi></math>-particles pass through the gold foil without decrease in speed.
- **(B)** <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><mi mathvariant="normal">&#945;</mi></math>-particles are deflected over a wide range of angles.
- **(C)** All <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><mi mathvariant="normal">&#945;</mi></math>-particles get bounced back by <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><mn>180</mn><mo>&#176;</mo></math>.
- **(D)** <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><mi mathvariant="normal">&#945;</mi></math>-particles pass through the gold foil deflected by small angles and with reduced speed.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><mi mathvariant="normal">&#945;</mi></math>-particles pass through the gold foil deflected by small angles and with reduced speed.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Thomson&#39;s plum pudding model, protons are diffused in a positively charged cloud (charge is not centred) and the negatively charged particles are embedded in it. So, the <math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><mi mathvariant="normal">&#945;</mi></math>- particles would deviate by small angles and due to repulsion from protons, their speed would decrease.
