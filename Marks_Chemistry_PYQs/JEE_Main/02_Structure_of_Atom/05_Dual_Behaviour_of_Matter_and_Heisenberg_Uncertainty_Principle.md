### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `1`

Consider two radiations of wavelengths
1. $\lambda_1 = 2000$ Å
2. $\lambda_2 = 6000$ Å
The ratio of the energies of these two radiations $\left(\dfrac{E_1}{E_2}\right)$ is ________ (Nearest integer).

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **3**

**Detailed Solution:**

The energy of a photon is given by the relation:

$E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

For the two radiations, the energies are $E_1 = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1}$ and $E_2 = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_2}$.

The ratio of their energies is:

$\dfrac{E_1}{E_2} = \dfrac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}$

Substituting the given values $\lambda_1 = 2000$ and $\lambda_2 = 6000$:

$\dfrac{E_1}{E_2} = \dfrac{6000}{2000} = 3$

Answer: $3$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The surface of sodium metal is irradiated with radiation of wavelength $x$ nm. The kinetic energy of ejected electrons is $2.8 \times 10^{-20}$ J. The work function of sodium is $2.3$ eV. The value of $x$ is _____ $\times 10^2$ nm. (Nearest integer)
(Given: $h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J s; $1$ eV $= 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ J; $c = 3.0 \times 10^8$ m s$^{-1}$)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **5**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work function of sodium in Joules is:

$\Phi = 2.3 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 3.68 \times 10^{-19}$ J

The kinetic energy of the ejected electrons is:

$K = 2.8 \times 10^{-20} = 0.28 \times 10^{-19}$ J

Using the photoelectric equation, the energy of the incident photon is:

$E = \Phi + K$

$E = 3.68 \times 10^{-19} + 0.28 \times 10^{-19} = 3.96 \times 10^{-19}$ J

The wavelength of the incident radiation is calculated using $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$:

$\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E} = \dfrac{6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^8}{3.96 \times 10^{-19}}$

$\lambda = \dfrac{19.8 \times 10^{-26}}{3.96 \times 10^{-19}} = 5 \times 10^{-7}$ m

Converting the wavelength to nanometers:

$\lambda = 500 \times 10^{-9}$ m $= 500$ nm

Given that the wavelength is $x$ nm, we get $x = 500$.

Expressing $x$ in the required format:

$x = 5 \times 10^2$

The value to be filled in the blank is $5$.

Answer: $5$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `1`

Two positively charged particles $\mathrm{m}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{m}_{2}$ have been accelerated across the same potential difference of 200 keV as shown below. 
 <img style="width:300px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/28S2_q_74_1.png"> 
 [Given mass of $\mathrm{m}_{1}=1$ amu and $\mathrm{m}_{2}=4$ amu]
 The deBroglie wavelength of $\mathrm{m}_{1}$ will be $x$ times of $\mathrm{m}_{2}$. The value of $x$ is $\_\_\_\_$ (nearest integer)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **2**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a charged particle accelerated through potential V, the de Broglie wavelength is $\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mVe}}$.
The wavelength is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{m}$.
Therefore: $\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = \sqrt{\frac{m_2}{m_1}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{1}} = 2$.
This means $\lambda_1 = 2\lambda_2$, so x = 2 (nearest integer).

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The work functions of two metals $\left(M_{A}\right.$ and $\left.M_{B}\right)$ are in the $1: 2$ ratio. When these metals are exposed to photons of energy 6 eV, the kinetic energy of liberated electrons of $M_{A}: M_{B}$ is in the ratio of $2.642: 1$. The work functions (in eV) of $M_{A}$ and $M_{B}$ are respectively.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.4,2.8$
- **(B)** $2.3,4.6$
- **(C)** $1.5,3.0$
- **(D)** $3.1,6.2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2.3,4.6$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work functions satisfy $W_A : W_B = 1:2$.
Let $W_A = W$ and $W_B = 2W$.
Using the photoelectric equation
$KE = h\nu - W$, we have $KE_A = 6 - W$ and $KE_B = 6 - 2W$.
Given the kinetic energy ratio $\frac{KE_A}{KE_B} = 2.642$, we get $\frac{6-W}{6-2W} = 2.642$.
Solving: $6 - W = 2.642(6 - 2W)$ gives $6 - W = 15.852 - 5.284W$
So $4.284W = 9.852$ and $W = 2.3$ eV.
Thus $W_A = 2.3$ eV and $W_B = 4.6$ eV.

</details>

If the radius of first orbit of $\mathrm{H}$ atom is $a_0$, the deBroglie wavelength of an electron in the third orbit is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4 \pi a_0$
- **(B)** $8 \pi a_0$
- **(C)** $6 \pi a_0$
- **(D)** $2 \pi a_0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $6 \pi a_0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$r_n=a_0 n^2$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& r=a_0 \times(3)^2=9 a_0 \\
& m v r=\frac{n h}{2 \pi} \\
& m v=\frac{n h}{2 \pi \mathrm{r}}=\frac{3 h}{2 \pi \times 9 a_0}=\frac{h}{6 \pi a_0} \\
& \lambda=\frac{h}{m v}=\frac{h}{h} \times 6 \pi a_0=6 \pi a_0
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q57. JEE Main 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

In an atom, an electron is moving with a speed of $600 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ with an accuracy of $0.005 \%$. Certainity with which the position of the electron can be located is $\left(h=6.6 \times 10^{-34} \mathrm{~kg} \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~s}^{-1}\right.$, mass of electron, $e_m=9.1 \times 10^{-31} \mathrm{~kg}$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.52 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(B)** $5.10 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(C)** $1.92 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(D)** $3.84 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.92 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Delta \mathrm{x} . \mathrm{m} \Delta \mathrm{v}=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{4 \pi} \\
& \Delta \mathrm{x}=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{4 \pi \mathrm{m} \Delta \mathrm{v}} \\
& \Delta \mathrm{v}=600 \times \frac{0.005}{100}=0.03 \\
& \Rightarrow \Delta \mathrm{x}=\frac{6.625 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.14 \times 9.1 \times 10^{-31} \times 0.03}=1.92 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.92 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~m}$**

$$
\begin{aligned}

&=\frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.14 \times 9.1 \times 3 \times 10^{-31} \times 10^{-3}} \\
&=0.01933 \\
&=1.93 \times 10^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$
