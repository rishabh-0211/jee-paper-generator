### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The Bohr radius of a hydrogen like species is $70.53$ pm. The species and the stationary state $(n)$ are respectively (Given : Hydrogen atom Bohr radius is $52.9$ pm)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{Li}^{2+}, 3$
- **(B)** $\text{He}^+, 3$
- **(C)** $\text{He}^+, 2$
- **(D)** $\text{Li}^{2+}, 2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\text{Li}^{2+}, 2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The radius of the $n$-th Bohr orbit for a hydrogen-like species is given by the formula:

$r_n = a_0 \dfrac{n^2}{Z}$

where $a_0$ is the Bohr radius of the hydrogen atom ($52.9$ pm), $n$ is the principal quantum number, and $Z$ is the atomic number.

Given $r_n = 70.53$ pm, we can write:

$70.53 = 52.9 \times \dfrac{n^2}{Z}$

$\dfrac{n^2}{Z} = \dfrac{70.53}{52.9} = 1.333 = \dfrac{4}{3}$

Checking the given options:
For $\text{Li}^{2+}$ and $n = 3$, $Z = 3 \Rightarrow \dfrac{n^2}{Z} = \dfrac{9}{3} = 3$
For $\text{He}^+$ and $n = 3$, $Z = 2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{n^2}{Z} = \dfrac{9}{2} = 4.5$
For $\text{He}^+$ and $n = 2$, $Z = 2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{n^2}{Z} = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2$
For $\text{Li}^{2+}$ and $n = 2$, $Z = 3 \Rightarrow \dfrac{n^2}{Z} = \dfrac{4}{3}$

The ratio matches for $\text{Li}^{2+}$ and $n = 2$.

Answer: $\text{Li}^{2+}, 2$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 1)

What is the energy (in J atom$^{-1}$) required for the following process?
$Li^{2+}(g) \rightarrow Li^{3+}(g) + e^-$
(Take the ionization energy for the H atom in the ground state as $2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J atom$^{-1}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8.72 \times 10^{-18}$
- **(B)** $1.962 \times 10^{-18}$
- **(C)** $1.962 \times 10^{-17}$
- **(D)** $6.54 \times 10^{-17}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.962 \times 10^{-17}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given process $Li^{2+}(g) \rightarrow Li^{3+}(g) + e^-$ represents the ionization of the hydrogen-like ion $Li^{2+}$ from its ground state.

The ionization energy for a hydrogen-like species is given by the formula:
$IE = IE_{H} \times Z^2$

For $Li^{2+}$, the atomic number $Z = 3$.

Given the ionization energy of the hydrogen atom $IE_{H} = 2.18 \times 10^{-18}$ J atom$^{-1}$.

Substituting the values:
$IE = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} \times (3)^2$

$IE = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} \times 9$

$IE = 19.62 \times 10^{-18}$ J atom$^{-1}$

$IE = 1.962 \times 10^{-17}$ J atom$^{-1}$

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The energy required by electrons, present in the first Bohr orbit of hydrogen atom to be excited to second Bohr orbit is $\_\_\_\_$ $\mathrm{J} \mathrm{mol}^{-1}$. Given: $R_{H}=2.18 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{ergs}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9.835 \times 10^{12}$
- **(B)** $1.635 \times 10^{-18}$
- **(C)** $1.635 \times 10^{-11}$
- **(D)** $9.835 \times 10^{5}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $9.835 \times 10^{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The energy of an electron in the $n^{th}$ Bohr orbit of a hydrogen atom is given by $E_n = -R_H \left( \frac{1}{n^2} \right)$.
The energy required for excitation from $n=1$ to $n=2$ for a single atom is $\Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = R_H \left( \frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{2^2} \right) = R_H \left( 1 - \frac{1}{4} \right) = \frac{3}{4} R_H$.
Given $R_H = 2.18 \times 10^{-11}$ ergs.
Converting ergs to Joules: $1 \text{ erg} = 10^{-7} \text{ J}$.
So, $R_H = 2.18 \times 10^{-11} \times 10^{-7} = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} \text{ J}$.
The energy required per atom is $\Delta E = \frac{3}{4} \times 2.18 \times 10^{-18} = 1.635 \times 10^{-18} \text{ J}$.
To find the energy required per mole of electrons, multiply by Avogadro's number $N_A \approx 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ mol}^{-1}$.
Energy per mole $= 1.635 \times 10^{-18} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} \text{ J mol}^{-1}$.
Energy per mole $\approx 9.846 \times 10^5 \text{ J mol}^{-1}$.
Comparing with the given options, $9.835 \times 10^5$ is the closest value.

</details>
