### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

If shortest wavelength of hydrogen atom in Lyman series is $x$, then longest wavelength in Balmer series of $\text{He}^+$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{9x}{5}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{36x}{5}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{x}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{5x}{9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{9x}{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the shortest wavelength in the Lyman series of the hydrogen atom ($Z=1$), the transition is from $n_2 = \infty$ to $n_1 = 1$.

$\dfrac{1}{x} = R(1)^2 \left(\dfrac{1}{1^2} - \dfrac{1}{\infty^2}\right) = R$

This gives $R = \dfrac{1}{x}$.

For the longest wavelength in the Balmer series of $\text{He}^+$ ($Z=2$), the transition is from $n_2 = 3$ to $n_1 = 2$.

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R(2)^2 \left(\dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2}\right)$

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 4R \left(\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9}\right)$

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 4R \left(\dfrac{5}{36}\right) = \dfrac{5R}{9}$

Substituting $R = \dfrac{1}{x}$:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = \dfrac{5}{9x}$

$\lambda = \dfrac{9x}{5}$

Answer: $\dfrac{9x}{5}$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

What is the ratio of wave number of first line (lowest energy line) of Balmer series of H atomic spectrum to first line of its Brackett series?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5:1$
- **(B)** $5:0.81$
- **(C)** $5:1.75$
- **(D)** $5:27$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $5:0.81$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The wave number $\bar{\nu}$ of a spectral line in the hydrogen emission spectrum is given by the Rydberg formula:

$\bar{\nu} = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$

For the first line (lowest energy) of the Balmer series, the transition is from $n_2 = 3$ to $n_1 = 2$:

$\bar{\nu}_{Balmer} = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2} \right) = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9} \right) = \dfrac{5R_H}{36}$

For the first line of the Brackett series, the transition is from $n_2 = 5$ to $n_1 = 4$:

$\bar{\nu}_{Brackett} = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{4^2} - \dfrac{1}{5^2} \right) = R_H \left( \dfrac{1}{16} - \dfrac{1}{25} \right) = \dfrac{9R_H}{400}$

Taking the ratio of the two wave numbers:

$\dfrac{\bar{\nu}_{Balmer}}{\bar{\nu}_{Brackett}} = \dfrac{\dfrac{5R_H}{36}}{\dfrac{9R_H}{400}} = \dfrac{5}{36} \times \dfrac{400}{9} = \dfrac{500}{81}$

To express this ratio in the form $5 : x$, we divide the numerator and the denominator by $100$:

$\dfrac{500}{81} = \dfrac{5}{0.81}$

The ratio is $5 : 0.81$.

Answer: $5:0.81$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The hydrogen spectrum consists of several spectral lines in Lyman series ($\mathrm{L}_{1}, \mathrm{~L}_{2}$, $\mathrm{L}_{3} \ldots ; \mathrm{L}_{1}$ has lowest energy among Lyman series). Similarly it consists of several spectral lines in Balmer series $\left(\mathrm{B}_{1}, \mathrm{~B}_{2}, \mathrm{~B}_{3} \ldots ; \mathrm{B}_{1}\right.$ has lowest energy among Balmer lines). The energy of $L_{1}$ is $x$ times the energy of $B_{1}$. The value of $x$ is $\_\_\_\_$ $\times 10^{-1}$ . (Nearest integer)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **54**

**Detailed Solution:**

The energy of a spectral line in the hydrogen spectrum is given by $E = 13.6 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$ eV.
For the Lyman series, the lowest energy line $L_1$ corresponds to the transition from $n=2$ to $n=1$.
$E(L_1) = 13.6 \left( \frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{2^2} \right) = 13.6 \left( 1 - \frac{1}{4} \right) = 13.6 \times \frac{3}{4}$ eV.
For the Balmer series, the lowest energy line $B_1$ corresponds to the transition from $n=3$ to $n=2$.
$E(B_1) = 13.6 \left( \frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{3^2} \right) = 13.6 \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{9} \right) = 13.6 \times \frac{5}{36}$ eV.
Given $E(L_1) = x \cdot E(B_1)$, we have:
$13.6 \times \frac{3}{4} = x \times 13.6 \times \frac{5}{36}$
$\frac{3}{4} = x \times \frac{5}{36}$
$x = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{36}{5} = 3 \times \frac{9}{5} = \frac{27}{5} = 5.4$
The value of $x$ is $5.4 = 54 \times 10^{-1}$.
Comparing with the required format $x = \text{integer} \times 10^{-1}$, the integer is 54.

</details>

Consider the following spectral lines for atomic hydrogen :

- **(B)** $\mathrm{D}<\mathrm{A}<\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{B}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{D}<\mathrm{B}<\mathrm{A}$

**Detailed Solution:**

The energy of a spectral line in the hydrogen atom is given by $E = 13.6 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$ eV.
A. First line of Paschen series: $n_1 = 3, n_2 = 4$.
$E_A = 13.6 \left( \frac{1}{3^2} - \frac{1}{4^2} \right) = 13.6 \left( \frac{1}{9} - \frac{1}{16} \right) = 13.6 \times \frac{7}{144} \approx 0.66$ eV.
