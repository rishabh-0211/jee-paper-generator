### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

Arrange the following resultant mixtures in increasing order of their pH values
A. $10$ mL $0.2$ M Ca(OH)$_2$ + $25$ mL $0.1$ M HCl
B. $10$ mL $0.01$ M H$_2$SO$_4$ + $10$ mL $0.01$ M Ca(OH)$_2$
C. $10$ mL $0.1$ M H$_2$SO$_4$ + $10$ mL $0.1$ M KOH
Choose the correct answer from the options given below:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $B < C < A$
- **(B)** $C < A < B$
- **(C)** $C < B < A$
- **(D)** $A < C < B$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $C < B < A$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For mixture A:
Millimoles of OH$^{-}$ = $10 \times 0.2 \times 2 = 4$
Millimoles of H$^{+}$ = $25 \times 0.1 \times 1 = 2.5$
Remaining OH$^{-}$ = $4 - 2.5 = 1.5$ mmol
The solution is basic, so pH $> 7$.

For mixture B:
Millimoles of H$^{+}$ = $10 \times 0.01 \times 2 = 0.2$
Millimoles of OH$^{-}$ = $10 \times 0.01 \times 2 = 0.2$
The solution is completely neutralized, so pH $= 7$.

For mixture C:
Millimoles of H$^{+}$ = $10 \times 0.1 \times 2 = 2$
Millimoles of OH$^{-}$ = $10 \times 0.1 \times 1 = 1$
Remaining H$^{+}$ = $2 - 1 = 1$ mmol
The solution is acidic, so pH $< 7$.

Therefore, the increasing order of pH values is C $<$ B $<$ A.

Answer: $C < B < A$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

$20$ mL of a solution of acetic acid required $28.4$ mL of $0.1$ M NaOH for its neutralization. A solution (X) was prepared by mixing $20$ mL of the above acetic acid and $14.2$ mL of $0.1$ M NaOH solution. What is the pH of the solution (X)? ($pK_a$ value of acetic acid is $4.75$).

**Options:**

- **(A)** $7.0$
- **(B)** $4.75$
- **(C)** $3.5$
- **(D)** $4.82$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4.75$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For complete neutralization of $20$ mL of acetic acid, the millimoles of NaOH required is:

$28.4 \times 0.1 = 2.84$ mmol

Thus, $20$ mL of the acetic acid solution contains $2.84$ mmol of $CH_3COOH$.

For the preparation of solution (X), the millimoles of NaOH added is:

$14.2 \times 0.1 = 1.42$ mmol

The reaction between acetic acid and sodium hydroxide is:

$CH_3COOH + NaOH \rightarrow CH_3COONa + H_2O$

Millimoles of $CH_3COOH$ remaining after the reaction = $2.84 - 1.42 = 1.42$ mmol

Millimoles of $CH_3COONa$ formed = $1.42$ mmol

Since the solution contains a weak acid and its conjugate base, it forms an acidic buffer. Using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:

$pH = pK_a + \log \left( \dfrac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]} \right)$

Substituting the values:

$pH = 4.75 + \log \left( \dfrac{1.42}{1.42} \right)$

$pH = 4.75 + \log(1)$

$pH = 4.75$

Answer: $4.75$

</details>

Given: $K_a = 5 \times 10^{-4}$, $pK_a = 3.3$, $\alpha << 1$
<table style="border: 1px solid black; border-collapse: collapse; width: auto;"><tr><th style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">(a)</th><th style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">(b)</th></tr></table>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.7 \quad 2.0$
- **(B)** $2.0 \quad 3.3$
- **(C)** $1.1 \quad 2.2$
- **(D)** $3.0 \quad 2.2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2.0 \quad 3.3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For part (a), at the start of the titration, the solution contains only the weak acid HX.

The concentration of HX is $C = 0.2$ M.

Using the formula for the hydrogen ion concentration of a weak acid with $\alpha \ll 1$:

$[H^+] = \sqrt{K_a \times C} = \sqrt{5 \times 10^{-4} \times 0.2} = \sqrt{10^{-4}} = 10^{-2}$ M

$pH = -\log(10^{-2}) = 2.0$

For part (b), when $10$ mL of $0.2$ M NaOH is added to $20$ mL of $0.2$ M HX:

Initial millimoles of HX $= 20 \times 0.2 = 4$ mmol

Millimoles of NaOH added $= 10 \times 0.2 = 2$ mmol

The added NaOH neutralizes half of the HX to form NaX, creating an acidic buffer.

Millimoles of HX remaining $= 4 - 2 = 2$ mmol

Millimoles of NaX formed $= 2$ mmol

Using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:

$pH = pK_a + \log \left( \dfrac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]} \right)$

Since the millimoles of salt and acid are equal, $\log(1) = 0$.

$pH = pK_a = 3.3$

Answer: $2.0 \quad 3.3$

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The first and second ionization constants of a weak dibasic acid $H_2 A$ are $8.1 \times 10^{-8}$ and $1.0 \times 10^{-13}$ respectively. $0.1$ mol of $H_2 A$ was dissolved in $1$ L of $0.1$ M HCl solution. The concentration of $HA^-$ in the resultant solution is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.1$ M
- **(B)** $9.53 \times 10^{-6}$ M
- **(C)** $8.1 \times 10^{-8}$ M
- **(D)** $1.0 \times 10^{-13}$ M

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $8.1 \times 10^{-8}$ M**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:
Weak dibasic acid $H_2A$ with $K_{a1} = 8.1 \times 10^{-8}$ and $K_{a2} = 1.0 \times 10^{-13}$
Initial concentration: $[H_2A]_0 = 0.1\text{ M}$
The solution also contains $0.1\text{ M HCl}$, which dissociates completely to give $[H^+]_0 = 0.1\text{ M}$.

The main source of $HA^-$ is the first dissociation:
$H_2A \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HA^-$

Let $x$ be the amount of $H_2A$ that dissociates. Then at equilibrium:
$[H_2A] = 0.1 - x$
$[H^+] = 0.1 + x$
$[HA^-] = x$

Applying the equilibrium expression:
$K_{a1} = \dfrac{[H^+][HA^-]}{[H_2A]}$

$8.1 \times 10^{-8} = \dfrac{(0.1 + x)(x)}{0.1 - x}$

Since $K_{a1}$ is very small and the common $H^+$ ion from HCl suppresses dissociation, $x \ll 0.1$. Hence:
$0.1 - x \approx 0.1$ and $0.1 + x \approx 0.1$

$8.1 \times 10^{-8} \approx \dfrac{(0.1)(x)}{0.1} = x$

Therefore:
$[HA^-] = x = 8.1 \times 10^{-8}\text{ M}$

The second dissociation ($K_{a2} = 1.0 \times 10^{-13}$) is negligible compared to the first, so the $HA^-$ consumed in that step is insignificant.

Hence, the correct option is $(3)\ 8.1 \times 10^{-8}\text{ M}$.

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `1`

Consider the dissociation equilibrium of the following weak acid 
 $\mathrm{HA} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{H}^{+}(\mathrm{aq})+\mathrm{A}^{-}(\mathrm{aq})$ 
 If the pKa of the acid is 4, then the pH of 10 mMHA solution is $\_\_\_\_$.(Nearest integer) 
 [Given: The degree of dissociation can be neglected with respect to unity]

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **3**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the weak acid dissociation $HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-$ with pKa = 4
We have Ka = 10⁻⁴.
For a 10 mM (0.01 M) solution where the degree of dissociation is negligible with respect to unity, the concentration of HA remains approximately 0.01 M.
The equilibrium expression gives
$Ka = \frac{[H^+]^2}{[HA]}$
Substituting: $10^{-4} = \frac{[H^+]^2}{0.01}$, which yields $[H^+]^2 = 10^{-6}$, so $[H^+] = 10^{-3}$ M.
Therefore, pH = $-\log(10^{-3}) = 3$.

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The first and second ionization constants of $\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{X}$ are $2.5 \times 10^{-8}$ and $1.0 \times 10^{-13}$ respectively. The concentration of $\mathrm{X}^{2-}$ in $0.1 \mathrm{M} \mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{X}$ solution is $\_\_\_\_$ $\times 10^{-15} \mathrm{M}$. (Nearest Integer)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **100**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a weak diprotic acid $H_2X$, the ionization steps are:
$H_2X \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HX^-$ with $K_{a1} = 2.5 \times 10^{-8}$
$HX^- \rightleftharpoons H^+ + X^{2-}$ with $K_{a2} = 1.0 \times 10^{-13}$
Since $K_{a1} \gg K_{a2}$, the concentration of $H^+$ is primarily determined by the first ionization.
$[H^+] \approx \sqrt{K_{a1} \cdot C} = \sqrt{2.5 \times 10^{-8} \times 0.1} = \sqrt{2.5 \times 10^{-9}} = 5 \times 10^{-5} M$
Also, from the first ionization, $[HX^-] \approx [H^+] = 5 \times 10^{-5} M$
For the second ionization:
$K_{a2} = \frac{[H^+][X^{2-}]}{[HX^-]}$
Substituting the values: $1.0 \times 10^{-13} = \frac{(5 \times 10^{-5})[X^{2-}]}{5 \times 10^{-5}}$
Thus, $[X^{2-}] = K_{a2} = 1.0 \times 10^{-13} M$
To express this in the form $x \times 10^{-15} M$:
$1.0 \times 10^{-13} = 100 \times 10^{-15} M$
The value is 100.

</details>
