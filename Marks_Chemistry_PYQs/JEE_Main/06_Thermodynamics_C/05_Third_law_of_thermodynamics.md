### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

Consider the reaction $X \rightleftharpoons Y$ at $300$ K. If $\Delta H^{\theta}$ and $K$ are $28.40$ kJ mol$^{-1}$ and $1.8 \times 10^{-7}$ at the same temperature, then the magnitude of $\Delta S^{\theta}$ for the reaction in J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$ is _______. (Nearest integer) (Given: $R = 8.3$ J K$^{-1}$ mol$^{-1}$, $\ln 10 = 2.3$, $\log 3 = 0.48$, $\log 2 = 0.30$)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **34**

**Detailed Solution:**

The standard Gibbs free energy change is given by:

$\Delta G^{\theta} = -RT \ln K = -2.3 RT \log K$

First, calculate $\log K$:

$\log K = \log(1.8 \times 10^{-7}) = \log(18 \times 10^{-8})$

$\log K = \log 18 - 8 = \log(2 \times 3^2) - 8$

$\log K = \log 2 + 2 \log 3 - 8$

Substituting the given values:

$\log K = 0.30 + 2(0.48) - 8 = 0.30 + 0.96 - 8 = 1.26 - 8 = -6.74$

Now, calculate $\Delta G^{\theta}$:

$\Delta G^{\theta} = -2.3 \times 8.3 \times 300 \times (-6.74) = 38599.98 \text{ J mol}^{-1}$

Using the relation $\Delta G^{\theta} = \Delta H^{\theta} - T \Delta S^{\theta}$:

$38599.98 = 28400 - 300 \Delta S^{\theta}$

$300 \Delta S^{\theta} = 28400 - 38599.98 = -10199.98$

$\Delta S^{\theta} = -\dfrac{10199.98}{300} \approx -34 \text{ J K}^{-1} \text{ mol}^{-1}$

The magnitude of $\Delta S^{\theta}$ is $34$.

Answer: $34$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

At the transition temperature $T$, $A \rightleftharpoons B$ and $\Delta G^0 = 105 - 35\log T$ where $A$ and $B$ are two states of substance $X$. The transition temperature in $°$C when pressure is $1$ atm is _______. (Nearest integer)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **727**

**Detailed Solution:**

At the transition temperature and $1$ atm pressure, the two states $A$ and $B$ are in equilibrium.

For a phase transition of a pure substance at $1$ atm, both states are in their standard states, so the reaction quotient $Q = 1$.

Using the relation $\Delta G = \Delta G^0 + RT \ln Q$, we get $\Delta G = \Delta G^0$.

At equilibrium, $\Delta G = 0$, which implies $\Delta G^0 = 0$.

Given $\Delta G^0 = 105 - 35\log T$, we can equate it to zero:

$105 - 35\log T = 0$

$35\log T = 105$

$\log T = 3$

$T = 10^3 = 1000$ K

Converting the temperature to Celsius:

$T(^{\circ}\text{C}) = 1000 - 273 = 727^{\circ}\text{C}$

Answer: $727$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2025 (4 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `1`

Let us consider a reversible reaction at temperature, T.
In this reaction, both $\Delta \mathrm{H}$ and $\Delta \mathrm{S}$ were observed to have positive values. If the equilibrium temperature is Te , then the reaction becomes spontaneous at :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{Te}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Te} \gt \mathrm{T}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{T} \gt \mathrm{Te}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{Te}=5 \mathrm{~T}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{T} \gt \mathrm{Te}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For reaction to be spontaneous according to $2^{\text {nd }}$ law:
$\begin{aligned}
& \Delta \mathrm{G} \lt 0 \\
& \Rightarrow \Delta \mathrm{H}-\mathrm{T} \Delta \mathrm{~S} \lt 0 \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{~T} \gt \left(\frac{\Delta \mathrm{H}}{\Delta \mathrm{~S}}\right)=\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{e}} \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{~T} \gt \mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{e}}
\end{aligned}$
