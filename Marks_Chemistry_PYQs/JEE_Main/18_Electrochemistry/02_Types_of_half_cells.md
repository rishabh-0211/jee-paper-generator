### Q5. JEE Main 2014 (12 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

How many electrons would be required to deposit $6.35 \mathrm{~g}$ of copper at the cathode during the electrolysis of an aqueous solution of copper sulphate? (Atomic mass of copper $=63.5 \mathrm{u}, \mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}=$ Avogadro's constant):

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}}{20}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}}{10}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}}{5}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}}{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\mathrm{Cu} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Cu}^{++}+2 \mathrm{e}^{-}$
i.e, to deposit 1 mole of $\mathrm{Cu}$ at cathode from $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} \mathrm{SO}_4^{2-}$ solution $=2$ moles of electrons are required
i.e, To deposit
$6.35 \mathrm{~g}=\frac{6.35}{63.5} \times 2=\frac{2}{10}=\frac{1}{5}$ moles
Thus total no. of electrons required $=\frac{\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{A}}}{5}$

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2013 (25 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

A solution of copper sulphate $\left(\mathrm{CuSO}_4\right)$ is electrolysed for $10$ minutes with a current of $1.5$ amperes. The mass of copper deposited at the cathode (at. mass of $\mathrm{Cu}=63 \mathrm{u}$ ) is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.3892 \mathrm{~g}$
- **(B)** $0.2938 \mathrm{~g}$
- **(C)** $0.2398 \mathrm{~g}$
- **(D)** $0.3928 \mathrm{~g}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.2938 \mathrm{~g}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\mathrm{W}=\mathrm{Zit}$
where $\mathrm{Z}$ = Electrochemical equivalent
Eq. wt. of copper $=\frac{63}{2}=31.5$
$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{Z}=\frac{31.5}{96500} \\
& \mathrm{~W}=\mathrm{Zit}=\frac{31.5}{96500} \times 1.5 \times 10 \times 60=0.2938 \mathrm{~g}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q7. JEE Main 2012 (26 May Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `3`

Given
$$
\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} / \mathrm{Cu}}^{\circ}=0.34 \mathrm{~V}, \mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} / \mathrm{Cu}}^{\circ}=0.15 \mathrm{~V}
$$
Standard electrode potential for the half cell $\mathrm{Cu}^{+} / \mathrm{Cu}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.38 \mathrm{~V}$
- **(B)** $0.53 \mathrm{~V}$
- **(C)** $0.19 \mathrm{~V}$
- **(D)** $0.49 \mathrm{~V}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.53 \mathrm{~V}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text { } \mathrm{Cu}^{++}+e^{-} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Cu}^{+} \text {; } \\
& E_1^0=0.15 \mathrm{~V} ; \Delta G_1^0=-n_1 E_1^0 F \\
& \mathrm{Cu}^{2+}+2 e \longrightarrow \mathrm{Cu} \quad \text {; } \\
& E_2^0=0.34 \mathrm{~V} ; \Delta G_2^0=-n_2 E_2^0 F \\
&
\end{aligned}
$$
On subracting eq.(i) from eq. (ii) we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{Cu}^{+}+e^{-} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Cu} ; \Delta G^0=\Delta G_2^0-\Delta G_1^0 \\
&-n E^0 F=-\left(n_2 E^0 F-n_1 E_1^0 F\right) \\
& E^0=\frac{n_2 E_2^0 F-n_1 E_1^0 F}{n F} \\
&=\frac{2 \times 0.34-0.15}{1} \\
&=0.53 \mathrm{~V}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
