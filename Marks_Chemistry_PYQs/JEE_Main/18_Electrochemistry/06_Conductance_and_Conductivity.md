### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

Consider the following data.
<table style="border: 1px solid black; border-collapse: collapse; width: auto;"><tr><th style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">Electrolyte</th><th style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">$\Lambda^{\circ}_m$ (S cm$^2$ mol$^{-1}$)</th></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">$\text{BaCl}_2$</td><td style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">$x_1$</td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">$\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$</td><td style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">$x_2$</td></tr><tr><td style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">HCl</td><td style="border: 1px solid black; padding: 5px;">$x_3$</td></tr></table>
$\text{BaSO}_4$ is sparingly soluble in water. If the conductivity of the saturated $\text{BaSO}_4$ solution is $x$ S cm$^{-1}$ then the solubility product of $\text{BaSO}_4$ can be given as (Here $\Lambda_m = \Lambda^{\circ}_m$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{10^6 x^2}{\alpha^2(x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3)^2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{x^2}{(x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3)^2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\alpha^2(x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3)^2}{10^6 x^2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{x^2}{(x_1 + x_2 + 2x_3)^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{10^6 x^2}{\alpha^2(x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3)^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions, the limiting molar conductivity of $\text{BaSO}_4$ is given by:

$\Lambda^{\circ}_m(\text{BaSO}_4) = \Lambda^{\circ}_m(\text{BaCl}_2) + \Lambda^{\circ}_m(\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4) - 2\Lambda^{\circ}_m(\text{HCl})$

Substituting the given values:

$\Lambda^{\circ}_m(\text{BaSO}_4) = x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3$

For a sparingly soluble salt, the solubility $S$ (in mol L$^{-1}$) is related to its conductivity $\kappa$ (in S cm$^{-1}$) and molar conductivity $\Lambda_m$ (in S cm$^2$ mol$^{-1}$) by the relation:

$\Lambda_m = \dfrac{\kappa \times 1000}{S}$

Given that $\kappa = x$ and $\Lambda_m = \Lambda^{\circ}_m$ (which implies complete dissociation, meaning the degree of dissociation $\alpha = 1$), we get:

$S = \dfrac{1000 x}{x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3}$

The solubility product $K_{sp}$ for $\text{BaSO}_4$ (which dissociates into $\text{Ba}^{2+}$ and $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) is:

$K_{sp} = S^2 = \left( \dfrac{1000 x}{x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3} \right)^2 = \dfrac{10^6 x^2}{(x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3)^2}$

Since $\alpha = 1$, this expression is equivalent to $\dfrac{10^6 x^2}{\alpha^2(x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3)^2}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{10^6 x^2}{\alpha^2(x_1 + x_2 - 2x_3)^2}$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

At $298\text{ K}$, the molar conductivity of $x\%$ (w/w) $MX$ solution (aqueous) is $123.5\text{ S cm}^2\text{ mol}^{-1}$. The conductance of same solution is $1.9 \times 10^{-3}\text{ S}$. The value of $x$ is _______ $\times 10^{-2}$.
(Given : cell constant $= 1.3\text{ cm}^{-1}$; molar mass of $MX$ is $75\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, density of aqueous solution of $MX$ at $298\text{ K}$ is $1.0\text{ g mL}^{-1}$)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **15**

**Detailed Solution:**

The conductivity (specific conductance), $\kappa$, is given by the product of conductance and cell constant:

$\kappa = G \times G^*$

$\kappa = 1.9 \times 10^{-3} \text{ S} \times 1.3 \text{ cm}^{-1} = 2.47 \times 10^{-3} \text{ S cm}^{-1}$

The molar conductivity, $\Lambda_m$, is related to the conductivity and molarity $C$ by the formula:

$\Lambda_m = \dfrac{\kappa \times 1000}{C}$

Substituting the given values:

$123.5 = \dfrac{2.47 \times 10^{-3} \times 1000}{C}$

$123.5 = \dfrac{2.47}{C}$

$C = \dfrac{2.47}{123.5} = 0.02 \text{ mol L}^{-1}$

Molarity $C$ can also be expressed in terms of mass percentage $x$ (w/w), density $d$, and molar mass $M$:

$C = \dfrac{x \times d \times 10}{M}$

Substituting the known values ($d = 1.0 \text{ g mL}^{-1}$, $M = 75 \text{ g mol}^{-1}$):

$0.02 = \dfrac{x \times 1.0 \times 10}{75}$

$1.5 = 10x$

$x = 0.15$

Expressing $x$ in the required format:

$x = 15 \times 10^{-2}$

The value is $15$.

Answer: $15$

</details>

 \(\begin{array}{|l|c|c|c|c|} \hline \mathrm{c}\;[\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}] & 0.04 & 0.09 & 0.16 & 0.25 \\ \hline \Lambda_{\mathrm{m}}\;[\mathrm{S\,cm^{2}\,mol^{-1}}] & 96.1 & 95.7 & 95.3 & 94.9 \\ \hline \end{array}\)

 [Given: degree of dissociation $(\alpha) \ll 1$ for both weak acids, $\lambda^{\circ}$ : limiting molar conductivity of ions]

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $124 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $1240 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $1.24 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $12.4 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $12.4 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~S} \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~mol}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

There is one mistake in Question paper.
Assuming concentration of solution is $0.2 \mathrm{M}$ instead of $0.02 \mathrm{M}$. Since resistance of $0.2 \mathrm{M}$ is $520 \Omega$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{R} & =100 \Omega \\
\mathrm{K} & =\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}}\left(\frac{\ell}{\mathrm{a}}\right) \\
1.29 & =\frac{1}{100}\left(\frac{\ell}{\mathrm{a}}\right) \\
\left(\frac{\ell}{\mathrm{a}}\right) & =129 \mathrm{~m}^{-1} \\
\mathrm{R} & =520 \Omega, \mathrm{C}=0.2 \mathrm{M} \\
\mathrm{K} & =\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}}\left(\frac{\ell}{\mathrm{a}}\right)=\frac{1}{520}(129) \Omega^{-1} \mathrm{~m}^{-1} \\
\mu & =\mathrm{K} \times \mathrm{V}_{\text {in } \mathrm{cm}} \\
& =\frac{1}{520} \times 129 \times \frac{1000}{0.2} \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{~m}^3 \\
& =\frac{129}{520} \times \frac{1000}{0.2} \times 10^{-6} \\
& =1.24 \times 10^{-3} \\
& =12.4 \times 10^{-4}
\end{aligned}
$$
