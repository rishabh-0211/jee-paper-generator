### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

Given below are two statements :
Statement I : The number of compounds among SO$_2$, SO$_3$, SF$_4$, SF$_6$ and H$_2$S in which sulphur does not obey the Octet rule is $3$.
Statement II : Among [H$_2$O, ClF$_3$, SF$_4$], [NH$_3$, BrF$_5$, SF$_4$], [BrF$_5$, ClF$_3$, XeF$_4$] and [XeF$_4$, ClF$_3$, H$_2$O], the number of sets in which all the molecules have one lone pair of electrons on the central atom is $1$.
In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

**Options:**

- **(A)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true
- **(B)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false
- **(C)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false
- **(D)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Statement I is false but Statement II is true**

**Detailed Solution:**

Statement I: Number of compounds in which sulphur does not follow the octet rule (from $SO_2$, $SO_3$, $SF_4$, $SF_6$ and $H_2S$).

$SO_2$: The formal-charge-minimised structure $O=S=O$ places $10$ electrons around sulphur (expanded octet).
$SO_3$: With three $S=O$ double bonds, sulphur has $12$ electrons around it (expanded octet).
$SF_4$: $4$ bond pairs $+ 1$ lone pair $= 10$ electrons around $S$ (expanded octet).
$SF_6$: $6$ bond pairs $= 12$ electrons around $S$ (expanded octet).
$H_2S$: $2$ bond pairs $+ 2$ lone pairs $= 8$ electrons around $S$ (obeys the octet rule).

Depending on whether formal-charge-minimised structures are used for $SO_2$ and $SO_3$, either $2$ ($SF_4, SF_6$) or $4$ ($SO_2, SO_3, SF_4, SF_6$) species violate the octet rule, but the number is never $3$.
Hence, Statement I is false.

Statement II: Among the given sets, exactly one contains only molecules having one lone pair on the central atom.

Number of lone pairs on the central atom, $\text{LP} = \dfrac{\text{valence electrons of central atom} - \text{number of bonded monovalent atoms}}{2}$:

$H_2O$: $\dfrac{6 - 2}{2} = 2$ lone pairs
$ClF_3$: $\dfrac{7 - 3}{2} = 2$ lone pairs
$SF_4$: $\dfrac{6 - 4}{2} = 1$ lone pair
$NH_3$: $\dfrac{5 - 3}{2} = 1$ lone pair
$BrF_5$: $\dfrac{7 - 5}{2} = 1$ lone pair
$XeF_4$: $\dfrac{8 - 4}{2} = 2$ lone pairs

Checking the sets:
$\{H_2O\ (2),\ ClF_3\ (2),\ SF_4\ (1)\}$: not all have $1$ lone pair.
$\{NH_3\ (1),\ BrF_5\ (1),\ SF_4\ (1)\}$: all have exactly $1$ lone pair.
$\{BrF_5\ (1),\ ClF_3\ (2),\ XeF_4\ (2)\}$: not all have $1$ lone pair.
$\{XeF_4\ (2),\ ClF_3\ (2),\ H_2O\ (2)\}$: not all have $1$ lone pair.

Exactly one set satisfies the condition, so Statement II is true.

Hence, Statement I is false but Statement II is true, which corresponds to option $(4)$.

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `3`

The pairs among $A = [\text{SO}_3^{2-}, \text{CO}_3^{2-}]$, $B = [\text{O}_2^{2-}, \text{F}_2]$, $C = [\text{CN}^-, \text{CO}]$, $D = [\text{NH}_3, \text{H}_3\text{O}^+]$ and $E = [\text{MnO}_4^{2-}, \text{CrO}_4^{2-}]$ that do not have similar Lewis dot structure are

**Options:**

- **(A)** A, B and E
- **(B)** A and E
- **(C)** B, C and D
- **(D)** C and D

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) A and E**

**Detailed Solution:**

Species with the same number of valence electrons and the same skeletal structure generally have similar Lewis dot structures (isoelectronic species).

For pair A: $\text{SO}_3^{2-}$ has $6 + 3(6) + 2 = 26$ valence electrons. $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ has $4 + 3(6) + 2 = 24$ valence electrons. Since they have different numbers of valence electrons, they do not have similar Lewis dot structures ($\text{SO}_3^{2-}$ has a lone pair on the central atom and is pyramidal, while $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ has no lone pair on the central atom and is trigonal planar).

For pair B: $\text{O}_2^{2-}$ has $2(6) + 2 = 14$ valence electrons. $\text{F}_2$ has $2(7) = 14$ valence electrons. Both have a single bond and three lone pairs on each atom, so they have similar Lewis dot structures.

For pair C: $\text{CN}^-$ has $4 + 5 + 1 = 10$ valence electrons. $\text{CO}$ has $4 + 6 = 10$ valence electrons. Both have a triple bond and one lone pair on each atom, so they have similar Lewis dot structures.

For pair D: $\text{NH}_3$ has $5 + 3(1) = 8$ valence electrons. $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ has $6 + 3(1) - 1 = 8$ valence electrons. Both have three single bonds and one lone pair on the central atom, so they have similar Lewis dot structures.

For pair E: $\text{MnO}_4^{2-}$ has $7 + 4(6) + 2 = 33$ valence electrons. $\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$ has $6 + 4(6) + 2 = 32$ valence electrons. Since they have different numbers of valence electrons ($\text{MnO}_4^{2-}$ has an unpaired electron), they do not have similar Lewis dot structures.

Thus, pairs A and E do not have similar Lewis dot structures.

Answer: A and E

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `1`

The covalent radii of atoms A and B are $r_A$ and $r_B$, respectively. The covalent bond length and total length of AB molecule are respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(r_A + r_B), 2(r_A + r_B)$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{2}(r_A + r_B), (r_A + r_B)$
- **(C)** $(r_A + r_B), (r_A + r_B)$
- **(D)** $2(r_A + r_B), \dfrac{1}{2}(r_A + r_B)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $(r_A + r_B), 2(r_A + r_B)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The covalent bond length of the AB molecule is the internuclear distance between atom A and atom B.

Bond length = $r_A + r_B$

The total length of the AB molecule is the distance between the outermost boundaries of the two atoms along the internuclear axis.

Total length = Radius of A + Bond length + Radius of B

Total length = $r_A + (r_A + r_B) + r_B = 2(r_A + r_B)$

Answer: $(r_A + r_B), 2(r_A + r_B)$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The formal charges on the atoms marked as (1) to (4) in the Lewis representation of $\mathrm{HNO}_{3}$ molecule respectively are
<img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/22S1_q_69_1.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0,0,-1,+1$
- **(B)** $0,-1,0,+1$
- **(C)** $+1,0,0,-1$
- **(D)** $0,+1,0,-1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $0,+1,0,-1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The formal charge (F.C.) of an atom in a Lewis structure is calculated using the formula:
F.C. = [Total valence electrons] - [Total non-bonding (lone pair) electrons] - $\frac{1}{2}$[Total bonding (shared) electrons]
For atom (1) Oxygen: Valence $e^- = 6$, Lone pair $e^- = 4$, Bonding $e^- = 4$.
$F.C. = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 0$
For atom (2) Nitrogen: Valence $e^- = 5$, Lone pair $e^- = 0$, Bonding $e^- = 8$.
$F.C. = 5 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = +1$
For atom (3) Oxygen: Valence $e^- = 6$, Lone pair $e^- = 4$, Bonding $e^- = 4$.
$F.C. = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 0$
For atom (4) Oxygen: Valence $e^- = 6$, Lone pair $e^- = 6$, Bonding $e^- = 2$.
$F.C. = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = -1$
The formal charges are $0, +1, 0, -1$ respectively.

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `2`

The correct increasing order of $C-H(A), C-O(B), C=O(C)$ and $C \equiv N(D)$ bonds in terms of covalent bond length is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{D}<\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{A}<\mathrm{B}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{A}<\mathrm{D}<\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{B}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{D}<\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{B}<\mathrm{A}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{A}<\mathrm{B}<\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{D}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{A}<\mathrm{D}<\mathrm{C}<\mathrm{B}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Bond lengths depend on bond multiplicity and atomic sizes.
C-H (single) ≈ 1.09 Å, C-O (single) ≈ 1.43 Å, C=O (double) ≈ 1.23 Å, C≡N (triple) ≈ 1.16 Å.
Triple bonds are shorter than double bonds, which are shorter than single bonds.
For single bonds, C-O is longer due to lower electronegativity difference compared to C-H.
Increasing order of bond length: $\text{C-H}(A) < \text{C}\equiv\text{N}(D) < \text{C}=\text{O}(C) < \text{C-O}(B)$, giving A < D < C < B.

</details>

### Q27. JEE Main 2005

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Difficulty Level:* `1`

Which of the following oxides is amphoteric in character?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{CaO}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{CO}_2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{SiO}_2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{SnO}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{SnO}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\mathrm{CaO} \longrightarrow$ basic
$\mathrm{SiO}_2 \& \mathrm{CO}_2 \longrightarrow$ acidic
$\mathrm{SnO}_2 \longrightarrow$ amphoteric

</details>
