### Q2. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

0.5 mole of an ideal gas at constant temperature $27^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ kept inside a cylinder of length $L$ and crosssection area A closed by a massless piston.

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/-iowH4MfsQxjSAxWlKQFCRBmNPye5ME0xQT28-7P41c.original.fullsize.png">


The cylinder is attached with a conducting rod of length $L,$ cross-section area $(1 / 9) \mathrm{m}^{2}$ and thermal conductivity $\mathrm{k}$, whose other end is maintained at $0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. If piston is moved such that rate of heat flow through the conducing rod is constant then velocity of piston when it is at height $L / 2$ from the bottom of cylinder is :

[Neglect any kind of heat loss from system

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\frac{\mathrm{k}}{R}\right) \mathrm{m} / \mathrm{sec}$
- **(B)** $\left(\frac{\mathrm{k}}{10 R}\right) \mathrm{m} / \mathrm{sec}$
- **(C)** $\left(\frac{\mathrm{k}}{100 R}\right) \mathrm{m} / \mathrm{sec}$
- **(D)** $\left(\frac{\mathrm{k}}{1000 R}\right) \mathrm{m} / \mathrm{sec}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\left(\frac{\mathrm{k}}{100 R}\right) \mathrm{m} / \mathrm{sec}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}=\frac{\Delta W}{\Delta t}=$ work done per unit time = $\frac{\mathrm{k} a \theta}{L}$

$$

\frac{d W}{d t}=P \frac{d v}{d t}=\mathrm{k} \frac{a \theta}{L}, P=\frac{n R T}{V}

$$

$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{0.5 R(300)}{V} A \cdot \frac{d \ell}{d t}=\frac{\mathrm{k} a \theta}{L}$

$$

\Rightarrow \frac{0.5 R(300)}{A \cdot \frac{L}{2}} A \cdot v=\frac{\mathrm{k} a \theta}{L}

$$

$\Rightarrow \quad v=\frac{\mathrm{k} a}{R}\left(\frac{27}{300}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{k}}{100 R}$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Figure shows a copper rod joined to a steel rod. The rods have equal length and equal crosssectional area. The free end of the copper rod is kept at $0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and that of steel rod is kept at $100^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. Find the temperature of the junction of the rod. Conductivity of copper $=390 \mathrm{~W} / \mathrm{m}^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

Conductivity of steel $=46 \mathrm{~W} / \mathrm{m}^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/pCFpa_b6RdhIUWc8PZceUFwlSkcvkwaLJIZ9Ypun41U.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $18.01^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(B)** $26^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(C)** $10.6^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(D)** $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $10.6^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Heat current in first rod (copper)

$$

=\frac{390 \times \mathrm{A}(0-\theta)}{\ell}

$$

Here $\theta$ is temperature of the junction and $\mathrm{A}$ $\& \ell$ are area and length of copper rod. Heat current in second rod (steel)

$$

=\frac{46 \times \mathrm{A}(\theta-100)}{\ell}

$$

In series combination, heat current remains same. So,

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\frac{390 \times \mathrm{A}(0-\theta)}{\ell}=\frac{46 \times \mathrm{A}(\theta-100)}{\ell} \\

-390 \theta==46 \theta-4600 \\

436 \theta=4600 \Rightarrow \theta=10.6^{\circ} \mathrm{C}

\end{array}

$$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body of length $1 \mathrm{~m}$ having cross-sectional area $0.75 \mathrm{~m}^{2}$ has heat flow through it at the rate of 6000 Joule/sec. Then find the temperature difference if $\mathrm{K}=200 \mathrm{Jm}^{-1} \mathrm{~K}^{-1}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(B)** $40^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(C)** $80^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(D)** $100^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $40^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\frac{Q}{t}=\frac{\operatorname{KA} \Delta \theta}{\ell} \Rightarrow 6000=\frac{200 \times 0.75 \times \Delta \theta}{1}$
$\therefore \Delta \theta=\frac{6000 \times 1}{200 \times 0.75}=40^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

</details>
