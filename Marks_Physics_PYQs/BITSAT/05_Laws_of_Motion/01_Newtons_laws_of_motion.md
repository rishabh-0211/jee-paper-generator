### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A light string passing over a smooth light pulley connects two blocks of masses $\mathrm{m}_1$ and $\mathrm{m}_2$ (where $m_2 \gt m_1$). If the acceleration of the system is $\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\sqrt{2}}$, then the ratio of the masses $\frac{m_1}{m_2}$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}+1}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}-1}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{2}-1}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{\sqrt{2}-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}+1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Acceleration is given as:
$a=\left(\frac{m_2-m_1}{m_1+m_2}\right) g$
$\Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{g}}{\sqrt{2}}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{m}_2-\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_1+\mathrm{m}_2}\right) \mathrm{g}$
$\Rightarrow \sqrt{2}\left(m_2-m_1\right)=m_1+m_2$
$\Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2}=\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}+1}\right)$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2022

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A particle of mass $m$ is projected with velocity $v$ at an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal. At its highest point, it explodes into two pieces of equal mass, one of the piece continue to move on the original trajectory, then the velocity of second piece is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 v \cos \theta$
- **(B)** $v \cos \theta$
- **(C)** $3 v \cos \theta$
- **(D)** $\frac{v}{2} \cos \theta$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $v \cos \theta$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the law of conservation of linear momentum at the highest point,
$$
m v \cos \theta=\frac{m}{2}(v \cos \theta)+\frac{m}{2} v^{\prime}
$$
$\Rightarrow \quad m v \cos \theta-\frac{m}{2} v \cos \theta=\frac{m}{2} v^{\prime}$
$\Rightarrow \quad v^{\prime}=2\left(v \cos \theta-\frac{v \cos \theta}{2}\right)=v \cos \theta$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2022

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

What will be the acceleration due to gravity at a depth $d$, where $g$ is acceleration due to gravity on the surface of earth?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{g}{\left[1+\frac{d}{R}\right]^2}$
- **(B)** $g\left[1-\frac{2 d}{R}\right]$
- **(C)** $\frac{g}{\left[1-\frac{d}{R}\right]^2}$
- **(D)** $g\left[1-\frac{d}{R}\right]$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $g\left[1-\frac{d}{R}\right]$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Acceleration due to gravity at the surface of earth,
$$
g=\frac{G M}{R^2}=\frac{4}{3} \pi \rho G R
$$
$$
\left[\because M=\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \cdot \rho\right]
$$
Where, $\rho=$ density of earth.
Acceleration due to gravity at depth $d$ from the surface of earth,
$$
g^{\prime}=\frac{4}{3} \pi \rho G(R-d)
$$
From Eqs. (i) and (ii), we get
$$
g^{\prime}=g\left[1-\frac{d}{R}\right]
$$

</details>

### Q7. BITSAT 2021

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ball of mass $0.2 \mathrm{~kg}$ is thrown vertically upwards by applying a force by hand. If the hand moves $0.2 \mathrm{~m}$ while applying the force and the ball goes upto $2 \mathrm{~m}$ height further, find the magnitude of the force. (Consider $\left.g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}\right)$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(B)** $16 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(C)** $20 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(D)** $22 \mathrm{~N}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $22 \mathrm{~N}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the velocity of the ball just when it leaves the hand is $u$ then applying,

$\begin{array}{l}

v^{2}-u^{2}=2 a \text { for upward journey } \\

\Rightarrow-u^{2}=2(-10) \times 2 \\

\Rightarrow u^{2}=40

\end{array}$

Again applying $v^{2}-u^{2}=2 a s$ for the upward journey of the ball, when the ball is in the hands of the thrower,

$\begin{array}{l}

v^{2}-u^{2}=2 a s \\

\Rightarrow 40-0=2(a) 0.2 \\

\Rightarrow a=100 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2} \\

\therefore \mathrm{F}=m a=0.2 \times 100=20 \mathrm{~N} \\

\Rightarrow \mathrm{N}-m g=20 \\

\Rightarrow \mathrm{N}=20+2=22 \mathrm{~N}

\end{array}$

</details>

They are pulled with a force $\mathrm{T}_{3}$ $=40 \mathrm{~N}$. If $m_{1}=10 \mathrm{~kg}, \mathrm{~m}_{2}=6 \mathrm{~kg}$ and $\mathrm{m}_{3}=4 \mathrm{~kg},$ the
tension $\mathrm{T}_{2}$ will be
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/ZlQID5EcMiOmt14P9NLA3jntPiW4zJgXRLqAZ2hfr0Q.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $20 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(B)** $40 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(C)** $10 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(D)** $32 \mathrm{~N}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $32 \mathrm{~N}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For equilibrium of all 3 masses,
$$
a=\frac{\mathrm{T}_{3}}{\mathrm{~m}_{1}+\mathrm{m}_{2}+\mathrm{m}_{3}}
$$
For equilibrium of $\mathrm{m}_{1} \& \mathrm{~m}_{2}$ $\mathrm{T}_{2}=\left(\mathrm{m}_{1}+\mathrm{m}_{2}\right) \cdot \mathrm{a}$ or, $\mathrm{T}_{2}=\frac{\left(\mathrm{m}_{1}+\mathrm{m}_{2}\right) \mathrm{T}_{3}}{\mathrm{~m}_{1}+\mathrm{m}_{2}+\mathrm{m}_{3}}$

$\therefore \mathrm{T}_{2}=\frac{(10+6) \cdot 40}{10+6+4}=32 \mathrm{~N}$

### Q14. BITSAT 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An object experiences a net force and accelerates from rest to its final position in $16 \mathrm{~s}$. How long would the object take to reach the same final position from rest if the object's mass was four times larger ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $64 \mathrm{~s}$
- **(B)** $32 \mathrm{~s}$
- **(C)** $16 \mathrm{~s}$
- **(D)** $8 \mathrm{~s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $32 \mathrm{~s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When the mass increases by a factor of 4 the acceleration must decrease by a factor of four if the same force is applied. The question asks about position so we need to relate acceleration and time to position. We can do this by the equation $: x_{f}-x_{i}=v_{x i} t+1 / 2 a_{x} t^{2}$ We want the change in position to stay the same. The initial velocity is zero so in order for the change in position to remain constant the term $(1 / 2)$ at $^{2}$ must remain the same. If the acceleration is reduced by a factor of 4 you can see that the time must be increased by a factor of 2 in order for the term to remain the same.

</details>
