### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The potential of a large liquid drop when eight liquid drops are combined is 20 V , then the potential of each single drop was

**Options:**

- **(A)** 10 V
- **(B)** 7.5 V
- **(C)** 5 V
- **(D)** 2.5 V

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 5 V**

**Detailed Solution:**

Volume of eight drops = Volume of a big drop
$\therefore \quad\left(\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3\right) \times 8=\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3$
$\Rightarrow \quad 2 r=R$...(i)
According to charge conservation,
$8 q=Q$...(ii)
Potential of one small drop, $V^{\prime}=\frac{q}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 r}$
Similarly, potential of big drop, $V=\frac{Q}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0 R}$
Now, $\quad \frac{V^{\prime}}{V}=\frac{q}{Q} \times \frac{R}{r}$
$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{V^{\prime}}{20}=\frac{q}{8 q} \times \frac{2 r}{r} \quad$ [from Eqs. (i) and (ii)]
$\therefore \quad V^{\prime}=5 \mathrm{~V}$

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-1$
- **(B)** $1$
- **(C)** $-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(D)** $-2 \sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $-2 \sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/Hts9_DP0I9bJeoH6q8wbY_mrR2-h2VwGI2mzTegRaJc.original.fullsize.png">


It is given that $\vec{F}_{A}+\vec{F}_{B}+\vec{F}_{D}=0$

Where $\vec{F}_{A}, \vec{F}_{B}$ and $\vec{F}_{D}$ are the forces applied by charges placed at $A, B$ and $D$ on the charge placed at $C$.

$\begin{array}{l}

\Rightarrow \overrightarrow{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{B}}+\overrightarrow{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{D}}=-\overrightarrow{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{A}} \\

\left|\overrightarrow{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{B}}+\overrightarrow{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{D}}\right|=\sqrt{2} \frac{\mathrm{KqQ}}{\mathrm{a}^{2}} \\

\Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{Q}}{q}=-2 \sqrt{2}

\end{array}$

### Q8. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

At the corners of an equilateral triangle of side a (1 metre), three point charges are placed (each of $0.1 \mathrm{C}$ ). If this system is supplied energy at the rate of $1 \mathrm{kw},$ then calculate the time required to move one of the mid-point of the line joining the other two.

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/8In6JAsI0AamkIYTHB0MdNAF6pH7Zrru4DZDMNygNqw.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $50 \mathrm{~h}$
- **(B)** $60 \mathrm{~h}$
- **(C)** $48 \mathrm{~h}$
- **(D)** $54 \mathrm{~h}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $50 \mathrm{~h}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial potential energy of the system

$$

\begin{array}{l}

=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}}\left[\frac{\mathrm{q}^{2}}{\mathrm{a}}+\frac{\mathrm{q}^{2}}{\mathrm{a}}+\frac{\mathrm{q}^{2}}{\mathrm{a}}\right]=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}}\left(\frac{3 \mathrm{q}^{2}}{\mathrm{a}}\right) \\

=9 \times 10^{9}\left(3 \times \frac{(0.1)^{2}}{1}\right)=27 \times 10^{7} \mathrm{~J}

\end{array}

$$

Let charge at $\mathrm{A}$ is moved to mid-point $\mathrm{O}$ Then final potential energy of thhe system

$$

\begin{aligned}

\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{f}} &=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}}\left[\frac{2 \mathrm{q}^{2}}{(\mathrm{a} / 2)}+\frac{\mathrm{q}^{2}}{\mathrm{a}}\right] \\

&=5 \times \frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{q}^{2}}{\mathrm{a}^{2}}\right)=45 \times 10^{7} \mathrm{~J}

\end{aligned}

$$

Work done $=\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{f}}-\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{i}}=18 \times 10^{7} \mathrm{~J}$

Also, energy supplied per sec $=1000 \mathrm{~J}$ (given) Time required to move one of the mid-point of the line joining the other two

$$

\mathrm{t}=\frac{18 \times 10^{7}}{1000}=18 \times 10^{4} \mathrm{~s}=50 \mathrm{~h}

$$

Two metallic spheres of radii $1 \mathrm{~cm}$ and $3 \mathrm{~cm}$ are given charges of $-1 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{C}$ and $5 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{C}$, respectively. If these are connected by a conducting wire, the final charge on the bigger sphere is

**Options:**

- **(C)** $4 \times 10^{-2} C$
- **(D)** $1 \times 10^{-2} C$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

$\quad \frac{k x}{1 \mathrm{~cm}}=\frac{k(Q-x)}{3 \mathrm{~cm}}$
$3 x=Q-x \Rightarrow 4 x=Q$
$x=\frac{Q}{4}=\frac{4 \times 10^{-2}}{4} C=1 \times 10^{-2}$
$Q^{\prime}=\mathrm{Q}-x=3 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{C}$
