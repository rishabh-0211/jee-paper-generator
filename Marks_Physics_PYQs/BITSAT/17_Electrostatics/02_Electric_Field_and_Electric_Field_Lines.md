### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Electric field at point $(30,30,0)$ due to a charge of $0.008 \mu \mathrm{C}$ placed at origin will be, (coordinates are in cm )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8000 \mathrm{NC}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $4000(\hat{\mathbf{i}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \mathrm{NC}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $200 \sqrt{2}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \mathrm{NC}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $400 \sqrt{2}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \mathrm{NC}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $200 \sqrt{2}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \mathrm{NC}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$E=\frac{K q}{r^2} \cdot \hat{r}=\frac{K q}{r^3} \cdot r$
Here,
$\begin{aligned}
r & =\sqrt{\left(x_2-x_1\right)^2+\left(y_2-y_1\right)^2+\left(z_2-z_1\right)^2} \\
& =\sqrt{(30)^2+(30)^2+0^2} \\
& =30 \sqrt{2} \mathrm{~cm} \\
& =30 \sqrt{2} \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~m}
\end{aligned}$
$\text { and } q=8 \times 10^{-3} \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{C}$
Also, $\quad \mathbf{r}=(30 \hat{\mathbf{i}}+30 \hat{\mathbf{j}}) \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~m}$
$\begin{aligned}
\text { So, } \mathrm{E} & =\frac{9 \times 10^9 \times 8 \times 10^{-3} \times 10^{-6}}{\left(30 \sqrt{2} \times 10^{-2}\right)^3} \times(30 \hat{\mathbf{i}}+30 \hat{\mathbf{j}}) \times 10^{-2} \\
& =\frac{9 \times 8 \times 10^9 \times 10^{-11}}{27 \times 2 \sqrt{2} \times 10^{-6} \times 10^3} \times 30(\hat{\mathbf{i}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \\
& =\frac{9 \times 8 \times 10^2 \times 3}{27 \times 2 \sqrt{2}}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \\
& =200 \sqrt{2}(\hat{\mathbf{i}}+\hat{\mathbf{j}}) \mathrm{NC}^{-1}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A dust particle of mass $4 \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{mg}$ in suspended in air under the influence of an electric field of $50 \mathrm{~N} / \mathrm{C}$ directed vertically upwards. How many electrons were removed from the neutral dust particle? [Take, $g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ ${ }^2$ ]

**Options:**

- **(A)** 15
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** 5
- **(D)** 4

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 5**

**Detailed Solution:**

Mass of dust particle, $m=4 \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{mg}$
$\begin{aligned}
& =4 \times 10^{-12} \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~g} \\
& =4 \times 10^{-12} \times 10^{-3} \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~kg} \\
& =4 \times 10^{-18} \mathrm{~kg}
\end{aligned}$
Electric field, $E=50 \mathrm{~N} / \mathrm{C}$
Weight of dust particle, $W=m g$
$=4 \times 10^{-18} \times 10=4 \times 10^{-17} \mathrm{~N}$
Electric force experienced by dust particle,
$\begin{aligned}
& F_e=q E \\
& F_e=n e \cdot E=n \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 50
\end{aligned}$
where, $n$ is the number of electrons removed from neutral dust particle.
$\therefore$ At balance condition,
$\begin{aligned}
& \text { Electric force }=\text { Weight of dust particle } \\
& n \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 50=4 \times 10^{-17} \\
& n=\frac{4 \times 10^{-17}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 50} \\
& =\frac{400}{80}=5
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2016

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The surface charge density of a thin charged disc of radius $\mathrm{R}$ is $\sigma$. The value of the electric field at the centre of the disc is $\frac{\sigma}{2 \epsilon_{0}}$. With respect to the field at the centre, the electric field along the axis at a distance $\mathrm{R}$ from the centre of the $\operatorname{disc}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** reduces by $70.7 \%$
- **(B)** reduces by $29.3 \%$
- **(C)** reduces by $9.7 \%$
- **(D)** reduces by $14.6 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) reduces by $70.7 \%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Electric field intensity at the centre of the disc.

\(\mathrm{E}=\frac{\sigma}{2 \epsilon_{0}} \text { (given) }\)

Electric field along the axis at any distance \(x\) from the centre of the disc

\(\mathrm{E}^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma}{2 \epsilon_{0}}\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{x}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{x}^{2}}-\mathrm{R}^{2}}\right)\)

From question, \(\mathrm{x}=\mathrm{R}\) (radius of disc)

\(\begin{aligned}

&\therefore E^{\prime}=\frac{\sigma}{2 \epsilon_{0}}\left(1-\frac{R}{\sqrt{R^{2}+R^{2}}}\right) \\

&=\frac{\sigma}{2 \epsilon_{0}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2} R-R}{\sqrt{2} R}\right) \\

&=\frac{4}{14} E

\end{aligned}\)

\(\therefore\) \% reduction in the value of electric field

\(=\frac{\left(\mathrm{E}-\frac{4}{14} \mathrm{E}\right) \times 100}{\mathrm{E}}=\frac{1000}{14} \%=70.7 \%\)

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid sphere of radius $R$ has a charge $Q$ distributed in its volume with a charge density $\rho$ $=k r^{a}$, where $k$ and $a$ are constants and $r$ is the distance from its centre. If the electric field at $r=$ $\frac{R}{2}$ is $\frac{1}{8}$ times that at $r=R,$ the value of $a$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 3
- **(B)** 5
- **(C)** 2
- **(D)** 7

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Gauss's law, we have

$$

\oint \vec{E} \cdot d \vec{A}=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}} \int(\rho d v)=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}} \int_{0}^{R} k r^{a} \times 4 \pi r^{2} d r

$$

or $E \times 4 \pi R^{2}=\left(\frac{4 \pi k}{\epsilon_{0}}\right) \frac{R^{(a+3)}}{(a+3)}$

$$

\therefore \quad E_{1}=\frac{k R^{(a+1)}}{\epsilon_{0}(a+3)}

$$

For $r=\frac{R}{2} \cdot E_{2}=\frac{k\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{a+1}}{\epsilon_{0}(a+3)}$

Given, $E_{2}=\frac{E_{1}}{8}$

or $\frac{k\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{a+1}}{\epsilon_{0}(a+3)}=\frac{1}{8} \frac{k R^{(a+1)}}{\epsilon_{0}(a+3)}$

$$

\begin{array}{ll}

\therefore & \quad \frac{1}{a+1}=\frac{1}{8} \\

\text { or } & a=2 .

\end{array}

$$

</details>

### Q8. BITSAT 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If an electron has an initial velocity in a direction different from that of an electric field, the path of the electron is

**Options:**

- **(A)** a straight line
- **(B)** a circle
- **(C)** an ellipse
- **(D)** a parabola

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) a parabola**

**Detailed Solution:**

The path is a parabola, because initial velocity can be resolved into two rectangular components, one along $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{E}}$ and other $\perp$ to $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{E}}$. The former decreases at a constant rate and latter is unaffected. The resultant path is therefore a parabola.

</details>
