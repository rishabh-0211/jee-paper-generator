### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A cube of ice floats partly in water and partly in kerosene oil. The radio of volume of ice immersed in water to that in kerosene oil (specific gravity of Kerosene oil $=0.8$, specific gravity of ice $=0.9)$
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/85A7ady0FX_q5EBVSVK_nfGSZm0-PuyhFV4f2W_GwFw.original.fullsize.png"/>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8: 9$
- **(B)** $5: 4$
- **(C)** $9: 10$
- **(D)** $1: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $V_1$ be, the volume immersed in water.
$V_2$ be the volume immersed in oil.
In equilibrium condition,
$v_1 \rho_w g+v_2 \rho_0 g=\left(v_1+v_2\right) \rho_e g$
$v_1+\frac{v_2 \rho_0}{\rho_w}=\left(v_1+v_2\right) \frac{\rho_c}{\rho_w}$
$\Rightarrow v_1+0.8 v_2=0.9 v_1+0.9 v_2 \Rightarrow 0.1 v_1=0.1 v_2$
$\Rightarrow v_1: v_2=1: 1$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2020

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A body of density $\rho^{\prime}$ is dropped from rest at a height h into a lake of density $\rho$ where $\rho>\rho^{\prime}$ neglecting all dissipative forces, calculate the maximum depth to which the body sinks before returning to float on the surface:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\rho-\rho^{\prime}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{h} \rho^{\prime}}{\rho}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{h} \rho^{\prime}}{\rho-\rho^{\prime}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{h \rho}{\rho-\rho^{\prime}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{\mathrm{h} \rho^{\prime}}{\rho-\rho^{\prime}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The effective acceleration of the body

$\mathrm{g}^{\prime}=\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}\right) \mathrm{g}$

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/oEm_LCHuM0OzQE1UaLX4fufNHlvQQinJ9ebj1CpNf08.original.fullsize.png">


Now, the depth to which the body sinks

$\mathrm{h}^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{u}^{2}}{2 \mathrm{~g}^{\prime}}\right)=\frac{2 \mathrm{gh}}{2 \mathrm{~g}^{\prime}}=\frac{\mathrm{gh}}{\mathrm{g}^{\prime}}=\frac{\mathrm{h} \times \rho^{\prime}}{\rho-\rho^{\prime}}$

</details>
