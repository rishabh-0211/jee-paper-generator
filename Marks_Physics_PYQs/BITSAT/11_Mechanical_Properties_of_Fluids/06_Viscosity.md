### Q1. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Eight equal drops of water are falling through air with a steady speed of $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$. If the drops coalesce, the new velocity is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(B)** $40 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(C)** $16 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(D)** $5 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $40 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\mathrm{r}$ be the radius of small drops of water.
$\mathrm{R}=$ radius of big drop formed as volume remain same.
$\therefore 8 . \frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{r}^3-\frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{R}^3 \Rightarrow \mathrm{R}=2 \mathrm{r}$
Terminal velocity,
$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{T}}=\frac{2}{9 \eta}(\rho-\sigma) \mathrm{r}^2 \mathrm{~g} \\
& \therefore \mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{T}} \propto \mathrm{r}^2 \\
& \therefore \frac{\mathrm{v}_1}{\mathrm{v}_2}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{r}}{\mathrm{R}}\right)^2 \\
& \Rightarrow \frac{10}{\mathrm{v}_2}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2\left(\because \mathrm{v}_1=10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s} \text { given }\right) \\
& \quad \Rightarrow \mathrm{v}_2=40 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Water is flowing on a horizontal fixed surface, such that its flow velocity varies with $y$ (vertical direction) as $v=k\left(\frac{2 y^{2}}{a^{2}}-\frac{y^{3}}{a^{3}}\right) .$ If coefficient of viscosity for water is $\eta$, what will be shear stress between layers of water at $y=a$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\eta k}{a}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\eta}{k a}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\eta a}{k}$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{\eta k}{a}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Newton's law of viscosity, $F=\eta A \frac{d v}{d y}$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\text { Stress }=\frac{F}{A}=\eta\left(\frac{d v}{d y}\right)=\eta k\left(\frac{4 y}{a^{2}}-\frac{3 y^{2}}{a^{3}}\right) \\

\text { At } y=a, \text { stress }=\eta k\left(\frac{4}{a}-\frac{3}{a}\right)=\frac{\eta k}{a}

\end{array}

$$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A large drop of oil (density $0.8 \mathrm{~g} / \mathrm{cm}^{3}$ and viscosity $\eta_{0}$ ) floats up through a column of another liquid (density $1.2 \mathrm{~g} / \mathrm{cm}^{3}$ and viscosity $\eta_{\mathrm{L}}$ ). Assuming that the two liquids do not mix, the velocity with which the oil drop rises will depend on:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\eta_{0}$ only
- **(B)** $\eta_{L}$ only
- **(C)** both on $\eta_{0}$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{L}}$
- **(D)** neither $\eta_{0}$ nor $\eta_{L}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\eta_{L}$ only**

**Detailed Solution:**

Forces of viscosity arise in a fluid when different sections of the fluid move with different velocities. Here, there is no such motion inside the oil drop and hence the viscosity of oil does not come into play.

</details>
