### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

For a particle inside a uniform spherical shell, the gravitational force on the particle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** infinite
- **(B)** zero
- **(C)** $\frac{-G m_1 m_2}{r^2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{Gm}_1 \mathrm{~m}_2}{\mathrm{r}^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) zero**

**Detailed Solution:**

Various regions of spherical shell attract the point mass inside it in various directions. These forces cancel each other completely. Therefore the gravitational force on the particle is zero.

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2016

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

What should be the velocity of rotation of earth due to rotation about its own axis so that the weight of a person becomes $\frac{3}{5}$ of the present weight at the equator. Equatorial radius of the earth is $6400 \mathrm{~km}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8.7 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(B)** $7.8 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(C)** $6.7 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(D)** $7.4 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $7.8 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

True weight at equator, $W=m g$ Observed weight at equator,

$$

\mathrm{W}^{\prime}=\mathrm{mg}^{\prime}=\frac{3}{5} \mathrm{mg}

$$

At equator, latitude $\lambda=0$ Using the formula,

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\mathrm{mg}^{\prime}=\mathrm{mg}-\mathrm{mR} \omega^{2} \cos ^{2} \lambda \\

\frac{3}{5} \mathrm{mg}=\mathrm{mg}-\mathrm{mR} \omega^{2} \cos ^{2} 0=\mathrm{mg}-\mathrm{mR} \omega^{2} \\

\Rightarrow \mathrm{mR} \omega^{2}=\mathrm{mg}-\frac{3}{5} \mathrm{mg}=\frac{2}{5} \mathrm{mg} \\

\therefore \quad \omega=\left(\frac{2}{5} \frac{\mathrm{g}}{\mathrm{R}}\right)^{1 / 2} \\

=\left(\frac{2 \times 9.8}{5 \times 6.4 \times 10^{6}}\right)^{1 / 2}=7.8 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}

\end{array}

$$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A man of mass m starts falling towards a planet of mass $\mathrm{M}$ and radius $\mathrm{R}$. As he reaches near to the surface, he realizes that he will pass through a small hole in the planet. As he enters the hole, he sees that the planet is really made of two pieces a spherical shell of negligible thickness of mass $2 \mathrm{M} / 3$ and a point mass $\mathrm{M} / 3$ at the centre. Change in the force of gravity experienced by the man is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{2}{3} \frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{\mathrm{R}^{2}}$
- **(B)** 0
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{3} \frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{\mathrm{R}^{2}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{4}{3} \frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{\mathrm{R}^{2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{2}{3} \frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{\mathrm{R}^{2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\quad$ Change in force of gravity
$$
=\frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{\mathrm{R}^{2}}-\frac{\mathrm{G} \frac{\mathrm{M}}{3} \mathrm{~m}}{\mathrm{R}^{2}}
$$
(only due to mass $\mathrm{M} / 3$ due to shell gravitational field is zero (inside the shell))
$$
=\frac{2 \mathrm{GMm}}{3 \mathrm{R}^{2}}
$$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Suppose the gravitational force varies inversely as the nth power of distance. Then the time period of a planet in circular orbit of radius ' $\mathrm{R}$ ' around the sun will be proportional to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{R}^{\mathrm{n}}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{R}^{\left(\frac{\mathrm{n}-1}{2}\right)}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{R}\left(\frac{\mathrm{n}+1}{2}\right)$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{R}^{\left(\frac{\mathrm{n}-2}{2}\right)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{R}\left(\frac{\mathrm{n}+1}{2}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{KR}^{-\mathrm{n}}=\mathrm{MR} \omega^{2} \Rightarrow \omega^{2}=\mathrm{KR}^{-(\mathrm{n}+1)}
$$
or $\omega=\mathrm{K}^{\prime} \mathrm{R}^{\frac{-(\mathrm{n}+1)}{2}}$
[where $\mathrm{K}^{\prime}=\mathrm{K}^{1 / 2}$, a constant $]$
$\frac{2 \pi}{T} \alpha R^{\frac{-(n+1)}{2}} \quad \therefore T \alpha R^{\frac{(n+1)}{2}}$

</details>
