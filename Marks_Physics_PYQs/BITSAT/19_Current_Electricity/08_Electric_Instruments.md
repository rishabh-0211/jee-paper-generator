### Q1. BITSAT 2022

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

In the arrangement shown in figure, when the switch $S_2$ is open, the galvanometer, shows no deflection for $l=50 \mathrm{~cm}$ when the switch $S_2$ is closed, the galvanometer shows no deflection for $l=0.416 \mathrm{~m}$. The internal resistance $(r)$ of $6 \mathrm{~V}$ cell is
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/RMx7mHemGAXNHHKGr7KSr50nRFhQiPlDYK2aY2iwQck.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \Omega$
- **(B)** $3 \Omega$
- **(C)** $5 \Omega$
- **(D)** $9 \Omega$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2 \Omega$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When $S_2$ open.
Assume resistance of $X Y=R$.
Resistance of wire per unit length, $x=\frac{R}{L}=R \Omega \mathrm{m}^{-1}$
$\because \quad I=E_0 / R$
Now, the potential drop across $50 \mathrm{~cm}$ length is $6 \mathrm{~V}$, So
$$
\begin{array}{cc} 
& \frac{E_0}{R} \times R \times \frac{50}{100}=6 \\
\Rightarrow & E_0=12 \mathrm{~V}
\end{array}
$$
When $S_2$ closed, potential drop across $0.416 \mathrm{~cm}$ length,
$$
V_1=\frac{E_0}{R} \times R \times 0.416=12 \times 0.416 \approx 5 \mathrm{~V}
$$
Hence, $\quad E-I r=5 \mathrm{~V}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Rightarrow & & 6-I r & =5 \\
& \because & I & =\frac{5}{10} \\
\therefore & & 6-5 & =\frac{5}{10} r \\
\Rightarrow & & r & =2 \Omega
\end{aligned}
$$

### Q4. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

For the circuit (figure), the current is to be measured. The ammeter shown is a galvanometer with a resistance $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{G}}=60.00 \Omega$ converted to an ammeter by a shunt resistance $\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{s}}=0.02 \Omega$. The value of the current is

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/vfB-JKwpncum6oMHA9UuZzsQQkqqNUDFpaf7OsxGMHQ.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.79 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(B)** $0.29 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(C)** $0.99 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(D)** $0.8 \mathrm{~A}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.99 \mathrm{~A}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{G}}=60.00 \Omega,$ shunt resistance, $\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{s}}=0.02 \Omega$

Total resistance in the circuit is $\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{G}}+3=63 \Omega$ Hence, $\mathrm{I}=3 / 63=0.048 \mathrm{~A}$

Resistance of the galvanometer converted to an ammeter is,

$$

\frac{\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{G}} \mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{s}}}{\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{G}}+\mathrm{r}_{\mathrm{s}}}=\frac{60 \Omega \times 0.02 \Omega}{(60+0.02) \Omega}=0.02 \Omega

$$

Total resistance in the circuit $=0.02+3$

$$

=3.02 \Omega

$$

Hence, $\mathrm{I}=3 / 3.02=0.99 \mathrm{~A}$

</details>

### Q6. BITSAT 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The length of a potentiometer wire is $\ell$. A cell of emf $E$ is balanced at a length $\ell / 3$ from the positive end of the wire. If the length of the wire is increased by $\ell / 2 .$ At what distance will be the same cell give a balance point.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \ell / 3$
- **(B)** $\ell / 2$
- **(C)** $\quad \ell / 6$
- **(D)** $4 \ell / 3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\ell / 2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Potential gradient in the first case $=\frac{\mathrm{E}_{0}}{\ell}$ $$
\therefore \quad \mathrm{E}=\left(\frac{\ell}{3}\right) \cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{E}_{0}}{\ell}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{E}_{0}}{3}
$$
Potential gradient in second case
$$
=\frac{E_{0}}{3 \ell / 2} \text { (x) } \frac{2 E_{0}}{3 \ell}
$$
From equations (i) and (ii),
$$
\frac{\mathrm{E}_{0}}{3}-\left(\frac{2 \mathrm{E}_{0}}{3 \ell}\right) \mathrm{x} \Rightarrow \mathrm{x}=\frac{\ell}{2}
$$

</details>
