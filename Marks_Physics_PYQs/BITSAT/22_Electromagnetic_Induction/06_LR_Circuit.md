### Q1. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

In an LC oscillator, if values of inductance and capacitance become twice and eight times, respectively, then the resonant frequency of oscillator becomes $\mathrm{x}$ times its initial resonant frequency $\omega_0$. The value of $x$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 / 4$
- **(B)** 16
- **(C)** $1 / 16$
- **(D)** 4

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1 / 4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The resonance frequency of LC oscillations circuit is
$\omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{L}^{\prime} \mathrm{C}^{\prime}}} \Rightarrow \mathrm{L}^{\prime} \rightarrow 2 \mathrm{~L}$
$\mathrm{C}^{\prime} \rightarrow 8 \mathrm{C}$
$\omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2 \mathrm{~L} \times 8 \mathrm{C}}}=\frac{1}{4 \sqrt{\mathrm{LC}}} 9 \omega_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathrm{LC}}}$
$\omega=\frac{\omega_0}{4}$ So, $x=\frac{1}{4}$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2016

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

An inductor of inductance $L=400 \mathrm{mH}$ and resistors of resistance $R_{1}=2 \Omega$ and $R_{2}=2 \Omega$ are connected to a battery of emf $12 \mathrm{~V}$ as shown in the figure. The internal resistance of the battery is negligible. The switch $S$ is closed at $t=0 .$ The potential drop across $L$ as a function of time is

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/7X35j8e3bYNhi279A5VMee8ezq0Qbq7rDaH9QG_3jeg.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{12}{t} e^{-3 t} \mathrm{~V}$
- **(B)** $6\left(1-e^{-t / 0.2}\right) \mathrm{V}$
- **(C)** $12 \mathrm{e}^{-5 t} \mathrm{~V}$
- **(D)** $6 \mathrm{e}^{-5 t} \mathrm{~V}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $12 \mathrm{e}^{-5 t} \mathrm{~V}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Growth in current in $L R_{2}$ branch when switch is closed is given by

$$

\begin{array}{l}

i=\frac{E}{R_{2}}\left[1-e^{-R_{2} t / L}\right] \\

\Rightarrow \frac{d i}{d t}=\frac{E}{R_{2}} \cdot \frac{R_{2}}{L} e^{-R_{2} t / L}=\frac{E}{L} e^{-\frac{R_{2} t}{L}}

\end{array}

$$

Hence, potential drop across $\mathbf{L}=\left(\frac{E}{L} e^{-R_{2} t / L}\right) L=E e^{-R_{2} t / L}$

$=12 e^{-\frac{2 t}{400 \times 10^{-3}}}=12 \mathrm{e}^{-5 t} \mathrm{~V}$

</details>
