### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The magnifying power of a telescope is 9 . When it is adjusted for parallel rays, the distance between the objective and eyepiece is 20 cm . The ratio of focal length of objective lens to focal length of eyepiece is found to be $m$, then the value of $m$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 8
- **(B)** 7
- **(C)** 9
- **(D)** 12

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 9**

**Detailed Solution:**

Magnifying power of telescope,m = 9
$\begin{array}{ll}
\Rightarrow & \frac{f_0}{f_e}=9 \\
\Rightarrow & f_0=9 f_e \ldots(i)
\end{array}$
Distance between objective and eyepiece is given as
$\begin{aligned}
& f_0+f_e=20 \ldots(ii)\\
& \Rightarrow \quad 9 f_e+f_e=20 \\
& \text { [from Eq. (i)] } \\
& \Rightarrow \quad 10 f_e=20 \\
& \Rightarrow \quad f_e=2 \mathrm{~cm}
\end{aligned}$
$\begin{aligned} & \therefore \text { From Eq. (i), } f_0=9 \times 2=18 \mathrm{~cm} \\ & \therefore \quad \frac{f_0}{f_e}=\frac{18}{2}=9\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

In normal adjustment, for a refracting telescope, the distance between objective and eye piece is 30 cm . The focal length of the objective, when the angular magnification of the telescope is 2 , will be:

**Options:**

- **(A)** 20 cm
- **(B)** 30 cm
- **(C)** 10 cm
- **(D)** 15 cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 20 cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\because \mathrm{f}_0+\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}=30$
And magnification, $\mathrm{m}=\frac{\mathrm{f}_0}{\mathrm{f}_e}$
$2=\frac{\mathrm{f}_0}{\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}} \Rightarrow \mathrm{f}_0=2 \mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}} \Rightarrow \mathrm{f}_0+\frac{\mathrm{f}_0}{2}=30 \therefore \mathrm{f}_0=20cm$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a reflecting telescope, a secondary mirror is used to:

**Options:**

- **(A)** reduce the problem of mechanical support
- **(B)** remove spherical aberration
- **(C)** make chromatic aberration zero
- **(D)** move the eyepiece outside the telescopic tube

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) move the eyepiece outside the telescopic tube**

**Detailed Solution:**

(d) To redirect the light that enters the telescope to the eyepiece or camera. The primary mirror of a reflecting telescopes gathers the light and reflects towards the secondary mirror which then reflect the light towards the eyepiece allowing the observer to see image.
It has advantage of a large focal length in a short telescope.

### Q4. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The magnifying power of a telescope is $9 .$ When it is adjusted for parallel rays, the distance between the objective and the eye piece is found to be $20 \mathrm{~cm}$. The focal length of lenses are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $18 \mathrm{~cm}, 2 \mathrm{~cm}$

- **(C)** $10 \mathrm{~cm}, 10 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(D)** $15 \mathrm{~cm}, 5 \mathrm{~cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $18 \mathrm{~cm}, 2 \mathrm{~cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\frac{\mathrm{f}_{0}}{\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}}=9, \quad \therefore \mathrm{f}_{0}=9 \mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}$

Also $\mathrm{f}_{0}+\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}=20(\because$ final image is at infinity $)$ $9 \mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}+\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}=20, \mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{e}}=2 \mathrm{~cm}, \quad \therefore \mathrm{f}_{0}=18 \mathrm{~cm}$

</details>
