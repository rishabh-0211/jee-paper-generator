### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The straight wire $A B$ carries a current $I$. The ends of the wire subtend angles $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ at the point $P$ as shown in figure. The magnetic field at the point $P$ is
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/eIzo0PjwrZuNUPihCK6GCTDX5gBm4fw_IxGyD4vWajA.original.fullsize.png"/>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d}\left(\sin \theta_1-\sin \theta_2\right)$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d}\left(\sin \theta_1+\sin \theta_2\right)$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d}\left(\cos \theta_1-\cos \theta_2\right)$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d}\left(\cos \theta_1+\cos \theta_2\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d}\left(\sin \theta_1-\sin \theta_2\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field at a point $P$ due to a current carrying straight wire, such that the ends of the wires, makes angle $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (see figure) is given by
$B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d}(\sin \alpha-\sin \beta)$
where, $d$ is the distance between the point $P$ and wire.
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/2CI_LkSVH326lX_ZCnfCzZtly31MBjgQ2LMqfxWopw0.original.fullsize.png"/>
In the given problem, $\alpha=\theta_1$ and $\beta=\theta_2$
$\therefore \quad B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{4 \pi d}\left(\sin \theta_1-\sin \theta_2\right)$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A long straight wire of radius a carries a steady current 1 . The current is uniformly distributed across its cross section. The ratio of the magnetic field at $\frac{\mathrm{a}}{2}$ and 2a from axis of the wire is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 4$
- **(B)** $4: 1$
- **(C)** $1: 1$
- **(D)** $3: 4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Magnetic field due to straight wire,
$B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{2 \pi r}$
Magnetic field at $\frac{a}{2}$ is,
$\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{a} / 2}=\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{I}}{2 \pi(\mathrm{a} / 2)}$
$\therefore \frac{\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{a} / 2}}{\mathrm{~B}_{2 \mathrm{a}}}=\frac{1}{1}=1: 1$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A solenoid of 1200 turns is wound uniformly in a single layer on a glass tube $2 \mathrm{~m}$ long and $0.2 \mathrm{~m}$ in diameter. The magnetic intensity at the center of the solenoid when a current of 2 A flows through it is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.4 \times 10^3 \mathrm{Am}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $1.2 \times 10^3 \mathrm{Am}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $1 \mathrm{Am}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $2.4 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~A} \mathrm{~m}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.2 \times 10^3 \mathrm{Am}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given that number of turns, $n=1200$
Current, $\mathrm{I}=2 \mathrm{~A}$
Magnetic field at centre inside the solenoid is given by,
$\mathrm{B}=\mu_0 \mathrm{nI}$
So magnetic intensity at centre of the solenoid,
$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{H}=\frac{\mathrm{B}}{\mu_0}=\mathrm{nI}=\left(\frac{1200}{2}\right)(2) \quad\left(\because \mathrm{B}=\mu_0 \mathrm{H}\right) \\
& \mathrm{H}=1.2 \times 10^3 \mathrm{Am}^{-1}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2021

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A solenoid is $2 \mathrm{~m}$ long and $3 \mathrm{~cm}$ in diameter. It has 5 layers of winding of 1000 turns each and carries a current of $5 \mathrm{~A}$. What is the magnetic field at its centre ? Use the standard value of $\mu_{0}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.57 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~T}$
- **(B)** $2.57 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~T}$
- **(C)** $1.57 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~T}$
- **(D)** $3.57 \times 10^{-6} \mathrm{~T}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.57 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~T}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Length of solenoid, $l=2 \mathrm{~m}$

Total number of turns, $\mathrm{N}=5 \times 1000=5000$

Number of turns per unit length,

$\mathrm{n}=\frac{\mathrm{N}}{l}=\frac{5000}{2}=2500 \mathrm{~m}^{-1}$

Current, I = 5A

Now, B $=\mu_{0} \mathrm{nI}=4 \pi \times 10^{-7} \times 2500 \times 5 \mathrm{~T}$

$=1.57 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~T}$

</details>

### Q8. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Two parallel conductors carry current in opposite directions as shown in figure. One conductor carries a current of $10.0 \mathrm{~A}$. Point $\mathrm{C}$ is a distance

$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{2}$ to the right of the $10.0 \mathrm{~A}$ current. If $\mathrm{d}=18 \mathrm{~cm}$ and I is adjusted so that the magnetic field at $\mathrm{Cis}$ zero, the value of the current $I$ is

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/RdRd2MV4dwY_55HYwA4zd3TvFOevZEZcgRkmRJagfQM.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10.0 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(B)** $30.0 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(C)** $8.0 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(D)** $18.0 \mathrm{~A}$

#

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $30.0 \mathrm{~A}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field at $\mathrm{C}$ due to first conductor

is $\mathrm{B}_{1}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2 \pi} \frac{\mathrm{I}}{3 \mathrm{~d} / 2}$ (since, point $\mathrm{C}$ is separated

by $\mathrm{d}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{2}=\frac{3 \mathrm{~d}}{2}$ from 1 st conductor). The direction of field is perpendicular to the plane of paper and directed outwards. The magnetic field at $\mathrm{C}$ due to second conductor is $\mathrm{B}_{2}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2 \pi} \frac{10}{\mathrm{~d} / 2}$ (since, point $\mathrm{C}$

is separated by $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{2}$ from 2 nd conductor) The direction of field is perpendicular to the plane of paper and directed inwards. Since, direction of $\mathrm{B}_{1}$ and $\mathrm{B}_{2}$ at point $\mathrm{C}$ is in opposite direction and the magnetic field at C is zero, therefore,

$$

\begin{array}{c}

\mathrm{B}_{1}=\mathrm{B}_{2} \\

\frac{\mu_{0}}{2 \pi} \frac{\mathrm{I}}{3 \mathrm{~d} / 2}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2 \pi} \frac{10}{\mathrm{~d} / 2}

\end{array}

$$

On solving $\mathrm{I}=30.0 \mathrm{~A}$

</details>
