### Q1. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `multipleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Consider two isosceles prisms 1 and 2 with prism angles $A_1$ and $A_2$ and refractive indices $n_1$ and $n_2$, respectively, as shown in the figure. The faces $a_1 b_1$ and $a_2 b_2$ are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the mirror $M$. If a ray of light is incident on the face $a_1 c_1$ and emerges from the face $a_2 c_2$, then the correct statement(s) is/are:
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/jee_adv/2026/watermarked_images/p2_english__q23_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** If both the prisms are at minimum deviation condition, then $\dfrac{n_2}{n_1} = \sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right) / \sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right)$.
- **(B)** If prism 2 is at minimum deviation condition, then $\sin i_1 = n_2 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right)$ is always true.
- **(C)** If both the prisms 1 and 2 are thin and are at minimum deviation condition with angles of deviation $\delta_{m1}$ and $\delta_{m2}$, respectively, then $\theta = \dfrac{\delta_{m1}}{2(n_1 - 1)} + \dfrac{\delta_{m2}}{2(n_2 - 1)}$.
- **(D)** If prism 1 is at minimum deviation condition, then $\sin i_2 = n_1 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right)$ is always true.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) If prism 1 is at minimum deviation condition, then $\sin i_2 = n_1 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right)$ is always true.**

**Detailed Solution:**

From the given geometry, faces $a_1b_1$ and $a_2b_2$ are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the horizontal mirror $M$. Thus, $a_1b_1$ and $a_2b_2$ are vertical.

The normal to $a_1b_1$ is horizontal. The ray emerging from Prism 1 makes an angle $e_1$ with this horizontal normal. By the law of reflection at mirror $M$, the reflected ray also makes an angle $e_1$ with the horizontal.

This reflected ray is incident on the vertical face $a_2b_2$ of Prism 2. The normal to $a_2b_2$ is horizontal, so the angle of incidence is $i_2 = e_1$.

Evaluating option (4): If Prism 1 is at minimum deviation, $i_1 = e_1$. The condition for minimum deviation gives $\sin i_1 = n_1 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right)$. Since $i_2 = e_1$, it follows that $i_2 = i_1$, yielding $\sin i_2 = n_1 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right)$. Statement (4) is correct.

Evaluating option (1): If both prisms are at minimum deviation, $i_1 = e_1$ and $i_2 = e_2$. Thus $i_1 = e_1 = i_2 = e_2$. Using the minimum deviation formula, $\sin i_1 = n_1 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right)$ and $\sin i_2 = n_2 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right)$. Equating them gives $n_1 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right) = n_2 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right) \Rightarrow \dfrac{n_2}{n_1} = \dfrac{\sin\left(\dfrac{A_1}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right)}$. Statement (1) is correct.

Evaluating option (2): If only Prism 2 is at minimum deviation, $\sin i_2 = n_2 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right)$. Since $i_2 = e_1$, we get $\sin e_1 = n_2 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right)$, which does not imply $\sin i_1 = n_2 \sin\left(\dfrac{A_2}{2}\right)$ in general. Statement (2) is incorrect.

Evaluating option (3): The dashed lines in the figure bisect the prism angles $A_1$ and $A_2$. Since faces $a_1b_1$ and $a_2b_2$ are vertical, the angle bisector of Prism 1 makes an angle $\dfrac{A_1}{2}$ with the vertical, and the angle bisector of Prism 2 makes an angle $\dfrac{A_2}{2}$ with the vertical.

The angle $\theta$ between these bisectors is $\theta = \dfrac{A_1}{2} + \dfrac{A_2}{2}$.

For thin prisms at minimum deviation, the deviation is $\delta_m = (n - 1)A$, giving $A_1 = \dfrac{\delta_{m1}}{n_1 - 1}$ and $A_2 = \dfrac{\delta_{m2}}{n_2 - 1}$.

Substituting these into the expression for $\theta$, we get $\theta = \dfrac{\delta_{m1}}{2(n_1 - 1)} + \dfrac{\delta_{m2}}{2(n_2 - 1)}$. Statement (3) is correct.

### Q2. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A beam of polychromatic light passes through a thin prism of prism angle $6^\circ$. The refractive index of the material of the prism varies with wavelength $(\lambda)$ as $n(\lambda) = \alpha\lambda + \dfrac{\beta}{\lambda^2}$, where $\alpha = 3\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ and $\beta = 0.096\ \mu\text{m}^2$. If $\lambda_{\min}$ is the wavelength at which the angle of minimum deviation $D_m$ is smallest, then the correct value of $D_m$ at $\lambda_{\min}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.4^\circ$
- **(B)** $4.8^\circ$
- **(C)** $3.2^\circ$
- **(D)** $2.4^\circ$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4.8^\circ$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a thin prism, the angle of deviation is given by $D = (n - 1)A$.

To find the smallest value of deviation $D_m$, we need to find the minimum value of the refractive index $n(\lambda)$.

Given $n(\lambda) = \alpha\lambda + \dfrac{\beta}{\lambda^2}$.

Differentiating $n(\lambda)$ with respect to $\lambda$ and equating to zero for minimum:

$\dfrac{dn}{d\lambda} = \alpha - \dfrac{2\beta}{\lambda^3} = 0$

$\lambda^3 = \dfrac{2\beta}{\alpha}$

Substituting the given values $\alpha = 3\ \mu\text{m}^{-1}$ and $\beta = 0.096\ \mu\text{m}^2$:

$\lambda^3 = \dfrac{2 \times 0.096}{3} = 0.064$

$\lambda = 0.4\ \mu\text{m}$

Now, substituting $\lambda = 0.4\ \mu\text{m}$ back into the expression for $n(\lambda)$:

$n_{\min} = 3(0.4) + \dfrac{0.096}{(0.4)^2}$

$n_{\min} = 1.2 + \dfrac{0.096}{0.16} = 1.2 + 0.6 = 1.8$

The smallest angle of deviation is:

$D_m = (n_{\min} - 1)A$

$D_m = (1.8 - 1) \times 6^\circ = 0.8 \times 6^\circ = 4.8^\circ$

Answer: $4.8^\circ$

</details>
