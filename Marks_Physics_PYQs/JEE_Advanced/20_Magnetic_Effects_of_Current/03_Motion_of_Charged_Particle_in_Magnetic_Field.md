### Q1. JEE Advanced 2026 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `multipleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

In a vacuum chamber, a particle of charge $1\ \mu\text{C}$ and mass $1$ mg is projected with a velocity $(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j})$ ms$^{-1}$ from the $XZ$ plane at time $t = 0$ in an electric field of $1\hat{i}$ Vm$^{-1}$. At $t = 0.2$ s, the electric field is switched off and a magnetic field of $6\hat{j}$ T is switched on. The acceleration due to gravity is $-10\hat{j}$ ms$^{-2}$. Correct option(s) is/are:

**Options:**

- **(A)** The vertical distance of the particle from the $XZ$ plane at $t = 0.3$ s is $15$ cm.
- **(B)** The vertical distance of the particle from the $XZ$ plane at $t = 0.4$ s is $10$ cm.
- **(C)** The radius of the trajectory of the particle for $t > 0.2$ s is $20$ cm.
- **(D)** The particle will be in the $XZ$ plane at $t = 0.35$ s.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The radius of the trajectory of the particle for $t > 0.2$ s is $20$ cm.**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the time interval $0 \le t \le 0.2$ s, the particle is under the influence of the electric field and gravity.
The charge-to-mass ratio of the particle is $\dfrac{q}{m} = \dfrac{10^{-6}}{10^{-6}} = 1$ C kg$^{-1}$.

The acceleration of the particle is:
$\vec{a} = \dfrac{q\vec{E}}{m} + \vec{g} = 1(\hat{i}) - 10\hat{j} = \hat{i} - 10\hat{j}$ ms$^{-2}$

Given the initial velocity $\vec{v}_0 = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j}$ ms$^{-1}$ and initial vertical position $y_0 = 0$ (since it is projected from the $XZ$ plane), we can find the velocity and position at $t = 0.2$ s.

Velocity at $t = 0.2$ s:
$\vec{v}(0.2) = \vec{v}_0 + \vec{a}t = (\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}) + (\hat{i} - 10\hat{j})(0.2) = 1.2\hat{i}$ ms$^{-1}$

Vertical position at $t = 0.2$ s:
$y(0.2) = y_0 + v_{0y}t + \dfrac{1}{2}a_y t^2 = 0 + 2(0.2) - \dfrac{1}{2}(10)(0.2)^2 = 0.4 - 0.2 = 0.2$ m $= 20$ cm

For $t > 0.2$ s, the electric field is switched off and the magnetic field $\vec{B} = 6\hat{j}$ T is switched on. The magnetic force $\vec{F}_m = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$ acts only in the $XZ$ plane because $\vec{B}$ is along the $y$-axis. The vertical motion is only affected by gravity.

Let $t' = t - 0.2$ be the time elapsed after the fields are switched. The initial vertical velocity for this phase is $v_y(0.2) = 0$.
The vertical position as a function of $t'$ is:
$y(t') = y(0.2) + v_y(0.2)t' - \dfrac{1}{2}gt'^2 = 0.2 - 5t'^2$

Evaluating the options:
At $t = 0.3$ s ($t' = 0.1$ s):
$y(0.1) = 0.2 - 5(0.1)^2 = 0.2 - 0.05 = 0.15$ m $= 15$ cm. (Option A is correct)

At $t = 0.4$ s ($t' = 0.2$ s):
$y(0.2) = 0.2 - 5(0.2)^2 = 0.2 - 0.2 = 0$ m $= 0$ cm. (Option B is incorrect)

At $t = 0.35$ s ($t' = 0.15$ s):
$y(0.15) = 0.2 - 5(0.15)^2 = 0.2 - 0.1125 = 0.0875$ m $\neq 0$. (Option D is incorrect)

For the radius of the trajectory for $t > 0.2$ s, the particle moves in a helical path. The radius of the circular projection in the $XZ$ plane is determined by the velocity perpendicular to the magnetic field, $v_{\perp} = 1.2$ ms$^{-1}$.
$R = \dfrac{mv_{\perp}}{qB} = \dfrac{10^{-6} \times 1.2}{10^{-6} \times 6} = 0.2$ m $= 20$ cm. (Option C is correct)

Answer: The vertical distance of the particle from the $XZ$ plane at $t = 0.3$ s is $15$ cm.; The radius of the trajectory of the particle for $t > 0.2$ s is $20$ cm.

</details>

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Average speed along the \(\mathrm{x}\)-axis
\(\left(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{x}}\right)=\frac{\int\left|\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}}_{\mathrm{x}}\right| \mathrm{dt}}{\int \mathrm{dt}}=\frac{\mathrm{d}_1+\mathrm{d}_2}{\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{t}_2} \rightarrow(1)\)

\(\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{r}_1=\frac{\mathrm{mv}}{\mathrm{qB}_1}, \mathrm{r}_2=\frac{\mathrm{mv}}{\mathrm{qB} \mathrm{B}_2} \\
&\text { since } \mathrm{B}_1=\frac{\mathrm{B}_2}{4}
\end{aligned}\)
\(\therefore \mathrm{r}_1=4 \mathrm{r}_2 \rightarrow(2)\)

Total distance along \(\mathrm{x}\)-axis \(\mathrm{d}_1+\mathrm{d}_2=2 \mathrm{r}_1+2 \mathrm{r}_2=2\left(\mathrm{r}_1+\mathrm{r}_2\right)=2\left(5 \mathrm{r}_2\right)\)
Total time \(\mathrm{T}=\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{t}_2=5 \mathrm{t}_2\)
\(\therefore \text { Average speed }=\frac{10 \mathrm{r}_2}{5 \mathrm{t}_2}=2 \frac{\mathrm{mv}}{\mathrm{qB}_2} \times \frac{\mathrm{qB}_2}{\pi \mathrm{m}}=2\)

- **(C)** If $\theta=10^{\circ}$, the charge undergoes helical motion with its pitch increasing with time, along the $y$-axis.
- **(D)** If $\theta=90^{\circ}$, the charge undergoes linear but accelerated motion along the $y$-axis.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) If $\theta=90^{\circ}$, the charge undergoes linear but accelerated motion along the $y$-axis.**

**Detailed Solution:**

If $\theta=0^{\circ}$, the charged particle is projected along $x$-axis, due to magnetic field, $\vec{B}$ the charged particle will tend to move in a circular path in $y-z$ plane but due to force of electric field $\vec{E}$, the particle will move in a helical path with increasing pitch. Hence options (A) and (B) are wrong. If $\theta=10^{\circ}$, we can resolve velocity into two rectangular components. One along $x$-axis $\left(v \cos 10^{\circ}\right)$ and one along $y$-axis $\left(v \sin 10^{\circ}\right)$. Due to $v \cos 10^{\circ}$, the particle will move in circular path and due to $v \sin 10^{\circ}$ plus the force due to electric field, the particle will undergo helical motion with its pitch increasing.

If $\theta=90^{\circ}$, the charge is moving along the magnetic field. Therefore the force due to magnetic field is zero. But the force due to electric field will accelerate the particle along $y$-axis.
