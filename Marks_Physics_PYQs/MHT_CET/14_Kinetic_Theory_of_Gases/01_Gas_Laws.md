### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The gas in a vessel is subjected to a pressure of $20$ atm. at $27^\circ$C. Now half of the gas is released from the vessel and the temperature is raised by $50^\circ$C. The pressure of a gas in a vessel is nearly

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8$ atm.
- **(B)** $11$ atm.
- **(C)** $12$ atm.
- **(D)** $17$ atm.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $12$ atm.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the ideal gas equation $PV = nRT$.

Since the volume of the vessel remains constant, we have $\dfrac{P_1}{n_1 T_1} = \dfrac{P_2}{n_2 T_2}$.

Given:
$P_1 = 20$ atm
$T_1 = 27^\circ\text{C} = 300$ K
$n_2 = \dfrac{n_1}{2}$ (as half of the gas is released)
$T_2 = 27^\circ\text{C} + 50^\circ\text{C} = 77^\circ\text{C} = 350$ K

Substituting the values:
$\dfrac{20}{n_1 \times 300} = \dfrac{P_2}{\dfrac{n_1}{2} \times 350}$

$P_2 = 20 \times \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{350}{300}$

$P_2 = 10 \times \dfrac{7}{6} = \dfrac{35}{3} \approx 11.67$ atm.

The pressure is nearly $12$ atm.

Answer: $12$ atm.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

At constant temperature, the volume of a gas is to be decreased by 4%. The pressure must be increased by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4\%$
- **(B)** $4.16\%$
- **(C)** $8\%$
- **(D)** $3.86\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4.16\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Boyle's law, at constant temperature, $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$.

Let initial volume $V_1 = V$ and initial pressure $P_1 = P$.

Given that the volume is decreased by $4\%$, the final volume $V_2 = V - 0.04V = 0.96V$.

Substituting the values into Boyle's law equation:

$P \times V = P_2 \times 0.96V$

$P_2 = \dfrac{P}{0.96} = \dfrac{100P}{96} = \dfrac{25P}{24}$

Increase in pressure $\Delta P = P_2 - P_1 = \dfrac{25P}{24} - P = \dfrac{P}{24}$

Percentage increase in pressure $= \dfrac{\Delta P}{P_1} \times 100 = \dfrac{P/24}{P} \times 100 = \dfrac{100}{24}\% = 4.16\%$

Answer: $4.16\%$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

At $10^\circ\text{C}$, the value of density of a fixed mass of a gas (ideal) divided by its pressure is $x$. At $110^\circ\text{C}$ this ratio is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{10x}{283}$
- **(C)** $283\,x$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{283x}{383}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{283x}{383}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

From the ideal gas equation, $PV = nRT$

$PV = \dfrac{m}{M}RT$

$P = \dfrac{\rho}{M}RT$

$\dfrac{\rho}{P} = \dfrac{M}{RT}$

For a fixed mass of a given gas, $M$ and $R$ are constants, so $\dfrac{\rho}{P} \propto \dfrac{1}{T}$

At $T_1 = 10^\circ\text{C} = 283\text{ K}$, $\dfrac{\rho_1}{P_1} = x$

At $T_2 = 110^\circ\text{C} = 383\text{ K}$, let the ratio be $x'$

Since $\dfrac{\rho}{P} T = \text{constant}$, we have $x' T_2 = x T_1$

$x' \times 383 = x \times 283$

$x' = \dfrac{283x}{383}$

Answer: $\dfrac{283x}{383}$

</details>

### Q17. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

### Q25. MHT CET 2020 (20 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

We have a sample of gas characterised by $\mathrm{P}, \mathrm{V}$ and $\mathrm{T}$ and another sample of gas characterised by $2 \mathrm{P}, \mathrm{V} / 4$, and $2 \mathrm{~T}$. What is the ratio of the number of molecules in the first and second samples?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2: 1$
- **(B)** $4: 1$
- **(C)** $8: 1$
- **(D)** $16: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{array}{ll}\text { PVT } & 2 \mathrm{P}, \mathrm{V} / 4,2 \mathrm{~T}\end{array}$
$\mathrm{P}_{1} \mathrm{V}_{1}=\mathrm{n}_{1} \mathrm{RT}_{1} ; \quad \mathrm{P}_{2} \mathrm{V}_{2}=\mathrm{n}_{2} \mathrm{RT}_{2}$
$\frac{\mathrm{n}_{1}}{\mathrm{n}_{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{P}_{1} \mathrm{V}_{1} / \mathrm{RT}_{1}}{\mathrm{P}_{2} \mathrm{V}_{2} / \mathrm{RT}_{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{P}_{1} \mathrm{V}_{1}}{\mathrm{T}_{1}} \times \frac{\mathrm{T}_{2}}{\mathrm{P}_{2} \mathrm{V}_{2}}$
$=\frac{\mathrm{PV}}{\mathrm{T}} \times \frac{2 \mathrm{T} \times 2}{2 \mathrm{P} \times \frac{\mathrm{V}}{4}}=4: 1$

</details>

### Q26. MHT CET 2020 (20 Oct Shift 1)

Two ideal gases $A$ and $B$ having the same temperature $\mathrm{T}$, same pressure $\mathrm{P}$ and
same volume $\mathrm{V}$, are mixed together. If the temperature of mixture is kept constant
and the volume occupied by the mixture is reduced to $\frac{\mathrm{V}}{2}$, then the pressure of the
mixture will become

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{P}{2}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{P}$
- **(C)** $4 \mathrm{P}$
- **(D)** $2 \mathrm{P}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4 \mathrm{P}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initially $\mathrm{P}_{1}=\mathrm{P}$ 
$\mathrm{V}_{1}=\mathrm{V}+\mathrm{V}=2 \mathrm{~V}$
Finally $P_{2}=P_{1} V_{2}=\frac{V}{2}$
$\mathrm{P}_{1} \mathrm{V}_{1}=\mathrm{P}_{2} \mathrm{V}_{2}$
$\mathrm{P}_{2}=\frac{\mathrm{P}_{1} \mathrm{V}_{1}}{\mathrm{V}_{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{P} \times 2 \mathrm{~V}}{\frac{\mathrm{V}}{2}}=4 \mathrm{P}$

</details>
