If '$m$' represents the mass of each molecule of a gas and '$T$' its absolute temperature respectively, then the root mean square velocity of the gaseous molecule is proportional to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $m^{-1/2} T^{-1/2}$
- **(B)** $m^{-1/2} T^{1/2}$
- **(C)** $m^{1/2} T^{-1/2}$
- **(D)** $m^1 T^1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $m^{-1/2} T^{1/2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The root mean square velocity of a gaseous molecule is given by the formula:

$v_{rms} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3kT}{m}}$

where $k$ is the Boltzmann constant, $T$ is the absolute temperature, and $m$ is the mass of a single molecule.

From this relation, we can write:

$v_{rms} \propto \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{m}}$

$v_{rms} \propto m^{-1/2} T^{1/2}$

Answer: $m^{-1/2} T^{1/2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An ideal gas ($\gamma = 1.5$) is expanded adiabatically. To reduce the root mean square velocity of molecules two times, the gas should be expanded

**Options:**

- **(A)** $20$ times
- **(B)** $16$ times
- **(C)** $12$ times
- **(D)** $8$ times

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $16$ times**

**Detailed Solution:**

The root mean square velocity of gas molecules is given by $v_{rms} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}}$.

This implies $v_{rms} \propto \sqrt{T}$.

To reduce the rms velocity by a factor of $2$, the absolute temperature must be reduced by a factor of $4$. Thus, $T_f = \dfrac{T_i}{4}$.

For an adiabatic process, the relation between temperature and volume is $TV^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$.

Therefore, $T_i V_i^{\gamma - 1} = T_f V_f^{\gamma - 1}$.

$\left( \dfrac{V_f}{V_i} \right)^{\gamma - 1} = \dfrac{T_i}{T_f}$

Given $\gamma = 1.5$, we have $\gamma - 1 = 0.5 = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

$\left( \dfrac{V_f}{V_i} \right)^{\dfrac{1}{2}} = 4$

$\dfrac{V_f}{V_i} = 4^2 = 16$

The gas should be expanded $16$ times.

Answer: $16$ times

</details>

The average velocity
$$
