### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The molar specific heat at constant pressure of an ideal gas is $\dfrac{7}{2} R$. The gas is made up of molecules which are

**Options:**

- **(A)** polyatomic
- **(B)** non-rigid diatomic
- **(C)** rigid diamotic
- **(D)** monoatomic

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) rigid diamotic**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $C_p = \dfrac{7}{2} R$.

Using Mayer's relation, $C_p - C_v = R$.

$C_v = C_p - R = \dfrac{7}{2} R - R = \dfrac{5}{2} R$.

The molar specific heat at constant volume is given by $C_v = \dfrac{f}{2} R$, where $f$ is the number of degrees of freedom.

Comparing the two expressions, we get $f = 5$.

A gas with $5$ degrees of freedom corresponds to a rigid diatomic gas, which has $3$ translational and $2$ rotational degrees of freedom.

Answer: rigid diamotic

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a gas, if the ratio of specific heats at constant pressure and volume is $\gamma$ then the value of degree of freedom is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{(3\gamma - 1)}{(2\gamma - 1)}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{2}{\gamma - 1}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{9(\gamma - 1)}{2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{25(\gamma - 1)}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{2}{\gamma - 1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The ratio of specific heats at constant pressure and volume is given by $\gamma = \dfrac{C_p}{C_v}$.

The molar heat capacity at constant volume is $C_v = \dfrac{f}{2}R$, where $f$ is the degree of freedom.

The molar heat capacity at constant pressure is $C_p = C_v + R = \left(\dfrac{f}{2} + 1\right)R$.

Substituting these into the expression for $\gamma$:

$\gamma = \dfrac{\left(\dfrac{f}{2} + 1\right)R}{\dfrac{f}{2}R} = 1 + \dfrac{2}{f}$

Rearranging to solve for $f$:

$\gamma - 1 = \dfrac{2}{f}$

$f = \dfrac{2}{\gamma - 1}$

Answer: $\dfrac{2}{\gamma - 1}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A rigid diatomic gas having molar mass m is contained in an insulated container. The container is moving with velocity V. If it is stopped suddenly, the change in temperature is (R - gas constant)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{mV^2}{R}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{mV^2}{3R}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{mV^2}{5R}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{mV^2}{7R}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{mV^2}{5R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $n$ be the number of moles of the gas. The total mass of the gas is $nm$.

The initial kinetic energy of the gas due to the motion of the container is $K = \dfrac{1}{2}(nm)V^2$.

When the container is suddenly stopped, this kinetic energy is completely converted into the internal energy of the gas because the container is insulated.

The change in internal energy is given by $\Delta U = nC_v\Delta T$.

For a rigid diatomic gas, the molar heat capacity at constant volume is $C_v = \dfrac{5}{2}R$.

Equating the kinetic energy to the change in internal energy:

$\dfrac{1}{2}nmV^2 = n\left(\dfrac{5}{2}R\right)\Delta T$

$\dfrac{1}{2}mV^2 = \dfrac{5}{2}R\Delta T$

$\Delta T = \dfrac{mV^2}{5R}$

Answer: $\dfrac{mV^2}{5R}$

</details>

### Q14. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 2)

A monoatomic ideal gas is heated at constant pressure. The percentage of total heat used in changing the internal energy is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30 \%$

- **(C)** $50 \%$
- **(D)** $60 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $60 \%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a monoatomic ideal gas, the molar heat capacity at constant volume is $C_V = \dfrac{3}{2}R$ and at constant pressure is $C_P = \dfrac{5}{2}R$.

The change in internal energy is given by $\Delta U = n C_V \Delta T$.

The percentage of total heat used in changing the internal energy is:

$\dfrac{\Delta U}{\Delta Q} \times 100 = \dfrac{n C_V \Delta T}{n C_P \Delta T} \times 100$

$\dfrac{\Delta U}{\Delta Q} \times 100 = \dfrac{C_V}{C_P} \times 100$

Substituting the values of $C_V$ and $C_P$:

$\dfrac{\Delta U}{\Delta Q} \times 100 = \dfrac{\dfrac{3}{2}R}{\dfrac{5}{2}R} \times 100 = \dfrac{3}{5} \times 100 = 60 \%$
