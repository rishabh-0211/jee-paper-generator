### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

At what temperature, the kinetic energy of a gas molecule is half of the value at $27^\circ$C ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $13.5^\circ$C
- **(B)** $-123^\circ$C
- **(C)** $75$K
- **(D)** $13.5$K

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $-123^\circ$C**

**Detailed Solution:**

The average kinetic energy of a gas molecule is directly proportional to its absolute temperature, $E = \dfrac{3}{2} k_B T$.

Given initial temperature, $T_1 = 27^\circ\text{C} = 27 + 273 = 300\text{ K}$.

Let the new temperature be $T_2$. Since the kinetic energy becomes half, the absolute temperature must also become half.

$T_2 = \dfrac{T_1}{2} = \dfrac{300}{2} = 150\text{ K}$

Converting to Celsius:

$T_2 = 150 - 273 = -123^\circ\text{C}$

Answer: $-123^\circ$C

</details>

$P = \dfrac{2}{3} \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \rho v_{rms}^2 \right)$

$P = \dfrac{2}{3} E$

Thus, the pressure is $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{rd}$ of the kinetic energy per unit volume of the gas.

### Q17. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In an ideal gas at temperature $T$, the average force that a molecule applies on the walls of a closed container depends on T as $\mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{x}}$. The value of $x$ is

- **(C)** 0.5

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

The average force exerted by a molecule on the walls of a container is equal to the rate of change of momentum.

Let the molecule have a velocity $v_x$ perpendicular to a wall of a cubical container of side $L$. The change in momentum per collision is $\Delta p = 2mv_x$.

The time interval between two successive collisions with the same wall is $\Delta t = \dfrac{2L}{v_x}$.

The average force exerted by the molecule is $F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{2mv_x}{\dfrac{2L}{v_x}} = \dfrac{mv_x^2}{L}$.

From the kinetic theory of gases, the average kinetic energy per degree of freedom is $\dfrac{1}{2}mv_x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}k_B T$, which gives $mv_x^2 = k_B T$.

Substituting this, we get $F = \dfrac{k_B T}{L}$.

Thus, the average force is directly proportional to the temperature $T$, meaning $F \propto T^1$.

Comparing with $F \propto T^x$, we get $x = 1$.

Answer: 1

</details>
