### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The total charge induced in a conducting loop when it is moved in a uniform magnetic field depends on

**Options:**

- **(A)** initial magnetic flux only.
- **(B)** final magnetic flux only.
- **(C)** the total change in magnetic flux.
- **(D)** the rate of change of magnetic flux.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) the total change in magnetic flux.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, the induced emf is given by:

$e = -\dfrac{d\phi}{dt}$

The induced current in the loop of resistance $R$ is:

$i = \dfrac{e}{R} = -\dfrac{1}{R} \dfrac{d\phi}{dt}$

The total charge induced $dq$ in time $dt$ is:

$dq = i dt = -\dfrac{1}{R} d\phi$

Integrating both sides, the total charge induced is:

$Q = \int dq = -\dfrac{1}{R} \int_{\phi_1}^{\phi_2} d\phi = -\dfrac{\phi_2 - \phi_1}{R} = -\dfrac{\Delta \phi}{R}$

Thus, the total charge induced depends only on the total change in magnetic flux and the resistance of the loop. It is independent of the rate of change of magnetic flux.

Answer: the total change in magnetic flux.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A magnetic field of $2 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~T}$ acts at right angles to a coil of area $100 \mathrm{~cm}^2$ with 50 turns. The average e.m.f. induced in the coil is $0.1 \mathrm{~V}$, when it is removed from the field in time ' $t$ '. The value of ' $t$ ' is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~s}$
- **(B)** $0.5 \mathrm{~s}$
- **(C)** $0.1 \mathrm{~s}$
- **(D)** $1 \mathrm{~s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.1 \mathrm{~s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{array}{ll} 
& \mathrm{e}=-\frac{\mathrm{d} \phi}{\mathrm{dt}}=-\frac{\left(\phi_2-\phi_1\right)}{\mathrm{t}}=-\frac{(0-\mathrm{NBA})}{\mathrm{t}} \\
\therefore \quad & 0.1=\frac{50 \times 2 \times 10^{-2} \times 10^{-2}}{\mathrm{t}}
\end{array}
$$
$\therefore \quad$ The value of ' $t$ ' is,
$$
\mathrm{t}=\frac{10^{-2}}{0.1}=0.1 \mathrm{~s}
$$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2022 (10 Aug Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The magnetic flux through a coil of resistance $R$ changes by an amount $\Delta \phi$ in time $\Delta t$. The total quantity of induced electric charge $Q$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t}$
- **(B)** $-\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t}+R$
- **(C)** $\frac{\Delta \phi}{R}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t} \times R$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t} \times R$**

**Detailed Solution:**

From Faraday's law of EMI, emf induced in the circuit is given by,
$e=-\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t}$
And if $R$ is the resistance in the circuit then it becomes,
$\begin{aligned} & I=\frac{e}{R} \\ & \quad \Rightarrow I=-\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t \cdot R}\end{aligned}$
So, the total amount of charge passing through the circuit will become,
$\begin{aligned} & \because \Delta Q=I \times \Delta t \\ & \Rightarrow \Delta Q=-\frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta t \cdot R} \cdot \Delta t \\ & \Rightarrow \Delta Q=-\frac{\Delta \phi}{R}\end{aligned}$
So, the total amount of charge passing through the circuit is given by $\frac{\Delta \phi}{R}$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2021 (24 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A conducting loop of resistance ' $R$ ' is moved into a magnetic field, the total induced charge depends upon

**Options:**

- **(A)** initial magnetic flux and $\mathrm{R}$.
- **(B)** final magnetic flux and $\mathrm{R}$.
- **(C)** the total change in magnetic flux and $\mathrm{R}$.
- **(D)** the rate of change of magnetic flux and $\mathrm{R}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) the total change in magnetic flux and $\mathrm{R}$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & \mathrm{i}=\frac{\mathrm{e}}{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}} \cdot \frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta \mathrm{t}} \\ & \frac{\Delta \mathrm{q}}{\Delta \mathrm{t}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}} \cdot \frac{\Delta \phi}{\Delta \mathrm{t}} \\ & \therefore \Delta \mathrm{q}=\frac{\Delta \phi}{\mathrm{R}}\end{aligned}$

</details>
