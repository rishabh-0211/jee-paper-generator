### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two similar circular loops carry equal currents in the same direction. On moving coils further apart, the electric current will

**Options:**

- **(A)** increase in both
- **(B)** decrease in both
- **(C)** remain unchanged
- **(D)** increase in one coil and decrease in second coil

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) increase in both**

**Detailed Solution:**

When two similar circular loops carry equal currents in the same direction, the magnetic flux linked with each loop is the sum of the flux due to its own current and the flux due to the current in the other loop.

The total magnetic flux linked with each coil is given by $\Phi = LI + MI$, where $L$ is the self-inductance and $M$ is the mutual inductance.

When the coils are moved further apart, the mutual inductance $M$ between them decreases. This results in a decrease in the total magnetic flux linked with each coil.

According to Lenz's law, an induced emf is generated in each coil to oppose this decrease in magnetic flux. To compensate for the loss of flux, the induced current will flow in the same direction as the original current.

Therefore, the electric current will increase in both the coils.

Answer: increase in both

</details>

### Q13. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two coils P and S have a mutual inductance of ($\pi$) mH. The secondary coil S has resistance $4\,\Omega$ and self inductance $(60/\pi)$ mH. If the current in the primary is $I_p = 12\sin(50\pi t)$, then the maximum value of the current induced in coil S is [Take $\pi^2 = 10$]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2$ A
- **(B)** $1.8$ A
- **(C)** $1.5$ A
- **(D)** $1.2$ A

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1.2$ A**

**Detailed Solution:**

The induced emf in the secondary coil is given by $e_s = -M \dfrac{dI_p}{dt}$.

Given $I_p = 12\sin(50\pi t)$, differentiating with respect to time gives $\dfrac{dI_p}{dt} = 600\pi \cos(50\pi t)$.

Substituting $M = \pi \times 10^{-3}$ H, we get:

$e_s = -(\pi \times 10^{-3}) \times 600\pi \cos(50\pi t) = -600\pi^2 \times 10^{-3} \cos(50\pi t)$

Using $\pi^2 = 10$, the maximum induced emf is $E_0 = 600 \times 10 \times 10^{-3} = 6$ V.

The angular frequency of the AC source is $\omega = 50\pi$ rad/s.

The inductive reactance of the secondary coil is $X_L = \omega L_s = 50\pi \times \dfrac{60}{\pi} \times 10^{-3} = 3\,\Omega$.

The impedance of the secondary coil is $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2} = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5\,\Omega$.

The maximum value of the induced current is $I_0 = \dfrac{E_0}{Z} = \dfrac{6}{5} = 1.2$ A.

Answer: $1.2$ A

</details>

- **(B)** $\mathrm{N}^3$

- **(D)** $\mathrm{N}^0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{N}^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The correct option is (C)
Concept: The flux associated with the loop is $\phi=\mathrm{BA}$ and the coefficient of self-inductance $\mathrm{L}$ is defined as $\mathrm{L}=\frac{\phi}{\mathrm{I}}$, where the I is the current flowing through the coil.
The magnetic magnetic field $B$ at the center of a coil carrying current $\mathrm{i}$, with radius $\mathrm{r}$ is given by,
$B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{2 r}$
So for a coil with $\mathrm{n}$ no. of turns: $\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{N}}=\mathrm{N}\left(\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{I}}{2 \mathrm{r}}\right)$
The self flux associated with one coil is : $\phi 1=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{N}} \mathrm{A}$
Now, the self flux associated with coil of $\mathrm{N}$ turns is: $\phi \mathrm{N}=\mathrm{N} \phi_1$
Therefore,
$\phi N=N\left\{N\left(\frac{\mu_0 I}{2 r}\right) A\right\}_1$
Now using the definition of self-inductance: $L=\frac{\theta}{I}$, we get, $\mathrm{L} \propto \mathrm{N}^2$
So, self-inductance is proportional to square of number of turns in the coil.
