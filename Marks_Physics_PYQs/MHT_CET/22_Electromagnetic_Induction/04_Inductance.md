### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two inductors, $L_1$ and $L_2$ are connected in parallel and a time varying current flows as shown in the figure. The ratio of currents $\dfrac{I_2}{I_1}$ at any time, it is
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/19_may_shift_2__q29_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{L_2}{L_1}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{L_1}{L_2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{L_1}{(L_1 + L_2)^2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{L_2}{(L_1 + L_2)^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{L_1}{L_2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since the two inductors $L_1$ and $L_2$ are connected in parallel, the potential difference across them is the same at any instant.

$V_1 = V_2$

$L_1 \dfrac{dI_1}{dt} = L_2 \dfrac{dI_2}{dt}$

Integrating both sides with respect to time, assuming the initial currents are zero:

$\int L_1 dI_1 = \int L_2 dI_2$

$L_1 I_1 = L_2 I_2$

Rearranging the terms, we get the ratio of the currents:

$\dfrac{I_2}{I_1} = \dfrac{L_1}{L_2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{L_1}{L_2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The self induced e.m.f. of a coil is $25$ V. When the current in it is changed at uniform rate from $10$ A to $25$ A in $1$ s, the change in the energy of the inductance is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $437.5$ J
- **(B)** $740$ J
- **(C)** $6375$ J
- **(D)** $540$ J

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $437.5$ J**

**Detailed Solution:**

The self-induced emf is given by $e = L \dfrac{di}{dt}$.

Given $e = 25$ V, $i_1 = 10$ A, $i_2 = 25$ A, and $dt = 1$ s.

$25 = L \left( \dfrac{25 - 10}{1} \right)$

$25 = 15 L \Rightarrow L = \dfrac{5}{3}$ H

The energy stored in an inductor is $U = \dfrac{1}{2} L i^2$.

The change in energy is $\Delta U = \dfrac{1}{2} L (i_2^2 - i_1^2)$.

$\Delta U = \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{5}{3} \times (25^2 - 10^2)$

$\Delta U = \dfrac{5}{6} \times (625 - 100)$

$\Delta U = \dfrac{5}{6} \times 525 = 437.5$ J

Answer: $437.5$ J

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two different coils have self-inductance $3L$ and $L$. The current in both the coils is increased at the same constant rate. At certain instant of time, the power given to the two coils is same. At that time there was current and voltage induced in the two coils. At the same instant, the ratio of energy stored in the first coil to that in the second coil is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:9$
- **(B)** $1:3$
- **(C)** $3:1$
- **(D)** $9:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1:3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the self-inductances of the two coils be $L_1 = 3L$ and $L_2 = L$.

The rate of change of current is the same for both coils, so $\dfrac{di_1}{dt} = \dfrac{di_2}{dt}$.

The induced voltage in a coil is given by $V = L \dfrac{di}{dt}$.

For the two coils, the induced voltages are:
$V_1 = 3L \dfrac{di_1}{dt}$
$V_2 = L \dfrac{di_2}{dt}$

The power given to the coils is $P = Vi$. Since the power is the same at the given instant:
$P_1 = P_2$

$V_1 i_1 = V_2 i_2$

$3L \dfrac{di_1}{dt} i_1 = L \dfrac{di_2}{dt} i_2$

$3 i_1 = i_2$

The energy stored in an inductor is $U = \dfrac{1}{2} L i^2$.

The ratio of the energy stored in the first coil to that in the second coil is:
$\dfrac{U_1}{U_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2} L_1 i_1^2}{\dfrac{1}{2} L_2 i_2^2}$

$\dfrac{U_1}{U_2} = \dfrac{3L}{L} \left( \dfrac{i_1}{i_2} \right)^2$

$\dfrac{U_1}{U_2} = 3 \left( \dfrac{1}{3} \right)^2 = \dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

Answer: $1:3$

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two coils have self inductance $L_1$ and $L_2$. The current through them is increasing at constant rate. If the power dissipated in both the coils is same, then the ratio of energy stored in the coil having inductance $L_1$ to that in $L_2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{L_1^2}{L_2^2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{L_2^2}{L_1^2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{L_1}{L_2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{L_2}{L_1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{L_2}{L_1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Power supplied to an inductor is given by $P = |\varepsilon| i = L \dfrac{di}{dt} i$

Given that the current is increasing at a constant rate, $\dfrac{di_1}{dt} = \dfrac{di_2}{dt} = k$

Since the power dissipated in both coils is the same, $P_1 = P_2$

$L_1 k i_1 = L_2 k i_2 \Rightarrow L_1 i_1 = L_2 i_2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{i_1}{i_2} = \dfrac{L_2}{L_1}$

The energy stored in an inductor is $U = \dfrac{1}{2} L i^2$

The ratio of energy stored is $\dfrac{U_1}{U_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2} L_1 i_1^2}{\dfrac{1}{2} L_2 i_2^2} = \dfrac{L_1}{L_2} \left( \dfrac{i_1}{i_2} \right)^2$

Substituting the ratio of currents, we get:

$\dfrac{U_1}{U_2} = \dfrac{L_1}{L_2} \left( \dfrac{L_2}{L_1} \right)^2 = \dfrac{L_2}{L_1}$

Answer: $\dfrac{L_2}{L_1}$

</details>

### Q16. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A coil of self-inductance $L$ is connected in series with a bulb and an a.c. source. Brightness of the bulb decreases when

**Options:**

- **(A)** an iron rod is inserted in the coil.
- **(B)** frequency of a.c. source is decreased.
- **(C)** number of turns in the coil is reduced.
- **(D)** a capacitance of reactance $\left(X_C-X_L\right)$ is included in the same circuit.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) an iron rod is inserted in the coil.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The brightness of the bulb depends on the current $I$ flowing through the circuit. The current in an AC circuit with a resistor (bulb) and an inductor is given by:

$I = \dfrac{V}{Z} = \dfrac{V}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}}$

where $X_L = \omega L = 2\pi f L$ is the inductive reactance.

To decrease the brightness of the bulb, the current $I$ must decrease, which requires the impedance $Z$ to increase. This happens when the inductive reactance $X_L$ increases.

When an iron rod is inserted into the coil, the magnetic permeability of the core increases (since $\mu_r > 1$ for iron). This increases the self-inductance $L$ of the coil. As $L$ increases, $X_L$ increases, which in turn increases the impedance $Z$. Consequently, the current $I$ decreases, leading to a decrease in the brightness of the bulb.

Decreasing the frequency or reducing the number of turns would decrease $X_L$, thereby increasing the current and brightness.

Answer: an iron rod is inserted in the coil.

</details>
