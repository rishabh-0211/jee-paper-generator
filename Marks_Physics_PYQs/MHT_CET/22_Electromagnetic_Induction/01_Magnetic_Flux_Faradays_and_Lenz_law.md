### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A coil having effective area 'A' is held with its plane normal to a magnetic field of induction 'B'. The magnetic induction is quickly reduced to $25\%$ of its initial value in $2$ second. Then e.m.f. induced across the coil will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{AB}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{AB}{4}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{3AB}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{3AB}{8}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{3AB}{8}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial magnetic flux linked with the coil is $\Phi_1 = BA$

The final magnetic induction is reduced to $25\%$ of its initial value, so $B_2 = \dfrac{B}{4}$

The final magnetic flux is $\Phi_2 = \dfrac{BA}{4}$

The magnitude of induced e.m.f. is given by Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction:

$e = \left| \dfrac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} \right| = \dfrac{|\Phi_2 - \Phi_1|}{\Delta t}$

Given the time interval $\Delta t = 2$ s:

$e = \dfrac{\left| \dfrac{BA}{4} - BA \right|}{2}$

$e = \dfrac{\left| -\dfrac{3BA}{4} \right|}{2} = \dfrac{3AB}{8}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A bar magnet falls from a height through a metal pipe. Its acceleration will be ($g$ = acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** less than $g$
- **(B)** greater than $g$
- **(C)** equal to $g$
- **(D)** zero

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) less than $g$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a bar magnet falls through a metal pipe, the magnetic flux linked with the pipe changes continuously.

This changing magnetic flux induces eddy currents in the metal pipe.

According to Lenz's law, the induced eddy currents oppose the relative motion of the magnet.

As a result, an upward magnetic force acts on the falling magnet, opposing its downward weight.

The net downward force is $F_{net} = mg - F_{magnetic}$, which gives an acceleration $a = g - \dfrac{F_{magnetic}}{m}$.

Therefore, the acceleration of the bar magnet will be less than $g$.

Answer: less than $g$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The magnetic flux through a coil of resistance '$R$' changes by an amount $\Delta\phi$ in the time $\Delta t$. The total quantity of induced current and charge is respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{\Delta t}$ and $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{\Delta t} \times R$ and $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{\Delta t}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R\Delta t}$ and $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{R\Delta t}{\Delta\phi}$ and $R\Delta\phi$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R\Delta t}$ and $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, the magnitude of induced emf is given by

$e = \dfrac{\Delta\phi}{\Delta t}$

The induced current $I$ in the coil of resistance $R$ is

$I = \dfrac{e}{R} = \dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R\Delta t}$

The total quantity of induced charge $Q$ is

$Q = I \Delta t = \left(\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R\Delta t}\right) \Delta t = \dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R}$

Thus, the induced current and charge are $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R\Delta t}$ and $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R}$ respectively.

Answer: $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R\Delta t}$ and $\dfrac{\Delta\phi}{R}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

A rectangular wire loop of resistance $1.5\,\Omega$ placed in a region of uniform magnetic field of magnitude $0.3$ T directed normal to the loop. If the area of the loop decreases at a rate of $0.05$ $\text{m}^2/s$ in this magnetic field, the heat produced in it is $x \times 10^{-6}$ W. The value of $x$ is

**Options:**

- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $150$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $150$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:
Resistance of the loop, $R = 1.5\,\Omega$
Magnetic field, $B = 0.3$ T
Rate of decrease of area, $\dfrac{dA}{dt} = 0.05$ $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$

The magnitude of the induced emf is given by Faraday's law:

### Q77. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following phenomenon exhibits particle's nature of light ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Interference
- **(B)** Diffraction
- **(C)** Polarisation
- **(D)** Photoelectric effect

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Photoelectric effect**

**Detailed Solution:**

Interference is a phenomenon in which two waves of same frequency superpose to give resultant intensity different from sum of their separate intensity. So, it cannot exhibit particle's nature of light.
(b) Diffraction is a phenomenon in which light bends at sharp ends of an obstacle or a hole. So, it also cannot exhibit particle's nature of light.
(c) Polarisation of light is a property owing to which a light ray after emerging through a crystal (a special kind like tourmaline) have vibrations in a plane perpendicular to its direction of propagation. So, it also can not explain particle's nature of light.
(d) Photoelectric effect states that light travels in the form of bundles or packets of energy, called photons. This effect is explained on the basis of quantum nature of light. So, it clearly explains the particle's nature of light.
Hence, (d) is correct.

</details>
