A long metal rod of length 'L' completes the circuit. The area of the coil is perpendicular to magnetic field 'B'. Total resistance of the circuit is 'R'. The force needed to move the rod in the direction as shown with constant speed 'V' is

<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/20_may_shift_1__q9_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{BL^2V^2}{2R}$

- **(C)** $\dfrac{B^2VL^2}{R}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{B^2VL^2}{R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A conducting rod AC of length $4L$ is rotated counter-clockwise about a point O in a uniform magnetic field $B$ directed into the paper. If $AO = L$ and $OC = 3L$ then ($\omega$ = angular speed)
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/15_may_shift_1__q25_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $V_A - V_O = \dfrac{B\omega L^2}{2}$
- **(B)** $V_O - V_C = \dfrac{7B\omega L^2}{2}$
- **(C)** $V_A - V_C = 4B\omega L^2$
- **(D)** $V_C - V_O = \dfrac{7B\omega L^2}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $V_A - V_C = 4B\omega L^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The motional emf induced in a conducting rod of length $l$ rotating with angular speed $\omega$ in a uniform magnetic field $B$ is given by $E = \dfrac{1}{2}B\omega l^2$.

Applying the Lorentz force relation $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$, positive charges in both parts OA and OC experience a force towards point O. Thus, point O is at a higher potential than both A and C.

For the part OA of length $L$, the potential difference is:

$V_O - V_A = \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega L^2$

For the part OC of length $3L$, the potential difference is:

$V_O - V_C = \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega (3L)^2 = \dfrac{9}{2} B \omega L^2$

We need to find the potential difference $V_A - V_C$. We can express this as:

$V_A - V_C = (V_O - V_C) - (V_O - V_A)$

Substituting the derived values:

$V_A - V_C = \dfrac{9}{2} B \omega L^2 - \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega L^2$

$V_A - V_C = \dfrac{8}{2} B \omega L^2 = 4 B \omega L^2$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A conducting rod AB of length $l$ is rotated about point O which lies between point A and B in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ directed into the paper. If length $AO=\dfrac{l}{3}$, then $(V_A-V_B)$ is $\{\omega=$ angular velocity$\}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{5Bl^2\omega}{6}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{6Bl^2\omega}{5}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{Bl^2\omega}{6}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{Bl^2\omega}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{Bl^2\omega}{6}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The motional emf induced across a rod of length $x$ rotating with angular velocity $\omega$ about one of its ends in a perpendicular magnetic field $B$ is given by $E = \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega x^2$.

For the segment OA of length $x_1 = \dfrac{l}{3}$, the potential difference between O and A is:

$|V_O - V_A| = \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega \left(\dfrac{l}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{B \omega l^2}{18}$

For the segment OB of length $x_2 = l - \dfrac{l}{3} = \dfrac{2l}{3}$, the potential difference between O and B is:

$|V_O - V_B| = \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega \left(\dfrac{2l}{3}\right)^2 = \dfrac{4 B \omega l^2}{18}$

Since both segments are rotating in the same sense about O, the point O is either at the highest or the lowest potential compared to both A and B. Thus, the magnitude of the potential difference between A and B is:

$|V_A - V_B| = |(V_O - V_B) - (V_O - V_A)|$

$|V_A - V_B| = \left| \dfrac{4 B \omega l^2}{18} - \dfrac{B \omega l^2}{18} \right| = \dfrac{3 B \omega l^2}{18} = \dfrac{B \omega l^2}{6}$

Answer: $\dfrac{Bl^2\omega}{6}$

</details>

The plane of the loop is perpendicular to the magnetic field. The work done in pulling the loop out of the magnetic field slowly and uniformly in one second will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.5 \times 10^{-3}$ J
- **(B)** $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ J
- **(C)** $1.0 \times 10^{-4}$ J
- **(D)** $5 \times 10^{-3}$ J

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ J**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:
Area of the loop, $A = 25 \text{ cm}^2 = 25 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2$
Resistance, $R = 10 \ \Omega$
Magnetic field, $B = 40 \text{ T}$
Time taken, $\Delta t = 1 \text{ s}$

The total change in magnetic flux as the loop is pulled out of the magnetic field is:
$\Delta \Phi = B A = 40 \times 25 \times 10^{-4} = 0.1 \text{ Wb}$

Since the loop is pulled out uniformly, the rate of change of flux is constant. The induced emf $E$ is:
$E = \dfrac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{0.1}{1} = 0.1 \text{ V}$

$W = P \Delta t = 10^{-3} \times 1 = 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ J}$

<img style="width: 200px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/11_april_shift_1__q23_d1.png" />

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{mgR}{B^2 l^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When the loop falls with a velocity $v$, the motional emf induced in the top horizontal segment of the loop is given by:

$\varepsilon = Bvl$

The induced current in the loop is:

$I = \dfrac{\varepsilon}{R} = \dfrac{Bvl}{R}$

$F_m = mg$

$v = \dfrac{mgR}{B^2l^2}$

### Q11. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A simple pendulum with bob of mass $m$ and conducting wire of length L swings under gravity through an angle $\theta$. The component of earth's magnetic field in the direction perpendicular to swing is B. Maximum e.m.f. induced across the pendulum is ( $g=$ acceleration due to gravity)

- **(B)** $2 \mathrm{BL}(\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}})(\sin \theta / 2)$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{BL}(\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}})^2(\sin \theta / 2)$
- **(D)** $2 \mathrm{BL}(\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}})\left(\sin ^2 \theta / 2\right)$

$E_{\max} = \dfrac{1}{2} B \omega_{\max} L^2$

where $\omega_{\max}$ is the maximum angular velocity of the pendulum, which occurs at its lowest point.

By conservation of mechanical energy, the maximum kinetic energy at the lowest point is equal to the maximum potential energy at the extreme position (angular amplitude $\theta$):

$\dfrac{1}{2} I \omega_{\max}^2 = m g h$

$\omega_{\max} = 2 \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{L}} \sin \dfrac{\theta}{2}$

$E_{\max} = \dfrac{1}{2} B \left( 2 \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{L}} \sin \dfrac{\theta}{2} \right) L^2$

$E_{\max} = B L \sqrt{g L} \sin \dfrac{\theta}{2}$

The angular velocity $\omega$ is related to the number of revolutions per second $f$ by

$\omega = 2 \pi f$

Substituting this into the e.m.f. equation, we get

$e = \dfrac{1}{2} B l^2 (2 \pi f)$

$e = B \pi l^2 f$

Rearranging for $f$, we obtain

$f = \dfrac{e}{B \pi l^2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\mathrm{e}}{\mathrm{B} \pi l^2}$
