### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An electron of mass $m$ and charge $e$ initially at rest gets accelerated by a constant electric field $E$. The rate of change of de-Broglie wavelength of the electron at time $t$ is
($h$ = Planck's constant)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-\dfrac{h}{eEt^2}$
- **(B)** $-\dfrac{eEt}{h}$
- **(C)** $-\dfrac{mh}{eEt^2}$
- **(D)** $-\dfrac{h}{eE}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $-\dfrac{h}{eEt^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Force on the electron is $F = eE$

Acceleration of the electron is $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{eE}{m}$

Velocity of the electron at time $t$ is $v = u + at = \dfrac{eEt}{m}$

The de-Broglie wavelength of the electron is $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv}$

Substituting the value of $v$, we get $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{m \left(\dfrac{eEt}{m}\right)} = \dfrac{h}{eEt}$

The rate of change of de-Broglie wavelength is $\dfrac{d\lambda}{dt} = \dfrac{d}{dt} \left( \dfrac{h}{eEt} \right) = -\dfrac{h}{eEt^2}$

Answer: $-\dfrac{h}{eEt^2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two particles A and B of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ ($m_1 > m_2$) have the same de-Broglie wavelength, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** their momenta are the same
- **(B)** their energies are the same
- **(C)** momenta of A is less than the momenta of B
- **(D)** energy of A is more than energy of B

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) their momenta are the same**

**Detailed Solution:**

The de-Broglie wavelength of a particle is given by $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p}$, where $h$ is Planck's constant and $p$ is the momentum of the particle.

Given that both particles A and B have the same de-Broglie wavelength, their momenta must be equal, so $p_A = p_B$.

The kinetic energy of a particle is given by $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$.

Since $p_A = p_B$ and $m_1 > m_2$, the kinetic energy of A is less than the kinetic energy of B ($K_A < K_B$).

Thus, their momenta are the same.

Answer: their momenta are the same

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The ratio of de-Broglie wavelength of an $\alpha$-particle and proton accelerated from rest by the same potential is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{m}}$. The value of $m$ is

**Options:**

- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

The de-Broglie wavelength of a particle accelerated through a potential difference $V$ is given by $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_0 qV}}$, where $m_0$ is the mass and $q$ is the charge of the particle.

$\dfrac{\lambda_\alpha}{\lambda_p} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_p \times e}{4m_p \times 2e}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{8}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{8}}$

Given that the ratio is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{m}}$, comparing the two expressions gives $m = 8$.

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The de Broglie wavelength of the electron in the ground state is $\lambda_1$ and that in the $n = 3$ level is $\lambda_3$ then $\lambda_3$ is given by

$mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

In a hydrogen-like atom, the radius of the $n$-th orbit is proportional to $n^2$, so $r_n = r_1 n^2$.

Therefore, the de Broglie wavelength in the $n$-th orbit is:

$\lambda_n = \dfrac{2\pi (r_1 n^2)}{n} = n(2\pi r_1)$

For the ground state ($n=1$), $\lambda_1 = 2\pi r_1$.

For the $n=3$ level, the de Broglie wavelength is:

$\lambda_3 = 3(2\pi r_1) = 3\lambda_1$

### Q13. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $E_p$ and $E_e$ represent kinetic energy of photon and electron respectively. If de-Broglie wavelength $\lambda_p$ of a photon is twice the de-Broglie wavelength $\lambda_e$ of electron, then $E_e / E_p$ is (Speed of electron $=\frac{\mathrm{C}}{100}$, where 'C' is the velocity of light)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 \times 10^{-2}$
- **(B)** $2 \times 10^{-2}$
- **(C)** $4 \times 10^{-2}$
- **(D)** $8 \times 10^{-2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1 \times 10^{-2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The kinetic energy of an electron is given by:

$E_e = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}(mv)v$

Using the de-Broglie wavelength relation for the electron, $\lambda_e = \dfrac{h}{mv}$, we can write $mv = \dfrac{h}{\lambda_e}$. Substituting this into the kinetic energy equation:

$E_e = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{h}{\lambda_e}\right)v = \dfrac{hv}{2\lambda_e}$

The energy of a photon is given by:

$E_p = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_p}$

Taking the ratio of the kinetic energy of the electron to the energy of the photon:

$\dfrac{E_e}{E_p} = \dfrac{\dfrac{hv}{2\lambda_e}}{\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_p}} = \dfrac{v}{2c} \times \dfrac{\lambda_p}{\lambda_e}$

We are given that the de-Broglie wavelength of the photon is twice that of the electron ($\lambda_p = 2\lambda_e$) and the speed of the electron is $v = \dfrac{c}{100}$. Substituting these values into the ratio:

$\dfrac{E_e}{E_p} = \dfrac{\dfrac{c}{100}}{2c} \times \dfrac{2\lambda_e}{\lambda_e}$

$\dfrac{E_e}{E_p} = \dfrac{1}{200} \times 2 = \dfrac{1}{100} = 1 \times 10^{-2}$

Answer: $1 \times 10^{-2}$

</details>

\therefore \frac{1}{2} \frac{\mathrm{m}^{2} \mathrm{~V}^{2}}{\mathrm{~m}}=\frac{\mathrm{hc}}{\lambda} \\
\therefore \mathrm{m}^{2} \mathrm{~V}^{2}=\frac{2 \mathrm{mhC}}{\lambda} \\
\therefore \mathrm{mV}=\sqrt{\frac{2 \mathrm{mhc}}{\lambda}}
\end{array}$
$\begin{aligned}
\text { de Broglie wavelength } &=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{mV}} \\
&=\mathrm{h} \sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2 \mathrm{mhC}}}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{h} \lambda}{2 \mathrm{mC}}}
\end{aligned}$

</details>
