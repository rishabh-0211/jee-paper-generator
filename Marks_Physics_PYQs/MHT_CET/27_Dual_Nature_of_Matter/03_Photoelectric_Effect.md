### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Light of incident frequency three times the threshold frequency is incident on a photo sensitive material. If the incident frequency is made $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{rd}$ and intensity is doubled then photoelectric current will

**Options:**

- **(A)** be zero
- **(B)** be halved
- **(C)** be increased
- **(D)** be decreased

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) be zero**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial frequency of incident light, $\nu_1 = 3\nu_0$, where $\nu_0$ is the threshold frequency.

New frequency of incident light, $\nu_2 = \dfrac{1}{3}\nu_1 = \dfrac{1}{3}(3\nu_0) = \nu_0$.

Since the new incident frequency is exactly equal to the threshold frequency, the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons is zero ($K_{max} = h\nu_2 - h\nu_0 = 0$).

As the photoelectrons have zero kinetic energy, they do not move towards the collector plate. Therefore, the photoelectric current will be zero, regardless of the increase in intensity.

Answer: be zero

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

When a metallic surface is illuminated with radiation of wavelength $\lambda$, the stopping potential is $V$. If the surface is now illuminated with a radiation of wavelength $4\lambda$, the stopping potential $\left(\dfrac{V}{8}\right)$. The threshold wavelength for the surface is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\lambda$
- **(B)** $3\lambda$
- **(C)** $5\lambda$
- **(D)** $7\lambda$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $7\lambda$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Einstein's photoelectric equation:

$eV = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_0}$

For the first case:

$eV = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_0}$

For the second case:

$\dfrac{eV}{8} = \dfrac{hc}{4\lambda} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_0}$

Multiplying the second equation by $8$ gives:

$eV = \dfrac{2hc}{\lambda} - \dfrac{8hc}{\lambda_0}$

Equating the two expressions for $eV$:

$\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_0} = \dfrac{2hc}{\lambda} - \dfrac{8hc}{\lambda_0}$

Rearranging the terms:

$\dfrac{8hc}{\lambda_0} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_0} = \dfrac{2hc}{\lambda} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

$\dfrac{7hc}{\lambda_0} = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

$\lambda_0 = 7\lambda$

Answer: $7\lambda$

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\lambda_1 < \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$

- **(C)** $\lambda_1 > \dfrac{\lambda}{2} < \lambda$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\lambda_1 > \dfrac{\lambda}{2} < \lambda$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Einstein's photoelectric equation:

$E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \phi$

When the maximum kinetic energy is doubled, the new wavelength is $\lambda_1$:

$2E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} - \phi$

Substituting the value of $E$ from the first equation into the second:

$2\left(\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \phi\right) = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} - \phi$

$\dfrac{2hc}{\lambda} - 2\phi = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} - \phi$

$\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} = \dfrac{2hc}{\lambda} - \phi$

Since the work function $\phi > 0$, we have:

$\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} < \dfrac{2hc}{\lambda} \Rightarrow \lambda_1 > \dfrac{\lambda}{2}$

$\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} + \left(\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \phi\right)$

Since $\left(\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} - \phi\right) > 0$, it follows that:

$\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_1} > \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} \Rightarrow \lambda_1 < \lambda$

Combining both inequalities, we get:

$\dfrac{\lambda}{2} < \lambda_1 < \lambda$

Answer: $\lambda_1 > \dfrac{\lambda}{2} < \lambda$

### Q164. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In bringing an electron towards another electron, the electrostatic potential energy of the system

**Options:**

- **(A)** decreases
- **(B)** increases
- **(C)** remains same
- **(D)** becomes zero

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) increases**

**Detailed Solution:**

The electron has negative charge. When an electron is approaching towards another electron, then due to same negative charge repulsive force is produced between them. So, to bring them closer a work is done against this repulsive force. This work is stored in the form of electrostatic potential energy. Thus, electrostatic potential energy of system increases.
Alternative Electrostatic potential energy of system of two electrons
$$
U=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}} \frac{(-e)(-e)}{r}=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}} \frac{e^{2}}{r}
$$
Thus, as $r$ decreases, potential energy $U$ increases.

</details>

Which one of the following processes depends on gravity?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Conduction
- **(B)** Convection
- **(C)** Radiation
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Convection**

**Detailed Solution:**

Conduction is the process of transmission of heat in a body from the hotter part to the colder part without any bodily movement of constituent atoms or molecules of the body.
(b) In convection, the heated lighter particles move upwards and colder heavier particles move downwards to their place. This depends on weight and hence, on gravity.
(c) Radiation is the process of transmission of heat from the body to another body through electromagnetic waves even through vacuum, irrespective of their temperatures. Hence, (b) is correct.

</details>
