### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A particle moves from position $3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 6\hat{k}$ to $14\hat{i} + 13\hat{j} + 9\hat{k}$ due to uniform force of $(4\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 3\hat{k})$ N. If displacement is in metre then work done will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $100$ J
- **(B)** $200$ J
- **(C)** $300$ J
- **(D)** $250$ J

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $100$ J**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial position vector, $\vec{r}_1 = 3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 6\hat{k}$

Final position vector, $\vec{r}_2 = 14\hat{i} + 13\hat{j} + 9\hat{k}$

Displacement vector, $\vec{s} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1$

$\vec{s} = (14 - 3)\hat{i} + (13 - 2)\hat{j} + (9 - (-6))\hat{k}$

$\vec{s} = 11\hat{i} + 11\hat{j} + 15\hat{k}$

Force vector, $\vec{F} = 4\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$

Work done, $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s}$

$W = (4\hat{i} + \hat{j} + 3\hat{k}) \cdot (11\hat{i} + 11\hat{j} + 15\hat{k})$

$W = (4 \times 11) + (1 \times 11) + (3 \times 15)$

$W = 44 + 11 + 45 = 100$ J

Answer: $100$ J

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The spring is initially in unstretched condition. It is first stretched by a length '$x$' and the work done is $W_1$. Then again it is stretched by further length '$x$' when the work done is $W_2$. The value of $W_2$ is given by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $W_1$
- **(B)** $2W_1$
- **(C)** $3W_1$
- **(D)** $4W_1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3W_1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Work done in stretching a spring from its natural length to an extension $x$ is given by $W_1 = \dfrac{1}{2}kx^2$.

When the spring is further stretched by a length $x$, the total extension becomes $2x$.

Total work done from the natural length to an extension $2x$ is $W = \dfrac{1}{2}k(2x)^2 = 4 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}kx^2\right) = 4W_1$.

Work done in the second step is $W_2 = W - W_1 = 4W_1 - W_1 = 3W_1$.

Answer: $3W_1$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A constant force $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{F}}=3 \hat{\mathrm{i}}-2 \hat{\mathrm{j}}-\hat{\mathrm{k}}$ newton has a displacement $\vec{r}=2 \hat{i}-3 \hat{j}-3 \hat{k}$ metre in 2 second. The work done and the power are respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** 20 joule , 10 watt
- **(B)** 15 joule, $7.5$ watt
- **(C)** 13 joule , $6.5$ watt
- **(D)** 10 joule , 5 watt

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 15 joule, $7.5$ watt**

**Detailed Solution:**

Work done is given by the dot product of force and displacement vectors.

$W = \overrightarrow{\mathrm{F}} \cdot \vec{r}$

$W = (3 \hat{\mathrm{i}} - 2 \hat{\mathrm{j}} - \hat{\mathrm{k}}) \cdot (2 \hat{\mathrm{i}} - 3 \hat{\mathrm{j}} - 3 \hat{\mathrm{k}})$

$W = (3)(2) + (-2)(-3) + (-1)(-3)$

$W = 6 + 6 + 3 = 15 \text{ joule}$

Power is the rate of work done.

$P = \dfrac{W}{t}$

$P = \dfrac{15}{2} = 7.5 \text{ watt}$

Answer: 15 joule, $7.5$ watt

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A particle is displaced from point $\mathrm{P}(3 \mathrm{~m}, 4 \mathrm{~m}, 5 \mathrm{~m})$ to a point $Q(2 m, 3 m, 4 m)$ under a constant force $\vec{F}=(3 \hat{i}+4 \hat{j}+5 \hat{k}) N$. The work done by the force in this process is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $+10 \mathrm{~J}$
- **(B)** $+4 \mathrm{~J}$
- **(C)** $-8 \mathrm{~J}$
- **(D)** $-12 \mathrm{~J}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $-12 \mathrm{~J}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial position vector $\vec{r}_1 = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$

Final position vector $\vec{r}_2 = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} + 4\hat{k}$

Displacement vector $\vec{s} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1 = (2-3)\hat{i} + (3-4)\hat{j} + (4-5)\hat{k} = -\hat{i} - \hat{j} - \hat{k}$

Force $\vec{F} = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$

Work done $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = (3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}) \cdot (-\hat{i} - \hat{j} - \hat{k})$

$W = 3(-1) + 4(-1) + 5(-1) = -3 - 4 - 5 = -12 \text{ J}$

Answer: $-12 \mathrm{~J}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In an electric field due to charge $Q$, a charge $q$ moves from point $A$ to $B$ as shown in the figure. The work done is ( $\varepsilon_0=$ permittivity of free space)
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/py35bdsq/qNrIIxDZjIxBYoi64XAttw52TXvLMBwL41vIPs_zxOo.original.fullsize.png"/>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \frac{\mathrm{Qq}}{\mathrm{r}^2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \frac{\mathrm{Qq}}{\mathrm{r}^2} \frac{\pi}{6}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_0} \frac{\mathrm{Qq}}{\mathrm{r}}$
- **(D)** zero

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) zero**

**Detailed Solution:**

The electric potential at a distance $r$ from a point charge $Q$ is given by $V = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{Q}{r}$.

From the given figure, both points $A$ and $B$ are at the same distance $r$ from the charge $+Q$.

Therefore, the electric potential at point $A$ is $V_A = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{Q}{r}$ and the electric potential at point $B$ is $V_B = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\dfrac{Q}{r}$.

Since $V_A = V_B$, the potential difference between points $A$ and $B$ is $\Delta V = V_B - V_A = 0$.

The work done in moving a charge $q$ from $A$ to $B$ is $W = q(V_B - V_A) = q(0) = 0$.

Alternatively, the path $AB$ is a circular arc centered at $+Q$, which means the electric force on charge $q$ is always radial and perpendicular to the tangential displacement. Since the angle between force and displacement is $90^{\circ}$ at every point, the work done is zero.

Answer: zero

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the work done in blowing a soap bubble of volume ' V ' is ' W ', then the work done in blowing a soap bubble of volume ' 2 V ' will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** W
- **(B)** 2 W
- **(C)** $\mathrm{w} \sqrt{2}$
- **(D)** $\quad \mathrm{W}(4)^{\frac{1}{3}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\quad \mathrm{W}(4)^{\frac{1}{3}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work done in blowing a soap bubble of radius $R$ and surface tension $T$ is given by:

$W = 8\pi R^2 T$

The volume of the soap bubble is:

$V = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$

From this, the radius $R$ in terms of volume $V$ is:

$R = \left(\dfrac{3V}{4\pi}\right)^{\dfrac{1}{3}}$

Substituting $R$ into the expression for work done:

$W = 8\pi T \left(\dfrac{3V}{4\pi}\right)^{\dfrac{2}{3}}$

This implies that the work done is directly proportional to $V^{\dfrac{2}{3}}$:

$W \propto V^{\dfrac{2}{3}}$

For a new volume $V' = 2V$, the new work done $W'$ is:

$\dfrac{W'}{W} = \left(\dfrac{2V}{V}\right)^{\dfrac{2}{3}} = 2^{\dfrac{2}{3}} = (2^2)^{\dfrac{1}{3}} = 4^{\dfrac{1}{3}}$

$W' = W(4)^{\dfrac{1}{3}}$

Answer: $\quad \mathrm{W}(4)^{\frac{1}{3}}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A block of mass 'm', kept on a horizontal surface, is moved through a distance 's'
by applying a horizontal force (F) to it. What is the work done by the normal
reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{F}}{\mathrm{s}}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Fs}$
- **(C)** zero
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{S}}{\mathrm{F}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) zero**

**Detailed Solution:**

In the first case, the centre of gravity is at a height $=\frac{b}{2}$ $\therefore$ Work done $=\mathrm{mgh}=\mathrm{mg}\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)$
In the second case, the height of the centre of gravity $=\frac{L}{2}$
$\therefore$ Work done $=m g \frac{L}{2} \therefore L>b$
$\therefore$ Extra work done $\quad=m g\left(\frac{L-b}{2}\right)$

</details>
