### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The maximum and minimum tensions in the string whirling in a circle of radius $2.5$ m are in the ratio $5:3$ then its velocity is ($g = 10\ \text{m/s}^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10\ \text{m/s}$
- **(B)** $100\ \text{m/s}$
- **(C)** $1\ \text{m/s}$
- **(D)** $5\ \text{m/s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $10\ \text{m/s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a particle whirling in a vertical circle with a constant speed $v$, the tension in the string is maximum at the lowest point and minimum at the highest point.

The maximum tension is given by:
$T_{\text{max}} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} + mg$

The minimum tension is given by:
$T_{\text{min}} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} - mg$

Given the ratio of maximum to minimum tension is $5:3$, we have:
$\dfrac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{min}}} = \dfrac{5}{3}$

$\dfrac{\dfrac{mv^2}{r} + mg}{\dfrac{mv^2}{r} - mg} = \dfrac{5}{3}$

Canceling the mass $m$ from the numerator and denominator:
$\dfrac{\dfrac{v^2}{r} + g}{\dfrac{v^2}{r} - g} = \dfrac{5}{3}$

Cross-multiplying yields:
$3 \left( \dfrac{v^2}{r} + g \right) = 5 \left( \dfrac{v^2}{r} - g \right)$

$3 \dfrac{v^2}{r} + 3g = 5 \dfrac{v^2}{r} - 5g$

$2 \dfrac{v^2}{r} = 8g$

$\dfrac{v^2}{r} = 4g$

$v = \sqrt{4gr}$

Substituting the given values $r = 2.5\ \text{m}$ and $g = 10\ \text{m/s}^2$:
$v = \sqrt{4 \times 10 \times 2.5}$

$v = \sqrt{100} = 10\ \text{m/s}$

Answer: $10\ \text{m/s}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A stone of mass m is tied to a string and is moved in a vertical circle of radius r making 'n' r.p.m. The total tension in the string when the stone is at the lowest point is ($g$ = acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $mg$
- **(B)** $m(g + \pi nr^2)$
- **(C)** $m(g + nr)$
- **(D)** $m\left[g + \dfrac{\pi^2 n^2 r}{900}\right]$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $m\left[g + \dfrac{\pi^2 n^2 r}{900}\right]$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\omega$ be the angular velocity of the stone. Since the stone makes $n$ revolutions per minute, the angular velocity in radians per second is given by

$\omega = \dfrac{2\pi n}{60} = \dfrac{\pi n}{30}$

At the lowest point of the vertical circle, the forces acting on the stone are the tension $T$ in the string directed upwards towards the center and the weight $mg$ directed downwards. The net force provides the necessary centripetal acceleration:

$T - mg = m\omega^2 r$

Substituting the value of $\omega$, we get

$T = mg + m\left(\dfrac{\pi n}{30}\right)^2 r$

$T = m\left[g + \dfrac{\pi^2 n^2 r}{900}\right]$

Answer: $m\left[g + \dfrac{\pi^2 n^2 r}{900}\right]$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body of mass 'm' attached at the end of a string is just completing the loop in a vertical circle. The apparent weight of the body at the lowest point in its path is
($g$ = gravitational acceleration)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6\,mg$
- **(B)** $3\,mg$
- **(C)** $1\,mg$
- **(D)** zero

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $6\,mg$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a body to just complete a vertical circle, the tension in the string at the highest point is zero.

$T_H + mg = \dfrac{m v_H^2}{r} \Rightarrow v_H = \sqrt{gr}$

By conservation of mechanical energy between the highest and lowest points:

$\dfrac{1}{2} m v_L^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} m v_H^2 + mg(2r)$

$v_L^2 = v_H^2 + 4gr = gr + 4gr = 5gr$

At the lowest point, the net force towards the center provides the centripetal acceleration:

$T_L - mg = \dfrac{m v_L^2}{r}$

$T_L = mg + \dfrac{m(5gr)}{r} = mg + 5mg = 6mg$

The apparent weight of the body is equal to the tension in the string at that point, which is $6mg$.

Answer: $6\,mg$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A bucket containing water is revolved in vertical circle of radius r. To prevent the water from falling down, the period of revolution required is ($g$ = gravitational acceleration)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{r}}$
- **(B)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{g}}$
- **(C)** $2\pi\sqrt{rg}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{rg}}{2\pi}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{g}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

At the highest point of the vertical circle, the forces acting on the water are its weight $mg$ downwards and the normal reaction $N$ from the bucket downwards.

The necessary centripetal force is provided by these forces:

$mg + N = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

To prevent the water from falling down, the normal reaction must be greater than or equal to zero ($N \ge 0$). The minimum speed $v$ is obtained when $N = 0$:

$mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{rg}$

The angular velocity $\omega$ corresponding to this minimum speed is:

$\omega = \dfrac{v}{r} = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{r}}$

The maximum period of revolution $T$ required to prevent the water from falling is:

$T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{g}}$

Answer: $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{r}{g}}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A point mass ' $m$ ' attached at one end of a massless, inextensible string of length ' $\ell$ ' performs a vertical circular motion and the string rotates in vertical plane, as shown in the diagram. The increase in the centripetal acceleration of the point mass when it moves from point $A$ to point C is
[ $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity.]
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/evjtqGmmQxvu2ioIjHtwt8fbT0_ma2px_jo5MrC-B_8.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 g$
- **(B)** $2 g$
- **(C)** $g$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{g}}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2 g$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the velocity of the point mass at the highest point A be $v_A$ and at the horizontal point C be $v_C$.

By the principle of conservation of mechanical energy between points A and C (taking the horizontal level of C as the reference for zero potential energy):

Total Energy at A = Total Energy at C

$\dfrac{1}{2} m v_A^2 + mg\ell = \dfrac{1}{2} m v_C^2$

Rearranging the above equation, we get:

$v_C^2 - v_A^2 = 2g\ell$

The centripetal acceleration of a particle moving in a circle of radius $\ell$ with velocity $v$ is given by $a_c = \dfrac{v^2}{\ell}$.

The increase in centripetal acceleration when the mass moves from A to C is:

$\Delta a_c = a_{cC} - a_{cA} = \dfrac{v_C^2}{\ell} - \dfrac{v_A^2}{\ell}$

$\Delta a_c = \dfrac{v_C^2 - v_A^2}{\ell}$

Substituting $v_C^2 - v_A^2 = 2g\ell$ into the equation:

$\Delta a_c = \dfrac{2g\ell}{\ell} = 2g$

Answer: $2 g$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A simple pendulum oscillates with an angular amplitude $\theta$. If the maximum tension in the string is 4 times the minimum tension then the value of $\theta$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\cos ^{-1}(0.75)$
- **(B)** $\cos ^{-1}(0.5)$
- **(C)** $\sin ^{-1}(0.5)$
- **(D)** $\sin ^{-1}(0.75)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\cos ^{-1}(0.5)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of the bob be $m$ and the length of the pendulum be $l$.

The tension in the string at an angle $\alpha$ with the vertical is given by:

$T = mg \cos \alpha + \dfrac{mv^2}{l}$

By conservation of energy, the velocity $v$ at angle $\alpha$ is:

$\dfrac{1}{2} mv^2 = mgl (\cos \alpha - \cos \theta)$

$\dfrac{mv^2}{l} = 2mg (\cos \alpha - \cos \theta)$

Substituting this in the tension equation, we get:

$T = mg \cos \alpha + 2mg (\cos \alpha - \cos \theta) = mg (3 \cos \alpha - 2 \cos \theta)$

The maximum tension occurs at the lowest point ($\alpha = 0$):

$T_{max} = mg (3 - 2 \cos \theta)$

The minimum tension occurs at the extreme position ($\alpha = \theta$):

$T_{min} = mg (3 \cos \theta - 2 \cos \theta) = mg \cos \theta$

Given that $T_{max} = 4 T_{min}$:

$mg (3 - 2 \cos \theta) = 4 mg \cos \theta$

$3 - 2 \cos \theta = 4 \cos \theta$

$6 \cos \theta = 3$

$\cos \theta = 0.5$

$\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.5)$

Answer: $\cos ^{-1}(0.5)$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A stone of mass 1 kg tied to a light inextensible string of length $\mathrm{L}=\frac{5}{3} \mathrm{~m}$ is rotating in a circular path of radius L in a vertical plane. If the ratio of maximum tension in the string to the minimum tension in the string is 3 , the speed of the stone at the highest point of the circle is ( $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}}$
- **(B)** 2 $\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}}$
- **(C)** 4 $\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}}$
- **(D)** 8 $\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 2 $\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $v_H$ and $v_L$ be the speeds of the stone at the highest and lowest points of the vertical circle, respectively.

The tension at the highest point (minimum tension) is given by:
$T_{min} = \dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} - mg$

The tension at the lowest point (maximum tension) is given by:
$T_{max} = \dfrac{m v_L^2}{L} + mg$

By the principle of conservation of mechanical energy between the highest and lowest points:
$\dfrac{1}{2} m v_L^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} m v_H^2 + mg(2L)$
$v_L^2 = v_H^2 + 4gL$

Substituting $v_L^2$ into the expression for $T_{max}$:
$T_{max} = \dfrac{m(v_H^2 + 4gL)}{L} + mg = \dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} + 5mg$

We are given that the ratio of maximum tension to minimum tension is 3:
$\dfrac{T_{max}}{T_{min}} = 3$

$\dfrac{\dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} + 5mg}{\dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} - mg} = 3$

$\dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} + 5mg = 3\left(\dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} - mg\right)$

$\dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} + 5mg = 3\dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} - 3mg$

$2\dfrac{m v_H^2}{L} = 8mg$

$\dfrac{v_H^2}{L} = 4g$

$v_H^2 = 4gL$

$v_H = 2\sqrt{gL}$

Answer: 2 $\sqrt{\mathrm{gL}}$

</details>
