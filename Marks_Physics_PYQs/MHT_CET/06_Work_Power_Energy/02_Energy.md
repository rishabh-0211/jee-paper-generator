### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A lead bullet moving with velocity '$v$' strikes a wall and stops. $75\%$ of its energy is converted into heat. If the increase in the temperature is $\dfrac{v^2}{nJs}$, then the value of $n$ is $\{s = \text{specific heat of lead}, J = \text{mechanical equivalent of heat}\}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{3}{8}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{3}{4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{8}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial kinetic energy of the bullet is $K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.

The energy converted into heat is $W = 75\% \text{ of } K = \dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{3}{8}mv^2$.

Using the relation $W = JH$, where the heat absorbed by the bullet is $H = ms\Delta T$:

$\dfrac{3}{8}mv^2 = Jms\Delta T$

$\Delta T = \dfrac{3v^2}{8Js} = \dfrac{v^2}{\left(\dfrac{8}{3}\right)Js}$

Comparing this with the given expression $\Delta T = \dfrac{v^2}{nJs}$, we get:

$n = \dfrac{8}{3}$

Answer: $\dfrac{8}{3}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A sphere of mass $50$ gram is placed on a vertical spring. It is compressed by $0.4$ m using a force $10$ N. When the spring is released the sphere will reach to a height of ($g = 10\,m/s^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8\,m$
- **(B)** $4\,m$
- **(C)** $2\,m$
- **(D)** $16\,m$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4\,m$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The spring constant $k$ can be calculated using the given force and compression:

$k = \dfrac{F}{x} = \dfrac{10}{0.4} = 25\, \text{N/m}$

The elastic potential energy stored in the spring when it is compressed by $0.4\, \text{m}$ is:

$U = \dfrac{1}{2} k x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times (0.4)^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times 0.16 = 2\, \text{J}$

When the spring is released, this elastic potential energy is converted into the gravitational potential energy of the sphere at its maximum height $h$ (measured from the compressed position). Using the law of conservation of energy:

$mgh = U$

Given $m = 50\, \text{g} = 0.05\, \text{kg}$ and $g = 10\, \text{m/s}^2$, substituting the values we get:

$0.05 \times 10 \times h = 2$

$0.5 h = 2$

$h = 4\, \text{m}$

Answer: $4\,m$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A block of mass 'm' moving on a frictionless horizontal surface collides with a spring of spring constant 'K' and compresses it through a distance 'x'. The maximum momentum of the block after collision is

**Options:**

- **(A)** zero
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\text{km}}\ x$
- **(C)** $\text{mx}^2/\text{K}$
- **(D)** $\text{Kx}^2/2\text{m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{\text{km}}\ x$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By the principle of conservation of mechanical energy, the maximum kinetic energy of the block is equal to the maximum potential energy stored in the spring at maximum compression.

$\dfrac{p^2}{2m} = \dfrac{1}{2} K x^2$

$p^2 = m K x^2$

$p = \sqrt{m K} x$

The maximum momentum of the block after the collision is equal in magnitude to its initial momentum, which is $\sqrt{m K} x$.

Answer: $\sqrt{\text{km}}\ x$

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A pendulum bob has a speed $4 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ at its lowest position. The pendulum is 1 m long. When the length of the string makes an angle of $60^{\circ}$ with the vertical, the speed of the bob at that position is (acceleration due to gravity, $g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2, \cos 60^{\circ}=0.5$ )

$\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgl(1 - \cos \theta)$

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A particle is moving in a circle with uniform speed. It has constant

**Options:**

- **(A)** velocity.
- **(B)** acceleration.
- **(C)** kinetic energy.
- **(D)** displacement.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) kinetic energy.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In uniform circular motion, the speed of the particle remains constant.

Velocity is a vector quantity. Although its magnitude (speed) is constant, its direction changes continuously along the tangent to the circular path. Hence, velocity is not constant.

Acceleration in uniform circular motion is centripetal, directed towards the centre. Its magnitude is constant ($v^2/r$), but its direction changes continuously. Hence, acceleration is not constant.

Displacement is a vector quantity that changes as the position of the particle changes.

Kinetic energy is a scalar quantity given by $K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. Since both mass $m$ and speed $v$ are constant, the kinetic energy remains constant.

Answer: kinetic energy.

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The string of pendulum of length ' $L$ ' is displaced through $90^{\circ}$ from the vertical and released. Then the maximum strength of the string in order to withstand the tension, as the pendulum passes through the mean position is ( $\mathrm{m}=$ mass of pendulum, $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** mg
- **(B)** 3 mg
- **(C)** 5 mg
- **(D)** 6 mg

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 3 mg**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of the pendulum be $m$ and its length be $L$.

When the pendulum is released from a $90^{\circ}$ angle, it falls through a vertical height $L$ to reach the mean position.

By conservation of mechanical energy, the potential energy lost is converted into kinetic energy at the lowest point:

$m g L = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$

$v^2 = 2 g L$

At the mean position, the tension $T$ in the string and the weight $m g$ provide the necessary centripetal force:

$T - m g = \dfrac{m v^2}{L}$

Substituting the value of $v^2$:

$T = m g + \dfrac{m (2 g L)}{L}$

$T = m g + 2 m g = 3 m g$

The maximum tension occurs at the mean position, so the string must be able to withstand a tension of $3 m g$.

Answer: 3 mg

</details>

### Q22. MHT CET 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A sphere is suspended by a thread of length $l$ What minimum horizontal velocity has to be imparted the ball for it to reach the height of the suspension?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{gl}$
- **(B)** $2 \mathrm{gl}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{2 g l}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{g l}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{g l}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

KE given to a sphere at lowest point
= potential energy at the height of suspension $\frac{1}{2} m v^{2}=m g l$
$\therefore$
$$
v=\sqrt{2 g l}
$$

</details>

### Q23. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A long spring is stretched by $2 \mathrm{~cm}$ and its potential energy is $U$. If the spring is stretched by $10 \mathrm{~cm}$; its potential energy will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $U / 5$
- **(B)** $U / 25$
- **(C)** $5 U$
- **(D)** $25 U$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $25 U$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The potential energy of a stretched spring is
$$
U=\frac{1}{2} k x^{2}
$$
Here, $k=$ spring constant, $x=$ elongation in spring. But given that, the elongation is $2 \mathrm{~cm}$.
So, $U=\frac{1}{2} k(2)^{2}$
$$
\Rightarrow \quad U=\frac{1}{2} k \times 4 ...(i)
$$
If elongation is $10 \mathrm{~cm}$ then potential energy
$$
\begin{aligned}
U^{\prime} &=\frac{1}{2} k(10)^{2} \\
U^{\prime} &=\frac{1}{2} k \times 100 ...(ii)
\end{aligned}
$$
On dividing Eq. (ii) by Eq. (i), we have
$$
\frac{U^{\prime}}{U}=\frac{\frac{1}{2} k \times 100}{\frac{1}{2} k \times 4}
$$
or $\frac{U^{\prime}}{U}=25 \Rightarrow U^{\prime}=25 U$

</details>
