### Q2. MHT CET 2020 (20 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Modulation is a process of superposing

**Options:**

- **(A)** low frequency radio signal on low frequency audio waves.
- **(B)** high frequency audio signal on low frequency radio waves.
- **(C)** low frequency audio signal on high frequency radio waves.
- **(D)** high frequency radio signal on low frequency audio signal.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) low frequency audio signal on high frequency radio waves.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Low frequency audio signal cannot be transmitted as such over long distances. Therefore. to overcome this difficulty, the low frequency audio signal is superposed on a high frequency wave (carrier wave). This process is called modulation.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2020 (19 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A carrier wave of peak voltage 16 volt is used to transmit a signal. If the
modulation index is $75 \%$, the peak voltage of the modulating signal is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 6 volt
- **(B)** 24 volt
- **(C)** 18 volt
- **(D)** 12 volt

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 12 volt**

**Detailed Solution:**

A carrier wave of peak voltage $16 \mathrm{~V}$ is used to transmit a signal. If the modulation index is $75 \%$, the peak voltage of the modulating signal is $12 \mathrm{~V}$.
Explanation:
Modulation Index
$(\mathrm{MI})=\frac{\text { Peak voltage of modulating signal }\left(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{m}}\right)}{\text { Peak voltage of carrier } \operatorname{signal}\left(\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{C}}\right)}$
Given, $\mathrm{Ml}=75 \%$ and $\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{C}}=16 \mathrm{~V}$
$\therefore \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{m}}=\mathrm{MI} \times \mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{75}{100} \times 16=12 \mathrm{~V}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A signal of frequency $3 \mathrm{kHz}$ is amplitude modulated on a carrier wave of frequency
$2 \cdot 5 \mathrm{MHz}$. The upper and lower sideband frequencies in the resultant signal are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2500 \mathrm{kHz}, 2503 \mathrm{kHz}$
- **(B)** $2503 \mathrm{kHz}, 2497 \mathrm{kHz}$
- **(C)** $2 \cdot 5 \mathrm{MHz}, 3 \mathrm{kHz}$
- **(D)** $5 \cdot 5 \mathrm{MHz}, 0 \cdot 5 \mathrm{MHz}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2503 \mathrm{kHz}, 2497 \mathrm{kHz}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} f_{R}=f_{C}+f_{m} &=2500 \mathrm{KHz}+3 \mathrm{kz} \\ &=2503 \mathrm{KHz} \\ f_{R}=f_{C}-f_{m} &=2500 \mathrm{kHz}-3 \mathrm{kHz} \\ &=2497 \mathrm{kHz} \end{aligned}$

</details>
