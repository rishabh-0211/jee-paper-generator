### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Three capacitors are connected to a battery as shown in figure. The ratio of charge on capacitors $C_3$ and $C_1$ is

<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/PJCK4JpbFQKVYtXnSUaDLkuJoK8CsbiuSK03VvV3Jvk.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1.5
- **(B)** 2.5
- **(C)** 3.5
- **(D)** 4.5

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 4.5**

**Detailed Solution:**

From the given circuit diagram, the capacitor $C_3$ is connected directly across the battery $V$.

Therefore, the potential difference across $C_3$ is $V$.

The charge on $C_3$ is given by:
$Q_3 = C_3 V = 3CV$

Capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$ are connected in series, and this series combination is connected in parallel with $C_3$ across the battery $V$.

The equivalent capacitance of the series combination of $C_1$ and $C_2$ is:
$C_{12} = \dfrac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2} = \dfrac{1C \times 2C}{1C + 2C} = \dfrac{2C^2}{3C} = \dfrac{2}{3}C$

The potential difference across this series combination is also $V$. In a series circuit, the charge on each capacitor is the same and equal to the total charge of the combination.

The charge on $C_1$ is:
$Q_1 = C_{12} V = \dfrac{2}{3}CV$

The ratio of the charge on capacitor $C_3$ to that on $C_1$ is:
$\dfrac{Q_3}{Q_1} = \dfrac{3CV}{\dfrac{2}{3}CV} = \dfrac{3 \times 3}{2} = \dfrac{9}{2} = 4.5$

Answer: 4.5

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Three condensers of capacities ' $\mathrm{C}_1$ ', ' $\mathrm{C}_2$ ', ' $\mathrm{C}_3$ ' are connected in series with a source of e.m.f. ' $V$ '. The potentials across the three condensers are in the ratio

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 1: 1$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{C}_1: \mathrm{C}_2: \mathrm{C}_3$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{C}_1^2: \mathrm{C}_2^2: \mathrm{C}_3^2$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_1}: \frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_2}: \frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_1}: \frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_2}: \frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a series combination of capacitors, the charge $Q$ on each capacitor is the same.

The potential difference across a capacitor is given by $V = \dfrac{Q}{C}$.

For the three capacitors, the potential differences are $V_1 = \dfrac{Q}{C_1}$, $V_2 = \dfrac{Q}{C_2}$, and $V_3 = \dfrac{Q}{C_3}$.

The ratio of the potentials across the three capacitors is $V_1 : V_2 : V_3 = \dfrac{Q}{C_1} : \dfrac{Q}{C_2} : \dfrac{Q}{C_3}$.

Since $Q$ is constant, the ratio becomes $\dfrac{1}{C_1} : \dfrac{1}{C_2} : \dfrac{1}{C_3}$.

Answer: $\frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_1}: \frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_2}: \frac{1}{\mathrm{C}_3}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2022 (10 Aug Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A parallel plate capacitor is charged and then disconnected from the charging battery. If the plates are now moved further apart by pulling them by means of insulting handles, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** the capacitance of the capacitor increases
- **(B)** the voltage across the capacitor increases
- **(C)** the energy stored in the capacitor decreases
- **(D)** the charge on the capacitor decreases

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) the voltage across the capacitor increases**

**Detailed Solution:**

When the capacitor is charged and then disconnected, the charge across the plates remains a constant, even when the plates are separated.
The capacitance is related to the structure of parallel plate capacitor as $\frac{A \varepsilon_0}{d}$. When the plate is separated, the distance between the plate $d$ increases and hence the capacitance decreases.
Hence, according to the $Q=C V$ relation, the voltage across the capacitor increases in order to keep the charge constant.

</details>

- **(C)** $100 \%$
- **(D)** $25 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

### Q6. MHT CET 2021 (21 Sep Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A condenser of capacity ' $\mathrm{C}_1$ ' is charged to potential ' $\mathrm{V}_1$ ' and then disconnected. Uncharged capacitor of capacity ' $\mathrm{C}_2$ ' is connected in parallel with ' $\mathrm{C}_1$ '. The resultant potential ' $\mathrm{V}_2$ ' is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{V}_1 \mathrm{C}_2}{\mathrm{C}_1}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{C}_2}{\mathrm{C}_1+\mathrm{C}_2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{C}_1 \mathrm{~V}_1}{\mathrm{C}_2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{C}_1 \mathrm{~V}_1}{\mathrm{C}_1+\mathrm{C}_2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\mathrm{C}_1 \mathrm{~V}_1}{\mathrm{C}_1+\mathrm{C}_2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The charge stored by the charged condenser is
$$
\mathrm{Q}=\mathrm{C}_1 \mathrm{~V}_1
$$
When the uncharged condenser is connected in parallel, the effective capacitance becomes $\left(\mathrm{C}_1+\mathrm{C}_2\right)$
Hence the potential, $\mathrm{V}_2=\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{\mathrm{C}_1+\mathrm{C}_2}=\frac{\mathrm{C}_1 \mathrm{~V}_1}{\mathrm{C}_1+\mathrm{C}_2}$

</details>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $9 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We know that in steady state the capacitor behaves like as an open circuit i.e., capacitor will not pass the current.

So, the potential difference across the capacitor $=45 \mathrm{~V}$
Hence, the final charge on the capacitor is
$$
q=C V
$$
Here, $C=20 \mu \mathrm{F}, V=45 \mathrm{~V}$
$\therefore$
$$
q=20 \times 10^{-6} \times 45
