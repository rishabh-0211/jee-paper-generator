### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Four parallel plate capacitors are connected in parallel. Each capacitor has a plate area $\dfrac{A}{4}$ and the separation between the plates is $d$, $2d$, $3d$ and $4d$ respectively. The equivalent capacity of the combination is [$\varepsilon_0 = $ permittivity of free space]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{5A\varepsilon_0}{12d}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{7A\varepsilon_0}{24d}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{9A\varepsilon_0}{36d}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{25A\varepsilon_0}{48d}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{25A\varepsilon_0}{48d}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is given by $C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A'}{d'}$.

For the four capacitors, the area is $A' = \dfrac{A}{4}$ and the distances are $d$, $2d$, $3d$, and $4d$.

The individual capacitances are:

$C_1 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \left(\dfrac{A}{4}\right)}{d} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{4d}$

$C_2 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \left(\dfrac{A}{4}\right)}{2d} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{8d}$

$C_3 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \left(\dfrac{A}{4}\right)}{3d} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{12d}$

$C_4 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \left(\dfrac{A}{4}\right)}{4d} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{16d}$

Since the capacitors are connected in parallel, the equivalent capacitance is the sum of the individual capacitances:

$C_{eq} = C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + C_4$

$C_{eq} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{4d} + \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{8d} + \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{12d} + \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{16d}$

$C_{eq} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} \left( \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{12} + \dfrac{1}{16} \right)$

Taking the common denominator as $48$:

$C_{eq} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} \left( \dfrac{12 + 6 + 4 + 3}{48} \right) = \dfrac{25A\varepsilon_0}{48d}$

Answer: $\dfrac{25A\varepsilon_0}{48d}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Consider the earth to be a sphere, having charge $Q$. Let $R$, $V$ and $A$ be its radius, volume and area respectively. The capacitance of the earth is [$\varepsilon_0$ = permittivity of free space]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4\pi\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{V}{A}$
- **(B)** $4\pi\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{A}{V}$
- **(C)** $12\pi\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{A}{V}$
- **(D)** $12\pi\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{V}{A}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $12\pi\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{V}{A}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The capacitance of a spherical conductor of radius $R$ is given by $C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 R$.

The volume of the sphere is $V = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$.

The surface area of the sphere is $A = 4\pi R^2$.

Dividing the volume by the area gives:

$\dfrac{V}{A} = \dfrac{\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3}{4\pi R^2} = \dfrac{R}{3}$

$R = 3\dfrac{V}{A}$

Substituting the value of $R$ in the expression for capacitance:

$C = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 \left(3\dfrac{V}{A}\right) = 12\pi\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{V}{A}$

Answer: $12\pi\varepsilon_0 \dfrac{V}{A}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A capacitor of unknown capacitance is connected across a battery of $'V'$ volt. The charge stored in it is $'Q'$ C. When potential across the capacitor is reduced by $'V_1'$ volt, the charge stored in it becomes $'Q_1'$ C. The potential $'V'$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{QV_1}{Q - Q_1}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{Q}{(Q - Q_1)}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{V_1}{(Q_1 - Q)}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{(Q - Q_1)}{V_1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{QV_1}{Q - Q_1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the capacitance be $C$.

The initial charge is $Q$ when the potential is $V$.

$Q = CV \Rightarrow C = \dfrac{Q}{V}$

When the potential is reduced by $V_1$, the new potential is $V - V_1$ and the new charge is $Q_1$.

$Q_1 = C(V - V_1)$

Substituting $C = \dfrac{Q}{V}$:

$Q_1 = \dfrac{Q}{V}(V - V_1)$

$Q_1 V = Q(V - V_1)$

$Q_1 V = QV - QV_1$

$QV_1 = QV - Q_1 V$

$QV_1 = V(Q - Q_1)$

$V = \dfrac{QV_1}{Q - Q_1}$

</details>

- **(C)** $\dfrac{q_1 - q_2}{2C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{q_1 - q_2}{2C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When two identical metal plates are given charges $q_1$ and $q_2$, the charge on the outer surfaces of both plates is $\dfrac{q_1 + q_2}{2}$.

The charge on the inner surface of the second plate is $q_2 - \dfrac{q_1 + q_2}{2} = -\left(\dfrac{q_1 - q_2}{2}\right)$.

The charge on the capacitor is the magnitude of the charge on the inner surfaces, which is $q = \dfrac{q_1 - q_2}{2}$.

The potential difference $V$ between the plates is given by $V = \dfrac{q}{C}$.

Substituting the value of $q$, we get $V = \dfrac{q_1 - q_2}{2C}$.

The ratio of the capacitances $\mathrm{C}_1: \mathrm{C}_2$. is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 1$
- **(B)** $1: 2$
- **(C)** $1: 4$
- **(D)** $4: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1: 4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is given by $C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \pi r^2}{d}$.

For the initial capacitor:
$C_1 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \pi r^2}{d}$

For the final capacitor, the radius is $r' = \sqrt{2}r$ and the distance is $d' = \dfrac{d}{2}$.

The new capacitance is:
$C_2 = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \pi (\sqrt{2}r)^2}{\dfrac{d}{2}} = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \pi (2r^2)}{\dfrac{d}{2}} = \dfrac{4\varepsilon_0 \pi r^2}{d}$

Taking the ratio of $C_1$ to $C_2$:
$\dfrac{C_1}{C_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \pi r^2}{d}}{\dfrac{4\varepsilon_0 \pi r^2}{d}} = \dfrac{1}{4}$

The ratio $C_1 : C_2$ is $1 : 4$.

Answer: $1: 4$
