### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Air capacitor has capacitance $C_1$. The space between two plates of capacitor is filled with two dielectric as shown in figure. The new capacitance of the capacitor is $C_2$. Then $\dfrac{C_1}{C_2}$ is [$d$ = distance between two plates, $K_1$ and $K_2$ are dielectric constants of first and second dielectrics respectively]

<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/20_may_shift_1__q25_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{11}{30}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{8}{30}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{7}{30}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{13}{30}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{8}{30}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial capacitance of the air capacitor is given by:

$C_1 = \dfrac{\epsilon_0 A}{d}$

From the given figure, the two dielectrics are placed in series between the plates. Each dielectric has a thickness of $d/2$ and the same cross-sectional area $A$.

The capacitance of the first dielectric region is:

$C'_1 = \dfrac{K_1 \epsilon_0 A}{d/2} = \dfrac{2 K_1 \epsilon_0 A}{d} = 2 K_1 C_1$

The capacitance of the second dielectric region is:

$C'_2 = \dfrac{K_2 \epsilon_0 A}{d/2} = \dfrac{2 K_2 \epsilon_0 A}{d} = 2 K_2 C_1$

Since these two capacitors are in series, the equivalent capacitance $C_2$ is:

$\dfrac{1}{C_2} = \dfrac{1}{C'_1} + \dfrac{1}{C'_2}$

$\dfrac{1}{C_2} = \dfrac{1}{2 K_1 C_1} + \dfrac{1}{2 K_2 C_1}$

$\dfrac{1}{C_2} = \dfrac{1}{2 C_1} \left( \dfrac{1}{K_1} + \dfrac{1}{K_2} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2 C_1} \left( \dfrac{K_1 + K_2}{K_1 K_2} \right)$

Rearranging to find the ratio $\dfrac{C_1}{C_2}$:

$\dfrac{C_1}{C_2} = \dfrac{K_1 + K_2}{2 K_1 K_2}$

Substituting the given values $K_1 = 5$ and $K_2 = 3$:

$\dfrac{C_1}{C_2} = \dfrac{5 + 3}{2 \times 5 \times 3} = \dfrac{8}{30}$

Answer: $\dfrac{8}{30}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A parallel plate capacitor having capacity $12$ pF is charged by a battery of $10$ V between its plates. The battery is now disconnected and a slab of dielectric constant $6$ is kept between the plates. The work done by the capacitor on the slab is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 \times 10^{-10}$ J
- **(B)** $2 \times 10^{-10}$ J
- **(C)** $5 \times 10^{-10}$ J
- **(D)** $6 \times 10^{-10}$ J

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5 \times 10^{-10}$ J**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial capacitance, $C_i = 12 \times 10^{-12}$ F

Initial voltage, $V_i = 10$ V

Initial energy stored in the capacitor is given by:

$U_i = \dfrac{1}{2} C_i V_i^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 10^{-12} \times (10)^2 = 6 \times 10^{-10}$ J

Since the battery is disconnected, the charge on the capacitor remains constant. The initial charge is $Q = C_i V_i$.

When a dielectric slab of constant $K = 6$ is inserted, the new capacitance becomes:

$C_f = K C_i = 6 \times 12 \times 10^{-12} = 72 \times 10^{-12}$ F

The final energy stored in the capacitor is:

$U_f = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C_f} = \dfrac{Q^2}{2(K C_i)} = \dfrac{U_i}{K}$

$U_f = \dfrac{6 \times 10^{-10}}{6} = 1 \times 10^{-10}$ J

The work done by the capacitor on the slab is equal to the decrease in its electrostatic potential energy:

$W = U_i - U_f = 6 \times 10^{-10} - 1 \times 10^{-10} = 5 \times 10^{-10}$ J

Answer: $5 \times 10^{-10}$ J

</details>

When two dielectric slabs of thickness $\dfrac{d}{2}$ are introduced between the plates, they act as two capacitors connected in series.

Capacitance of the first part is $C_1 = \dfrac{K_1 \varepsilon_0 A}{d/2} = \dfrac{2K_1 \varepsilon_0 A}{d} = 2K_1 C$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When a dielectric is placed in an electric field, the electric field inside the dielectric

**Options:**

- **(A)** increases
- **(B)** decreases
- **(C)** is constant
- **(D)** is zero

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) decreases**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a dielectric is placed in an external electric field $\vec{E}_{0}$, the molecules of the dielectric get polarized.

This polarization induces an internal electric field $\vec{E}_{p}$ in a direction opposite to the external electric field.

The net electric field inside the dielectric is given by $\vec{E} = \vec{E}_{0} - \vec{E}_{p}$.

Since the induced electric field opposes the external electric field, the net electric field inside the dielectric decreases.

Answer: decreases

</details>

The original capacitance of the parallel plate capacitor is given by:

$C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$

$C' = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d - t + \dfrac{t}{k}}$

### Q9. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When the dielectric is placed in an external electric field, the electric field inside the dielectric is

**Options:**

- **(A)** larger than the external electric field.
- **(B)** less than the external electric field.
- **(C)** equal to the external electric field.
- **(D)** equal to or greater than external electric field.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) less than the external electric field.**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a dielectric material is placed in an external electric field $\vec{E}_0$, the molecules of the dielectric get polarized. This polarization induces an internal electric field $\vec{E}_p$ which opposes the external electric field.

The net electric field inside the dielectric is given by $\vec{E} = \vec{E}_0 - \vec{E}_p$.

Since the induced electric field $\vec{E}_p$ is in the opposite direction to $\vec{E}_0$, the magnitude of the net electric field inside the dielectric is $E = E_0 - E_p$.

Alternatively, the electric field inside a dielectric is $E = \dfrac{E_0}{K}$, where $K$ is the dielectric constant. Since $K > 1$ for all dielectrics, $E < E_0$.

Thus, the electric field inside the dielectric is always less than the external electric field.

Answer: less than the external electric field.

</details>

- **(D)** both V and E decrease.

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

The new energy is $E' = \dfrac{Q^2}{2C'} = \dfrac{Q^2}{2KC} = \dfrac{E}{K}$.

Since $K > 1$, $E' < E$, so the energy also decreases.

Therefore, both $V$ and $E$ decrease.

### Q16. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 1)

A parallel plate capacitor has plate area $40 \text{ cm}^2$ and plates separation $2$ mm. The space between the plates is filled with a dielectric medium of a thickness $1$ mm and dielectric constant $5$. The capacitance of the system is

**Options:**

- **(B)** $\dfrac{3}{10} \epsilon_0 \text{ F}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{10}{3} \epsilon_0 \text{ F}$

### Q55. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Tangent galvanometer measures

**Options:**

- **(A)** capacitance
- **(B)** current
- **(C)** resistance
- **(D)** potential difference

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) current**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a current is passed through the galvanometer coil, then a magnetic field $B$ is produced at right angles to the plane of the coil, ie, at right angles to the horizontal component of earth's magnetic field $H$. Under the influence of two crossed magnetic fields $B$ and $H$, the magnetic needle of galvanometerundergoes a deflection $\theta$ which is given by the tangent law. Using tangent law, we can find a relation
$I \propto \tan \theta$
Which clearly indicates that tangent galvanometer is an instrument used for detection of electric current in a circuit. Note A tangent galvanometer is most accurate when its deflection is $45^{\circ}$.

</details>
