**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2\pi \sqrt{mr}}{T}$

- **(C)** $\dfrac{2\pi T}{\sqrt{mr}}$

$\sqrt{F} = \sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi^2 m r}{T^2}} = \dfrac{2\pi \sqrt{mr}}{T}$

The ratio of angular speed of B to that of A will be

**Options:**

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

### Q11. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

A particle is moving with constant angular acceleration $4\,\text{rad/s}^2$ in circular path. At what time the magnitudes of its tangential acceleration and centripetal acceleration will be equal ?

- **(C)** $0.5$ s
- **(D)** $0.6$ s

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.5$ s**

$\alpha^2 t^2 = \alpha$

Substituting $\alpha = 4 \text{ rad/s}^2$:

### Q12. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle describes a horizontal circle of radius 'r' in conical funnel with smooth inner surface with a speed of $0.5$ m/s. The height of the plane of the circle from the vertex of the funnel is (acceleration due to gravity, $g = 10\,\text{m/s}^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.5$ cm
- **(B)** $2.5$ cm
- **(C)** $3.5$ cm
- **(D)** $0.5$ cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2.5$ cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\theta$ be the semi-vertical angle of the conical funnel.
The radius of the circular path is $r$ and its height from the vertex is $h$.
From the geometry of the cone, we have:
$\tan \theta = \dfrac{r}{h}$

The forces acting on the particle are its weight $mg$ acting vertically downwards and the normal reaction $N$ acting perpendicular to the inner surface of the funnel.
The normal reaction makes an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal. Resolving $N$ into vertical and horizontal components, we get:
$N \sin \theta = mg$
$N \cos \theta = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

Dividing the vertical component by the horizontal component, we obtain:
$\tan \theta = \dfrac{rg}{v^2}$

Equating the two expressions for $\tan \theta$:
$\dfrac{r}{h} = \dfrac{rg}{v^2}$

$\Rightarrow h = \dfrac{v^2}{g}$

Given $v = 0.5$ m/s and $g = 10$ m/s$^2$, substituting the values:
$h = \dfrac{(0.5)^2}{10} = \dfrac{0.25}{10} = 0.025$ m

Converting to centimeters:
$h = 0.025 \times 100$ cm $= 2.5$ cm

Answer: $2.5$ cm

</details>

- **(B)** 9 $\mathrm{Cm}$
- **(C)** 6 Cn
- **(D)** $12 \mathrm{Cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

Let $\ell$ be the original length of the spring.
Let the initial angular velocity be $\omega$ and the corresponding elongation $\mathbf{e}_{1}=1 \mathrm{~cm}$.

$$
\begin{array}{l}

Dividing Eq(1) by Eq(2), we get

solving we get $\ell=9 \mathrm{~cm}$
