### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the initial velocity of projection be doubled, keeping the angle of projection same, the maximum height reached by it will

**Options:**

- **(A)** remain the same
- **(B)** be doubled
- **(C)** become four times
- **(D)** be halved

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) become four times**

**Detailed Solution:**

The maximum height reached by a projectile is given by the formula:

$H = \dfrac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}$

where $u$ is the initial velocity and $	heta$ is the angle of projection.

When the initial velocity is doubled, $u' = 2u$. The new maximum height $H'$ is:

$H' = \dfrac{(2u)^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}$

$H' = 4 \left( \dfrac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g} \right)$

$H' = 4H$

Therefore, the maximum height becomes four times.

Answer: become four times

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle of mass m is projected with velocity V making an angle of $45^\circ$ with the horizontal. The magnitude of the angular momentum of the projectile about an axis of projection when the particle is at maximum height h is ($g$ = acceleration due to gravity) $\left(\sin 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$ $\left(\cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** zero
- **(B)** $\dfrac{mV^3}{4\sqrt{2}g}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{mV^3}{\sqrt{2}g}$
- **(D)** $m\sqrt{2gh^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{mV^3}{4\sqrt{2}g}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

At the maximum height, the vertical component of velocity is zero. The particle only has a horizontal velocity component given by:

$v_x = V \cos 45^{\circ} = \dfrac{V}{\sqrt{2}}$

The maximum height $h$ reached by the projectile is the perpendicular distance from the point of projection to the line of motion at the highest point.

$h = \dfrac{V^2 \sin^2 45^{\circ}}{2g} = \dfrac{V^2 \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2}{2g} = \dfrac{V^2}{4g}$

The magnitude of angular momentum $L$ about the point of projection is the product of the magnitude of linear momentum and the perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of motion.

$L = p \times h = m v_x h$

Substituting the values of $v_x$ and $h$:

$L = m \left( \dfrac{V}{\sqrt{2}} \right) \left( \dfrac{V^2}{4g} \right)$

$L = \dfrac{m V^3}{4\sqrt{2}g}$

</details>

A ball P is projected at an angle of $60^\circ$ with the vertical with certain initial speed. Another ball Q of the same mass as that of ball P is projected vertically upwards with the same initial speed as that of P. At the highest point, the ratio of potential energy of ball P to that of ball Q is
$(\sin 30^\circ = 0.5)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 : 4$
- **(B)** $4 : 1$
- **(C)** $2 : 3$
- **(D)** $3 : 2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1 : 4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial speed of both balls be $u$ and their mass be $m$.

For ball P, the angle of projection with the vertical is $60^\circ$, so the angle with the horizontal is $\theta_P = 90^\circ - 60^\circ = 30^\circ$.

The maximum height reached by ball P is $H_P = \dfrac{u^2 \sin^2 30^\circ}{2g} = \dfrac{u^2 (1/2)^2}{2g} = \dfrac{u^2}{8g}$.

The potential energy of ball P at the highest point is $U_P = mgH_P = mg \left(\dfrac{u^2}{8g}\right) = \dfrac{mu^2}{8}$.

For ball Q, it is projected vertically upwards, so the angle of projection with the horizontal is $\theta_Q = 90^\circ$.

The maximum height reached by ball Q is $H_Q = \dfrac{u^2 \sin^2 90^\circ}{2g} = \dfrac{u^2}{2g}$.

### Q22. MHT CET 2021 (21 Sep Shift 1)

A bomb is dropped by an aero plane flying horizontally with a velocity $200 \mathrm{~km} / \mathrm{hr}$ and at a height of $980 \mathrm{~m}$. At the time of dropping a bomb, the distance of the aero plane from the target on the ground to hit directly is $\left(\mathrm{g}=9.8 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2\right.$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\sqrt{2} \times 10^4}{9} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(B)** $\frac{10^4}{9} \mathrm{~m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}

$$
The horizontal distance to be covered is
$$
\mathrm{d}=\mathrm{Vt}=\frac{1000}{18} \times 10 \sqrt{2}=\frac{10^4}{9 \sqrt{2}} \mathrm{~m}
$$

</details>
