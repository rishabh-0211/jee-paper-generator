### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

When a large bubble rises from bottom of a water lake to its surface, then its radius doubles. If the atmospheric pressure is equal to the pressure of height $H$ of a certain water column then the depth of lake will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2H$
- **(B)** $H$
- **(C)** $7H$
- **(D)** $4H$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $7H$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the radius of the bubble at the bottom be $r$ and at the surface be $2r$.

Volume of the bubble at the bottom, $V_2 = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$

Volume of the bubble at the surface, $V_1 = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi (2r)^3 = 8V_2$

Let the depth of the lake be $h$ and the density of water be $\rho$.

Pressure at the surface, $P_1 = P_{atm} = \rho g H$

Pressure at the bottom, $P_2 = P_{atm} + \rho g h = \rho g (H + h)$

Assuming the temperature remains constant, Boyle's law is applicable:

$P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$

$\rho g H (8V_2) = \rho g (H + h) V_2$

$8H = H + h$

$h = 7H$

Answer: $7H$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The pressure at half the depth of a lake is equal to two-third pressure at the bottom of the lake. So the depth 'h' of the lake is ($\rho$ = density of water in the lake, $g$ = acceleration due to gravity, $P_0$ = atmospheric pressure).

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2P_0}{\rho g}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{P_0}{\rho g}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2P_0}{3\rho g}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{P_0}{2\rho g}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{2P_0}{\rho g}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the depth of the lake be $h$.

Pressure at half the depth of the lake is given by $P_1 = P_0 + \dfrac{\rho g h}{2}$.

Pressure at the bottom of the lake is given by $P_2 = P_0 + \rho g h$.

According to the given condition, $P_1 = \dfrac{2}{3} P_2$.

Substituting the expressions for $P_1$ and $P_2$:

$P_0 + \dfrac{\rho g h}{2} = \dfrac{2}{3} (P_0 + \rho g h)$

$P_0 - \dfrac{2}{3} P_0 = \dfrac{2}{3} \rho g h - \dfrac{1}{2} \rho g h$

$\dfrac{P_0}{3} = \dfrac{\rho g h}{6}$

$h = \dfrac{2P_0}{\rho g}$

Answer: $\dfrac{2P_0}{\rho g}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The temperature of an air bubble while rising from bottom to surface of a lake remains constant but diameter is doubled. If pressure on the surface is h meter of mercury column and relative density of mercury is '$\rho$' then the depth of the lake is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 5 h $\rho$
- **(B)** 7 h $\rho$
- **(C)** $\frac{3 \mathrm{~h}}{\rho}$
- **(D)** $\frac{4 \mathrm{~h}}{\rho}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 7 h $\rho$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $V_1$ and $V_2$ be the volumes of the air bubble at the bottom and surface of the lake respectively.

Since the diameter is doubled, the radius is also doubled. The volume of a sphere is proportional to the cube of its radius.

$V_2 = \dfrac{4}{3} \pi (2r)^3 = 8 \left( \dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \right) = 8 V_1$

Since the temperature remains constant, Boyle's law is applicable:

$P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$

$P_1 V_1 = P_2 (8 V_1)$

$P_1 = 8 P_2$

Let $H$ be the depth of the lake, $\rho_w$ be the density of water, and $\rho_m$ be the density of mercury. The relative density of mercury is $\rho$, so $\rho_m = \rho \rho_w$.

Pressure at the surface of the lake is given by $h$ meter of mercury column:

$P_2 = h \rho_m g = h \rho \rho_w g$

Pressure at the bottom of the lake is:

$P_1 = P_2 + H \rho_w g$

Substituting $P_1$ into the equation $P_1 = 8 P_2$:

$P_2 + H \rho_w g = 8 P_2$

$H \rho_w g = 7 P_2$

Substituting the value of $P_2$:

$H \rho_w g = 7 (h \rho \rho_w g)$

$H = 7 h \rho$

Answer: 7 h $\rho$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

What should be the diameter of a soap bubble, in order that the excess pressure inside it is $25.6 \mathrm{Nm}^{-2}$ ? [surface tension of soap solution $\left.=3.2 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{Nm}^{-2}\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(B)** $1.5 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(C)** $1 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(D)** $0.5 \mathrm{~cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1 \mathrm{~cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The formula for excess pressure inside the bubble is given as $P_0=\frac{4 T}{R}$
Where, $\mathrm{T}$ is surface tension and $\mathrm{R}$ is the radius of the sphere
Rearranging the formula for $\mathrm{R}$ and substituting the values,$\begin{aligned}
& R=\frac{4 T}{P_0} \\
& R=\frac{4 \times 3.2 \times 10^{-2}}{25.6} \\
& R=0.5 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~m}=0.5 \mathrm{~cm}
\end{aligned}$
The diameter then becomes, $2 \mathrm{R}=1 \mathrm{~cm}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2020 (19 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The compressibility of water is $5 \times 10^{-10} \mathrm{~m}^{2} / \mathrm{N}$. Pressure of $15 \times 10^{6} \mathrm{~Pa}$ is applied
on $100 \mathrm{ml}$ volume of water. The change in the volume of water is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.75 \mathrm{ml}$ increase.
- **(B)** $1.50 \mathrm{ml}$ increase.
- **(C)** $0.75 \mathrm{ml}$ decrease.
- **(D)** zero.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.75 \mathrm{ml}$ decrease.**

**Detailed Solution:**

(A)
Given,
$\begin{array}{l}
\beta=\frac{1}{\mathrm{~K}}=5 \times 10^{-10} \mathrm{~m}^{2} / \mathrm{N} \\
\Delta \mathrm{P}=15 \mathrm{MPa}=15 \times 10^{6} \mathrm{~Pa}
\end{array}$
Bulk modulus,
$\mathrm{K}=-\frac{\Delta \mathrm{P}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{V}}}$
where, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{V}}=$ volumetric strain
$\begin{array}{l}
\beta=-\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{V}}}{\Delta \mathrm{P}} \\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{V}}=-\beta \Delta \mathrm{P}=-5 \times 10^{-10} \times 15 \times 10^{6} \\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{V}}=7.5 \times 10^{-3}
\end{array}$
The correct option is A.

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

We consider a thermodynamic system. If $\Delta U$ represents the increase in its internal energy and $W$ the work done by the system, which of the following statements is true?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\Delta U=-W$ is an adiabatic process
- **(B)** $\Delta U=W$ in an isothermal process
- **(C)** $\Delta U=-W$ in an isothermal process
- **(D)** $\Delta U=W$ in an adiabatic process

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\Delta U=-W$ is an adiabatic process**

**Detailed Solution:**

An isothermal process is a constant temperature process. In this process, $T=$ constant or $\Delta T=0$.
$$
\therefore \quad \Delta U=n C_{V} \Delta T=0
$$
An adiabatic process is defined as one with no heat transfer into or out of a system. Therefore, $Q=0 .$ From the first law of thermodynamics.
$W=-\Delta U$
or $\Delta U=-W$

</details>
