### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A tiny water droplet falls through the air at a uniform terminal speed of $1$ cm/s. If $27$ such identical droplets coalesce to form a single larger drop, what will be the new terminal velocity of the resulting drop, assuming the air remains the same?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$ cm/s
- **(B)** $27$ cm/s
- **(C)** $81$ cm/s
- **(D)** $9$ cm/s

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $9$ cm/s**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the radius of a single tiny droplet be $r$ and its terminal velocity be $v$.

The terminal velocity of a spherical drop of radius $r$ falling through a viscous medium is given by Stokes' law:

$v = \dfrac{2}{9} \dfrac{r^2 (\rho - \sigma) g}{\eta}$

This implies $v \propto r^2$.

When $27$ such identical droplets coalesce to form a single larger drop of radius $R$, the total volume remains conserved:

$\dfrac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = 27 \times \dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3$

$R^3 = 27 r^3$

$R = 3r$

The new terminal velocity $v'$ of the larger drop will be:

$v' \propto R^2$

$\dfrac{v'}{v} = \left(\dfrac{R}{r}\right)^2$

$\dfrac{v'}{1} = (3)^2$

$v' = 9$ cm/s

Answer: $9$ cm/s

</details>
