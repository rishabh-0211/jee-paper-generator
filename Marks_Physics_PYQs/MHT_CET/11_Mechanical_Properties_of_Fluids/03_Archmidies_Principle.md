### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The velocity of a small ball of mass $M$ and density $d$ when dropped in a container filled with glycerine becomes constant after some time. If density of glycerine is $\dfrac{d}{2}$ then the viscous force acting on the ball will be ($g$=acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $Mg$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3Mg}{2}$
- **(C)** $2Mg$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{Mg}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{Mg}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When the velocity of the ball becomes constant, the net force acting on it is zero.

The forces acting on the ball are its weight $W$ downwards, buoyant force $F_B$ upwards, and viscous force $F_V$ upwards.

$W = F_B + F_V$

$F_V = W - F_B$

The volume of the ball is $V = \dfrac{M}{d}$.

The buoyant force is given by the weight of the displaced liquid:

$F_B = V \times \text{density of glycerine} \times g$

$F_B = \dfrac{M}{d} \times \dfrac{d}{2} \times g = \dfrac{Mg}{2}$

Substituting the values, we get:

$F_V = Mg - \dfrac{Mg}{2} = \dfrac{Mg}{2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{Mg}{2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A wooden cube (density $d$) of side $L$ floats on a liquid of density $\rho$ with its upper and lower surfaces horizontal. If the cube is pushed slightly down and released, it performs S.H.M. of period $T$. The value of $T$ is ($g$ = acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L\rho}{(\rho - d)g}}$
- **(B)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{Ld}{\rho g}}$
- **(C)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L\rho}{dg}}$
- **(D)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{Ld}{(\rho - d)g}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{Ld}{\rho g}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Mass of the cube is $m = L^3 d$.

When the cube is pushed down by a small distance $x$, the additional volume submerged is $L^2 x$.

The restoring force acting on the cube is the additional buoyant force:

$F = - (L^2 x) \rho g$

Using Newton's second law, $F = ma$:

$L^3 d \cdot a = - L^2 \rho g x$

$a = - \left(\dfrac{\rho g}{L d}\right) x$

Comparing this with the standard equation of S.H.M., $a = - \omega^2 x$, we get:

$\omega^2 = \dfrac{\rho g}{L d} \Rightarrow \omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{\rho g}{L d}}$

The time period of oscillation is:

$T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{L d}{\rho g}}$

Answer: $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{Ld}{\rho g}}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A vessel contains oil (density$=0.6$ g/cm$^3$) over mercury (density$=13.6$ g/cm$^3$). A homogeneous sphere floats with half its volume immersed in mercury and the remaining half in oil. The density of the material of the sphere in g/cm$^3$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.8$
- **(B)** $6.3$
- **(C)** $7.1$
- **(D)** $7.2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $7.1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the total volume of the sphere be $V$ and its density be $\rho$.

The sphere is in equilibrium, so its weight is balanced by the total buoyant force exerted by the two liquids.

Weight of the sphere $= V \rho g$

Buoyant force due to mercury $= \dfrac{V}{2} \rho_{Hg} g$

Buoyant force due to oil $= \dfrac{V}{2} \rho_{oil} g$

Equating the weight to the total buoyant force:

$V \rho g = \dfrac{V}{2} \rho_{Hg} g + \dfrac{V}{2} \rho_{oil} g$

$\rho = \dfrac{\rho_{Hg} + \rho_{oil}}{2}$

Substituting the given values, $\rho_{Hg} = 13.6$ g/cm$^3$ and $\rho_{oil} = 0.6$ g/cm$^3$:

$\rho = \dfrac{13.6 + 0.6}{2}$

$\rho = \dfrac{14.2}{2} = 7.1$ g/cm$^3$

Answer: $7.1$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A rectangular block of mass 'm' and cross-sectional area 'A' floats on a liquid of density '$\varrho$'. It is given a small vertical displacement from equilibrium, it starts oscillating with frequency (g=acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{m}}{\text{A}\varrho\text{g}}}$
- **(B)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{A}\varrho\text{g}}{\text{m}}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{A}\varrho\text{g}}{\text{m}}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{m}}{\text{A}\varrho\text{g}}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{A}\varrho\text{g}}{\text{m}}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the block be displaced downwards by a small distance $x$ from its equilibrium position.

The additional volume submerged in the liquid is $Ax$.

The extra buoyant force acting upwards provides the restoring force, which is given by $F = -(\text{additional volume}) \times \varrho \times g$

$F = -A x \varrho g$

Comparing this with the standard equation of simple harmonic motion $F = -kx$, we get the effective force constant $k = A\varrho g$.

The angular frequency of oscillation is $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{A\varrho g}{m}}$.

The frequency of oscillation is $f = \dfrac{\omega}{2\pi} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{A\varrho g}{m}}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{A}\varrho\text{g}}{\text{m}}}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A vessel contains oil (density $= 0.8 \text{ gm}/\text{cm}^3$) over mercury (density $= 13.6 \text{ gm}/\text{cm}^3$). A homogeneous sphere floats with half of its volume immersed in mercury and the other half in oil. The density of the material of the sphere in $\text{gm}/\text{cm}^3$ is
<img style="width: 250px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/11_april_shift_1__q21_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $12.8$
- **(B)** $7.2$
- **(C)** $6.4$
- **(D)** $3.3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $7.2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the total volume of the sphere be $V$ and its density be $\rho$.

According to the principle of flotation, the weight of the sphere is balanced by the total buoyant force exerted by the two liquids.

Weight of the sphere, $W = V \rho g$

The sphere has half of its volume in mercury and the other half in oil. The total buoyant force $F_B$ is the sum of the buoyant forces from both liquids:
$F_B = F_{B,\text{mercury}} + F_{B,\text{oil}}$
$F_B = \left(\dfrac{V}{2}\right) \rho_{\text{Hg}} g + \left(\dfrac{V}{2}\right) \rho_{\text{oil}} g$

Equating the weight of the sphere to the total buoyant force:
$V \rho g = \dfrac{V}{2} \rho_{\text{Hg}} g + \dfrac{V}{2} \rho_{\text{oil}} g$

Dividing both sides by $Vg$, we get:
$\rho = \dfrac{\rho_{\text{Hg}} + \rho_{\text{oil}}}{2}$

Given $\rho_{\text{Hg}} = 13.6 \text{ gm/cm}^3$ and $\rho_{\text{oil}} = 0.8 \text{ gm/cm}^3$, substituting these values:
$\rho = \dfrac{13.6 + 0.8}{2}$
$\rho = \dfrac{14.4}{2} = 7.2 \text{ gm/cm}^3$

The density of the material of the sphere is $7.2 \text{ gm/cm}^3$.

Answer: $7.2$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid cylinder of length $\ell$ and cross-sectional area $\frac{\mathrm{a}}{5}$ is immersed such that it floats with its axis vertical at the liquid-liquid interface with length $\ell / 4$ in the denser liquid as shown in figure. The lower density liquid $(\varrho)$ is open to atmosphere having pressure $P_0$. The density d of solid cylinder is
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/RMm7cmJgaoP2BsvtwhCcjSCd9BGQYA2yW820CPh6I1o.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2} \varrho$
- **(B)** $\frac{3}{2} \varrho$
- **(C)** $\frac{3}{4} \varrho$
- **(D)** $\varrho$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{3}{2} \varrho$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the cross-sectional area of the cylinder be $A = \dfrac{a}{5}$.

For the cylinder to float in equilibrium, its weight must be balanced by the total buoyant force exerted by the two liquids.

The total length of the cylinder is $\ell$. It is given that the length of the cylinder in the denser liquid is $\dfrac{\ell}{4}$. Since the cylinder is completely immersed across the interface, the length of the cylinder in the lighter liquid is:
$\ell - \dfrac{\ell}{4} = \dfrac{3\ell}{4}$

The weight of the solid cylinder is:
$W = \text{Volume} \times \text{density} \times g = (A\ell) d g$

The total buoyant force $F_B$ is the sum of the weights of the displaced liquids:
$F_B = \text{Weight of displaced lighter liquid} + \text{Weight of displaced denser liquid}$
$F_B = A \left(\dfrac{3\ell}{4}\right) \varrho g + A \left(\dfrac{\ell}{4}\right) (3\varrho) g$

Equating the weight of the cylinder to the buoyant force ($W = F_B$):
$A \ell d g = A \left(\dfrac{3\ell}{4}\right) \varrho g + A \left(\dfrac{\ell}{4}\right) (3\varrho) g$

Dividing both sides by $A \ell g$, we get:
$d = \dfrac{3}{4}\varrho + \dfrac{3}{4}\varrho$

$d = \dfrac{6}{4}\varrho = \dfrac{3}{2}\varrho$

Answer: $\frac{3}{2} \varrho$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A small metal sphere of density $\rho$ is dropped from height $h$ into a jar containing liquid of density $\sigma(\sigma>\rho)$. The maximum depth up to which the sphere sinks is (Neglect damping forces)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\rho}{(\rho-\sigma)}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{h} \sigma}{(\rho-\sigma)}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\sigma}{(\rho-\sigma)}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{h} \rho}{(\sigma-\rho)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\mathrm{h} \rho}{(\sigma-\rho)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $V$ be the volume of the sphere. The mass of the sphere is $m = V \rho$.

Let $x$ be the maximum depth up to which the sphere sinks in the liquid. The total downward displacement of the sphere from its initial position is $h + x$.

The work done by gravity on the sphere is:
$W_g = mg(h + x) = V \rho g(h + x)$

The buoyant force acts upwards on the sphere only when it is inside the liquid. The magnitude of the buoyant force is:
$F_B = V \sigma g$

The work done by the buoyant force is:
$W_B = -F_B x = -V \sigma g x$

According to the work-energy theorem, the net work done is equal to the change in kinetic energy. Since the sphere starts from rest and momentarily comes to rest at the maximum depth, the change in kinetic energy is zero.

$W_g + W_B = 0$

$V \rho g(h + x) - V \sigma g x = 0$

Dividing by $V g$:
$\rho(h + x) - \sigma x = 0$

$\rho h + \rho x - \sigma x = 0$

$x(\sigma - \rho) = \rho h$

$x = \dfrac{h \rho}{\sigma - \rho}$

Answer: $\frac{\mathrm{h} \rho}{(\sigma-\rho)}$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A ball rises to surface at a constant velocity in liquid whose density is 3 times greater than that of the material of the ball. The ratio of force of friction acting on the rising ball to its weight is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2: 1$
- **(B)** $3: 1$
- **(C)** $4: 1$
- **(D)** $6: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $V$ be the volume of the ball and $\rho$ be the density of the material of the ball.

Weight of the ball, $W = V \rho g$

Density of the liquid is given as $3\rho$.

Buoyant force acting on the ball in the upward direction, $F_B = V (3\rho) g = 3 V \rho g = 3W$

Since the ball rises at a constant velocity, its acceleration is zero, meaning the net force acting on it is zero. The forces acting on the ball are the upward buoyant force, the downward weight, and the downward force of friction (viscous drag).

$F_B = W + F_v$

$3W = W + F_v$

$F_v = 2W$

The ratio of the force of friction to its weight is:

$\dfrac{F_v}{W} = \dfrac{2W}{W} = \dfrac{2}{1}$

The ratio is $2:1$.

Answer: $2: 1$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A hemispherical portion of radius ' $R$ ' is removed from the bottom of a cylinder of radius ' R '. The volume of the remaining cylinder is ' $V$ ' and its mass is ' $M$ '. It is suspended by a string in a liquid of density ' $\rho$ ' where it stays vertical. The upper surface of the cylinder is at a depth ' $h$ ' below the liquid surface. The force on the bottom of the cylinder is

**Options:**

- **(A)** Mg
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Mg}-\mathrm{V} \rho \mathrm{g}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{Mg}+\pi \mathrm{R}^2 \mathrm{~h} \rho g$
- **(D)** ($\rho g (V + \pi R^2 h)$)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) ($\rho g (V + \pi R^2 h)$)**

**Detailed Solution:**

The net upward force exerted by the liquid on the cylinder is the buoyant force, which is given by Archimedes' principle as:

$F_B = V \rho g$

This buoyant force is the resultant of the forces exerted by the liquid on all surfaces of the cylinder. Since the horizontal forces cancel out due to symmetry, the buoyant force is the difference between the upward force on the bottom surface and the downward force on the top surface:

$F_B = F_{bottom} - F_{top}$

The top surface of the cylinder is flat, has an area $A = \pi R^2$, and is at a depth $h$. The downward force on the top surface is:

$F_{top} = P_{top} A = (\rho g h)(\pi R^2)$

Substituting $F_{top}$ and $F_B$ into the force balance equation, we get the upward force on the bottom surface:

$F_{bottom} = F_B + F_{top}$

$F_{bottom} = V \rho g + \rho g h \pi R^2$

$F_{bottom} = \rho g (V + \pi R^2 h)$

Answer: ($\rho g (V + \pi R^2 h)$)

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3: 1$
- **(B)** $4: 1$
- **(C)** $1: 3$
- **(D)** $1: 4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $3: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the volume of the ball be $V$ and the density of its material be $\rho$.

Weight of the ball, $W = V \rho g$

Density of the liquid is given as $4\rho$.

Buoyant force on the ball, $F_B = V (4\rho) g = 4W$

Since the ball rises with a constant velocity, the net force acting on it is zero. The forces acting on the ball are the buoyant force upwards, weight downwards, and viscous friction downwards.

Equating the forces, we get:

$F_B = W + F_f$

$F_f = F_B - W$

$F_f = 4W - W = 3W$

The ratio of the force of friction to its weight is:

$\dfrac{F_f}{W} = \dfrac{3W}{W} = 3:1$
