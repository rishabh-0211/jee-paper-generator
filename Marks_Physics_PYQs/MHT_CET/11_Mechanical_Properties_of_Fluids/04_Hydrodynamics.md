### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

An in-compressible fluid flows steadily through a horizontal cylindrical pipe. The pipe has radius '$3R$' at point A and '$1.5R$' at point B, further along the flow direction at the same level. If the velocity at point A is '$v$', then that at point B is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $v$
- **(B)** $2v$
- **(C)** $3v$
- **(D)** $4v$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $4v$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the equation of continuity for an incompressible fluid:

$A_A v_A = A_B v_B$

$\pi (3R)^2 v = \pi (1.5R)^2 v_B$

$9 \pi R^2 v = 2.25 \pi R^2 v_B$

$v_B = \dfrac{9}{2.25} v = 4v$

Answer: $4v$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A horizontal pipe carries water in a streamline flow. At point along the pipe, where the cross-sectional area is $A_1$, the velocity of water is $V_1$ and the pressure is $P_1$. What is the pressure of water at another point where the cross-sectional area is $A_2$?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $P_1 - \dfrac{\varrho V_2^2}{2A_1^2}(A_2^2 - A_1^2)$
- **(B)** $P_1 + \dfrac{\varrho V_1^2}{2A_2^2}(A_2^2 - A_1^2)$
- **(C)** $P_1\varrho V_1^2(A_1^2 - A_2^2)$
- **(D)** $P/\varrho V_1^2 A_2^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $P_1 + \dfrac{\varrho V_1^2}{2A_2^2}(A_2^2 - A_1^2)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the equation of continuity:

$A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 \Rightarrow V_2 = \dfrac{A_1 V_1}{A_2}$

Using Bernoulli's theorem for a horizontal pipe:

$P_1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\varrho V_1^2 = P_2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\varrho V_2^2$

$P_2 = P_1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\varrho (V_1^2 - V_2^2)$

Substituting the value of $V_2$:

$P_2 = P_1 + \dfrac{1}{2}\varrho \left(V_1^2 - \dfrac{A_1^2 V_1^2}{A_2^2}\right)$

$P_2 = P_1 + \dfrac{\varrho V_1^2}{2A_2^2}(A_2^2 - A_1^2)$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

- **(D)** $\left[\dfrac{2(P_2 - P_1)}{\varrho}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

$v^2 = \dfrac{2(P_1 - P_2)}{\varrho}$

$v = \left[\dfrac{2(P_1 - P_2)}{\varrho}\right]^{\dfrac{1}{2}}$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Water flows through a horizontal pipe at a speed 'V'. Internal diameter of the pipe is 'd'. If the water is emerging at a speed '$V_1$' then the diameter of the nozzle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\text{d}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{V}}{\text{V}_1}}$
- **(B)** $\text{d}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{V}_1}{\text{V}}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\text{dV}}{\text{V}_1}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\text{dV}_1}{\text{V}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\text{d}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{V}}{\text{V}_1}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the equation of continuity, the volume flow rate of an incompressible fluid remains constant throughout the pipe.

$A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2$

Let the diameter of the nozzle be $d_1$. The cross-sectional area of the pipe is $A_1 = \dfrac{\pi d^2}{4}$ and the cross-sectional area of the nozzle is $A_2 = \dfrac{\pi d_1^2}{4}$.

Substituting the values into the equation of continuity:

$\dfrac{\pi d^2}{4} V = \dfrac{\pi d_1^2}{4} V_1$

$d^2 V = d_1^2 V_1$

$d_1^2 = d^2 \dfrac{V}{V_1}$

$d_1 = d \sqrt{\dfrac{V}{V_1}}$

Answer: $\text{d}\sqrt{\dfrac{\text{V}}{\text{V}_1}}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A wind with speed $50 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ blows parallel to the roof of a house. The area of the roof is $300 \mathrm{~m}^2$. Assume that the pressure inside the house is atmospheric pressure. Density of air is $1.2 \mathrm{~kg} / \mathrm{m}^3$. The magnitude of the force exerted by the wind on the roof will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.5 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(B)** $3.0 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(C)** $4.5 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~N}$
- **(D)** $9.0 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~N}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4.5 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~N}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Bernoulli's equation just inside and outside the roof:

$P_{in} + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_{in}^2 = P_{out} + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_{out}^2$

Since the air inside the house is at rest, $v_{in} = 0$ and $P_{in} = P_0$ (atmospheric pressure).

$P_0 = P_{out} + \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$

The pressure difference across the roof is $\Delta P = P_0 - P_{out} = \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2$.

The upward force exerted on the roof is $F = \Delta P \times A = \dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2 A$.

Substituting the given values:

$F = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 1.2 \times (50)^2 \times 300$

$F = 0.6 \times 2500 \times 300$

$F = 450000 \text{ N} = 4.5 \times 10^5 \text{ N}$

Answer: $4.5 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~N}$

A cylinder contains water upto a height 'H'. It has three orifices $\mathrm{O}_1, \mathrm{O}_2, \mathrm{O}_3$ as shown in the figure. Let $V_1, V_2, V_3$ be the speed of efflux of water from the three orifices. Then
