### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In an a.c. circuit, if the frequency is doubled, what happens to the capacitive reactance ($X_c$) and the current ($I$)?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $X_c$ is halved and $I$ is doubled
- **(B)** $X_c$ is doubled, $I$ is halved
- **(C)** Both $X_c$ and $I$ are halved
- **(D)** Both $X_c$ and $I$ are doubled

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $X_c$ is halved and $I$ is doubled**

**Detailed Solution:**

The capacitive reactance is given by $X_c = \dfrac{1}{\omega C} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi f C}$.

When the frequency $f$ is doubled, the new capacitive reactance becomes $X_c' = \dfrac{1}{2\pi (2f) C} = \dfrac{X_c}{2}$. Thus, the capacitive reactance is halved.

The current in the circuit is given by $I = \dfrac{V}{X_c}$.

With the new capacitive reactance, the new current is $I' = \dfrac{V}{X_c'} = \dfrac{V}{\dfrac{X_c}{2}} = 2 \left(\dfrac{V}{X_c}\right) = 2I$.

Therefore, the current is doubled.

Answer: $X_c$ is halved and $I$ is doubled

</details>

### Q106. MHT CET 2009

In an AC circuit, the instantaneous values of emf and current are $e=200 \sin 314 t$ volt and $I=\sin \left(314 t+\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$ amp. The average power

**Options:**

### Q107. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In an AC circuit the emf $(e)$ and the current (i) at any instant are given respectively by
$$
\begin{array}{l}
e=E_{0} \sin \omega t \\
i=I_{0} \sin (\omega t-\phi)
\end{array}
$$
The average power in the circuit over one cycle of AC is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{E_{0} I_{0}}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{E_{0} I_{0}}{2} \sin \phi$
- **(C)** $\frac{E_{0} I_{0}}{2} \cos \phi$
- **(D)** $E_{0} I_{0}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{E_{0} I_{0}}{2} \cos \phi$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Power $=$ rate of work done in one complete cycle.
Or
$$
P_{\mathrm{av}}=\frac{W}{T}
$$
Or
$$
P_{\mathrm{av}}=\frac{\left(E_{0} I_{0} \cos \phi\right) T / 2}{T}
$$
or
$$
P_{\mathrm{av}}=\frac{E_{0} I_{0} \cos \phi}{2}
$$
where $\cos \phi$ is called the power factor of an $\mathrm{AC}$ circuit.

</details>
