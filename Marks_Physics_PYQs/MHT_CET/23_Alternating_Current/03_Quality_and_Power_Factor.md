### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In an a.c. circuit, if the power factor changes from $0.5$ to $0.25$ then the increase in impedance is

**Options:**

- **(A)** twice
- **(B)** half
- **(C)** four times
- **(D)** eight times

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) twice**

**Detailed Solution:**

The power factor of an a.c. circuit is given by $\cos \phi = \dfrac{R}{Z}$

Rearranging for impedance, we get $Z = \dfrac{R}{\cos \phi}$

For the initial state, the power factor is $\cos \phi_1 = 0.5$

$Z_1 = \dfrac{R}{0.5} = 2R$

For the final state, the power factor is $\cos \phi_2 = 0.25$

$Z_2 = \dfrac{R}{0.25} = 4R$

Taking the ratio of the final and initial impedance:

$\dfrac{Z_2}{Z_1} = \dfrac{4R}{2R} = 2$

$Z_2 = 2Z_1$

Thus, the impedance becomes twice its initial value.

Answer: twice

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In an a.c. circuit containing L, C and R in series, the ratio of apparent power to the true power is
(Z= impedance of the circuit and R= resistance )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $RZ$
- **(B)** $\cos \phi$
- **(C)** $\cot \phi$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{Z}{R}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{Z}{R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Apparent power is given by $P_{app} = V_{rms} I_{rms}$

True power is given by $P_{true} = V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos \phi$

The ratio of apparent power to true power is $\dfrac{P_{app}}{P_{true}} = \dfrac{V_{rms} I_{rms}}{V_{rms} I_{rms} \cos \phi} = \dfrac{1}{\cos \phi}$

In a series LCR circuit, the power factor is $\cos \phi = \dfrac{R}{Z}$

Substituting this value, the ratio becomes $\dfrac{Z}{R}$

Answer: $\dfrac{Z}{R}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In LCR series circuit, when ' $L$ ' is removed from the circuit, the phase difference between voltage and current in the circuit is $\frac{\pi}{3}$. If ' $C$ ' is removed from the circuit instead of $L$ then phase difference is again $\frac{\pi}{3}$. The power factor of the circuit is $\left(\tan 60^{\circ}=\sqrt{3}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(D)** 1

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

When $L$ is removed, the circuit becomes an RC circuit. The phase difference is $\phi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

$\tan \phi = \dfrac{X_C}{R} \Rightarrow \tan \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{X_C}{R} \Rightarrow X_C = \sqrt{3}R$

When $C$ is removed, the circuit becomes an RL circuit. The phase difference is again $\phi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

$\tan \phi = \dfrac{X_L}{R} \Rightarrow \tan \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{X_L}{R} \Rightarrow X_L = \sqrt{3}R$

For the LCR series circuit, the net reactance is $X_L - X_C$.

Since $X_L = X_C = \sqrt{3}R$, the net reactance is zero. The circuit is in resonance.

The impedance of the circuit is $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} = R$.

The power factor of the circuit is $\cos \phi = \dfrac{R}{Z} = \dfrac{R}{R} = 1$.

Answer: 1

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The power factor of a CR circuit is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, If the frequency of a. c. signal is halved, then the power factor of the circuit will become

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{11}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The power factor of a CR circuit is given by $\cos \phi = \dfrac{R}{Z} = \dfrac{R}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_C^2}}$.

Given that the initial power factor is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, we have:

$\dfrac{R}{\sqrt{R^2 + X_C^2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$\dfrac{R^2}{R^2 + X_C^2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$2R^2 = R^2 + X_C^2 \Rightarrow R = X_C$

The capacitive reactance is $X_C = \dfrac{1}{2\pi f C}$.

When the frequency $f$ is halved, the new frequency is $f' = \dfrac{f}{2}$. The new capacitive reactance becomes:

$X_C' = \dfrac{1}{2\pi \left(\dfrac{f}{2}\right) C} = 2X_C$

Since $X_C = R$, we have $X_C' = 2R$.

The new impedance of the circuit is:

$Z' = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_C')^2} = \sqrt{R^2 + (2R)^2} = \sqrt{5R^2} = \sqrt{5}R$

The new power factor is:

$\cos \phi' = \dfrac{R}{Z'} = \dfrac{R}{\sqrt{5}R} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$

Answer: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In an electrical circuit ' R ', ' L ', ' C ' and an a.c. voltage source are all connected in series. When ' $L$ ' is removed from the circuit, the phase difference between the voltage and the current in the circuit is $\frac{\pi}{3}$. If instead 'C' is removed from the circuit, the phase difference is again $\frac{\pi}{3}$. The power factor of the circuit is $\left(\tan \frac{\pi}{3}=\sqrt{3}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When $L$ is removed, the circuit becomes an $RC$ series circuit. The phase difference is $\phi_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

$\tan \phi_1 = \dfrac{X_C}{R} \Rightarrow \dfrac{X_C}{R} = \tan \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \sqrt{3} \Rightarrow X_C = \sqrt{3} R$

When $C$ is removed, the circuit becomes an $RL$ series circuit. The phase difference is $\phi_2 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

$\tan \phi_2 = \dfrac{X_L}{R} \Rightarrow \dfrac{X_L}{R} = \tan \dfrac{\pi}{3} = \sqrt{3} \Rightarrow X_L = \sqrt{3} R$

For the $LCR$ series circuit, the net reactance is $X_L - X_C$.

Since $X_L = X_C = \sqrt{3} R$, the net reactance is zero.

The impedance of the circuit is $Z = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2} = \sqrt{R^2 + 0} = R$.

The power factor of the circuit is $\cos \phi = \dfrac{R}{Z} = \dfrac{R}{R} = 1$.

Answer: $1$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An a.c. source is applied to a series LR circuit with $X_L=3 R$ and power factor is $X_1$. Now a capacitor with $X_C=R$ is added in series to LR circuit and the power factor is $X_2$. The ratio $X_1$ to $X_2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 2$
- **(B)** $2: 1$
- **(C)** $1: \sqrt{2}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{2}: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1: \sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the series LR circuit, the impedance is $Z_1 = \sqrt{R^2 + X_L^2}$.

Given $X_L = 3R$, we have $Z_1 = \sqrt{R^2 + (3R)^2} = \sqrt{10}R$.

The power factor $X_1 = \dfrac{R}{Z_1} = \dfrac{R}{\sqrt{10}R} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$.

When a capacitor with $X_C = R$ is added in series, the new impedance is $Z_2 = \sqrt{R^2 + (X_L - X_C)^2}$.

Substituting the values, $Z_2 = \sqrt{R^2 + (3R - R)^2} = \sqrt{R^2 + 4R^2} = \sqrt{5}R$.

The new power factor $X_2 = \dfrac{R}{Z_2} = \dfrac{R}{\sqrt{5}R} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$.

The ratio $\dfrac{X_1}{X_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}}{\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{10}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.

Thus, the ratio $X_1 : X_2$ is $1 : \sqrt{2}$.

Answer: $1: \sqrt{2}$

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2023 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The power factor of an R-L circuit is $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. If the frequency of $\mathrm{AC}$ is doubled the power factor will now be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{7}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{11}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The power factor of an R-L circuit is given as,
$\begin{aligned}
& \quad \cos \phi=\frac{\mathrm{R}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{R}^2+\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{L}}{ }^2}} \\
& \therefore \quad \frac{\mathrm{R}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{R}^2+\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{L}}{ }^2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
& \therefore \quad \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mathrm{X}_{\mathrm{L}}}{\mathrm{R}}\right)^2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
& \therefore \quad \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\omega \mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}}\right)^2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
& \therefore \quad\left(\frac{\omega \mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}}\right)^2+1=2 \\
& \therefore \quad \frac{\omega \mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}}=1
\end{aligned}$
So, when the AC frequency is doubled,
$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\omega \mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}} & =2 \\
\therefore \quad \cos \phi & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\omega \mathrm{L}}{\mathrm{R}}\right)^2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(2)^2}} \\
\therefore \quad \cos \phi & =\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q17. MHT CET 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The $Q$ -factor of a resonant circuit is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{L} \sqrt{\frac{R}{C}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{R} \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{R L} \sqrt{C}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{C} \sqrt{\frac{R}{L}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{R} \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The quality factor (Q-factor) of a resonant circuit
$$
Q=\frac{\omega_{0} L}{R}
$$
At resonance, $\omega_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L C}}$
$$
Q=\frac{1}{\sqrt{L} C} \cdot \frac{L}{R}=\frac{1}{R} \sqrt{\frac{L}{C}}
$$

</details>
