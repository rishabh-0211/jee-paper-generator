### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following sentence is true?
a. Moment of a couple is independent of the axis of rotation or the points of application of forces.
b. Moment of a couple produces translational acceleration.
c. Moment of a couple can be balanced by single force.
d. Moment of a couple depends upon the axis of rotation or the points of application of forces.

**Options:**

- **(A)** d
- **(B)** c
- **(C)** b
- **(D)** a

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) a**

**Detailed Solution:**

A couple consists of two equal and opposite forces whose lines of action do not coincide. Since the net force is zero, a couple does not produce translational acceleration and cannot be balanced by a single force.

Let the forces be $\vec{F}$ applied at position $\vec{r}_1$ and $-\vec{F}$ applied at position $\vec{r}_2$. The total moment (torque) about an arbitrary origin is given by:

$\vec{\tau} = \vec{r}_1 \times \vec{F} + \vec{r}_2 \times (-\vec{F}) = (\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2) \times \vec{F}$

The term $(\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2)$ represents the relative position vector between the points of application on the two lines of action. This shows that the moment of a couple depends only on the perpendicular distance between the lines of action and the magnitude of the forces. It is entirely independent of the choice of the origin (axis of rotation). Furthermore, by the principle of transmissibility, the moment remains unchanged regardless of the specific points of application chosen along the respective lines of action.

Therefore, the moment of a couple is independent of the axis of rotation or the points of application of forces.

Answer: a

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

A force of $3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - \hat{k}$ N acts on a particle with position vector $\hat{i} + \hat{j} - \hat{k}$ m. The magnitude of torque of given force is

**Options:**

- **(D)** $\sqrt{10}\,\text{N}\,m$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

$\vec{\tau} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} - \hat{k}$

Magnitude of torque $|\vec{\tau}| = \sqrt{1^2 + (-2)^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 1} = \sqrt{6}$ N m

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A same torque is applied to a disc and a ring of equal mass and radii then

**Options:**

- **(A)** the ring will rotate with greater angular frequency.
- **(B)** both will rotate with same angular frequency.
- **(C)** the disc will rotate with greater angular frequency.
- **(D)** both will rotate with same angular velocity.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) the disc will rotate with greater angular frequency.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of the disc and the ring be $M$ and their radii be $R$.

The moment of inertia of the disc is $I_d = \dfrac{1}{2} M R^2$.

The moment of inertia of the ring is $I_r = M R^2$.

Since $I_d < I_r$, the angular acceleration $\alpha = \dfrac{\tau}{I}$ will be greater for the disc for the same applied torque $\tau$.

Angular frequency is given by $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t$. Assuming both start from rest, $\omega = \alpha t$.

Since $\alpha_d > \alpha_r$, it follows that $\omega_d > \omega_r$.

Therefore, the disc will rotate with greater angular frequency.

Answer: the disc will rotate with greater angular frequency.

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A disc of mass 25 kg and radius 0.2 m is rotating at 240 r.p.m. A retarding torque brings it to rest in 20 second. If the torque is due to a force applied tangentially on the rim of the disc then the magnitude of the force is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\pi}{2} \mathrm{~N}$
- **(B)** $2 \pi \mathrm{~N}$
- **(C)** $\pi \mathrm{N}$
- **(D)** $4 \pi \mathrm{~N}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\pi \mathrm{N}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial angular velocity is $\omega_0 = 240 \text{ r.p.m.} = 240 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{60} = 8\pi \text{ rad/s}$.

Final angular velocity is $\omega = 0$.

Time taken to come to rest is $t = 20 \text{ s}$.

Angular retardation is given by $\alpha = \dfrac{\omega_0 - \omega}{t} = \dfrac{8\pi - 0}{20} = \dfrac{2\pi}{5} \text{ rad/s}^2$.

Moment of inertia of the disc is $I = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times (0.2)^2 = 0.5 \text{ kg m}^2$.

Retarding torque is $\tau = I\alpha = 0.5 \times \dfrac{2\pi}{5} = \dfrac{\pi}{5} \text{ N m}$.

Since the torque is due to a tangential force $F$ on the rim, $\tau = F \times R$.

Therefore, $F = \dfrac{\tau}{R} = \dfrac{\pi/5}{0.2} = \pi \text{ N}$.

Answer: $\pi \mathrm{N}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A thin uniform rod of mass ' m ' and length ' L ' is pivoted at one end so that it can rotate in a vertical plane. The free end is held vertically above pivot and then released. The angular acceleration of the rod when it makes an angle ' $\theta$ ' with the vertical is [consider negligible friction at the pivot] ( $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/TlVhqDoMGsePlUAqzdgmTFEHVparIgRvWYBUAOa7AhY.original.fullsize_2.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{3 \mathrm{~g} \sin \theta}{2 \mathrm{~L}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{3 \mathrm{~g} \cos \theta}{2 \mathrm{~L}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{2 \mathrm{~g} \sin \theta}{3 \mathrm{~L}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 \mathrm{~g} \cos \theta}{3 \mathrm{~L}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{3 \mathrm{~g} \sin \theta}{2 \mathrm{~L}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The torque acting on the rod about the pivot is due to the gravitational force acting at its center of mass.

The center of mass of a uniform rod of length $L$ is at a distance of $\dfrac{L}{2}$ from the pivot.

When the rod makes an angle $\theta$ with the vertical, the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the gravitational force is $\dfrac{L}{2} \sin \theta$.

Therefore, the torque $\tau$ is given by:

$\tau = mg \left( \dfrac{L}{2} \sin \theta \right)$

The moment of inertia $I$ of the rod about an axis passing through its end is:

$I = \dfrac{mL^2}{3}$

Using Newton's second law for rotational motion, $\tau = I \alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angular acceleration:

$mg \left( \dfrac{L}{2} \sin \theta \right) = \left( \dfrac{mL^2}{3} \right) \alpha$

Solving for $\alpha$, we get:

$\alpha = \dfrac{3g \sin \theta}{2L}$

Answer: $\frac{3 \mathrm{~g} \sin \theta}{2 \mathrm{~L}}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $\vec{F}=(5 \hat{i}-10 \hat{j})$ and $\vec{r}=(4 \hat{i}-3 \hat{j})$, then the torque acting on the object will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\hat{\mathrm{i}}-2 \hat{\mathrm{j}}$
- **(B)** $2 \hat{\mathrm{i}}-\hat{\mathrm{j}}$
- **(C)** $25 \hat{\mathrm{k}}$
- **(D)** $-25 \hat{\mathrm{k}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $-25 \hat{\mathrm{k}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The torque $\vec{\tau}$ acting on an object is given by the cross product of the position vector $\vec{r}$ and the force vector $\vec{F}$.

$\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$

Given $\vec{r} = 4\hat{i} - 3\hat{j}$ and $\vec{F} = 5\hat{i} - 10\hat{j}$, we can calculate the cross product using the determinant method:

$\vec{\tau} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 4 & -3 & 0 \\ 5 & -10 & 0 \end{vmatrix}$

Expanding the determinant along the first row:

$\vec{\tau} = \hat{i}(0 - 0) - \hat{j}(0 - 0) + \hat{k}[4(-10) - (-3)(5)]$

$\vec{\tau} = \hat{k}[-40 + 15]$

$\vec{\tau} = -25\hat{k}$

Answer: $-25 \hat{\mathrm{k}}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The power $(\mathrm{P})$ is supplied to a rotating body having moment of inertia ' I ' and angular acceleration ' $\alpha$ '. Its instantaneous angular velocity ' $\omega$ ' is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\quad \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{I} \alpha)^{-1}$
- **(B)** $\quad \mathrm{P}^{-1}(\mathrm{I} \alpha)^{-1}$
- **(C)** $\quad \mathrm{P} \alpha^{-1} \mathrm{I}$
- **(D)** PI $\alpha$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\quad \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{I} \alpha)^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The power supplied to a rotating body is given by the product of torque and angular velocity:

$\mathrm{P} = \tau \omega$

According to Newton's second law for rotational motion, the torque is given by:

$\tau = \mathrm{I} \alpha$

Substituting the value of torque in the power equation, we get:

$\mathrm{P} = (\mathrm{I} \alpha) \omega$

Rearranging the equation to solve for the instantaneous angular velocity $\omega$:

$\omega = \dfrac{\mathrm{P}}{\mathrm{I} \alpha} = \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{I} \alpha)^{-1}$

Answer: $\quad \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{I} \alpha)^{-1}$

</details>

### Q21. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The moment of inertia of a uniform circular disc of radius $R$ and mass $M$ about an axis touching the disc at its diameter and normal to the disc is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $M R^{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2}{5} M R^{2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{3}{2} M R^{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{2} M R^{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{3}{2} M R^{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The moment of inertia about an axis passing through centre of mass of disc and perpendicular to its plane is
$$
I_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{1}{2} M R^{2}
$$
Where $M$ is the mass of disc and $R$ its radius According to theorem of parallel axis, moment of inertia of circular disc about an axis
touching the disc at its diameter and normal to the disc is
$$
\begin{aligned}
I &=I_{\mathrm{CM}}+M R^{2} \\
&=\frac{1}{2} M R^{2}+M R^{2} \\
&=\frac{3}{2} M R^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
