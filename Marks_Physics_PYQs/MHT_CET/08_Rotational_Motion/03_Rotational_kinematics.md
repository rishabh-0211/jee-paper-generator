### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The moment of inertia of two rotating bodies 'X' and 'Y' are $I_1$ and $I_2$ respectively where $I_2 > I_1$. If $K_x$ and $K_y$ are their rotational kinetic energies and their angular momenta are equal, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $K_x = K_y$
- **(B)** $K_x > K_y$
- **(C)** $K_x < K_y$
- **(D)** $K_x = \dfrac{K_y}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $K_x > K_y$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rotational kinetic energy $K$ of a body is related to its angular momentum $L$ and moment of inertia $I$ by the relation:

$K = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$

Given that the angular momenta of both bodies are equal ($L_x = L_y = L$), the rotational kinetic energy is inversely proportional to the moment of inertia:

$K \propto \dfrac{1}{I}$

It is given that $I_2 > I_1$, which means the moment of inertia of body Y is greater than that of body X.

Therefore, $K_x > K_y$.

Answer: $K_x > K_y$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The difference between angular speed of minute hand and second hand of a clock is (in rad/s)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{59\pi}{900}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{59\pi}{1800}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{59\pi}{2400}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{59\pi}{3600}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{59\pi}{1800}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Angular speed of the minute hand is $\omega_m = \dfrac{2\pi}{T_m}$

Since the minute hand completes one revolution in $60$ minutes, $T_m = 60 \times 60 = 3600$ s.

$\omega_m = \dfrac{2\pi}{3600} = \dfrac{\pi}{1800}$ rad/s

Angular speed of the second hand is $\omega_s = \dfrac{2\pi}{T_s}$

Since the second hand completes one revolution in $60$ seconds, $T_s = 60$ s.

$\omega_s = \dfrac{2\pi}{60} = \dfrac{\pi}{30}$ rad/s

The difference between their angular speeds is $\Delta\omega = \omega_s - \omega_m$

$\Delta\omega = \dfrac{\pi}{30} - \dfrac{\pi}{1800} = \dfrac{60\pi - \pi}{1800} = \dfrac{59\pi}{1800}$ rad/s

Answer: $\dfrac{59\pi}{1800}$

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two bodies rotate with kinetic energies $E_1$ and $E_2$, moment of inertia about their axes of rotation is $I_1$ and $I_2$. If $3I_1 = I_2$ and $E_1 = 27 E_2$ then the ratio of the angular momenta $L_1$ to $L_2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9:1$
- **(B)** $3:1$
- **(C)** $27:1$
- **(D)** $1:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rotational kinetic energy is given by $E = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$, where $L$ is the angular momentum and $I$ is the moment of inertia.

Rearranging for $L$, we get $L = \sqrt{2IE}$.

The ratio of the angular momenta is:

$\dfrac{L_1}{L_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{I_1 E_1}{I_2 E_2}}$

Given $3I_1 = I_2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{I_1}{I_2} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $E_1 = 27E_2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{E_1}{E_2} = 27$.

Substituting these values:

$\dfrac{L_1}{L_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{3} \times 27} = \sqrt{9} = 3$

Thus, the ratio $L_1 : L_2$ is $3:1$.

Answer: $3:1$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the case of rotational dynamics, which of the following statements is correct? {$S$ = linear displacement, $\alpha$ = angular acceleration, $r$ = radius vector, $a$ = linear acceleration, $L$ = angular momentum, $p$ = linear momentum, $\theta$ = angular displacement}

1. $S = r \times \theta, a = r \times \alpha, L = p \times r$
2. $S = \theta \times r, a = \alpha \times r, L = r \times p$
3. $S = r \cdot \theta, a = r \cdot \alpha, L = p \cdot r$

- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

Linear displacement is the cross product of angular displacement and the radius vector: $S = \theta \times r$

Angular momentum is the cross product of the radius vector and linear momentum: $L = r \times p$

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{P}{I\alpha}$**

Power $P$ supplied to a rotating body is given by the product of torque $\tau$ and angular velocity $\omega$.

$P = \tau \omega$

$\tau = I \alpha$

Substituting the value of $\tau$ in the power equation:

$P = (I \alpha) \omega$

Rearranging for instantaneous angular velocity $\omega$:

$\omega = \dfrac{P}{I \alpha}$

Answer: $\dfrac{P}{I\alpha}$

A solid sphere is in purely rotational motion about its diameter. The ratio of its angular momentum and kinetic energy is $\frac{\pi}{22}$. Find angular velocity of the sphere. ( $\pi=22 / 7$ )

### Q13. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A rigid body rotates about a fixed axis with variable angular velocity ($\alpha-\beta \mathrm{t}$) at time t, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are constants. The angle through which it rotates before it comes to rest is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\alpha}{\beta}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\alpha^2}{\beta}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\alpha^2}{2 \beta}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\alpha}{2 \beta}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{\alpha^2}{2 \beta}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular velocity of the rigid body is given by $\omega = \alpha - \beta t$.

The body comes to rest when its angular velocity becomes zero. Setting $\omega = 0$:

$\alpha - \beta t = 0 \Rightarrow t = \dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}$

The angular displacement $\theta$ is the integral of angular velocity with respect to time:

$\theta = \int_{0}^{t} \omega dt = \int_{0}^{\alpha/\beta} (\alpha - \beta t) dt$

$\theta = \left[ \alpha t - \dfrac{\beta t^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{\alpha/\beta}$

Substituting the upper limit $t = \dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}$:

$\theta = \alpha \left( \dfrac{\alpha}{\beta} \right) - \dfrac{\beta}{2} \left( \dfrac{\alpha}{\beta} \right)^2$

$\theta = \dfrac{\alpha^2}{\beta} - \dfrac{\alpha^2}{2\beta} = \dfrac{\alpha^2}{2\beta}$

Alternatively, using the kinematic equation $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\gamma\theta$:

Initial angular velocity $\omega_0 = \alpha$ and angular acceleration $\gamma = \dfrac{d\omega}{dt} = -\beta$.

$0 = \alpha^2 - 2\beta\theta \Rightarrow \theta = \dfrac{\alpha^2}{2\beta}$

Answer: $\frac{\alpha^2}{2 \beta}$

</details>

When released from the rest, the bob starts falling vertically downwards. If the bob has covered a vertical distance ' $h$ ', then angular speed of wheel will be (There is no slipping between string and wheel, $g$ - acceleration due to gravity)

### Q17. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid cylinder of mass ' M ' and radius ' R ' is rotating about its geometrical axis. A solid sphere of same mass and same radius is also rotating about its diameter with an angular speed half that of the cylinder. The ratio of the kinetic energy of rotation of the sphere to that of the cylinder will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2: 3$
- **(B)** $3: 2$
- **(C)** $1: 5$
- **(D)** $5: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1: 5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Moment of inertia of a solid cylinder about its geometrical axis is $I_c = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$.

Moment of inertia of a solid sphere about its diameter is $I_s = \dfrac{2}{5}MR^2$.

Let the angular speed of the cylinder be $\omega_c = \omega$. Then the angular speed of the sphere is $\omega_s = \dfrac{\omega}{2}$.

Rotational kinetic energy of the cylinder is:
$K_c = \dfrac{1}{2}I_c\omega_c^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \left(\dfrac{1}{2}MR^2\right) \omega^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}MR^2\omega^2$

Rotational kinetic energy of the sphere is:
$K_s = \dfrac{1}{2}I_s\omega_s^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \left(\dfrac{2}{5}MR^2\right) \left(\dfrac{\omega}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{5}MR^2 \left(\dfrac{\omega^2}{4}\right) = \dfrac{1}{20}MR^2\omega^2$

The ratio of the kinetic energy of rotation of the sphere to that of the cylinder is:
$\dfrac{K_s}{K_c} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{20}MR^2\omega^2}{\dfrac{1}{4}MR^2\omega^2} = \dfrac{4}{20} = \dfrac{1}{5}$

The required ratio is $1:5$.

Answer: $1: 5$

</details>

### Q20. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A disc of mass ' $m$ ' and radius ' $r$ ' rolls down an inclined plane of height ' h '. When it reaches the bottom of the plane, its rotational kinetic energy is ( $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{mgh}}{3}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{mgh}}{6}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{mgh}}{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{mgh}}{4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{\mathrm{mgh}}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By conservation of energy, the total kinetic energy at the bottom of the inclined plane is equal to the potential energy at the top.

$E_{total} = mgh$

For a rolling body, the total kinetic energy is the sum of translational and rotational kinetic energies.

$E_{total} = E_{trans} + E_{rot} = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$

For a disc, the moment of inertia is $I = \dfrac{1}{2}mr^2$ and for pure rolling, $v = r\omega$.

The rotational kinetic energy is:

$E_{rot} = \dfrac{1}{4}mv^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} \left(\dfrac{4}{3}mgh\right) = \dfrac{mgh}{3}$

Answer: $\frac{\mathrm{mgh}}{3}$

</details>
