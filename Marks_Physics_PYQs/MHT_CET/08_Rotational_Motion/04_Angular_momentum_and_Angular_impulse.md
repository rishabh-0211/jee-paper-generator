### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A disc of moment of inertia $I_1$ is rotating in horizontal plane about an axis passing through centre and perpendicular to its plane with constant angular speed $\omega_1$. Another disc of moment of inertia $I_2$ having zero angular speed is placed co-axially on a rotating disc. Now, both the discs are rotating with constant angular speed $\omega_2$. The energy lost by the entire system is $\dfrac{x\omega_1^2}{2}$ where the value of $x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left[\dfrac{(I_1 + I_2)}{I_1 I_2}\right]$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{I_1 I_2}{(I_1 - I_2)}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{(I_1 - I_2)}{I_1 I_2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{I_1 I_2}{(I_1 + I_2)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{I_1 I_2}{(I_1 + I_2)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By the principle of conservation of angular momentum:

$I_1 \omega_1 + I_2 (0) = (I_1 + I_2) \omega_2$

$\omega_2 = \dfrac{I_1 \omega_1}{I_1 + I_2}$

Initial kinetic energy of the system:

$K_i = \dfrac{1}{2} I_1 \omega_1^2$

Final kinetic energy of the system:

$K_f = \dfrac{1}{2} (I_1 + I_2) \omega_2^2$

Substituting the value of $\omega_2$:

$K_f = \dfrac{1}{2} (I_1 + I_2) \left( \dfrac{I_1 \omega_1}{I_1 + I_2} \right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} \dfrac{I_1^2 \omega_1^2}{I_1 + I_2}$

Loss in kinetic energy of the system:

$\Delta K = K_i - K_f = \dfrac{1}{2} I_1 \omega_1^2 - \dfrac{1}{2} \dfrac{I_1^2 \omega_1^2}{I_1 + I_2}$

$\Delta K = \dfrac{1}{2} \omega_1^2 \left( I_1 - \dfrac{I_1^2}{I_1 + I_2} \right)$

$\Delta K = \dfrac{1}{2} \omega_1^2 \left( \dfrac{I_1^2 + I_1 I_2 - I_1^2}{I_1 + I_2} \right)$

$\Delta K = \dfrac{1}{2} \omega_1^2 \left( \dfrac{I_1 I_2}{I_1 + I_2} \right)$

Comparing this with the given loss $\dfrac{x \omega_1^2}{2}$, we get:

$x = \dfrac{I_1 I_2}{I_1 + I_2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{I_1 I_2}{(I_1 + I_2)}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle of mass $m$ is projected with speed $v$ at $45^\circ$ with the horizontal. The magnitude of angular momentum of the particle about the point of projection when the particle is at its maximum height $h$ is $\left(\cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** zero
- **(B)** $\dfrac{mvh^2}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{mv^2h}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{mvh}{\sqrt{2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{mvh}{\sqrt{2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

At the maximum height $h$, the velocity of the particle is purely horizontal.

The horizontal component of the velocity remains constant throughout the projectile motion and is given by:

$v_x = v \cos 45^{\circ} = \dfrac{v}{\sqrt{2}}$

The angular momentum $L$ about the point of projection is the product of the linear momentum and the perpendicular distance from the point of projection to the line of motion.

At the maximum height, the line of motion is horizontal and its perpendicular distance from the point of projection is $h$.

Therefore, the magnitude of angular momentum is:

$L = (mv_x) \times h$

$L = m \left( \dfrac{v}{\sqrt{2}} \right) h$

$L = \dfrac{mvh}{\sqrt{2}}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The angular momentum of a rotating body is 'L'. When the frequency of rotating body is tripled and its kinetic energy is made one-third, the new angular momentum becomes

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9L$
- **(B)** $6L$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{L}{3}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{L}{9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{L}{9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The kinetic energy of a rotating body is given by $K = \dfrac{1}{2} L \omega$

Since $\omega = 2 \pi f$, we can write $K = \pi L f$

Therefore, the angular momentum is $L = \dfrac{K}{\pi f}$

Given that the new frequency is $f' = 3f$ and the new kinetic energy is $K' = \dfrac{K}{3}$

The new angular momentum is $L' = \dfrac{K'}{\pi f'} = \dfrac{\dfrac{K}{3}}{\pi (3f)} = \dfrac{1}{9} \dfrac{K}{\pi f} = \dfrac{L}{9}$

Answer: $\dfrac{L}{9}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A mass tied to a string is whirled in a horizontal circular path with a constant angular velocity and its angular momentum is 'L'. If the length of the string is now halved, keeping angular velocity same then the angular momentum will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{L}{4}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{L}{2}$
- **(C)** $L$
- **(D)** $2L$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{L}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular momentum of a particle of mass $m$ moving in a circle of radius $r$ with angular velocity $\omega$ is given by $L = I\omega = mr^2\omega$.

Given that the initial angular momentum is $L = mr^2\omega$.

The angular velocity $\omega$ is kept constant.

The new angular momentum is $L' = m(r')^2\omega = m\left(\dfrac{r}{2}\right)^2\omega = \dfrac{mr^2\omega}{4}$.

Substituting the initial angular momentum, we get $L' = \dfrac{L}{4}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{L}{4}$

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A man standing on a turn-table is rotating at a certain angular frequency with his arms outstretched. He suddenly folds his arms. If his moment of inertia with folded arms is $75 \%$ of that with outstretched arms, then his rotational kinetic energy will

**Options:**

- **(A)** increase by 33.3 %
- **(B)** decrease by 33.3 %
- **(C)** increase by $25.0 \%$
- **(D)** decrease by $25.0 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) increase by 33.3 %**

**Detailed Solution:**

By the law of conservation of angular momentum, $L_1 = L_2 = L$.

The rotational kinetic energy is given by $K = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$.

Let the initial moment of inertia be $I_1 = I$.

The final moment of inertia is $I_2 = 75\% \text{ of } I = \dfrac{3}{4}I$.

Initial kinetic energy, $K_1 = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}$.

Final kinetic energy, $K_2 = \dfrac{L^2}{2I_2} = \dfrac{L^2}{2 \left(\dfrac{3}{4}I\right)} = \dfrac{4}{3} \left(\dfrac{L^2}{2I}\right) = \dfrac{4}{3}K_1$.

Percentage increase in kinetic energy $= \dfrac{K_2 - K_1}{K_1} \times 100\% = \dfrac{\dfrac{4}{3}K_1 - K_1}{K_1} \times 100\% = \dfrac{1}{3} \times 100\% = 33.3\%$.

Answer: increase by 33.3 %

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

The moment of inertia of a ring about an axis passing through its centre and perpendicular to its plane is I. It is rotating with angular velocity $\omega$. Another identical ring is gently placed on it so that their centres coincide. If both the rings are rotating about the same axis then loss in kinetic energy is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{I} \omega^2}{16}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{I} \omega^2}{8}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{I} \omega^2}{4}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{I} \omega^2}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{\mathrm{I} \omega^2}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial moment of inertia is $I$ and initial angular velocity is $\omega$.

Initial kinetic energy is $K_i = \dfrac{1}{2} I \omega^2$.

When another identical ring is gently placed on it, the total moment of inertia becomes $I' = I + I = 2I$.

By conservation of angular momentum, $I \omega = I' \omega'$.

$I \omega = 2I \omega' \Rightarrow \omega' = \dfrac{\omega}{2}$.

Final kinetic energy is $K_f = \dfrac{1}{2} I' (\omega')^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} (2I) \left(\dfrac{\omega}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} I \omega^2$.

Loss in kinetic energy is $\Delta K = K_i - K_f = \dfrac{1}{2} I \omega^2 - \dfrac{1}{4} I \omega^2 = \dfrac{1}{4} I \omega^2$.
