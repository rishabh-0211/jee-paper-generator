### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A circular ring and a solid sphere having same radius roll down on an inclined plane from rest without slipping. The ratio of their velocities when reached at the bottom of the plane $\sqrt{\dfrac{x}{5}}$ where the value of $x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $3.5$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $2.5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3.5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By conservation of mechanical energy for a body rolling down an incline from rest:

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$

Since $v = \omega R$, we have $mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 \left(1 + \dfrac{I}{mR^2}\right)$

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2gh}{1 + \dfrac{I}{mR^2}}}$

For the circular ring, $I = mR^2$, so $1 + \dfrac{I}{mR^2} = 2$.

$v_{ring} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2gh}{2}} = \sqrt{gh}$

For the solid sphere, $I = \dfrac{2}{5}mR^2$, so $1 + \dfrac{I}{mR^2} = \dfrac{7}{5}$.

$v_{sphere} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2gh}{7/5}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{10gh}{7}}$

The ratio of their velocities is:

$\dfrac{v_{ring}}{v_{sphere}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{gh}}{\sqrt{\dfrac{10gh}{7}}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{7}{10}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3.5}{5}}$

Comparing with $\sqrt{\dfrac{x}{5}}$, we get $x = 3.5$.

Answer: $3.5$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid sphere rolls down from the top of an inclined plane. On reaching the bottom of the plane, its velocity is '$V_1$'. When the same sphere slides down from the top of the same plane of same height, its velocity on reaching the bottom is '$V_2$'. The ratio $V_1 : V_2$ is (neglect friction)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{7} : \sqrt{5}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{7} : \sqrt{3}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{3} : \sqrt{5}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{5} : \sqrt{7}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{5} : \sqrt{7}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a solid sphere rolls down an inclined plane of height $h$, by conservation of mechanical energy:

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mV_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$

For a solid sphere, the moment of inertia is $I = \dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$ and for pure rolling, $\omega = \dfrac{V_1}{r}$.

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mV_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\dfrac{V_1}{r}\right)^2$

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mV_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{5}mV_1^2 = \dfrac{7}{10}mV_1^2$

$V_1 = \sqrt{\dfrac{10gh}{7}}$

When the sphere slides down the same plane without friction, it only acquires translational kinetic energy. By conservation of mechanical energy:

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mV_2^2$

$V_2 = \sqrt{2gh}$

The ratio of the velocities is:

$\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{\dfrac{10gh}{7}}}{\sqrt{2gh}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{7 \times 2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{7}}$

Thus, $V_1 : V_2 = \sqrt{5} : \sqrt{7}$.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid sphere rolls without slipping on an inclied plane at an angle $\theta$. The ratio of total kinetic energy to its rotational kinetic energy is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{5}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{7}{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{5}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{5}{7}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{7}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a solid sphere rolling without slipping, the velocity of the center of mass $v$ and angular velocity $\omega$ are related by $v = \omega R$.

The translational kinetic energy is $K_{trans} = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2$.

The rotational kinetic energy is $K_{rot} = \dfrac{1}{2} I \omega^2$.

Substituting the moment of inertia of a solid sphere $I = \dfrac{2}{5} m R^2$ and $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R}$:

$K_{rot} = \dfrac{1}{2} \left(\dfrac{2}{5} m R^2\right) \left(\dfrac{v}{R}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{5} m v^2$

The total kinetic energy is the sum of translational and rotational kinetic energies:

$K_{total} = K_{trans} + K_{rot} = \dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 + \dfrac{1}{5} m v^2 = \dfrac{7}{10} m v^2$

The ratio of total kinetic energy to rotational kinetic energy is:

$\dfrac{K_{total}}{K_{rot}} = \dfrac{\dfrac{7}{10} m v^2}{\dfrac{1}{5} m v^2} = \dfrac{7}{2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{7}{2}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid cylinder and a solid sphere having the same mass and radius roll down on the same smooth inclined plane. The ratio of the acceleration of the cylinder $(a_c)$ to that of the sphere $(a_s)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{14}{15}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{15}{14}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{13}{14}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{11}{15}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{14}{15}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The acceleration of a body rolling down an inclined plane is given by $a = \dfrac{g \sin \theta}{1 + \dfrac{I}{MR^2}}$.

For a solid cylinder, $I_c = \dfrac{1}{2}MR^2$.

$a_c = \dfrac{g \sin \theta}{1 + \dfrac{1}{2}} = \dfrac{2}{3}g \sin \theta$

For a solid sphere, $I_s = \dfrac{2}{5}MR^2$.

$a_s = \dfrac{g \sin \theta}{1 + \dfrac{2}{5}} = \dfrac{5}{7}g \sin \theta$

The ratio of the acceleration of the cylinder to that of the sphere is:

$\dfrac{a_c}{a_s} = \dfrac{\dfrac{2}{3}g \sin \theta}{\dfrac{5}{7}g \sin \theta} = \dfrac{14}{15}$

Answer: $\dfrac{14}{15}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A solid sphere of mass $1$ kg rolls without slipping on a plane surface. Its kinetic energy is $7 \times 10^{-3}$ J. The speed of the center of mass of the sphere in cm s$^{-1}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $10$
- **(C)** $100$
- **(D)** $1000$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $10$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Total kinetic energy of a solid sphere rolling without slipping is the sum of its translational and rotational kinetic energies.

$K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$

For a solid sphere, $I = \dfrac{2}{5}mr^2$ and for pure rolling, $\omega = \dfrac{v}{r}$.

$K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{2}{5}mr^2\right)\left(\dfrac{v}{r}\right)^2$

$K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{5}mv^2 = \dfrac{7}{10}mv^2$

Given $K = 7 \times 10^{-3}$ J and $m = 1$ kg.

$7 \times 10^{-3} = \dfrac{7}{10} \times 1 \times v^2$

$v^2 = 10^{-2}$

$v = 0.1$ m s$^{-1}$

$v = 10$ cm s$^{-1}$

Answer: $10$

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body slides down a smooth inclined plane of inclination $\theta$ and reaches the bottom with velocity 'V'. If the same body is a ring which rolls down the same inclined plane then linear velocity at the bottom of the plane is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{V}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{V}{2}$
- **(C)** $V$
- **(D)** $2V$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{V}{\sqrt{2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the height of the inclined plane be $h$.

For the body sliding down the smooth inclined plane, the conservation of mechanical energy gives:

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mV^2$

$V = \sqrt{2gh}$

For the ring rolling down the same inclined plane, the total kinetic energy at the bottom is the sum of translational and rotational kinetic energy:

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$

For a ring, the moment of inertia is $I = mR^2$ and for pure rolling, $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R}$. Substituting these values:

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}(mR^2)\left(\dfrac{v}{R}\right)^2$

$mgh = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = mv^2$

$v = \sqrt{gh}$

Comparing the two velocities:

$v = \dfrac{\sqrt{2gh}}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{V}{\sqrt{2}}$

### Q8. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid sphere rolling without slipping on a horizontal surface with a constant speed of $2 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$, rolls up on an inclined ramp which is inclined at $30^{\circ}$. The maximum distance travelled by the sphere on the inclined ramp is (acceleration due to gravity $g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2, \sin 30^{\circ}=1 / 2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** 56 cm
- **(B)** 25 cm
- **(C)** 47 cm
- **(D)** 30 cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 56 cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

By the law of conservation of energy, the total initial kinetic energy of the rolling solid sphere is converted into potential energy at the maximum height on the inclined ramp.

The total kinetic energy of a solid sphere rolling without slipping is given by:
$K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2$

For a solid sphere, the moment of inertia is $I = \dfrac{2}{5}mR^2$ and the angular velocity is $\omega = \dfrac{v}{R}$.

$K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{2}{5}mR^2\right)\left(\dfrac{v}{R}\right)^2$
$K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 + \dfrac{1}{5}mv^2 = \dfrac{7}{10}mv^2$

The potential energy at the maximum distance $s$ along the incline is:
$U = mgh = mg(s \sin \theta)$

Equating kinetic and potential energy:
$\dfrac{7}{10}mv^2 = mgs \sin 30^{\circ}$

Substituting the given values $v = 2 \text{ m/s}$, $g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2$, and $\sin 30^{\circ} = \dfrac{1}{2}$:
$\dfrac{7}{10}(2)^2 = 10 \times s \times \dfrac{1}{2}$
$\dfrac{28}{10} = 5s$
$s = \dfrac{28}{50} \text{ m} = 0.56 \text{ m}$

Converting to centimeters:
$s = 0.56 \times 100 \text{ cm} = 56 \text{ cm}$

Answer: 56 cm

</details>

### Q14. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A carpet of mass ' $M$ ' made of a material is rolled along its length in the form of a cylinder of radius ' $R$ ' and kept above the rough floor. If the carpet is unrolled without sliding to a radius ' $R / 2$ '. The change in potential energy is ( $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** MgR
- **(B)** $\dfrac{7}{8} \mathrm{MgR}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{5}{7} \mathrm{MgR}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{3}{4} \mathrm{MgR}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{7}{8} \mathrm{MgR}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial mass of the rolled carpet be $M$ and its radius be $R$. The center of mass of the cylindrical roll is at a height $R$ from the floor.

Initial potential energy of the carpet, $U_i = MgR$

When the carpet is unrolled to a radius $R/2$, the mass of the remaining rolled part becomes $M'$. Since the mass of the roll is proportional to its cross-sectional area (assuming uniform density and constant length):

$M' = M \dfrac{\pi (R/2)^2}{\pi R^2} = \dfrac{M}{4}$

The center of mass of this remaining rolled part is at a height of $R/2$ from the floor. The unrolled part of the carpet lies flat on the floor, so its center of mass is at a height of approximately zero.

Final potential energy of the carpet, $U_f = M' g \left(\dfrac{R}{2}\right) + (M - M') g (0)$

$U_f = \left(\dfrac{M}{4}\right) g \left(\dfrac{R}{2}\right) = \dfrac{MgR}{8}$

Change in potential energy, $\Delta U = U_i - U_f = MgR - \dfrac{MgR}{8} = \dfrac{7}{8} MgR$

Answer: $\dfrac{7}{8} \mathrm{MgR}$

</details>
