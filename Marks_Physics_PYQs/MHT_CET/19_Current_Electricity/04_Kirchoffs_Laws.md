**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.4\,\text{A}$ , $0.4\,\text{A}$ , $-0.6\,\text{A}$
- **(B)** $0.4\,\text{A}$ , $-0.6\,\text{A}$ , $1.4\,\text{A}$
- **(C)** $1.4\,\text{A}$ , $-0.6\,\text{A}$ , $0.4\,\text{A}$
- **(D)** $-0.6\,\text{A}$ , $1.4\,\text{A}$ , $0.4\,\text{A}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.4\,\text{A}$ , $-0.6\,\text{A}$ , $0.4\,\text{A}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the nodes of the network be identified by their positions.

Applying Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) at the top-left node:
The current entering the node is $I_5$ and the current leaving is $I_6$.
$I_6 = I_5$
Given $I_5 = 0.4\,\text{A}$, we get $I_6 = 0.4\,\text{A}$.

Applying KCL at the bottom-left node:
The currents entering the node are $I_3$ and $I_4$, and the current leaving is $I_5$.
$I_3 + I_4 = I_5$
Substituting the given values $I_4 = 1\,\text{A}$ and $I_5 = 0.4\,\text{A}$:
$I_3 + 1 = 0.4$
$I_3 = -0.6\,\text{A}$

Applying KCL at the bottom-right node:
The currents entering the node are $I_1$ and $I_2$, and the current leaving is $I_4$.
$I_1 + I_2 = I_4$
Substituting the given values $I_1 = -0.4\,\text{A}$ and $I_4 = 1\,\text{A}$:
$-0.4 + I_2 = 1$
$I_2 = 1.4\,\text{A}$

Therefore, the values of $I_2$, $I_3$, and $I_6$ are respectively $1.4\,\text{A}$, $-0.6\,\text{A}$, and $0.4\,\text{A}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) energy, charge**

**Detailed Solution:**

Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL) states that the algebraic sum of changes in potential around any closed loop is zero. This is a consequence of the conservation of energy.

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{3} \mathrm{~A}$ and 3 V**

### Q8. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the circuit shown in the figure, $\mathrm{P} \neq \mathrm{R}$. The reading of the galvanometer remains the same with switch 'S' open or closed. Then
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/13HKYhLNLWK2S4Oa0MnFa2ojLR9Hdw3fIcNCRfLScFw.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{Q}}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{G}}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{Q}}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{R}}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{G}}$
- **(D)** $I_P=I_G$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{G}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the voltage of the battery be $V$.

When the switch S is open, the resistors $R$ and the galvanometer $G$ are in series. The current through the galvanometer is:
$I_G = \dfrac{V}{R+G}$

In this state, since $R$ and $G$ are in the same branch, the current through $R$ is equal to the current through $G$, so $I_R = I_G$.

When the switch S is closed, the potential at node D is determined by the voltage divider formed by the parallel combinations of $P, R$ and $Q, G$. The current through the galvanometer when S is closed is:
$I_G' = \dfrac{V_D}{G} = \dfrac{V}{G} \left( \dfrac{\dfrac{QG}{Q+G}}{\dfrac{PR}{P+R} + \dfrac{QG}{Q+G}} \right)$

Given that the reading of the galvanometer remains the same, $I_G = I_G'$:
$\dfrac{V}{R+G} = \dfrac{V \cdot Q}{(Q+G) \left( \dfrac{PR}{P+R} + \dfrac{QG}{Q+G} \right)}$

$\dfrac{PR}{P+R} + \dfrac{QG}{Q+G} = \dfrac{Q(R+G)}{Q+G}$

$\dfrac{PR}{P+R} = \dfrac{QR + QG - QG}{Q+G} = \dfrac{QR}{Q+G}$

$P(Q+G) = Q(P+R)$

$PQ + PG = PQ + QR \Rightarrow PG = QR$

This is the condition for a balanced Wheatstone bridge. Therefore, the potential at node B is equal to the potential at node D ($V_B = V_D$).

Because $V_B = V_D$, no current flows through the branch BD even when the switch S is closed.

Applying Kirchhoff's Current Law at node D, the current entering from $R$ must equal the current leaving through $G$. Thus, $I_R = I_G$ holds true whether the switch S is open or closed.

Answer: $\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{G}}$

</details>

### Q31. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A parallel plate condenser with oil (dielectric constant 2 ) between the plates has capacitance
C. If oil is removed, the capacitance of capacitor becomes

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{2 C}$
- **(B)** $2 C$
- **(C)** $\frac{C}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{C}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{C}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor with dielectric (oil) between its plates is.
$$
C=\frac{K \varepsilon_{0} A}{d}
$$
where $\varepsilon_{0}=$ electric permittivity of free space $K=$ dielectric constant $A=$ area of each plate of capacitor $d=$ distance between two plates When dielectric (oil) is removed, so capacitance
$$
C_{0}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0} A}{d}
$$
Comparing Eqs. (i) and (ii), we get
$$
C=K C_{0}
$$
$(\because K=2)$

</details>
