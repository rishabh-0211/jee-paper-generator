### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

By increasing the temperature, the specific resistance of a conductor and a semiconductor respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** increases , increases.
- **(B)** decreases , decreases.
- **(C)** increases , decreases.
- **(D)** decreases , increases.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) increases , decreases.**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a conductor, as temperature increases, the thermal agitation of atoms increases, leading to more frequent collisions between free electrons and atoms. This decreases the relaxation time, which in turn increases the specific resistance (resistivity).

For a semiconductor, as temperature increases, more covalent bonds break, generating a larger number of electron-hole pairs. The significant increase in the number density of charge carriers causes the specific resistance to decrease.

Therefore, the specific resistance of a conductor increases and that of a semiconductor decreases.

Answer: increases , decreases.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2023 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A $10 \mathrm{~m}$ long wire of resistance $20 \Omega$ is connected in series with a battery of e.m.f. $3 \mathrm{~V}$ (negligible internal resistance) and a resistance of $10 \Omega$. The potential gradient along the wire is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 \mathrm{~V} / \mathrm{m}$
- **(B)** $0.1 \mathrm{~V} / \mathrm{m}$
- **(C)** $0.2 \mathrm{~V} / \mathrm{m}$
- **(D)** $0.3 \mathrm{~V} / \mathrm{m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.2 \mathrm{~V} / \mathrm{m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Two resistances are in series
$\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{net}}=20 \Omega+10 \Omega=30 \Omega$
Current $\mathrm{I}=\frac{3}{30}=0.1 \mathrm{~A}$
Potential through the wire is $\mathrm{V}=0.1 \times 20=2 \mathrm{~V}$
$\therefore \quad$ Potential gradient $=\frac{\mathrm{V}}{10}=\frac{2}{10}=0.2 \frac{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{m}}$

</details>
