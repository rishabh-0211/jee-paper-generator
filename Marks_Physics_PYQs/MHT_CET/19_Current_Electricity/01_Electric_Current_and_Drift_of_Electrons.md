### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A current of $0.01$ A passes through the potentiometer wire of resistivity $10^6$ $\Omega$ cm and area of cross-section $10^{-2}$ $\text{cm}^2$. The potential gradient is (in V/m)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10^9$
- **(B)** $10^{11}$
- **(C)** $10^{10}$
- **(D)** $10^8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $10^8$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:

Current, $I = 0.01\text{ A} = 10^{-2}\text{ A}$

Resistivity, $\rho = 10^6\ \Omega\text{ cm} = 10^6 \times 10^{-2}\ \Omega\text{ m} = 10^4\ \Omega\text{ m}$

Area of cross-section, $A = 10^{-2}\text{ cm}^2 = 10^{-2} \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2 = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2$

The potential gradient $K$ is defined as the potential drop per unit length:

$K = \dfrac{V}{L}$

Using Ohm's law, $V = IR$. The resistance is $R = \rho \dfrac{L}{A}$.

$K = \dfrac{I \left( \rho \dfrac{L}{A} \right)}{L} = \dfrac{I \rho}{A}$

Substituting the values:

$K = \dfrac{10^{-2} \times 10^4}{10^{-6}}$

$K = \dfrac{10^2}{10^{-6}}$

$K = 10^8\text{ V/m}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

In the given circuit, current flowing through the circuit is
<img width=250px src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/LnbgcJqWpRstiQLAFvjur0n25UZkbkKnL3BW_tKQcng.original.fullsize.png">

- **(A)** 2 A
- **(B)** 2.5 A
- **(C)** 5 A
- **(D)** 4 A

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 5 A**

**Detailed Solution:**

In the given circuit, the two voltage sources are connected in opposition. The $200\text{ V}$ source drives the current in the clockwise direction, while the $10\text{ V}$ source opposes it.

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The potential difference $\left(V_A-V_B\right)$ between the points $A$ and $B$ in the given figure is
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/RhW549aJgvSM2b17Ww2nPIpCTGqFUJ8N5smAYfLQJmU.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** 6 V
- **(B)** $-3 \mathrm{~V}$
- **(C)** 9 V
- **(D)** 3 V

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 9 V**

**Detailed Solution:**

Applying Kirchhoff's voltage law from point A to point B:

Starting at point A with potential $V_A$, we move in the direction of the current ($I = 2\text{ A}$).

1. The potential drop across the $2\ \Omega$ resistor is $I \times R_1 = 2\text{ A} \times 2\ \Omega = 4\text{ V}$.
2. Moving across the battery from the positive to the negative terminal, there is a potential drop of $3\text{ V}$.
3. The potential drop across the $1\ \Omega$ resistor is $I \times R_2 = 2\text{ A} \times 1\ \Omega = 2\text{ V}$.

Writing the potential equation:
$V_A - 4 - 3 - 2 = V_B$

$V_A - 9 = V_B$

$V_A - V_B = 9\text{ V}$

### Q5. MHT CET 2023 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A charged spherical conductor of radius ' $R$ ' is connected momentarily to another uncharged spherical conductor of radius ' $r$ ' by means of a thin conducting wire, then the ratio of the surface charge density of the first to the second conductor is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{R}: \mathrm{r}^2$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{R}: \mathrm{r}$
- **(C)** $r: R$
- **(D)** $1: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $r: R$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
V_1=V_2
$$
( $\because$ both the conductors have the same potential as they are connected)
$$
\begin{gathered}
\Rightarrow \frac{q_1}{R}=\frac{q_2}{r} \\
\sigma=\frac{q}{4 \pi R^2}
\end{gathered}
$$
$\therefore \quad$ The ratio of their charge densities is:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\Rightarrow \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}=\frac{\frac{\mathrm{q}_1}{\mathrm{R}^2}}{\frac{\mathrm{q}_2}{\mathrm{r}^2}} \\
\therefore \quad \frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_2}=\frac{\mathrm{r}}{\mathrm{R}}
\end{gathered}
$$

### Q7. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An electron of mass ' $\mathrm{m}$ ' and charge ' $\mathrm{q}$ ' is accelerated from rest in a uniform electric field of strength ' $E$ '. The velocity acquired by the electron, when it travels a distance ' $\mathrm{L}$ ', is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\frac{2 q E}{m L}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\frac{2 \mathrm{qEL}}{\mathrm{m}}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\frac{2 \mathrm{Em}}{\mathrm{qL}}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{qE}}{\mathrm{mL}}}$

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{\frac{2 \mathrm{qEL}}{\mathrm{m}}}$**

We know
$\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{ma}$ and $\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{qE}$
$\Rightarrow q E=m a$
$\therefore \quad \frac{\mathrm{qE}}{\mathrm{m}}=\mathrm{a}$

$\begin{aligned}
& v^2-u^2=2 a L \\
& v^2-0^2=2 a L \\
& v^2=2 a L \\
& v=\sqrt{2 a L} \\
\therefore \quad & v=\sqrt{\frac{2 q E L}{m}}

**Options:**

- **(C)** 4 cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 4 cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/mht_cet/VUNbtNrQEyPD3AEgE5eDeRc5WfXJ8MN7i1gld-IaLH0.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A},-0 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~A}, 1 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(B)** $-0 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~A}, 1 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A}, 0 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(C)** $1 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A}, 0 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A},-0 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(D)** $1 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A},-0 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~A}, 0 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A},-0 \cdot 6 \mathrm{~A}, 0 \cdot 4 \mathrm{~A}$**

$$
\begin{aligned}

& \mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2}=\mathrm{I}_{4} \\

\therefore \mathrm{I}_{3} &=\mathrm{I}_{5}-\mathrm{I}_{4}=0.4-1=-0.6 \mathrm{~A} \\
\mathrm{I}_{6} &=\mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2}+\mathrm{I}_{3} \\
&=-0.4 \mathrm{~A}+1.4-0.6=0.4 \mathrm{~A}
\end{aligned}
$$
