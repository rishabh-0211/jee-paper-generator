### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If ' m ' is the mass of electron, ' V ' is its velocity, ' r ' is the radius of stationary circular orbit around a nucleus with charge 'Ze' then from Bohr's first postulate the kinetic energy of the electron is
$\left(\mathrm{K}=1 / 4 \pi \epsilon_0\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{Ze}^2}{2 \mathrm{r}} \mathrm{K}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{Ze}^2}{2 \mathrm{r}^2} \mathrm{~K}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{Ze}^2}{\mathrm{r}} \mathrm{K}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{Ze}}{\mathrm{r}^2} \mathrm{~K}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{\mathrm{Ze}^2}{2 \mathrm{r}} \mathrm{K}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Bohr's first postulate, the centripetal force required for the circular motion of the electron is provided by the electrostatic force of attraction between the nucleus and the electron.

$\dfrac{mV^2}{r} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \dfrac{(Ze)(e)}{r^2}$

Given $K = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$, we can write:

$\dfrac{mV^2}{r} = K \dfrac{Ze^2}{r^2}$

$mV^2 = K \dfrac{Ze^2}{r}$

The kinetic energy of the electron is given by:

$\text{K.E.} = \dfrac{1}{2} mV^2$

Substituting the value of $mV^2$, we get:

$\text{K.E.} = \dfrac{1}{2} \left( K \dfrac{Ze^2}{r} \right) = \dfrac{Ze^2}{2r} K$

Answer: $\frac{\mathrm{Ze}^2}{2 \mathrm{r}} \mathrm{K}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

When the electron orbiting in hydrogen atom goes from one orbit to another orbit (principal quantum number $=n$ ), the de-Broglie wavelength $(\lambda)$ associated with it is related to n as

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\lambda \alpha \frac{1}{\mathrm{n}^2}$
- **(B)** $\lambda \alpha \mathrm{n}^2$
- **(C)** $\lambda \alpha \frac{1}{n}$
- **(D)** $\lambda \alpha \mathrm{n}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\lambda \alpha \mathrm{n}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to de-Broglie's hypothesis, the wavelength associated with an electron is given by $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv}$.

For an electron in the $n$-th orbit of a hydrogen atom, the velocity is inversely proportional to the principal quantum number $n$, i.e., $v \propto \dfrac{1}{n}$.

Therefore, $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{1/n} \Rightarrow \lambda \propto n$.

Alternatively, using Bohr's quantization condition, $mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$.

Since $mv = \dfrac{h}{\lambda}$, we have $\dfrac{h}{\lambda} r = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$, which gives $\lambda = \dfrac{2\pi r}{n}$.

Since the radius of the $n$-th orbit $r \propto n^2$, we get $\lambda \propto \dfrac{n^2}{n} \Rightarrow \lambda \propto n$.

Answer: $\lambda \alpha \mathrm{n}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Using Bohr's quantization condition, the rotational kinetic energy in the third orbit for a diatomic molecule is ( $h=$ Planck's constant, $I=$ moment of inertia of diatomic molecule)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{9 h^2}{8 \pi^2 I}$
- **(B)** $\frac{3 \mathrm{~h}^2}{8 \pi^2 \mathrm{I}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{6 \mathrm{~h}^2}{8 \pi \mathrm{I}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{12 h^2}{7 \pi^2 I}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{9 h^2}{8 \pi^2 I}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Bohr's quantization condition, the angular momentum $L$ of the molecule is an integral multiple of $\dfrac{h}{2 \pi}$.

$L = \dfrac{n h}{2 \pi}$

For the third orbit, $n = 3$.

$L = \dfrac{3 h}{2 \pi}$

The rotational kinetic energy is given by:

$E = \dfrac{L^2}{2 I}$

Substituting the value of $L$:

$E = \dfrac{1}{2 I} \left( \dfrac{3 h}{2 \pi} \right)^2$

$E = \dfrac{9 h^2}{8 \pi^2 I}$

Answer: $\frac{9 h^2}{8 \pi^2 I}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An electron is revolving in a circular orbit of radius $r$ in a hydrogen atom. The angular momentum of the electron is L. The relation between dipole moment ( m ) associated with it, gyromagnetic ratio ( R ) and L is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $m=-\frac{L}{R}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{m}=-\mathrm{RL}$
- **(C)** $\quad \mathrm{m}=-\mathrm{RL}^2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{m}=\frac{\mathrm{R}}{\mathrm{L}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{m}=-\mathrm{RL}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic dipole moment of a revolving electron is given by $m = IA = \left(\dfrac{e}{T}\right) \pi r^2 = \dfrac{evr}{2}$.

The orbital angular momentum of the electron is $L = m_e vr$.

The ratio of the magnitude of the magnetic dipole moment to the angular momentum is defined as the gyromagnetic ratio $R$.

$R = \dfrac{m}{L} = \dfrac{e}{2m_e}$

Since the electron is a negatively charged particle, the direction of the conventional current is opposite to its motion. As a result, the magnetic dipole moment vector $\vec{m}$ is directed opposite to the angular momentum vector $\vec{L}$.

Therefore, the relation in vector form (or considering the sign for opposite directions) is:

$m = -RL$

Answer: $\mathrm{m}=-\mathrm{RL}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Acceleration of an electron in the first Bohr's orbit is proportional to $\mathrm{m}=$ mass of electron, $\mathrm{r}=$ radius of the orbit, $\mathrm{h}=$ Planck's constant)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{m}^3 \mathrm{r}^3}{\mathrm{~h}^2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{h}^2}{\mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{r}^3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{h}^2}{\mathrm{mr}^3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{mr}^3}{\mathrm{~h}^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{\mathrm{h}^2}{\mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{r}^3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The centripetal acceleration of an electron in a circular orbit is given by:

$a = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$

According to Bohr's quantization condition for angular momentum:

$mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

For the first Bohr's orbit, $n = 1$:

$mvr = \dfrac{h}{2\pi}$

$v = \dfrac{h}{2\pi mr}$

Substituting the value of $v$ in the expression for acceleration:

$a = \dfrac{1}{r} \left( \dfrac{h}{2\pi mr} \right)^2$

$a = \dfrac{h^2}{4\pi^2 m^2 r^3}$

Since $4\pi^2$ is a constant, the acceleration is proportional to:

$a \propto \dfrac{h^2}{m^2 r^3}$

Answer: $\frac{\mathrm{h}^2}{\mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{r}^3}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Using Bohr's model, the orbital period of electron in hydrogen atom in $\mathrm{n}^{\text {th }}$ orbit is ( $\mathrm{m}=$ mass of electron, $\mathrm{h}=$ Planck's constant, $\mathrm{e}=$ electronic charge, $\varepsilon_0=$ permittivity of free space)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2 \varepsilon_0^2 \mathrm{n}^2 \mathrm{~h}^2}{\mathrm{me}^4}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{4 \varepsilon_0^2 \mathrm{n}^2 \mathrm{~h}^2}{\mathrm{me}^2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{4 \varepsilon_0^2 \mathrm{n}^3 \mathrm{~h}^3}{m \mathrm{e}^4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{4 \varepsilon_0 \mathrm{n}^2 \mathrm{~h}^2}{\pi \mathrm{me}^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{4 \varepsilon_0^2 \mathrm{n}^3 \mathrm{~h}^3}{m \mathrm{e}^4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Bohr's model, the radius of the $n^{\text{th}}$ orbit of a hydrogen atom is given by:

$r = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 n^2 h^2}{\pi m e^2}$

The velocity of the electron in the $n^{\text{th}}$ orbit is given by:

$v = \dfrac{e^2}{2 \varepsilon_0 n h}$

The orbital period $T$ is the time taken for one complete revolution, which is:

$T = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v}$

Substituting the expressions for $r$ and $v$ into the equation for $T$:

$T = \dfrac{2\pi \left( \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 n^2 h^2}{\pi m e^2} \right)}{\dfrac{e^2}{2 \varepsilon_0 n h}}$

$T = \dfrac{2 \varepsilon_0 n^2 h^2}{m e^2} \times \dfrac{2 \varepsilon_0 n h}{e^2}$

$T = \dfrac{4 \varepsilon_0^2 n^3 h^3}{m e^4}$

Answer: $\dfrac{4 \varepsilon_0^2 \mathrm{n}^3 \mathrm{~h}^3}{m \mathrm{e}^4}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2023 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

For an electron moving in the $\mathrm{n}^{\text {th }}$ Bohr orbit the deBroglie wavelength of an electron is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{n} \pi \mathrm{r}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\pi \mathrm{r}}{\mathrm{n}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{n} \mathrm{r}}{2\pi}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2\pi \mathrm{r}}{\mathrm{n}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{2\pi \mathrm{r}}{\mathrm{n}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

From de Broglie's hypothesis,
$\lambda=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{p}_{\mathrm{n}}}=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{mv}}$
and from Bohr's atomic model,
$\mathrm{L}=\frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi}$
Also,
$\mathrm{L}=\mathrm{mvr}_{\mathrm{n}}$
Due to quantization of angular momentum, we can write,
$\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi}=\mathrm{mvr}_{\mathrm{n}} \\
\therefore \quad & \mathrm{v}=\frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi \mathrm{mr}}
\end{aligned}$
putting (ii) into (i), we get,
$\therefore \quad \lambda=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{nh} / 2 \pi \mathrm{mr})}=\frac{2 \pi \mathrm{r}}{\mathrm{n}}$
