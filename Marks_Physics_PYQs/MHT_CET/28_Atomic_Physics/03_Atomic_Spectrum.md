### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which one of the following spectral series of hydrogen atom lies in ultraviolet region?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Lyman
- **(B)** Paschen
- **(C)** Brackett
- **(D)** Pfund

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Lyman**

**Detailed Solution:**

The spectral series of the hydrogen atom are classified based on the region of the electromagnetic spectrum they fall into.

Lyman series corresponds to transitions to the ground state ($n_1 = 1$) and lies in the ultraviolet region.

Balmer series corresponds to transitions to the first excited state ($n_1 = 2$) and lies in the visible region.

Paschen, Brackett, and Pfund series correspond to transitions to higher states ($n_1 = 3, 4, 5$) and lie in the infrared region.

Thus, the Lyman series lies in the ultraviolet region.

Answer: Lyman

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ are the wavelengths of the first members of the Lyman and Paschen series respectively then $\lambda_1:\lambda_2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:35$
- **(B)** $1:30$
- **(C)** $7:50$
- **(D)** $7:108$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $7:108$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the first member of the Lyman series, the transition is from $n=2$ to $n=1$. Using the Rydberg formula:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_1} = R \left( \dfrac{1}{1^2} - \dfrac{1}{2^2} \right) = R \left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{4} \right) = \dfrac{3R}{4}$

$\lambda_1 = \dfrac{4}{3R}$

For the first member of the Paschen series, the transition is from $n=4$ to $n=3$.

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_2} = R \left( \dfrac{1}{3^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2} \right) = R \left( \dfrac{1}{9} - \dfrac{1}{16} \right) = \dfrac{7R}{144}$

$\lambda_2 = \dfrac{144}{7R}$

Taking the ratio of the two wavelengths:

$\dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{4}{3R}}{\dfrac{144}{7R}} = \dfrac{4}{3R} \times \dfrac{7R}{144} = \dfrac{28}{432} = \dfrac{7}{108}$

Answer: $7:108$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

The ratio of minimum wavelengths of Lyman and Balmer series will be

**Options:**

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$

For the minimum wavelength, the energy of the transition must be maximum, which corresponds to $n_2 = \infty$.

For the Lyman series, $n_1 = 1$ and $n_2 = \infty$:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{L}} = R \left( \dfrac{1}{1^2} - \dfrac{1}{\infty} \right) = R \Rightarrow \lambda_{L} = \dfrac{1}{R}$

For the Balmer series, $n_1 = 2$ and $n_2 = \infty$:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{B}} = R \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{\infty} \right) = \dfrac{R}{4} \Rightarrow \lambda_{B} = \dfrac{4}{R}$

The ratio of the minimum wavelengths of the Lyman and Balmer series is:

$\dfrac{\lambda_{L}}{\lambda_{B}} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{R}}{\dfrac{4}{R}} = \dfrac{1}{4} = 0.25$

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An electron in the hydrogen atom jumps from $n^{th}$ energy state to the ground state. The wavelength so emitted illuminates a photosensitive material having work function $2.65$ eV. If the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons is $10.1$ eV, then the value of '$n$' is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2$
- **(B)** $3$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Einstein's photoelectric equation:

$E = K_{max} + \Phi$

Substituting the given values:

$E = 10.1 \text{ eV} + 2.65 \text{ eV} = 12.75 \text{ eV}$

The energy of the photon emitted during the transition from the $n^{th}$ state to the ground state ($n=1$) in a hydrogen atom is given by:

$E = 13.6 \left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{n^2} \right) \text{ eV}$

Equating the two energies:

$12.75 = 13.6 \left( 1 - \dfrac{1}{n^2} \right)$

$1 - \dfrac{1}{n^2} = \dfrac{12.75}{13.6} = \dfrac{15}{16}$

$\dfrac{1}{n^2} = 1 - \dfrac{15}{16} = \dfrac{1}{16}$

$n^2 = 16 \Rightarrow n = 4$

Answer: $4$
