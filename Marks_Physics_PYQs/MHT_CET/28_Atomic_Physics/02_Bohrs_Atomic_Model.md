### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The relation between magnetic moment (M) of revolving electron of charge (e) and mass (m) having principle quantum number (n) is (h = Planck's constant)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{M}{n} = \dfrac{eh}{4\pi m}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{M}{n} = \dfrac{2\pi m}{eh}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{M}{4\pi} = \dfrac{nm}{eh}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{M}{eh} = 4\pi nm$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{M}{n} = \dfrac{eh}{4\pi m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Magnetic moment of a revolving electron is given by $M = IA$

Current $I = \dfrac{e}{T} = \dfrac{ev}{2\pi r}$

Area $A = \pi r^2$

$M = \left(\dfrac{ev}{2\pi r}\right)(\pi r^2) = \dfrac{evr}{2}$

Multiplying and dividing by $m$, we get $M = \dfrac{e}{2m}(mvr) = \dfrac{e}{2m}L$

From Bohr's quantization condition, angular momentum $L = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

Substituting $L$ in the expression for $M$, we get $M = \dfrac{e}{2m}\left(\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}\right) = \dfrac{neh}{4\pi m}$

Rearranging the terms, we get $\dfrac{M}{n} = \dfrac{eh}{4\pi m}$

Answer: $\dfrac{M}{n} = \dfrac{eh}{4\pi m}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In hydrogen like spectrum if wavelength of transition from $3^{rd}$ to $2^{nd}$ orbit is $\lambda_1$ then wavelength of transition from $4^{th}$ to $3^{rd}$ orbit (Bohr's) is $\lambda_2$. If $\lambda_2/\lambda_1 = 20/x$ then value of $x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5$
- **(B)** $6$
- **(C)** $7$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $7$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Rydberg's formula for the wavelength of a transition:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R Z^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$

For the transition from the $3^{rd}$ to the $2^{nd}$ orbit:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_1} = R Z^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2} \right) = R Z^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9} \right) = \dfrac{5 R Z^2}{36}$

$\Rightarrow \lambda_1 = \dfrac{36}{5 R Z^2}$

For the transition from the $4^{th}$ to the $3^{rd}$ orbit:

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_2} = R Z^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{3^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2} \right) = R Z^2 \left( \dfrac{1}{9} - \dfrac{1}{16} \right) = \dfrac{7 R Z^2}{144}$

$\Rightarrow \lambda_2 = \dfrac{144}{7 R Z^2}$

Taking the ratio of $\lambda_2$ to $\lambda_1$:

$\dfrac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \dfrac{\dfrac{144}{7 R Z^2}}{\dfrac{36}{5 R Z^2}} = \dfrac{144}{7} \times \dfrac{5}{36} = \dfrac{4 \times 5}{7} = \dfrac{20}{7}$

Given that $\dfrac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \dfrac{20}{x}$, we get:

$x = 7$

Answer: $7$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

Bohr model is applied to a particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ is moving in a plane under the influence of a transverse magnetic field $B$. The energy of the charged particle in $n^{th}$ level will be ($h$ = Planck's constant)

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2nhqB}{\pi m}$

- **(D)** $\dfrac{nhqB}{\pi m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{nhqB}{4\pi m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a charged particle moves in a transverse magnetic field, the magnetic force provides the necessary centripetal force for circular motion:

$qvB = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

$\Rightarrow v = \dfrac{qBr}{m}$

According to Bohr's quantization condition, the angular momentum of the particle is an integral multiple of $\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$:

$mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

Substituting the expression for $v$ into the quantization condition:

$m \left(\dfrac{qBr}{m}\right) r = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

$qBr^2 = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$

$\Rightarrow r^2 = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi qB}$

The total energy of the particle is purely kinetic, as the magnetic force does no work:

$E = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$

Substituting $v = \dfrac{qBr}{m}$ into the energy equation:

$E = \dfrac{1}{2}m \left(\dfrac{qBr}{m}\right)^2 = \dfrac{q^2B^2r^2}{2m}$

Now, substituting the value of $r^2$ into the energy expression:

$E = \dfrac{q^2B^2}{2m} \left(\dfrac{nh}{2\pi qB}\right)$

$E = \dfrac{nhqB}{4\pi m}$

Answer: $\dfrac{nhqB}{4\pi m}$

### Q10. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In Bohr's theory of hydrogen atom, in any series of spectral lines, wavelength of first line and corresponding series limit is respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** longest and shortest
- **(B)** shortest and shortest
- **(C)** longest and longest
- **(D)** shortest and longest

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) longest and shortest**

**Detailed Solution:**

In any spectral series of the hydrogen atom, the transition occurs from a higher energy level $n_2$ to a lower energy level $n_1$.

The energy difference between the levels is given by $\Delta E = E_{n_2} - E_{n_1} = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$.

For the first line of the series, the transition is from the immediately next higher level, i.e., $n_2 = n_1 + 1$. This transition involves the minimum energy difference ($\Delta E_{\text{min}}$). Since wavelength is inversely proportional to energy ($\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{\Delta E}$), the first line has the longest wavelength.

For the series limit, the transition is from $n_2 = \infty$ to $n_1$. This transition involves the maximum energy difference ($\Delta E_{\text{max}}$). Therefore, the series limit corresponds to the shortest wavelength.

Thus, the wavelength of the first line is the longest and the series limit is the shortest.

Answer: longest and shortest

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $n=5$ to $n=2$
- **(B)** $n=2$ to $n=5$
- **(C)** $n=2$ to $n=3$
- **(D)** $n=3$ to $n=2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

Emission of a photon takes place when an electron transitions from a higher energy state to a lower energy state. Thus, the transitions $n=2$ to $n=5$ and $n=2$ to $n=3$ represent absorption of energy, not emission.

For emission transitions, the energy (and hence frequency) of the emitted photon is given by $\Delta E = h\nu = 13.6 \left( \dfrac{1}{n_f^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_i^2} \right)$ eV.

For the transition $n=5$ to $n=2$:
$\Delta E = 13.6 \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{5^2} \right) = 13.6 \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{25} \right) = 13.6 \times \dfrac{21}{100} = 2.856$ eV.

For the transition $n=3$ to $n=2$:
$\Delta E = 13.6 \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2} \right) = 13.6 \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9} \right) = 13.6 \times \dfrac{5}{36} \approx 1.889$ eV.

Since the energy difference is greater for the $n=5$ to $n=2$ transition, it emits a photon of the highest frequency.

Answer: $n=5$ to $n=2$
