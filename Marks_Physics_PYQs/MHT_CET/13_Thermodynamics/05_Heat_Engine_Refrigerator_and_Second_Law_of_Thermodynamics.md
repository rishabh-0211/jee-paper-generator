### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A carnot engine having efficiency $\dfrac{1}{6}$, operates between the source temperature $T_H$ and the sink temperature $T_C$. Its efficiency increases to $\dfrac{1}{3}$, when $T_C$ is decreased by 64 K. The temperatures $T_H$ and $T_C$ are repectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** $374$ K , $310$ K
- **(B)** $384$ K , $320$ K
- **(C)** $384$ K , $340$ K
- **(D)** $320$ K , $256$ K

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $384$ K , $320$ K**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_C}{T_H}$.

Initially, $\eta = \dfrac{1}{6}$, so:

$\dfrac{1}{6} = 1 - \dfrac{T_C}{T_H}$

$\dfrac{T_C}{T_H} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{5}{6}$

$T_C = \dfrac{5}{6} T_H$

When $T_C$ is decreased by $64 \text{ K}$, the new sink temperature is $T_C - 64$. The new efficiency is $\dfrac{1}{3}$.

$\dfrac{1}{3} = 1 - \dfrac{T_C - 64}{T_H}$

$\dfrac{T_C - 64}{T_H} = 1 - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

$\dfrac{T_C}{T_H} - \dfrac{64}{T_H} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

Substitute $\dfrac{T_C}{T_H} = \dfrac{5}{6}$ into the equation:

$\dfrac{5}{6} - \dfrac{64}{T_H} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

$\dfrac{64}{T_H} = \dfrac{5}{6} - \dfrac{2}{3}$

$\dfrac{64}{T_H} = \dfrac{5 - 4}{6} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

$T_H = 64 \times 6 = 384 \text{ K}$

Now, find $T_C$:

$T_C = \dfrac{5}{6} T_H = \dfrac{5}{6} \times 384 = 5 \times 64 = 320 \text{ K}$

Therefore, $T_H = 384 \text{ K}$ and $T_C = 320 \text{ K}$.

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Carnot engine operating between temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ has efficiency $0.2$. When $T_2$ is lowered by $45$ K, its efficiency becomes $0.5$. Temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ are respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** $150$ K, $120$ K
- **(B)** $120$ K, $150$ K
- **(C)** $60$ K, $80$ K
- **(D)** $80$ K, $60$ K

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $150$ K, $120$ K**

**Detailed Solution:**

Efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

$1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = 0.2 \Rightarrow \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = 0.8 \Rightarrow T_2 = 0.8 T_1$

When $T_2$ is lowered by $45$ K, efficiency becomes $0.5$

$1 - \dfrac{T_2 - 45}{T_1} = 0.5 \Rightarrow \dfrac{T_2 - 45}{T_1} = 0.5$

$T_2 - 45 = 0.5 T_1$

Substituting $T_2 = 0.8 T_1$

$0.8 T_1 - 45 = 0.5 T_1$

$0.3 T_1 = 45 \Rightarrow T_1 = 150$ K

$T_2 = 0.8 \times 150 = 120$ K

Answer: $150$ K, $120$ K

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If heat energy $\Delta Q$ is supplied to an ideal diatomic gas at constant pressure, the increase in internal energy is $\Delta \mathrm{U}$ and the amount of work done by the gas is $\Delta \mathrm{W}$. The ratio $\Delta \mathrm{W}: \Delta \mathrm{U}: \Delta \mathrm{Q}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2: 3: 5$
- **(B)** $2: 5: 7$
- **(C)** $7: 5: 9$
- **(D)** $1: 2: 5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2: 5: 7$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For an ideal diatomic gas, the number of degrees of freedom is $f = 5$.

The molar heat capacity at constant volume is $C_V = \dfrac{5}{2}R$ and at constant pressure is $C_P = \dfrac{7}{2}R$.

When heat is supplied at constant pressure, the heat energy is $\Delta Q = n C_P \Delta T = \dfrac{7}{2}nR\Delta T$.

The increase in internal energy is $\Delta U = n C_V \Delta T = \dfrac{5}{2}nR\Delta T$.

The work done by the gas is $\Delta W = n R \Delta T$.

The ratio is $\Delta W : \Delta U : \Delta Q = nR\Delta T : \dfrac{5}{2}nR\Delta T : \dfrac{7}{2}nR\Delta T = 1 : \dfrac{5}{2} : \dfrac{7}{2} = 2 : 5 : 7$.

Answer: $2: 5: 7$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Heat engine operating between temperature $T_1$ and $T_2$ has efficiency $\frac{1}{6}$. When $T_2$ is lowered by 62 K , its efficiency increases to $\frac{1}{3}$. Then $\mathrm{T}_1$ and $\mathrm{T}_2$ respectively are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $372 \mathrm{~K}, 310 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(B)** $372 \mathrm{~K}, 330 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(C)** $330 \mathrm{~K}, 268 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(D)** $310 \mathrm{~K}, 248 \mathrm{~K}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $372 \mathrm{~K}, 310 \mathrm{~K}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of a heat engine is given by $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$.

Given $\eta_1 = \dfrac{1}{6}$, we have:

$\dfrac{1}{6} = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{5}{6}$

$T_2 = \dfrac{5}{6} T_1$

When the sink temperature is lowered by $62 \text{ K}$, the new efficiency is $\dfrac{1}{3}$:

$\dfrac{1}{3} = 1 - \dfrac{T_2 - 62}{T_1}$

$\dfrac{T_2 - 62}{T_1} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

$T_2 - 62 = \dfrac{2}{3} T_1$

Substituting $T_2 = \dfrac{5}{6} T_1$ into the above equation:

$\dfrac{5}{6} T_1 - 62 = \dfrac{2}{3} T_1$

$\dfrac{5}{6} T_1 - \dfrac{4}{6} T_1 = 62$

$\dfrac{1}{6} T_1 = 62$

$T_1 = 372 \text{ K}$

Now, substituting $T_1$ back to find $T_2$:

$T_2 = \dfrac{5}{6} \times 372 = 310 \text{ K}$

Thus, $T_1 = 372 \text{ K}$ and $T_2 = 310 \text{ K}$.

Answer: $372 \mathrm{~K}, 310 \mathrm{~K}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Two cylinders A and B fitted with piston contain equal amount of an ideal diatomic as at temperature ' $T$ ' K . The piston of cylinder A is free to move while that of B is held fixed. The same amount of heat is given to the gas in each cylinder. If the rise temperature of the gas in A is ' $\mathrm{dT}_{\mathrm{A}}$ ', then the rise in temperature of the gas in cylinder B is $\left(\gamma=\frac{\dot{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{P}}}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{V}}}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\quad 2 \mathrm{dT}_{\mathrm{A}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{dT}_{\mathrm{A}}}{2}$
- **(C)** $\gamma \mathrm{dT}_{\mathrm{A}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{dT}_{\mathrm{A}}}{\gamma}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\gamma \mathrm{dT}_{\mathrm{A}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For cylinder A, the piston is free to move, which means the pressure remains constant. The process is isobaric. The heat supplied is given by:

$dQ_A = n C_P dT_A$

For cylinder B, the piston is held fixed, which means the volume remains constant. The process is isochoric. The heat supplied is given by:

$dQ_B = n C_V dT_B$

Given that the same amount of heat is supplied to both cylinders, we have:

$dQ_A = dQ_B$

$n C_P dT_A = n C_V dT_B$

$dT_B = \dfrac{C_P}{C_V} dT_A$

Since the ratio of specific heats is $\gamma = \dfrac{C_P}{C_V}$, we get:

$dT_B = \gamma dT_A$

Answer: $\gamma \mathrm{dT}_{\mathrm{A}}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A diatomic ideal gas is used in Carnot engine as a working substance. If during the adiabatic expansion part of the cycle, the volume of the gas increases from V to 32 V , the efficiency of the engine is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0.25
- **(B)** 0.50
- **(C)** 0.75
- **(D)** 0.90

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 0.75**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a diatomic ideal gas, the ratio of specific heats is $\gamma = \dfrac{7}{5}$.

During the adiabatic expansion, the relation between temperature and volume is given by $T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$.

Let $T_1$ be the temperature of the source and $T_2$ be the temperature of the sink. We have:

$T_1 V^{\gamma - 1} = T_2 (32V)^{\gamma - 1}$

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\dfrac{V}{32V}\right)^{\gamma - 1} = \left(\dfrac{1}{32}\right)^{\dfrac{7}{5} - 1} = \left(\dfrac{1}{32}\right)^{\dfrac{2}{5}}$

Since $32 = 2^5$, we get:

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\dfrac{1}{2^5}\right)^{\dfrac{2}{5}} = \dfrac{1}{2^2} = \dfrac{1}{4} = 0.25$

The efficiency of the Carnot engine is given by:

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

$\eta = 1 - 0.25 = 0.75$

Answer: 0.75

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The first operation involved in a carnot cycle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** isothermal expansion.
- **(B)** adiabatic expansion.
- **(C)** isothermal compression.
- **(D)** adiabatic compression.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) isothermal expansion.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A Carnot cycle consists of four reversible processes performed in the following sequence:

1. Reversible isothermal expansion: The working substance absorbs heat from the source at a constant high temperature.

2. Reversible adiabatic expansion: The working substance expands adiabatically, and its temperature falls to the temperature of the sink.

3. Reversible isothermal compression: The working substance rejects heat to the sink at a constant low temperature.

4. Reversible adiabatic compression: The working substance is compressed adiabatically, and its temperature rises back to the temperature of the source.

Therefore, the first operation involved in a Carnot cycle is isothermal expansion.

Answer: isothermal expansion.

</details>
