### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (5 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The relation between efficiency $(\eta)$ of Carnot engine and coefficient of performance ( $\eta_1$ ) of refrigerator is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\eta=\frac{1}{1-\eta_1}$
- **(B)** $\eta=\frac{1}{1+\eta_1}$
- **(C)** $\eta=\frac{\eta_1}{1-\eta_1}$
- **(D)** $\eta=\frac{1+\eta_1}{\eta_1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\eta=\frac{1}{1+\eta_1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by:

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{T_1 - T_2}{T_1}$

The coefficient of performance of a refrigerator is given by:

$\eta_1 = \dfrac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2}$

Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $T_1$, we get:

$\eta_1 = \dfrac{\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}}{1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}}$

Since $\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = 1 - \eta$, substituting this into the equation gives:

$\eta_1 = \dfrac{1 - \eta}{\eta}$

$\eta_1 = \dfrac{1}{\eta} - 1$

$\eta_1 + 1 = \dfrac{1}{\eta}$

$\eta = \dfrac{1}{1 + \eta_1}$

Answer: $\eta=\frac{1}{1+\eta_1}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A Carnot heat engine has an efficiency of $20 \%$. The energy is supplied to the engine at the rate of 2 kW . The output power of the engine is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 400 W
- **(B)** 300 W
- **(C)** 500 W
- **(D)** 600 W

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 400 W**

**Detailed Solution:**

Efficiency of the Carnot engine, $\eta = 20\% = 0.2$

Rate of energy supplied (input power), $P_{in} = 2 \text{ kW} = 2000 \text{ W}$

Output power of the engine, $P_{out} = \eta \times P_{in}$

$P_{out} = 0.2 \times 2000 \text{ W} = 400 \text{ W}$

Answer: 400 W

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A Carnot engine has efficiency $\frac{1}{6}$. It becomes $\frac{1}{3}$, when the temperature of sink is lowered by 57 K . The temperature of the source is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 171 K
- **(B)** 399 K
- **(C)** 342 K
- **(D)** 285 K

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 342 K**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$, where $T_1$ is the temperature of the source and $T_2$ is the temperature of the sink.

Given the initial efficiency is $\dfrac{1}{6}$:

$1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{5}{6}$

$T_2 = \dfrac{5}{6} T_1$

When the temperature of the sink is lowered by $57$ K, the new efficiency becomes $\dfrac{1}{3}$:

$1 - \dfrac{T_2 - 57}{T_1} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

$\dfrac{T_2 - 57}{T_1} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

$T_2 - 57 = \dfrac{2}{3} T_1$

Substituting $T_2 = \dfrac{5}{6} T_1$ into the above equation:

$\dfrac{5}{6} T_1 - 57 = \dfrac{2}{3} T_1$

$\dfrac{5}{6} T_1 - \dfrac{4}{6} T_1 = 57$

$\dfrac{1}{6} T_1 = 57$

$T_1 = 57 \times 6 = 342$ K

Answer: 342 K

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An engine operating between temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ has efficiency $\frac{1}{5}$. When $\mathrm{T}_2$ is lowered by 45 K , its efficiency becomes $\frac{1}{2}$. Temperatures $\mathrm{T}_1$ and $\mathrm{T}_2$ are respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** $100 \mathrm{~K}, 70 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(B)** $160 \mathrm{~K}, 120 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(C)** $140 \mathrm{~K}, 110 \mathrm{~K}$
- **(D)** $150 \mathrm{~K}, 120 \mathrm{~K}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $150 \mathrm{~K}, 120 \mathrm{~K}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of an engine is given by $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

Given the initial efficiency $\eta_1 = \dfrac{1}{5}$:

$1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{1}{5}$

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{4}{5}$

$T_2 = \dfrac{4}{5} T_1$

When $T_2$ is lowered by $45$ K, the new efficiency is $\eta_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$:

$1 - \dfrac{T_2 - 45}{T_1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\dfrac{T_2 - 45}{T_1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$T_2 - 45 = \dfrac{1}{2} T_1$

Substituting $T_2 = \dfrac{4}{5} T_1$ into the above equation:

$\dfrac{4}{5} T_1 - 45 = \dfrac{1}{2} T_1$

$\left(\dfrac{4}{5} - \dfrac{1}{2}\right) T_1 = 45$

$\left(\dfrac{8 - 5}{10}\right) T_1 = 45$

$\dfrac{3}{10} T_1 = 45$

$T_1 = \dfrac{45 \times 10}{3} = 150$ K

Now, finding $T_2$:

$T_2 = \dfrac{4}{5} \times 150 = 120$ K

The temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$ are $150$ K and $120$ K respectively.

Answer: $150 \mathrm{~K}, 120 \mathrm{~K}$

</details>
