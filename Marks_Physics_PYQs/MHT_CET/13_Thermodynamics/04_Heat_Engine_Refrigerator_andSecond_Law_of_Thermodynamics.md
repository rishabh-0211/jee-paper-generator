### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A Carnot engine has an efficiency of $30\%$. The energy is supplied to the engine at the rate of $3$ kW. The output power of the engine is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $100$ W
- **(B)** $300$ W
- **(C)** $900$ W
- **(D)** $1200$ W

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $900$ W**

**Detailed Solution:**

Efficiency of the Carnot engine, $\eta = 30\% = 0.3$

Input power, $P_{in} = 3 \text{ kW} = 3000 \text{ W}$

Output power, $P_{out} = \eta \times P_{in}$

$P_{out} = 0.3 \times 3000 = 900 \text{ W}$

Answer: $900$ W

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

There is an expansion of a gas in a thermodynamic system. But this change cannot be plotted on a P-V diagram. Then it is

**Options:**

- **(A)** an isothermal expansion.
- **(B)** a free expansion
- **(C)** an adiabatic expansion
- **(D)** an isobaric expansion

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) a free expansion**

**Detailed Solution:**

A process can be plotted on a P-V diagram only if the system passes through a sequence of equilibrium states, meaning the process must be quasi-static. In such processes, thermodynamic variables like pressure and volume are well-defined at every instant.

Free expansion is a highly irreversible and non-quasi-static process where a gas expands into a vacuum. During this expansion, the gas is not in thermodynamic equilibrium, and its pressure and temperature are not well-defined in the intermediate states. Therefore, the intermediate states cannot be represented by points on a P-V diagram, and the process cannot be plotted as a continuous curve.

Isothermal, adiabatic, and isobaric expansions are typically idealized as quasi-static processes and can be plotted on a P-V diagram.

Answer: a free expansion

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

An ideal gas heat engine operate in Carnot cycle between $327^\circ C$ and $227^\circ C$. It absorbs $6 \times 10^4$ cal of heat at higher temperature. How much is the amount of heat converted in to work?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \times 10^4\,cal$
- **(B)** $1 \times 10^4\,cal$
- **(C)** $4 \times 10^4\,cal$
- **(D)** $3 \times 10^4\,cal$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1 \times 10^4\,cal$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The temperatures of the source and sink are:

$T_1 = 327^\circ C = 327 + 273 = 600$ K

$T_2 = 227^\circ C = 227 + 273 = 500$ K

The heat absorbed at the higher temperature is $Q_1 = 6 \times 10^4$ cal.

The efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by:

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{500}{600} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

The amount of heat converted into work is:

$W = \eta Q_1$

$W = \dfrac{1}{6} \times 6 \times 10^4 = 1 \times 10^4$ cal

Answer: $1 \times 10^4\,cal$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The efficiency of Carnot engine which operates between the two temperatures $T_1 = 500$ K and $T_2 = 300$ K is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $75\ \%$
- **(B)** $50\ \%$
- **(C)** $25\ \%$
- **(D)** $40\ \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $40\ \%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by the formula:

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

Given the source temperature $T_1 = 500$ K and the sink temperature $T_2 = 300$ K, substituting these values into the formula gives:

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{300}{500}$

$\eta = 1 - \dfrac{3}{5}$

$\eta = \dfrac{2}{5} = 0.4$

To express the efficiency as a percentage, multiply by $100\%$:

$\eta = 0.4 \times 100\% = 40\%$

Answer: $40\ \%$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A Carnot engine, whose efficiency is 40% takes heat from a source maintained at temperature 600K. To have an efficiency 60%, the intake temperature for the same exhaust temperature should be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1800$ K
- **(B)** $900$ K
- **(C)** $720$ K
- **(D)** $360$ K

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $900$ K**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$

Given $\eta_1 = 40\% = 0.4$ and $T_1 = 600$ K

$0.4 = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{600}$

$\dfrac{T_2}{600} = 0.6 \Rightarrow T_2 = 360$ K

For the new efficiency $\eta_2 = 60\% = 0.6$ with the same exhaust temperature $T_2 = 360$ K, let the new intake temperature be $T_1'$

$0.6 = 1 - \dfrac{360}{T_1'}$

$\dfrac{360}{T_1'} = 0.4$

$T_1' = \dfrac{360}{0.4} = 900$ K

Answer: $900$ K

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A Carnot engine with efficiency 50% takes heat from a source at 600 K. To increase the efficiency by 20%, keeping temperature of the sink same, the new temperature of the source will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $300\ \text{K}$
- **(B)** $900\ \text{K}$
- **(C)** $1000\ \text{K}$
- **(D)** $360\ \text{K}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1000\ \text{K}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The efficiency of a Carnot engine is given by $\eta = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$, where $T_1$ is the source temperature and $T_2$ is the sink temperature.

Given initial efficiency $\eta_1 = 50\% = 0.5$ and source temperature $T_1 = 600\ \text{K}$.

$0.5 = 1 - \dfrac{T_2}{600}$

$\dfrac{T_2}{600} = 0.5 \Rightarrow T_2 = 300\ \text{K}$

The efficiency is increased by $20\%$, so the new efficiency is $\eta_2 = 50\% + 20\% = 70\% = 0.7$.

Let the new source temperature be $T_1'$. The sink temperature $T_2$ remains the same.

$0.7 = 1 - \dfrac{300}{T_1'}$

$\dfrac{300}{T_1'} = 0.3$

$T_1' = \dfrac{300}{0.3} = 1000\ \text{K}$

Answer: $1000\ \text{K}$

</details>
