### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two identical containers A and B with friction-less pistons contain the same ideal gas at the same temperature and the same volume, $V$. The mass of gas in A is $m_A$ and that in B is $m_B$. The gas in each cylinder is now allowed to expand isothermally to the same final volume $2V$. The changes in pressure in A and B are found to be $\Delta p$ and $3\Delta p$ respectively. Then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $m_A = m_B$
- **(B)** $3m_A = 2m_B$
- **(C)** $3m_A = m_B$
- **(D)** $3m_A = 4m_B$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3m_A = m_B$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial pressure in container A is $p_A = \dfrac{m_A R T}{M V}$

Initial pressure in container B is $p_B = \dfrac{m_B R T}{M V}$

After isothermal expansion to volume $2V$, the final pressures are $p_A' = \dfrac{p_A}{2}$ and $p_B' = \dfrac{p_B}{2}$

Change in pressure for A is $\Delta p_A = p_A - \dfrac{p_A}{2} = \dfrac{p_A}{2} = \Delta p$

Change in pressure for B is $\Delta p_B = p_B - \dfrac{p_B}{2} = \dfrac{p_B}{2} = 3\Delta p$

Dividing the two equations gives $\dfrac{p_B}{p_A} = 3$

Substituting the expressions for initial pressures gives $\dfrac{m_B}{m_A} = 3$

Therefore, $3m_A = m_B$

Answer: $3m_A = m_B$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In an adiabatic process, which one of the following statement is true ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The molar heat capacity is infinite
- **(B)** Work done by the gas equals the increase in internal energy
- **(C)** The molar heat capacity is zero
- **(D)** The internal energy of the gas decreases as the temperature increases

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The molar heat capacity is zero**

**Detailed Solution:**

In an adiabatic process, there is no exchange of heat between the system and the surroundings, so $dQ = 0$.

The molar heat capacity is given by $C = \dfrac{dQ}{n dT}$.

Since $dQ = 0$ and $dT \neq 0$, we get $C = 0$.

According to the first law of thermodynamics, $dQ = dU + dW \Rightarrow dW = -dU$. Thus, work done by the gas equals the decrease in internal energy.

The internal energy of an ideal gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature. Hence, internal energy increases as temperature increases.

Therefore, the only true statement is that the molar heat capacity is zero.

Answer: The molar heat capacity is zero

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $PV^2 = \text{constant}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For an adiabatic process, the relation between temperature and volume is given by $TV^{\gamma - 1} = \text{constant}$.

Let the initial temperature and volume be $T_1$ and $V_1$.

Given that the final temperature $T_2 = 2T_1$ and final volume $V_2 = \dfrac{V_1}{2}$.

$T_1 V_1^{\gamma - 1} = T_2 V_2^{\gamma - 1}$

$T_1 V_1^{\gamma - 1} = (2T_1) \left(\dfrac{V_1}{2}\right)^{\gamma - 1}$

$1 = 2 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{\gamma - 1}$

$2^{\gamma - 1} = 2^1$

$\gamma - 1 = 1 \Rightarrow \gamma = 2$

The equation of state for an adiabatic process is $PV^{\gamma} = \text{constant}$.

Substituting $\gamma = 2$, we get $PV^2 = \text{constant}$.

Answer: $PV^2 = \text{constant}$

### Q129. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two drops of equal radius coalesce to form a bigger drop. What is ratio of surface energy of bigger drop to smaller one?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2^{1 / 2}: 1$
- **(B)** $1: 1$
- **(C)** $2^{2 / 3}: 1$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2^{2 / 3}: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Volume remains constant after coalescing. Thus,
$$
\frac{4}{3} \pi R^{3}=2 \times \frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3}
$$
where $R$ is radius of bigger drop and $r$ is radius of each smaller drop.
$\therefore$
$$
R=2^{1 / 3} r
$$
Now, surface energy per unit surface area is the surface tension. So,
Surface energy, $W=T \Delta A$
Or
$$
W=4 \pi R^{2} T
$$
Therefore, surface energy of bigger drop
$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{1} &=4 \pi\left(2^{1 / 3} r\right)^{2} T \\
&=\left(2^{2 / 3}\right) 4 \pi r^{2} T
\end{aligned}
$$
Surface energy of smaller drop
$$
W_{2}=4 \pi r^{2} T
$$
Hence, required ratio
$$
\frac{W_{1}}{W_{2}}=2^{2 / 3}: 1
$$

</details>
