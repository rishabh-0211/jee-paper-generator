### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An ideal gas having pressure P, volume V and temperature T undergoes a thermodynamic process in which $dW = 0$ and $dQ < 0$. Then, for the gas

**Options:**

- **(A)** V will increase
- **(B)** T will increase
- **(C)** P first decreases and then increases
- **(D)** T will decrease

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) T will decrease**

**Detailed Solution:**

From the first law of thermodynamics, $dQ = dU + dW$.

Given $dW = 0$, the equation simplifies to $dQ = dU$.

Since $dQ < 0$, it implies that $dU < 0$.

For an ideal gas, the change in internal energy is given by $dU = nC_v dT$.

Since $dU < 0$, we must have $dT < 0$, which means the temperature $T$ of the gas will decrease.

Additionally, $dW = 0$ implies the volume $V$ remains constant. From the ideal gas equation $PV = nRT$, a decrease in temperature at constant volume results in a decrease in pressure $P$.

Answer: T will decrease

</details>
