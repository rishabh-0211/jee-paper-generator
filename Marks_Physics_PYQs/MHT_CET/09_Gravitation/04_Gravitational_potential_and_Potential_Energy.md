### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The minimum energy required to launch a satellite of mass $m$ from the surface of a planet of mass $M$ and radius $R$ in a circular orbit at an altitude of $3R$ is $\{G = \text{gravitational constant}\}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{3GMm}{8R}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{5GMm}{4R}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{3GMm}{4R}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{7GMm}{8R}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{7GMm}{8R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial energy of the satellite on the surface of the planet is:

$E_i = -\dfrac{GMm}{R}$

The radius of the circular orbit at an altitude of $3R$ is $r = R + 3R = 4R$.

Final total energy of the satellite in the circular orbit is:

$E_f = -\dfrac{GMm}{2r} = -\dfrac{GMm}{2(4R)} = -\dfrac{GMm}{8R}$

The minimum energy required to launch the satellite is the difference between the final and initial energies:

$\Delta E = E_f - E_i$

$\Delta E = -\dfrac{GMm}{8R} - \left(-\dfrac{GMm}{R}\right)$

$\Delta E = \dfrac{GMm}{R} - \dfrac{GMm}{8R} = \dfrac{7GMm}{8R}$

Answer: $\dfrac{7GMm}{8R}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body of mass 'm' is raised to a height '$10R$' from the surface of the earth, where 'R' is the radius of the earth. Suppose it is released now. The speed with which it will strike the surface of the earth is nearly
[$g$ = acceleration due to gravity]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{20gR}{11}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{10gR}{11}}$
- **(C)** $2\sqrt{gR}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{gR}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\sqrt{\dfrac{20gR}{11}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By the principle of conservation of mechanical energy:

$K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f$

The initial height from the surface is $10R$, so the initial distance from the center of the earth is $r_i = R + 10R = 11R$. The final distance is $r_f = R$.

$0 - \dfrac{GMm}{11R} = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 - \dfrac{GMm}{R}$

$\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{GMm}{R} - \dfrac{GMm}{11R}$

$\dfrac{1}{2}v^2 = \dfrac{10GM}{11R}$

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{20GM}{11R}}$

Using the relation $g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$, we substitute $GM = gR^2$:

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{20gR^2}{11R}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{20}{11}gR}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\dfrac{20gR}{11}}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle of mass $m$ is projected with a velocity $v = KV_e$ ($K < 1$) from the surface of the earth. The maximum height above the surface reached by the particle is ($V_e$ = escape velocity, $R$ is radius of earth)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $R \left(\dfrac{K}{1+K}\right)^2$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{R^2 K}{1+K}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{RK^2}{1-K^2}$
- **(D)** $R \left(\dfrac{K}{1-K}\right)^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{RK^2}{1-K^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By conservation of mechanical energy:

$K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f$

$\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 - \dfrac{GMm}{R} = 0 - \dfrac{GMm}{R+h}$

Given $v = KV_e$ and $V_e = \sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}$, we have $v^2 = K^2 \dfrac{2GM}{R}$.

Substituting $v^2$ in the energy equation:

$\dfrac{1}{2}m \left( K^2 \dfrac{2GM}{R} \right) - \dfrac{GMm}{R} = - \dfrac{GMm}{R+h}$

$\dfrac{GMmK^2}{R} - \dfrac{GMm}{R} = - \dfrac{GMm}{R+h}$

Dividing by $GMm$:

$\dfrac{K^2 - 1}{R} = - \dfrac{1}{R+h}$

$\dfrac{1 - K^2}{R} = \dfrac{1}{R+h}$

$R+h = \dfrac{R}{1-K^2}$

$h = \dfrac{R}{1-K^2} - R = \dfrac{RK^2}{1-K^2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{RK^2}{1-K^2}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

A body is projected vertically upwards from earth's surface. If its kinetic energy of projection is equal to half of its minimum value required to escape from the gravitational influence then the height up to which it rises is ($R$ = radius of earth)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4R$
- **(B)** $3R$
- **(C)** $R$
- **(D)** $2R$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $R$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The minimum kinetic energy required for a body to escape earth's gravitational field is given by:

$K_e = \dfrac{1}{2}mv_e^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}m\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}\right)^2 = \dfrac{GMm}{R}$

Given that the kinetic energy of projection is half of the escape kinetic energy:

$K_i = \dfrac{1}{2}K_e = \dfrac{GMm}{2R}$

Let the body rise to a height $h$ above the surface of the earth. At the maximum height, its velocity becomes zero, so the final kinetic energy $K_f = 0$.

$K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f$

$\dfrac{GMm}{2R} - \dfrac{GMm}{R} = 0 - \dfrac{GMm}{R+h}$

$-\dfrac{GMm}{2R} = -\dfrac{GMm}{R+h}$

$\dfrac{1}{2R} = \dfrac{1}{R+h}$

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The masses and radii of the earth and moon are $M_1$, $R_1$ and $M_2$, $R_2$ and respectively, Their centres are at a distance 'd' apart. The minimum speed with which body of mass 'm' should be projected from a distance $2d/3$ from the centre of $M_1$ so as to escape to infinity is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{6G}{d}(2M_1 + M_2)}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{6G}{d}(M_1 - 2M_2)}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{3G}{d}(M_1 + 2M_2)}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{8G}{d}(M_1 - 2M_2)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{\dfrac{3G}{d}(M_1 + 2M_2)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the body of mass $m$ be projected from a point $P$ on the line joining the centres of the two bodies.

The distance of point $P$ from the centre of $M_1$ is $r_1 = \dfrac{2d}{3}$.

The distance of point $P$ from the centre of $M_2$ is $r_2 = d - \dfrac{2d}{3} = \dfrac{d}{3}$.

The total gravitational potential energy of the body at point $P$ is the sum of the potential energies due to $M_1$ and $M_2$:

$U = -\dfrac{G M_1 m}{r_1} - \dfrac{G M_2 m}{r_2}$

$U = -\dfrac{G M_1 m}{\dfrac{2d}{3}} - \dfrac{G M_2 m}{\dfrac{d}{3}}$

$U = -\dfrac{3G M_1 m}{2d} - \dfrac{3G M_2 m}{d}$

$U = -\dfrac{3G m}{2d} (M_1 + 2M_2)$

For the body to just escape to infinity, its total mechanical energy must be zero. By conservation of energy:

$K + U = 0$

$\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 - \dfrac{3G m}{2d} (M_1 + 2M_2) = 0$

$\dfrac{1}{2} m v^2 = \dfrac{3G m}{2d} (M_1 + 2M_2)$

$v^2 = \dfrac{3G}{d} (M_1 + 2M_2)$

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{3G}{d} (M_1 + 2M_2)}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\dfrac{3G}{d}(M_1 + 2M_2)}$

</details>

### Q55. MHT CET 2008

If $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity on earth's surface, the gain of the potential energy of an object of mass $m$ raised from the surface of the earth to a height equal to the radius $R$ of the earth is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \mathrm{mgR}$
- **(B)** $m g R$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{2} m g R$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{4} m g R$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{1}{2} m g R$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The potential energy of an object at the surface of the earth
$$
U_{1}=-\frac{G M m}{R}
$$
The potential energy of the object at a height $h=R$ from the surface of the earth
$$
U_{2}=-\frac{G M m}{R+h}=-\frac{G M m}{R+R}
$$
Hence, the gain in potential energy of the object
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U &=U_{2}-U_{1} \\
\Delta U &=-\frac{G M m}{R+R}+\frac{G M m}{R} \\
\Delta U &=-\frac{G M m}{2 R}+\frac{G M m}{R} \\
\Delta U &=\frac{1}{2} \frac{G M m}{R}
\end{aligned}
$$
But we know that $G M=g R^{2}$
Hence,
$$
\Delta U=\frac{1}{2} \frac{g R^{2} m}{R}
$$
or
$$
\Delta U=\frac{1}{2} g R m
$$
or
$$
\Delta U=\frac{1}{2} m g R
$$
