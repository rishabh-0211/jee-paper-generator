### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A geostationary satellite is orbiting the earth at a height of $4R$ above the surface of the earth, where $R$ is the radius of the earth. Another satellite is orbiting the earth at a height $1.5R$ from the surface of the earth with periodic time 'T' in hour. The value of 'T' in hour is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(B)** $6\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $6\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The orbital radius of the geostationary satellite is $r_1 = R + 4R = 5R$.

The time period of a geostationary satellite is $T_1 = 24$ hours.

The orbital radius of the second satellite is $r_2 = R + 1.5R = 2.5R$.

Let the time period of the second satellite be $T_2 = T$.

According to Kepler's third law, the square of the time period is proportional to the cube of the orbital radius:

$T^2 \propto r^3$

$\left(\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^3$

$\left(\dfrac{T}{24}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{2.5R}{5R}\right)^3$

$\left(\dfrac{T}{24}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \dfrac{1}{8}$

$\dfrac{T}{24} = \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}$

$T = \dfrac{24}{2\sqrt{2}} = 6\sqrt{2}$ hours

Answer: $6\sqrt{2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The time period of a satellite of earth is 24 hours. If the separation between the earth and the satellite is decreased to one fourth of the previous value then its new time period will become

**Options:**

- **(A)** 3 hours
- **(B)** 6 hours
- **(C)** 24 hours
- **(D)** 12 hours

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 3 hours**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Kepler's third law, the square of the time period of a satellite is directly proportional to the cube of its orbital radius.

$T^2 \propto r^3$

$\left(\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^3$

Given $T_1 = 24$ hours and $r_2 = \dfrac{r_1}{4}$.

$\left(\dfrac{T_2}{24}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^3$

$\left(\dfrac{T_2}{24}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{64}$

$\dfrac{T_2}{24} = \dfrac{1}{8}$

$T_2 = 3$ hours.

Answer: 3 hours

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A satellite of mass ' $m$ ' is revolving around the earth of mass ' $M$ ' in an orbit of radius ' $r$ ' with constant angular velocity ' $\omega$ '. The angular. momentum of satellite is
( $\mathrm{G}=$ Universal constant of gravitation)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{GMr})^{3 / 2}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{GMr})$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{GMr})^{1 / 2}$
- **(D)** $\quad \mathrm{m}(\mathrm{GMr})^{-1 / 2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{GMr})^{1 / 2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular momentum of the satellite is given by $L = mvr$.

The orbital velocity of the satellite is $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$.

Substituting the value of $v$ in the expression for $L$:

$L = m \left( \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}} \right) r$

$L = m \sqrt{GMr} = m(GMr)^{1/2}$

Answer: $\mathrm{m}(\mathrm{GMr})^{1 / 2}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A small planet is revolving around a very massive star in a circular orbit of radius ' $R$ ' with a period of revolution ' $T$ '. If the gravitational force between the planet and the star were proportional to ' $\mathrm{R}{ }^{-5 / 2}$, then ' T ', would be proportional to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{R}^{3 / 2}$
- **(B)** $R^{3 / 5}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{R}^{7 / 2}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{R}^{7 / 4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{R}^{7 / 4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The gravitational force between the planet and the star provides the necessary centripetal force for circular motion.

$F = m\omega^2 R = m\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2 R$

Given that the gravitational force is proportional to $R^{-5/2}$:

$F \propto R^{-5/2}$

Equating the two expressions for force:

$m\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T}\right)^2 R \propto R^{-5/2}$

$\dfrac{1}{T^2} \propto R^{-5/2 - 1}$

$\dfrac{1}{T^2} \propto R^{-7/2}$

$T^2 \propto R^{7/2}$

$T \propto R^{7/4}$

Answer: $\mathrm{R}^{7 / 4}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A satellite is revolving around a planet in a circular orbit close to its surface. Let ' $\rho$ ' be the mean density and ' $R$ ' be the radius of the planet. Then the period of the satellite is ( $\mathrm{G}=$ universal constant of gravitation)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{4 \pi}{\rho G}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{\rho G}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{3 \pi}{\rho G}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{2 \pi}{\rho G}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{\dfrac{3 \pi}{\rho G}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of a satellite revolving close to the surface of a planet is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{R^3}{GM}}$.

The mass of the planet can be expressed in terms of its mean density $\rho$ and radius $R$ as $M = \dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \rho$.

Substituting the value of $M$ in the expression for $T$:

$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{R^3}{G \left(\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \rho\right)}}$

$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4\pi G \rho}}$

$T = \sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi^2 \times 3}{4\pi G \rho}}$

$T = \sqrt{\dfrac{3\pi}{\rho G}}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\dfrac{3 \pi}{\rho G}}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The period of a planet around the sun is 8 times that of earth. The ratio of radius of planet's orbit to the radius of the earth's orbit is .

**Options:**

- **(A)** 4
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** 16
- **(D)** 64

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 4**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Kepler's third law:

$T^2 \propto R^3$

$\Rightarrow \left(\dfrac{R_p}{R_e}\right)^3 = \left(\dfrac{T_p}{T_e}\right)^2$

Given $T_p = 8T_e$, substituting this value:

$\left(\dfrac{R_p}{R_e}\right)^3 = (8)^2 = 64$

$\Rightarrow \dfrac{R_p}{R_e} = (64)^{1/3} = 4$

Answer: 4

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 1)

The distance of the two planets A and B from the sun are $r_A$ and $r_B$ respectively. Also $r_B$ is equal to $100 r_A$. If the orbital speed of the planet $A$ is ' $v$ ' then the orbital speed of the planet $B$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{v}{10}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{v}{2}$.
- **(C)** $\sqrt{2} \mathrm{v}$
- **(D)** 10 v

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{v}{10}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The orbital speed of a planet revolving around the sun is given by $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$

Since $G$ and $M$ are constants, $v \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}}$

$\dfrac{v_B}{v_A} = \sqrt{\dfrac{r_A}{r_B}}$

Given $r_B = 100 r_A$ and $v_A = v$

$\dfrac{v_B}{v} = \sqrt{\dfrac{r_A}{100 r_A}} = \dfrac{1}{10}$

$v_B = \dfrac{v}{10}$

Answer: $\dfrac{v}{10}$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two satellites of earth, $S_{1}$ and $S_{2}$, are moving in the same orbit. The mass of $S_{1}$ is four times the mass of $S_{2}$. Which one of the following statements is true ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The time period of $S_{1}$ is four times that of $S_{2}$
- **(B)** The potential energies of earth and satellite in the two cases are equal
- **(C)** $S_{1}$ and $S_{2}$ are moving with the same speed
- **(D)** The kinetic energies of the two satellites are equal

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $S_{1}$ and $S_{2}$ are moving with the same speed**

**Detailed Solution:**

When two satellites of earth are moving in same orbit, then time period of both are equal. From Kepler's third law
$$
T^{2} \propto r^{3}
$$
Time period is independent of mass, hence their time periods will be equal. The potential energy and kinetic energy are mass dependent, hence the PE and $\mathrm{KE}$ of satellites are not equal. But, if they are orbiting in a same orbit, then they have equal orbital speed.

</details>
