### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The geostationary satellite is revolving round the earth of mean radius $R$. If $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity at earth's surface, $\omega$ is the angular speed of rotation of earth about its own axis and $r$ is the radius of orbit of a satellite, then the correct relation between $r$, $\omega$, $g$ and $R$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $r^2 = \dfrac{gR^2}{w^3}$
- **(B)** $R^2 = \dfrac{r^3 w^2}{g}$
- **(C)** $R^3 = \dfrac{r^2 w}{g^2}$
- **(D)** $R = \sqrt{\dfrac{rw^3}{g}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $R^2 = \dfrac{r^3 w^2}{g}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a geostationary satellite, the angular speed of revolution is equal to the angular speed of rotation of the earth about its own axis, $w$.

The necessary centripetal force for the circular orbit is provided by the gravitational force of the earth.

$\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = m r w^2$

$\Rightarrow GM = r^3 w^2$

The acceleration due to gravity at the surface of the earth is given by:

$g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$

$\Rightarrow GM = g R^2$

Equating the two expressions for $GM$:

$g R^2 = r^3 w^2$

$\Rightarrow R^2 = \dfrac{r^3 w^2}{g}$

Answer: $R^2 = \dfrac{r^3 w^2}{g}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

The kinetic energy of a revolving satellite (mass $m$) at a height equal to thrice the radius of the earth ($R$) is ($g$ = acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{mgR}{8}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{mgR}{16}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{mgR}{2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{mgR}{4}$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

The orbital velocity of the satellite is $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$.

Using the relation $g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$, we get $GM = gR^2$.

Substituting $GM = gR^2$ and $r = 4R$ into the expression for kinetic energy:

$K = \dfrac{gR^2 m}{2(4R)} = \dfrac{mgR}{8}$

Answer: $\dfrac{mgR}{8}$

$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4\pi G \rho}}$

$T = \sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi^2 \times 3}{4\pi G \rho}}$

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The total energy of a circularly orbiting satellite is

**Options:**

- **(A)** half the kinetic energy of the satellite.
- **(B)** half the potential energy of the satellite.
- **(C)** twice the kinetic energy of the satellite.
- **(D)** twice the potential energy of the satellite.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) half the potential energy of the satellite.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The kinetic energy of a satellite of mass $m$ orbiting a planet of mass $M$ in a circular orbit of radius $r$ is given by $K = \dfrac{GMm}{2r}$.

The potential energy of the satellite is $U = -\dfrac{GMm}{r}$.

The total energy of the satellite is the sum of its kinetic and potential energies:

$E = K + U = \dfrac{GMm}{2r} - \dfrac{GMm}{r} = -\dfrac{GMm}{2r}$

Comparing the expressions for total energy and potential energy, we get:

$E = \dfrac{1}{2} \left( -\dfrac{GMm}{r} \right) = \dfrac{U}{2}$

Thus, the total energy of a circularly orbiting satellite is half of its potential energy.

Answer: half the potential energy of the satellite.

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two particles of equal mass ' m ' move in a circle of radius ' r ' under the action of their mutual gravitational attraction. The speed of each particle will be ( $\mathrm{G}=$ Universal gravitational constant)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\frac{G m}{4 r}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\frac{G m}{r}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\frac{G m}{2 r}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\frac{4 \mathrm{Gm}}{\mathrm{r}}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\sqrt{\frac{G m}{4 r}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Two particles of mass $m$ are moving in a circle of radius $r$.

The distance between the two particles is the diameter of the circle, which is $2r$.

The gravitational force of attraction between them provides the necessary centripetal force for circular motion.

Gravitational force $F = \dfrac{G m m}{(2r)^2} = \dfrac{G m^2}{4r^2}$

Centripetal force $F_c = \dfrac{m v^2}{r}$

Equating the two forces:

$\dfrac{m v^2}{r} = \dfrac{G m^2}{4r^2}$

$v^2 = \dfrac{G m}{4r}$

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{G m}{4r}}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\frac{G m}{4 r}}$

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The minimum energy required to launch a satellite of mass $m$ from the surface of a planet of mass M and radius R in a circular orbit at an altitude of $2 R$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{5 \mathrm{GMm}}{6 \mathrm{R}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2 \mathrm{GMm}}{3 \mathrm{R}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{2 \mathrm{R}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{3 \mathrm{R}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{5 \mathrm{GMm}}{6 \mathrm{R}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial total energy of the satellite on the surface of the planet is:

$E_i = -\dfrac{GMm}{R}$

The satellite is launched into a circular orbit at an altitude $h = 2R$. The orbital radius is:

$r = R + h = R + 2R = 3R$

The final total energy of the satellite in this circular orbit is:

$E_f = -\dfrac{GMm}{2r} = -\dfrac{GMm}{2(3R)} = -\dfrac{GMm}{6R}$

The minimum energy required to launch the satellite is the difference between its final and initial total energies:

$\Delta E = E_f - E_i$

$\Delta E = -\dfrac{GMm}{6R} - \left(-\dfrac{GMm}{R}\right)$

$\Delta E = \dfrac{GMm}{R} - \dfrac{GMm}{6R} = \dfrac{5GMm}{6R}$

Answer: $\frac{5 \mathrm{GMm}}{6 \mathrm{R}}$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The gravitational potential energy required to raise a satellite of mass ' $m$ ' to height ' $h$ ' above the earth's surface is ' $E_1$ '. Let the energy required to put this satellite into the orbit at the same height be ' $E_2$ '. If $M$ and $R$ are the mass and radius of the earth respectively then $E_1: E_2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{h}: \mathrm{R}$
- **(B)** $h: 2 R$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{R}: \mathrm{h}$
- **(D)** $2 \mathrm{~h}: \mathrm{R}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2 \mathrm{~h}: \mathrm{R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The gravitational potential energy required to raise the satellite to a height $h$ is the change in its potential energy:

$E_1 = \Delta U = U_f - U_i$

$E_1 = -\dfrac{GMm}{R+h} - \left(-\dfrac{GMm}{R}\right)$

The energy required to put the satellite into orbit at this height is the kinetic energy needed for orbital motion:

$E_2 = \dfrac{1}{2} m v_o^2$

$E_2 = \dfrac{1}{2} m \left(\dfrac{GM}{R+h}\right) = \dfrac{GMm}{2(R+h)}$

Taking the ratio of $E_1$ to $E_2$:

$\dfrac{E_1}{E_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{GMmh}{R(R+h)}}{\dfrac{GMm}{2(R+h)}} = \dfrac{2h}{R}$
