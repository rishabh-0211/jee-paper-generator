### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Select the correct statement out of the following

**Options:**

- **(A)** Acceleration due to gravity decreases as we go up or down from earth's surface.
- **(B)** Acceleration due to gravity is same at a height 'h' and depth 'd' from earth's surface, if $h = d$.
- **(C)** Acceleration due to gravity at 640 km above the earth surface decreases by 10% of its value at earth's surface (earth's radius is 6400 km)
- **(D)** There is reduction in acceleration due to gravity at poles due to rotation of earth as the poles are lying on the axis of rotation and do not revolve.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Acceleration due to gravity decreases as we go up or down from earth's surface.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The acceleration due to gravity at a height $h$ above the earth's surface is given by $g_h = g\left(1 + \dfrac{h}{R}\right)^{-2}$. This shows that $g_h < g$ for any $h > 0$.

The acceleration due to gravity at a depth $d$ below the earth's surface is given by $g_d = g\left(1 - \dfrac{d}{R}\right)$. This shows that $g_d < g$ for any $d > 0$.

Thus, acceleration due to gravity decreases as we go up or down from the earth's surface.

For $h = d$ (small values), $g_h \approx g\left(1 - \dfrac{2h}{R}\right)$ and $g_d = g\left(1 - \dfrac{h}{R}\right)$, so $g_h \neq g_d$.

At $h = 640$ km, $g_h = g\left(1 + \dfrac{640}{6400}\right)^{-2} = g\left(\dfrac{11}{10}\right)^{-2} = \dfrac{100}{121}g \approx 0.826g$, which is a decrease of about $17.4\%$, not $10\%$.

At the poles, the latitude $\lambda = 90^{\circ}$. The effective gravity is $g' = g - R\omega^2 \cos^2 \lambda = g - 0 = g$. There is no reduction in gravity at the poles due to the earth's rotation.

Hence, only the first statement is correct.

Answer: Acceleration due to gravity decreases as we go up or down from earth's surface.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The percentage decrease in the weight of a body when taken to a height of $48$ km above the surface of the earth is (Radius of the earth is $6400$ km)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1\%$
- **(B)** $1.5\%$
- **(C)** $2\%$
- **(D)** $2.5\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.5\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The acceleration due to gravity at a height $h$ above the surface of the earth, where $h \ll R$, is given by

$g' = g \left(1 - \dfrac{2h}{R}\right)$

The fractional decrease in weight is

$\dfrac{\Delta W}{W} = \dfrac{g - g'}{g} = \dfrac{2h}{R}$

The percentage decrease in weight is

$\dfrac{\Delta W}{W} \times 100 = \dfrac{2h}{R} \times 100$

Substituting $h = 48$ km and $R = 6400$ km:

Percentage decrease $= \dfrac{2 \times 48}{6400} \times 100$

Percentage decrease $= \dfrac{96}{64} = 1.5\%$

Answer: $1.5\%$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The lengths of seconds pendulums on the surface of the earth and at an altitude '$h$' from the surface of the earth are $l_s$ and $l_h$ respectively. The radius of the earth is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{h\sqrt{l_h}}{\sqrt{l_s}-\sqrt{l_h}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{h\sqrt{l_h}}{\sqrt{l_h}-\sqrt{l_s}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{l_h}}{h(\sqrt{l_s}-\sqrt{l_h})}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{l_s}}{h(\sqrt{l_h}-\sqrt{l_s})}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{h\sqrt{l_h}}{\sqrt{l_s}-\sqrt{l_h}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of a simple pendulum is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$.

For a seconds pendulum, the time period is constant ($T = 2$ s) at both locations.

Therefore, $T_s = T_h \Rightarrow 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{l_s}{g_s}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{l_h}{g_h}}$.

This gives $\dfrac{l_s}{l_h} = \dfrac{g_s}{g_h}$.

The acceleration due to gravity on the surface of the earth is $g_s = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$ and at an altitude $h$ is $g_h = \dfrac{GM}{(R+h)^2}$.

Substituting these values, we get:

$\dfrac{l_s}{l_h} = \dfrac{\dfrac{GM}{R^2}}{\dfrac{GM}{(R+h)^2}} = \dfrac{(R+h)^2}{R^2} = \left(1 + \dfrac{h}{R}\right)^2$

Taking the square root on both sides:

$\sqrt{\dfrac{l_s}{l_h}} = 1 + \dfrac{h}{R}$

$\dfrac{h}{R} = \dfrac{\sqrt{l_s}}{\sqrt{l_h}} - 1 = \dfrac{\sqrt{l_s} - \sqrt{l_h}}{\sqrt{l_h}}$

$R = \dfrac{h\sqrt{l_h}}{\sqrt{l_s} - \sqrt{l_h}}$

Answer: $\dfrac{h\sqrt{l_h}}{\sqrt{l_s}-\sqrt{l_h}}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

Time period of a simple pendulum is $T_1$ when on the earth's surface and $T_2$ when taken to a height '2R' above the earth's surface, where 'R' is the radius of the earth. The ratio $T_1 : T_2$ is

- **(B)** $1 : 3$
- **(C)** $1 : 4$
- **(D)** $1 : 5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1 : 3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of a simple pendulum is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$.

Substituting this into the time period formula gives $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L r^2}{GM}}$, which implies $T \propto r$.

On the surface of the earth, the distance from the center is $r_1 = R$, so $T_1 \propto R$.

At a height $h = 2R$ above the surface, the distance from the center is $r_2 = R + 2R = 3R$, so $T_2 \propto 3R$.

The ratio of the time periods is $\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = \dfrac{R}{3R} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

Thus, $T_1 : T_2 = 1 : 3$.

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{5R}}$**

$mg' = \dfrac{3}{5}mg$

$g - R\omega^2 = \dfrac{3}{5}g$

$R\omega^2 = g - \dfrac{3}{5}g$

$R\omega^2 = \dfrac{2}{5}g$

$\omega^2 = \dfrac{2g}{5R}$

$\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{5R}}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\dfrac{2g}{5R}}$

### Q61. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Ionization potential of hydrogen atom is $13.6 \mathrm{eV}$. Hydrogen atoms in the ground state are excited by monochromatic radiation of photon energy $12.1 \mathrm{eV} .$ According to Bohr's theory, the spectral lines emitted by hydrogen will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** two
- **(B)** three
- **(C)** four
- **(D)** one

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) three**

**Detailed Solution:**

Ionization energy corresponding to ionization potential $=-13.6 \mathrm{eV}$
Photon energy incident $=12.1 \mathrm{eV}$
So, the energy of electron in excited state
$$
=-13.6+12.1=-1.5 \mathrm{eV}
$$
$$
\begin{array}{lc}
\text { ie, } \quad E_{n}=-\frac{13.6}{n^{2}} \mathrm{eV} \\
-1.5=\frac{-13.6}{n^{2}} \\
\Rightarrow \quad n^{2}=\frac{-13.6}{-1.5} \approx 9 \\
\therefore \quad n=3
\end{array}
$$
ie, energy of electron in excited state corresponds to third orbit. The possible spectral lines are when electron jumps from orbit 3rd to 2nd; 3rd to 1 st and 2nd to 1 st. Thus, 3 spectral lines are emitted.

</details>
