### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Figure shows a composite system of two uniform rods of length as shown. The co-ordinates of the centre of mass of the system of rods are
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/13_may_shift_2__q4_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\dfrac{L}{2}, \dfrac{2L}{3}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(\dfrac{L}{4}, \dfrac{2L}{3}\right)$
- **(C)** $\left(\dfrac{L}{6}, \dfrac{2L}{3}\right)$
- **(D)** $\left(\dfrac{L}{6}, \dfrac{L}{3}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\left(\dfrac{L}{6}, \dfrac{2L}{3}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the linear mass density of the uniform rods be $\lambda$.

For the rod along the y-axis:
Mass $m_1 = 2\lambda L$
Center of mass $(x_1, y_1) = (0, L)$

For the rod along the x-axis:
Mass $m_2 = \lambda L$
Center of mass $(x_2, y_2) = \left(\dfrac{L}{2}, 0\right)$

The x-coordinate of the center of mass of the system is:
$X_{cm} = \dfrac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2}{m_1 + m_2}$
$X_{cm} = \dfrac{(2\lambda L)(0) + (\lambda L)\left(\dfrac{L}{2}\right)}{2\lambda L + \lambda L} = \dfrac{\dfrac{\lambda L^2}{2}}{3\lambda L} = \dfrac{L}{6}$

The y-coordinate of the center of mass of the system is:
$Y_{cm} = \dfrac{m_1 y_1 + m_2 y_2}{m_1 + m_2}$
$Y_{cm} = \dfrac{(2\lambda L)(L) + (\lambda L)(0)}{2\lambda L + \lambda L} = \dfrac{2\lambda L^2}{3\lambda L} = \dfrac{2L}{3}$

The coordinates of the center of mass are $\left(\dfrac{L}{6}, \dfrac{2L}{3}\right)$.

Answer: $\left(\dfrac{L}{6}, \dfrac{2L}{3}\right)$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2023 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If ' $\mathrm{I}$ ' is moment of inertia of a thin circular disc about an axis passing through the tangent of the disc and in the plane of disc. The moment of inertia of same circular disc about an axis perpendicular to plane and passing through its centre is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{4 \mathrm{I}}{5}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2 I}{5}$
- **(C)** $\frac{4 \mathrm{I}}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 I}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{2 I}{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

M.I. of thin circular disc through the tangent in the plane of the disc is $I=\frac{5}{4} \mathrm{MR}^2$
$\Rightarrow \mathrm{MR}^2=\frac{4}{5} \mathrm{I}$
$\therefore \quad$ M.I. of thin circular disc about an axis perpendicular to plane and passing through its
$\text { centre }=\frac{M^2}{2}=\frac{\left(\frac{4}{5} \mathrm{~L}\right)}{2}=\frac{2 \mathrm{I}}{5}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A disc of radius $R$ and thickness $\frac{R}{6}$ has moment of inertia I about an axis passing through its centre and perpendicular to its plane. Disc is melted and recast into a solid sphere. The moment of inertia of a sphere about its diameter is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{I}}{5}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{I}}{6}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{I}}{32}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{I}}{64}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{\mathrm{I}}{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

M.I. of disc, $I=\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{MR}_{\mathrm{d}}^2$... (i)
M.I. of sphere, $I_{\text {sphere }}=\frac{2}{5} \mathrm{MR}_{\mathrm{s}}^2$... (ii)
$\because \quad$ volume of disc $=$ volume of sphere
$\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore & \pi \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{d}}^2\left(\frac{\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{d}}}{6}\right)=\frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{s}}^3 \\
\therefore & \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{d}}^3=8 \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{s}}^3 \\
\therefore & \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{S}}=\frac{\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{d}}}{2} ... (iii)
\end{array}$
Substitute equation (iii) in equation (ii)
$\begin{aligned}
\therefore \quad \mathrm{I}_{\text {sphere }} & =\frac{2}{5} \mathrm{M}\left(\frac{\mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{d}}}{2}\right)^2=\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{1}{4} \mathrm{MR}_{\mathrm{d}}^2 \\
& =\frac{1}{5}\left(\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{MR}_{\mathrm{d}}^2\right)=\frac{\mathrm{I}}{5}....[from (i)]
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A thin rod of length ' $L$ ' is bent in the form of a circle. Its mass is ' $M$ '. What force will act on mass ' $\mathrm{m}$ ' placed at the centre of this circle?
$(\mathrm{G}=\text { constant of gravitation) }$

**Options:**

- **(A)** zero
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{GMm}}{4 \mathrm{~L}^2 \pi^2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{4 \pi^2 \mathrm{GMm}}{\mathrm{L}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 \mathrm{GMm}}{\mathrm{L}^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) zero**

**Detailed Solution:**

Consider two diametrically and equal small and equal mass segments $\mathrm{dM}_1$ and $\mathrm{dM}_2$
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/mht_cet/btu3-AmMq0pB77MIGCBor-2k5gs8TlkoT_d2r5EV0nc.original.fullsize.png">

$\therefore \quad$ Force at the centre due to $\mathrm{dM}_1$ is
$\mathrm{F}_1=\frac{\mathrm{GmdM}_1}{\mathrm{r}^2}$
Similarly,
Force at the centre due to $\mathrm{dM}_2$ is
$\mathrm{F}_2=\frac{\mathrm{GmdM}_2}{\mathrm{r}^2}$
But $\mathrm{F}_1=-\mathrm{F}_2$
$\Rightarrow \mathrm{F}_1+\mathrm{F}_2=0 \quad(\because$ the forces cancel each other out as they are equal and opposite)
If this process is done for all such dM segments, we find the net force at the centre of the circle to be zero.

</details>
