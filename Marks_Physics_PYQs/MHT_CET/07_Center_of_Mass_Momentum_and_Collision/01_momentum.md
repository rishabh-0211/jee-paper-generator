### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The kinetic energy of light body and a heavy body is same. Which one of the following statement is true?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Both bodies have same momentum
- **(B)** Heavy body has greater momentum
- **(C)** Lighter body has greater momentum
- **(D)** A body having high velocity has greater momentum

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Heavy body has greater momentum**

**Detailed Solution:**

The kinetic energy $K$ and momentum $p$ of a body of mass $m$ are related by the equation $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$.

Rearranging this, we get $p = \sqrt{2mK}$.

Given that the kinetic energy $K$ is the same for both bodies, the momentum is directly proportional to the square root of the mass, i.e., $p \propto \sqrt{m}$.

Since the heavy body has a larger mass $m$, it will have a greater momentum.

Answer: Heavy body has greater momentum

</details>

If 'm' is the mass of the body moving with velocity 'v' having kinetic energy 'E', then the linear momentum of the body is

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A $6 \text{ kg}$ mass and $1.5 \text{ kg}$ mass are moving with equal kinetic energies. The ratio of the magnitudes of their linear momenta is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4:1$
- **(B)** $2:1$
- **(C)** $1:1$
- **(D)** $1:3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The kinetic energy $K$ and linear momentum $p$ of a body of mass $m$ are related by the equation $K = \dfrac{p^2}{2m}$.

This gives $p = \sqrt{2mK}$.

Given that the kinetic energies are equal, $K_1 = K_2 = K$.

The ratio of the magnitudes of their linear momenta is:

$\dfrac{p_1}{p_2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2m_1K}}{\sqrt{2m_2K}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_1}{m_2}}$

Substituting $m_1 = 6 \text{ kg}$ and $m_2 = 1.5 \text{ kg}$:

$\dfrac{p_1}{p_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{6}{1.5}} = \sqrt{4} = \dfrac{2}{1}$

The ratio is $2:1$.

Answer: $2:1$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

$100$ balls each of mass $m$ moving with speed $v$ simultaneously strike a wall normally and reflected back with same speed, in time $t$ s. The total magnitude of force exerted by the balls on the wall is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $100mv/t$
- **(B)** $200mv/t$
- **(C)** $200mvt$
- **(D)** $mv/100t$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $200mv/t$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial momentum of one ball is $p_i = mv$.

Final momentum of one ball after reflection is $p_f = -mv$.

Change in momentum of one ball is $\Delta p = p_f - p_i = -mv - mv = -2mv$.

Magnitude of change in momentum for one ball is $2mv$.

Total change in momentum for $100$ balls is $100 \times 2mv = 200mv$.

The total magnitude of force exerted on the wall is the rate of change of momentum, given by $F = \dfrac{\Delta p_{\text{total}}}{t} = \dfrac{200mv}{t}$.

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A stationary body explodes into two parts of masses $M_1$ and $M_2$. They move in opposite directions with velocities $V_1$ and $V_2$. The ratio of their kinetic energies is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{M_1}{M_2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{M_2}{M_1}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{M_1^2}{M_2^2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{M_2^2}{M_1^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{M_2}{M_1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By the law of conservation of linear momentum, the initial momentum of the stationary body is zero, so the final momentum of the system must also be zero.

$M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2$

The kinetic energy of a body in terms of its momentum $P$ is given by $K = \dfrac{P^2}{2M}$.

Since both parts have the same magnitude of momentum $P$, the ratio of their kinetic energies is:

$\dfrac{K_1}{K_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{P^2}{2M_1}}{\dfrac{P^2}{2M_2}} = \dfrac{M_2}{M_1}$

Answer: $\dfrac{M_2}{M_1}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A moving body with mass ' $\mathrm{m}_1$ ' elastically strikes a stationary mass ' $m_2$ '. What should be the ratio $\frac{m_1}{m_2}$ so as to decrease the velocity of the first by a factor of 1.5 after the collision?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 25$
- **(B)** $1: 5$
- **(C)** $5: 1$.
- **(D)** $25: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5: 1$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial velocity of the first body be $u$.

Since the collision is elastic and the second body is stationary, the final velocity of the first body is given by the formula:

$v_1 = \left( \dfrac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} \right) u$

According to the given condition, the velocity of the first body decreases by a factor of $1.5$. This means the new velocity is:

$v_1 = \dfrac{u}{1.5} = \dfrac{2u}{3}$

Equating the two expressions for $v_1$:

$\dfrac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

Cross-multiplying to solve for the ratio of the masses:

$3(m_1 - m_2) = 2(m_1 + m_2)$

$3m_1 - 3m_2 = 2m_1 + 2m_2$

$3m_1 - 2m_1 = 2m_2 + 3m_2$

$m_1 = 5m_2$

$\dfrac{m_1}{m_2} = \dfrac{5}{1}$

Thus, the required ratio is $5:1$.

Answer: $5: 1$.

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In projectile motion two particles of masses $\mathrm{m}_1$ and $m_2$ have velocities $\vec{V}_1$, and $\vec{V}_2$ respectively at time $\mathrm{t}=0$. Their velocities become $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}_1{ }^{\prime}}$ and $\overrightarrow{\mathrm{V}_2{ }^{\prime}}$ at time 2 t while still moving in air. The value of $\left[\left(m_1 \overrightarrow{V_1{ }^{\prime}}+m_2 \overrightarrow{V_2{ }^{\prime}}\right)-\left(m_1 \overrightarrow{V_1}+m_2 \overrightarrow{V_2}\right)\right]$ is ( $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** zero
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{~m}_1+\mathrm{m}_2\right) \mathrm{gt}$
- **(C)** $\left(\mathrm{m}_1+\mathrm{m}_2\right) \mathrm{gt}$
- **(D)** $2\left(m_1+m_2\right) g t$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2\left(m_1+m_2\right) g t$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given expression represents the change in the total momentum of the system of two particles.

The initial momentum of the system at time $t = 0$ is $\vec{P}_i = m_1 \vec{V}_1 + m_2 \vec{V}_2$.

The final momentum of the system at time $2t$ is $\vec{P}_f = m_1 \vec{V}_1' + m_2 \vec{V}_2'$.

The change in momentum is $\Delta \vec{P} = \vec{P}_f - \vec{P}_i$.

The time interval is $\Delta t = 2t - 0 = 2t$.

Therefore, the change in momentum is $\Delta \vec{P} = \vec{F}_{ext} \Delta t = (m_1 + m_2)\vec{g}(2t) = 2(m_1 + m_2)\vec{g}t$.

The magnitude of this change is $2(m_1 + m_2)gt$.

Answer: $2\left(m_1+m_2\right) g t$

</details>
