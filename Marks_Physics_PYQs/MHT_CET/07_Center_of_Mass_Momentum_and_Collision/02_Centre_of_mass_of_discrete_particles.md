### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two particles of masses $2$ g and $4$ g are situated at the opposite ends, A and B of a wooden bar respectively. Let $l(AB) = 9$ cm. The center of mass of the system will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6$ cm from B.
- **(B)** $3$ cm from A.
- **(C)** $2$ cm from B.
- **(D)** $6$ cm from A.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $6$ cm from A.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the position of particle A be at the origin, so $x_A = 0$.

The position of particle B is $x_B = 9$ cm.

The masses are $m_A = 2$ g and $m_B = 4$ g.

The position of the center of mass from A is given by:

$x_{cm} = \dfrac{m_A x_A + m_B x_B}{m_A + m_B}$

$x_{cm} = \dfrac{2 \times 0 + 4 \times 9}{2 + 4}$

$x_{cm} = \dfrac{36}{6} = 6$ cm.

Thus, the center of mass is at a distance of $6$ cm from A.

Answer: $6$ cm from A.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (5 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\frac{a}{3}, \frac{a}{3}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(a, \frac{a}{2}\right)$
- **(C)** $\left(2 a, \frac{a}{2}\right)$
- **(D)** $\left(\frac{2 a}{3}, \frac{a}{3}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\left(\frac{2 a}{3}, \frac{a}{3}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of each rod be $m$.

The center of mass of a uniform rod lies at its midpoint.

For the rod along the x-axis (from $(0,0)$ to $(2a,0)$), the center of mass is $C_1 = (a, 0)$.

For the rod along the y-axis (from $(0,0)$ to $(0,a)$), the center of mass is $C_2 = \left(0, \dfrac{a}{2}\right)$.

For the rod forming the hypotenuse (from $(2a,0)$ to $(0,a)$), the center of mass is $C_3 = \left(\dfrac{2a+0}{2}, \dfrac{0+a}{2}\right) = \left(a, \dfrac{a}{2}\right)$.

The x-coordinate of the center of mass of the system is:

$X_{cm} = \dfrac{m x_1 + m x_2 + m x_3}{m + m + m} = \dfrac{m(a) + m(0) + m(a)}{3m} = \dfrac{2a}{3}$

$Y_{cm} = \dfrac{m y_1 + m y_2 + m y_3}{m + m + m} = \dfrac{m(0) + m\left(\dfrac{a}{2}\right) + m\left(\dfrac{a}{2}\right)}{3m} = \dfrac{a}{3}$

Therefore, the coordinates of the center of mass of the system are $\left(\dfrac{2a}{3}, \dfrac{a}{3}\right)$.

Answer: $\left(\frac{2 a}{3}, \frac{a}{3}\right)$

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Three identical metal balls each of radius ' $r$ ' are placed such that an equilateral triangle is formed when centres of three ball are joined. The centre of mass of the system is located at

**Options:**

- **(A)** centre of one of the balls.
- **(B)** point of intersection of medians.
- **(C)** line joining centres of any two balls.
- **(D)** on the circumference of any one of the balls.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) point of intersection of medians.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of each identical metal ball be $m$.

The centres of the three balls are located at the vertices of an equilateral triangle.

For a system of three equal masses placed at the vertices of a triangle, the centre of mass is given by the average of their position vectors.

$\vec{r}_{cm} = \dfrac{\vec{r}_1 + \vec{r}_2 + \vec{r}_3}{3}$

This position corresponds to the centroid of the triangle formed by the centres of the balls.

The centroid of a triangle is the point of intersection of its medians.

Thus, the centre of mass of the system is located at the point of intersection of the medians.

Answer: point of intersection of medians.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In case of system of two-particles of different masses, the centre of mass lies

**Options:**

- **(A)** at the mid-point of line joining the two particles.
- **(B)** on the line joining the two particles.
- **(C)** at one end of the line joining the two particles.
- **(D)** on the line perpendicular to the line joining the two particles.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) on the line joining the two particles.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The position vector of the centre of mass of a two-particle system is given by $\vec{r}_{cm} = \dfrac{m_1 \vec{r}_1 + m_2 \vec{r}_2}{m_1 + m_2}$.

This equation represents a point that divides the line segment joining the two particles in the ratio $m_2 : m_1$ internally.

Therefore, the centre of mass always lies on the line joining the two particles.

Since the masses are different ($m_1 \neq m_2$), the centre of mass will not be at the mid-point, but it will be closer to the heavier particle.

Answer: on the line joining the two particles.

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A metal rod of weight ' $W$ ' is supported by two parallel knife-edges $A$ and $B$. The rod is. in equilibrium in horizontal position. The distance between two knife-edges is ' $r$ '. The centre of mass of the rod is at a distance ' $x$ ' from A. The normal reaction on A is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{W} \cdot \mathrm{r}}{\mathrm{x}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{W} \cdot \mathrm{x}}{\mathrm{r}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{W \cdot(r-x)}{x}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{W} \cdot(\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{x})}{\mathrm{r}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{\mathrm{W} \cdot(\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{x})}{\mathrm{r}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $N_A$ and $N_B$ be the normal reactions at knife-edges A and B respectively.

For translational equilibrium, the net vertical force is zero:
$N_A + N_B = W$

For rotational equilibrium, the net torque about any point is zero. Taking torque about knife-edge B:
$N_A \times r - W \times (r - x) = 0$

$N_A \times r = W(r - x)$

$N_A = \dfrac{W(r - x)}{r}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\mathrm{W} \cdot(\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{x})}{\mathrm{r}}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the system of two particles of masses ' $\mathrm{m}_1$ ' and ' $\mathrm{m}_2$ ', the first particle is moved by a distance 'd' towards the centre of mass. To keep the centre of mass unchanged, the second particle will have to be moved by a distance

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{m}_2}{\mathrm{~m}_1} \mathrm{~d}$, towards the centre of mass.
- **(B)** $\dfrac{m_1}{m_2} d$, away from the centre of mass.
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2} \mathrm{~d}$, towards the centre of mass.
- **(D)** $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1} \mathrm{~d}$, away from the centre of mass.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2} \mathrm{~d}$, towards the centre of mass.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The position of the centre of mass of a two-particle system is given by

$X_{CM} = \dfrac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2}{m_1 + m_2}$

For the centre of mass to remain unchanged, the shift in the centre of mass must be zero, i.e., $\Delta X_{CM} = 0$.

$\Rightarrow \dfrac{m_1 \Delta x_1 + m_2 \Delta x_2}{m_1 + m_2} = 0$

$\Rightarrow m_1 \Delta x_1 + m_2 \Delta x_2 = 0$

Let the centre of mass be at the origin. The first particle is at a negative coordinate and the second is at a positive coordinate. When the first particle is moved towards the centre of mass by a distance $d$, its displacement is $\Delta x_1 = d$.

Substituting this into the equation:

$m_1 (d) + m_2 \Delta x_2 = 0$

$\Rightarrow \Delta x_2 = -\dfrac{m_1}{m_2} d$

The negative sign indicates that the displacement of the second particle is in the negative direction, which means it is also moving towards the centre of mass.

Therefore, the second particle must be moved by a distance $\dfrac{m_1}{m_2} d$ towards the centre of mass.

Answer: $\dfrac{\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2} \mathrm{~d}$, towards the centre of mass.

</details>
