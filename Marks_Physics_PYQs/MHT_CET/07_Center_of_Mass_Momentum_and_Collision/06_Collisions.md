### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A block of mass $m$ moving on a frictionless surface at speed $V$ collides elastically with a block of same mass, initially at rest. Now, the first block moves at an angle $\theta$ with its initial direction and has speed $V_1$. The speed of the second block after collision is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{V_1^2 - V^2}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{V^2 - V_1^2}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{V^2 + V_1^2}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{V - V_1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{V^2 - V_1^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since the collision is elastic, the kinetic energy of the system is conserved.

Initial kinetic energy of the system is:

$K_i = \dfrac{1}{2}mV^2$

Final kinetic energy of the system is:

$K_f = \dfrac{1}{2}mV_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}mV_2^2$

Equating initial and final kinetic energies:

$\dfrac{1}{2}mV^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}mV_1^2 + \dfrac{1}{2}mV_2^2$

$V^2 = V_1^2 + V_2^2$

$V_2^2 = V^2 - V_1^2$

$V_2 = \sqrt{V^2 - V_1^2}$

Answer: $\sqrt{V^2 - V_1^2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Three identical blocks A, B and C are placed on a horizontal frictionless surface. The blocks B and C are at rest. Block A is approaching towards B with a speed $10$ m/s. The coefficient of restitution for all collisions is $0.5$. The speed of block C just after collision is
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/14_may_shift_2__q42_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5.6$ m/s
- **(B)** $6$ m/s
- **(C)** $8$ m/s
- **(D)** $10$ m/s

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $5.6$ m/s**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of each identical block be $m$.

For the first collision between block A and block B:
Initial velocity of A, $u_A = 10$ m/s
Initial velocity of B, $u_B = 0$
Let their velocities after collision be $v_A$ and $v_B$.
By conservation of linear momentum:
$m u_A + m u_B = m v_A + m v_B$
$\Rightarrow v_A + v_B = 10$
Using the coefficient of restitution ($e = 0.5$):
$v_B - v_A = e(u_A - u_B) = 0.5 \times 10 = 5$
Adding the two equations:
$2v_B = 15 \Rightarrow v_B = 7.5$ m/s

For the second collision between block B and block C:
Initial velocity of B before this collision is $v_B = 7.5$ m/s
Initial velocity of C, $u_C = 0$
Let their velocities after collision be $v_B'$ and $v_C$.
By conservation of linear momentum:
$m v_B + m u_C = m v_B' + m v_C$
$\Rightarrow v_B' + v_C = 7.5$
Using the coefficient of restitution ($e = 0.5$):
$v_C - v_B' = e(v_B - u_C) = 0.5 \times 7.5 = 3.75$
Adding the two equations:
$2v_C = 7.5 + 3.75 = 11.25$
$\Rightarrow v_C = 5.625$ m/s

Rounding to one decimal place, the speed of block C is approximately $5.6$ m/s.

Answer: $5.6$ m/s

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A block of mass 'm' collides with another stationary block of mass '2m'. The lighter block comes to rest after collision. If the velocity of the first block is 'V' then the coefficient of restitution is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.5$
- **(B)** $0.4$
- **(C)** $0.6$
- **(D)** $0.8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $0.5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By conservation of linear momentum:

$m u_1 + 2m u_2 = m v_1 + 2m v_2$

Given $u_1 = V$, $u_2 = 0$, and $v_1 = 0$:

$m V + 0 = 0 + 2m v_2$

$v_2 = \dfrac{V}{2}$

The coefficient of restitution $e$ is given by the ratio of relative velocity of separation to relative velocity of approach:

$e = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2}$

$e = \dfrac{\dfrac{V}{2} - 0}{V - 0}$

$e = 0.5$

Answer: $0.5$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ moving with velocities $V_1$ and $V_2$ in opposite directions collide elastically and after collision $m_1$ and $m_2$ move with velocities $V_2$ and $V_1$ respectively. The ratio $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.25$
- **(B)** $0.50$
- **(C)** $0.75$
- **(D)** $1.0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1.0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By the principle of conservation of linear momentum:

$m_1 \vec{V}_1 + m_2 \vec{V}_2 = m_1 \vec{V}_1' + m_2 \vec{V}_2'$

Given that the final velocities are $\vec{V}_1' = \vec{V}_2$ and $\vec{V}_2' = \vec{V}_1$, we substitute these into the momentum equation:

$m_1 \vec{V}_1 + m_2 \vec{V}_2 = m_1 \vec{V}_2 + m_2 \vec{V}_1$

Rearranging the terms yields:

$m_1 (\vec{V}_1 - \vec{V}_2) = m_2 (\vec{V}_1 - \vec{V}_2)$

Since the masses are moving in opposite directions, $\vec{V}_1 \neq \vec{V}_2$, which implies $\vec{V}_1 - \vec{V}_2 \neq 0$.

Dividing both sides by $(\vec{V}_1 - \vec{V}_2)$ gives:

$m_1 = m_2$

Therefore, the ratio $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1} = 1.0$.

Note: In a perfectly elastic collision in one dimension, the velocities of the two bodies are exchanged if and only if their masses are equal.

Answer: $1.0$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A sphere of mass 'm', moving with velocity '3u' collides head-on with another identical sphere at rest. If ' e ' is coefficient of restitution then what will be the ratio of velocity of the second sphere to that of first sphere after collision?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1-\mathrm{e}}{1+\mathrm{e}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1+e}{1-e}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{e}+1}{\mathrm{e}-1}$
- **(D)** $\frac{e-1}{e+1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1+e}{1-e}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial velocity of the first sphere be $u_1 = 3u$ and that of the second sphere be $u_2 = 0$.

Let their final velocities after collision be $v_1$ and $v_2$.

By the principle of conservation of linear momentum:

$m u_1 + m u_2 = m v_1 + m v_2$

$3u + 0 = v_1 + v_2$

$v_1 + v_2 = 3u$  ... (i)

By the definition of the coefficient of restitution:

$e = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{u_1 - u_2}$

$e = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{3u - 0}$

$v_2 - v_1 = 3eu$  ... (ii)

Adding equations (i) and (ii), we get:

$2v_2 = 3u(1 + e)$

$v_2 = \dfrac{3u(1 + e)}{2}$

Subtracting equation (ii) from (i), we get:

$2v_1 = 3u(1 - e)$

$v_1 = \dfrac{3u(1 - e)}{2}$

The ratio of the velocity of the second sphere to that of the first sphere after collision is:

$\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = \dfrac{\dfrac{3u(1 + e)}{2}}{\dfrac{3u(1 - e)}{2}} = \dfrac{1 + e}{1 - e}$

Answer: $\frac{1+e}{1-e}$

</details>

- **(B)** $100 \mathrm{~J} .$
- **(C)** $140 \mathrm{~J} .$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $60 \mathrm{~J} .$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Apply conservation of momentum,
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\mathrm{m}_{1} \mathrm{v}_{1}=\left(\mathrm{m}_{1}+\mathrm{m}_{2}\right) \mathrm{v} \\
\mathrm{v}=\frac{\mathrm{m}_{1} \mathrm{v}_{1}}{\left(\mathrm{~m}_{1}+\mathrm{m}_{2}\right)}
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{l}

\end{array}

Loss in K.E. $=100-40=60 \mathrm{~J}$

$-\Delta \mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{k}}=\frac{1}{2} \frac{\mathrm{m}_{1} \mathrm{~m}_{2}}{\left(\mathrm{~m}_{1}+\mathrm{m}_{2}\right)}\left(\mathrm{u}_{1}-\mathrm{u}_{2}\right)^{2}$
