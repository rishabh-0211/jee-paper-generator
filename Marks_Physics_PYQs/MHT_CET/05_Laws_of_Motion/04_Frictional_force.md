### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

On a dry curved (banked) road, with banking angle of zero, the maximum safe speed for a moving vehicle is '$v$'. After heavy rain, the maximum safe speed for the same road was reduced by 60%. The ratio of the coefficient of friction for the dry road to that of the wet road is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $25:9$
- **(B)** $24:5$
- **(C)** $24:9$
- **(D)** $25:4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $25:4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The maximum safe speed on a curved road with zero banking angle is given by $v = \sqrt{\mu r g}$, where $\mu$ is the coefficient of friction, $r$ is the radius of the curve, and $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

For the dry road, the maximum safe speed is $v$, so the coefficient of friction $\mu_d$ is:

$\mu_d = \dfrac{v^2}{rg}$

After heavy rain, the maximum safe speed is reduced by $60\%$. The new maximum safe speed $v'$ on the wet road is:

$v' = v - 0.60v = 0.40v$

The coefficient of friction for the wet road $\mu_w$ is:

$\mu_w = \dfrac{v'^2}{rg} = \dfrac{(0.40v)^2}{rg} = \dfrac{0.16v^2}{rg}$

The ratio of the coefficient of friction for the dry road to that of the wet road is:

$\dfrac{\mu_d}{\mu_w} = \dfrac{\dfrac{v^2}{rg}}{\dfrac{0.16v^2}{rg}}$

$\dfrac{\mu_d}{\mu_w} = \dfrac{1}{0.16} = \dfrac{100}{16} = \dfrac{25}{4}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A motor cyclist has to rotate in horizontal circles inside the cylindrical wall of inner radius ' R ' metre. If the coefficient of friction between the wall and the tyres is ' $\mu_{\mathrm{s}}$ ', then the minimum speed required is ( $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\mu_{\mathrm{s}} \mathrm{Rg}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Rg}}{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}}{\mathrm{Rg}}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{R}^2 \mathrm{~g}}{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Rg}}{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a motorcyclist moving in a horizontal circle inside a cylindrical wall, the normal reaction $N$ provides the necessary centripetal force.

$N = \dfrac{mv^2}{R}$

The frictional force $f$ acts upwards to balance the weight $mg$ of the motorcyclist.

$f = mg$

To prevent sliding down, the frictional force must be less than or equal to the maximum static friction.

$f \le \mu_s N$

$mg \le \mu_s \left( \dfrac{mv^2}{R} \right)$

$v^2 \ge \dfrac{Rg}{\mu_s}$

The minimum speed required is given by:

$v_{\text{min}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{Rg}{\mu_s}}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Rg}}{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}}}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (14 May Shift 1)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{2 \mu}{3}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mu}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mu}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{3 \mu}{4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{\mu}{4}$**

The equation for maximum velocity is $\mathrm{V}=\sqrt{\mu \mathrm{rg}}$

When the road becomes wet the equation becomes
$$
\frac{\mathrm{V}}{2}=\sqrt{\mu^{\prime} \mathrm{rg}}
$$

Dividing equation (i) with equation (ii),
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{V}} \\
& =\frac{\sqrt{\mu \mathrm{rg}}}{\sqrt{\mu^{\prime} \mathrm{rg}}} \\
\therefore \quad 2 & =\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{\sqrt{\mu^{\prime}}} \\
\therefore \quad \mu^{\prime} & =\frac{\mu}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
