### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A spring has length $l$ and force constant $k$. It is cut into 2 springs of length $l_1$ and $l_2$ such that $l_1 = nl_2$ ($n$=an integer). The force constant of the spring of length $l_2$ is $\left(k \propto \dfrac{1}{l}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $k(n+1)$
- **(B)** $(n+1)\dfrac{k}{n}$
- **(C)** $k$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{k}{(n+1)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $k(n+1)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $l_1 = n l_2$ and the total length of the spring is $l = l_1 + l_2$.

Substituting $l_1$, we get $l = n l_2 + l_2 = (n+1) l_2$.

The force constant of a spring is inversely proportional to its length, which means $k \propto \dfrac{1}{l}$ or $k l = \text{constant}$.

Let $k_2$ be the force constant of the spring of length $l_2$.

$k_2 l_2 = k l$

$k_2 = \dfrac{k l}{l_2}$

Substituting $l = (n+1) l_2$, we get:

$k_2 = \dfrac{k (n+1) l_2}{l_2} = k(n+1)$

Answer: $k(n+1)$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two massless springs of spring constants $K_1$ and $K_2$ are connected one after the other forming a single chain, suspended vertically and a certain mass is attached to the free end. If $x_1$ and $x_2$ are their respective extensions and 'F' is their stretching force, the total extension produced is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $F(K_1 + K_2)$
- **(B)** $F(1/K_1 - 1/K_2)$
- **(C)** $F(K_1 - K_2)$
- **(D)** $F(1/K_1 + 1/K_2)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $F(1/K_1 + 1/K_2)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When springs are connected in series, the same stretching force $F$ acts on each spring.

The extension in the first spring is $x_1 = \dfrac{F}{K_1}$

The extension in the second spring is $x_2 = \dfrac{F}{K_2}$

The total extension produced is $x = x_1 + x_2$

$x = \dfrac{F}{K_1} + \dfrac{F}{K_2} = F\left(\dfrac{1}{K_1} + \dfrac{1}{K_2}\right)$

Answer: $F(1/K_1 + 1/K_2)$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A body of mass 100 gram is tied to a spring of spring constant $8 \mathrm{~N} / \mathrm{m}$, while the other end of a spring is fixed. If the body moves in a circular path on smooth horizontal surface with constant angular speed $8 \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$ then the ratio of extension in the spring to its natured length will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1:1
- **(B)** 8:1
- **(C)** 2:1
- **(D)** $4: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $4: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the natural length of the spring be $L$ and the extension in the spring be $x$.

The radius of the circular path is $r = L + x$.

The centripetal force required for circular motion is provided by the restoring force of the spring.

$m \omega^2 r = kx$

$m \omega^2 (L + x) = kx$

Given $m = 100 \text{ g} = 0.1 \text{ kg}$, $k = 8 \text{ N/m}$, and $\omega = 8 \text{ rad/s}$. Substituting these values:

$0.1 \times (8)^2 \times (L + x) = 8x$

$0.1 \times 64 \times (L + x) = 8x$

$6.4(L + x) = 8x$

$6.4L + 6.4x = 8x$

$8x - 6.4x = 6.4L$

$1.6x = 6.4L$

$\dfrac{x}{L} = \dfrac{6.4}{1.6} = 4$

The ratio of extension in the spring to its natural length is $4:1$.

Answer: $4: 1$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A spring has length $L$ and force constant K . It is cut into two springs of length $L_1$ and $L_2$ such that $\mathrm{L}_1=\mathrm{NL}_2$ ( N is an integer). The force constant of spring of length $L_1$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(\mathrm{N}+1) \mathrm{K}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{K}}{\mathrm{N}}(1+\mathrm{N})$
- **(C)** K
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{K}}{\mathrm{N}+1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{\mathrm{K}}{\mathrm{N}}(1+\mathrm{N})$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The force constant of a spring is inversely proportional to its length, so $K \propto \dfrac{1}{L}$.

Let the original length of the spring be $L = L_1 + L_2$.

Given $L_1 = N L_2$, we have $L_2 = \dfrac{L_1}{N}$.

Substituting this into the equation for $L$, we get $L = L_1 + \dfrac{L_1}{N} = L_1 \left(\dfrac{N+1}{N}\right)$.

Since $K_1 L_1 = K L$, the force constant of the spring of length $L_1$ is $K_1 = K \dfrac{L}{L_1}$.

Substituting the value of $L$, we get $K_1 = K \dfrac{L_1 \left(\dfrac{N+1}{N}\right)}{L_1} = \dfrac{K}{N}(1+N)$.

Answer: $\frac{\mathrm{K}}{\mathrm{N}}(1+\mathrm{N})$

</details>
