### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The weight of man in lift moving in upward direction with an acceleration 'a' is $660$ N. When the lift moves in the downward direction with the same acceleration, his weight is found to be $380$ N. The real weight of the man when the lift is at rest is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $400$ N
- **(B)** $460$ N
- **(C)** $520$ N
- **(D)** $640$ N

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $520$ N**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the mass of the man be $m$ and the acceleration of the lift be $a$.

When the lift moves upwards with acceleration $a$, the apparent weight is given by:

$W_1 = m(g + a) = 660$

When the lift moves downwards with acceleration $a$, the apparent weight is given by:

$W_2 = m(g - a) = 380$

Adding the two equations, we get:

$m(g + a) + m(g - a) = 660 + 380$

$2mg = 1040$

$mg = 520$ N

The real weight of the man when the lift is at rest is $mg = 520$ N.

Answer: $520$ N

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A mass ' $M$ ' is suspended by a rope from a rigid support at point ' $P$ ' as shown in figure. Another rope is tied at end 'Q' and pulled horizontally with a force ' $F$ '. If the rope makes an angle ' $\theta$ ' with vertical then the tension in the string ' PQ ' is

<img width=250px src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/FUNWomNBp6T4Hc4v58Jq-hwutVW1EoUCK7V1LCKRm8E.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $F \sin \theta$
- **(B)** $\frac{F}{\sin \theta}$
- **(C)** $F \cos \theta$
- **(D)** $\frac{F}{\cos \theta}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{F}{\sin \theta}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Consider the equilibrium of point Q. The forces acting at point Q are:
1. Tension $T$ in the string PQ, acting at an angle $\theta$ with the vertical.
2. Horizontal force $F$ pulling to the right.
3. Downward force due to the suspended mass $M$, which is $Mg$.

Resolving the tension $T$ into horizontal and vertical components:
Horizontal component of tension = $T \sin \theta$ (acting towards the left)
Vertical component of tension = $T \cos \theta$ (acting upwards)

For the system to be in equilibrium, the net force in the horizontal direction must be zero. Therefore, the horizontal component of the tension must balance the applied horizontal force $F$:
$T \sin \theta = F$

Rearranging the equation to solve for $T$, we get:
$T = \dfrac{F}{\sin \theta}$

Answer: $\frac{F}{\sin \theta}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two bodies of mass 10 kg and 5 kg are moving in concentric circular orbits of radii ' R ' and ' r ' respectively such that their periods are same. The ratio between their centripetal acceleration is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{R} / \mathrm{r}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{r} / \mathrm{R}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{R}^2 / \mathrm{r}^2$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{r}^2 / \mathrm{R}^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{R} / \mathrm{r}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The centripetal acceleration of a body moving in a circular orbit of radius $r$ with angular velocity $\omega$ is given by $a = r\omega^2$.

Since the time periods of both bodies are the same ($T_1 = T_2 = T$), their angular velocities are also the same, as $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$.

For the first body of mass $10$ kg in an orbit of radius $R$, the centripetal acceleration is $a_1 = R\omega^2$.

For the second body of mass $5$ kg in an orbit of radius $r$, the centripetal acceleration is $a_2 = r\omega^2$.

The ratio of their centripetal accelerations is:

$\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{R\omega^2}{r\omega^2} = \dfrac{R}{r}$

Answer: $\mathrm{R} / \mathrm{r}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

The weight of man in a stationary lift is $\mathrm{w}_1$ and when it is moving downwards with uniform acceleration ' $a$ ' is $w_2$. If the ratio $\mathrm{w}_1: \mathrm{w}_2=4: 3$, then the value of ' a ' is ( $\mathrm{g}=$ acceleration due to gravity)

- **(B)** $\frac{g}{4}$

$\dfrac{g}{g - a} = \dfrac{4}{3}$

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The figure shows two masses ' m ' and ' M ' connected by a light string that passes through a small hole 'O' at the centre of the table. Mass ' m ' is moved round in a horizontal circle with 'O' as the centre. The frequency with which ' m ' should be revolved so that ' M ' remains stationary is
(g = gravitational acceleration)
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/fvMU_5Pv6QaSdAcMOMwSHwTyt6YPj8HmNB6p06XqvG0.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{\pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{ML}}{\mathrm{mg}}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Mg}}{\mathrm{mL}}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{\pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Mg}}{\mathrm{mL}}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{ML}}{\mathrm{mg}}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Mg}}{\mathrm{mL}}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the mass $M$ to remain stationary, the tension $T$ in the string must balance its weight:

$T = Mg$

For the mass $m$ revolving in a horizontal circle of radius $L$, the necessary centripetal force is provided by the tension $T$ in the string. If $\omega$ is the angular velocity of mass $m$, then:

$T = m \omega^2 L$

Equating the two expressions for tension, we get:

$Mg = m \omega^2 L$

Since angular velocity $\omega = 2 \pi f$, where $f$ is the frequency of revolution, we can substitute this into the equation:

$Mg = m (2 \pi f)^2 L$

$Mg = 4 \pi^2 m f^2 L$

Solving for $f^2$:

$f^2 = \dfrac{Mg}{4 \pi^2 mL}$

Taking the square root on both sides, we get the frequency:

$f = \dfrac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{Mg}{mL}}$

Answer: $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Mg}}{\mathrm{mL}}}$

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The mass of the lift is 200 kg , when it ascends with an acceleration of $4 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$ then the tension in the cable supporting the lift will be [Given: Acceleration due to gravity $\left.g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2\right]$

- **(A)** 800 N

- **(C)** 4200 N
- **(D)** 2000 N

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 2800 N**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $T$ be the tension in the cable supporting the lift.

The forces acting on the lift are the tension $T$ upwards and the weight $mg$ downwards.

$T - mg = ma$

$T = 200(10 + 4)$

$T = 200 \times 14$
