### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A block of mass $1$ kg is kept on ice surface. When velocity of $4$ m/s is given to it, it stops by friction in $5$ second. The coefficient of friction is (acceleration due to gravity, $g = 10$ m/s$^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.02$
- **(B)** $0.04$
- **(C)** $0.08$
- **(D)** $0.09$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.08$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the first equation of motion:

$v = u + at$

Substituting the given values $u = 4 \text{ m/s}$, $v = 0 \text{ m/s}$, and $t = 5 \text{ s}$:

$0 = 4 + a(5)$

$a = -0.8 \text{ m/s}^2$

The retardation is provided by the kinetic friction. Equating the frictional force to mass times acceleration:

$\mu mg = m|a|$

$\mu = \dfrac{|a|}{g}$

Substituting $g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2$:

$\mu = \dfrac{0.8}{10} = 0.08$

Answer: $0.08$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An inextensible string passing over a smooth pulley connects two blocks of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ ($m_2 > m_1$) vertically. If the acceleration of the system is $\left(\dfrac{g}{n}\right)$, then the ratio of masses $\left(\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}\right)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{n}{n+1}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{n}{n-1}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{n+1}{n-1}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{n+1}{n}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{n+1}{n-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For an Atwood machine with masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ ($m_2 > m_1$), the acceleration $a$ is given by:

$a = \dfrac{m_2 - m_1}{m_2 + m_1}g$

Given that $a = \dfrac{g}{n}$, we can write:

$\dfrac{m_2 - m_1}{m_2 + m_1}g = \dfrac{g}{n}$

$\dfrac{m_2 - m_1}{m_2 + m_1} = \dfrac{1}{n}$

Dividing the numerator and denominator of the left side by $m_1$:

$\dfrac{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1} - 1}{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1} + 1} = \dfrac{1}{n}$

Cross-multiplying yields:

$n\left(\dfrac{m_2}{m_1} - 1\right) = \dfrac{m_2}{m_1} + 1$

$n\dfrac{m_2}{m_1} - n = \dfrac{m_2}{m_1} + 1$

$\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}(n - 1) = n + 1$

$\dfrac{m_2}{m_1} = \dfrac{n + 1}{n - 1}$

Answer: $\dfrac{n+1}{n-1}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (5 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ball is dropped on the floor from a height of 20 m . It rebounds to a height of 5 m . Ball remains in contact with floor for 1 s . The average acceleration during contact is
(acceleration due to gravity $=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$
- **(B)** $20 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$
- **(C)** $40 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$
- **(D)** $35 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $30 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Velocity of the ball just before hitting the floor is given by $v_1 = \sqrt{2gh_1}$.

Substituting the given values, $v_1 = \sqrt{2 \times 10 \times 20} = 20 \text{ m/s}$ (downwards).

Velocity of the ball just after rebounding is given by $v_2 = \sqrt{2gh_2}$.

Substituting the values, $v_2 = \sqrt{2 \times 10 \times 5} = 10 \text{ m/s}$ (upwards).

Taking the upward direction as positive, the change in velocity is $\Delta v = v_2 - (-v_1) = 10 - (-20) = 30 \text{ m/s}$.

The average acceleration during contact is $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$.

Substituting $\Delta t = 1 \text{ s}$, we get $a = \dfrac{30}{1} = 30 \text{ m/s}^2$.

Answer: $30 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

A constant force acts on two different masses independently and produces accelerations $A_1$ and $A_2$. When the same force acts on their combined mass, the acceleration produced is

**Options:**

- **(B)** $A_1+A_2$
- **(C)** $\frac{A_1 A_2}{A_1+A_2}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\mathrm{A}_1^2+\mathrm{A}_2^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{A_1 A_2}{A_1+A_2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the constant force be $F$.

When the force acts on the first mass $m_1$, the acceleration is $A_1$.

$F = m_1 A_1 \Rightarrow m_1 = \dfrac{F}{A_1}$

When the force acts on the second mass $m_2$, the acceleration is $A_2$.

$F = m_2 A_2 \Rightarrow m_2 = \dfrac{F}{A_2}$

When the same force acts on the combined mass $M = m_1 + m_2$, the acceleration is $A$.

$A = \dfrac{F}{m_1 + m_2}$

Substituting the values of $m_1$ and $m_2$:

$A = \dfrac{F}{\dfrac{F}{A_1} + \dfrac{F}{A_2}}$

$A = \dfrac{F}{F \left( \dfrac{1}{A_1} + \dfrac{1}{A_2} \right)}$

$A = \dfrac{1}{\dfrac{A_1 + A_2}{A_1 A_2}}$

$A = \dfrac{A_1 A_2}{A_1 + A_2}$

Answer: $\frac{A_1 A_2}{A_1+A_2}$

### Q10. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A conveyor belt is moving with constant velocity (V). Sand is being dropped on the belt at the rate of $\mathrm{M} \mathrm{~kg} / \mathrm{s}$. The force necessary to keep the belt moving with a constant velocity $\mathrm{V} \mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s}$ will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{MV}}{2} \mathrm{~N}$
- **(B)** 2 MV N
- **(C)** Zero N
- **(D)** MV N

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) MV N**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Newton's second law of motion, the force applied is equal to the rate of change of momentum.

$F = \dfrac{dp}{dt} = \dfrac{d(mv)}{dt}$

Since the velocity of the conveyor belt is constant, $v = V$ and $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = 0$.

$F = v\dfrac{dm}{dt} + m\dfrac{dv}{dt} = V\dfrac{dm}{dt}$

Given that sand is dropped at the rate of $M \text{ kg/s}$, we have $\dfrac{dm}{dt} = M$.

$F = V \times M = MV \text{ N}$

Answer: MV N

</details>

$W_2 = m\left(g + \dfrac{g}{3}\right) = \dfrac{4mg}{3}$

Substituting $mg = 30$ N:

$W_2 = \dfrac{4}{3} \times 30 = 40$ N
