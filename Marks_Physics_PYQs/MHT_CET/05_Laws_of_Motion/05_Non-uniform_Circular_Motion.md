### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A stone of mass $40$ g is tied to a light inextensible string of length $50$ cm and whirled in a vertical circle at the rate of $30$ revolutions per minute. The tension in the string when it is at the lowest point of the circle is
(Take gravitational acceleration, $g = 10\,\text{m/s}^2$ and $\pi^2 = 10$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.4$ N
- **(B)** $0.5$ N
- **(C)** $0.6$ N
- **(D)** $0.7$ N

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.6$ N**

**Detailed Solution:**

Mass of the stone, $m = 40$ g $= 0.04$ kg

Length of the string, $r = 50$ cm $= 0.5$ m

Frequency of revolution, $f = 30$ rev/min $= \dfrac{30}{60}$ rev/s $= 0.5$ rev/s

Angular velocity, $\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 0.5 = \pi$ rad/s

At the lowest point of the vertical circle, the net force towards the center provides the necessary centripetal force. The forces acting on the stone are tension $T$ upwards and weight $mg$ downwards.

$T - mg = m\omega^2 r$

$T = m(g + \omega^2 r)$

Substituting the given values and using $\pi^2 = 10$:

$T = 0.04(10 + \pi^2 \times 0.5)$

$T = 0.04(10 + 10 \times 0.5)$

$T = 0.04(10 + 5)$

$T = 0.04 \times 15 = 0.6$ N

</details>

- **(A)** $2 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$

- **(C)** $1 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(D)** $0.5 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$

<details>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\theta$ be the angle made by the slant surface of the cone with the horizontal.

The normal reaction $N$ exerted by the smooth inner surface on the particle is perpendicular to the surface. Thus, $N$ makes an angle $\theta$ with the vertical.

Balancing the forces in the vertical direction:

$N \cos \theta = mg$

$N \sin \theta = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

$\tan \theta = \dfrac{v^2}{rg}$

$v = \sqrt{0.1 \times 10} = \sqrt{1} = 1 \text{ m/s}$

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Radius of curved road is ' R ', width of road is ' b '. The outer edge of road is raised by ' $h$ ' with respect to inner edge so that a car with velocity ' $V$ ' can pass safe over it, then value of ' $h$ ' is ( $g=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{Rgb}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{V^2 R}{g}$

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A car is driven on the banked road of radius of curvature 20 m with maximum safe speed. In order to increase its safety speed by $20 \%$, without changing the angle of banking, the increase in the radius of curvature will be
[Assume friction is same on the road]

**Options:**

- **(A)** 28.8 m
- **(B)** $14.4 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(C)** 8.8 m
- **(D)** $4.8 \mathrm{~m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 8.8 m**

**Detailed Solution:**

The maximum safe speed on a banked road is given by $v = \sqrt{rg \left( \dfrac{\mu_s + \tan \theta}{1 - \mu_s \tan \theta} \right)}$.

Since the angle of banking $\theta$ and the coefficient of friction $\mu_s$ remain constant, $v \propto \sqrt{r}$, which implies $r \propto v^2$.

Given the initial radius of curvature $r_1 = 20$ m and the new speed $v_2 = v_1 + 0.20 v_1 = 1.2 v_1$.

Using the proportionality relation, $\dfrac{r_2}{r_1} = \left( \dfrac{v_2}{v_1} \right)^2$.

$\dfrac{r_2}{20} = (1.2)^2 = 1.44$

$r_2 = 20 \times 1.44 = 28.8$ m.

The increase in the radius of curvature is $\Delta r = r_2 - r_1 = 28.8 - 20 = 8.8$ m.

Answer: 8.8 m

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A vehicle is moving with uniform speed along 3 different shaped roads as horizontal, concave and convex. The surface of road on which, the normal reaction on vehicle is maximum is

**Options:**

- **(A)** convex
- **(B)** concave
- **(C)** horizontal
- **(D)** same on all the 3 surface

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) concave**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $m$ be the mass of the vehicle and $v$ be its uniform speed.

For a horizontal road, the normal reaction $N$ balances the weight of the vehicle:
$N_{\text{horizontal}} = mg$

For a convex road, the centripetal force is directed downwards towards the center of curvature:
$mg - N_{\text{convex}} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \Rightarrow N_{\text{convex}} = mg - \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

For a concave road, the centripetal force is directed upwards towards the center of curvature:
$N_{\text{concave}} - mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} \Rightarrow N_{\text{concave}} = mg + \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$

Comparing the normal reactions, we get $N_{\text{concave}} > N_{\text{horizontal}} > N_{\text{convex}}$.

Thus, the normal reaction is maximum on the concave road.

Answer: concave

</details>
