### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A linear harmonic oscillator of force constant $2 \times 10^6$ N/m has amplitude $0.01$ m. Its

**Options:**

- **(A)** maximum potential energy is $100$ J.
- **(B)** maximum kinetic energy is $160$ J.
- **(C)** maximum potential energy is $160$ J.
- **(D)** maximum potential energy is zero.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) maximum potential energy is $100$ J.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The maximum potential energy of a linear harmonic oscillator is given by $U_{\text{max}} = \dfrac{1}{2} k A^2$.

Substituting the given values $k = 2 \times 10^6$ N/m and $A = 0.01$ m:

$U_{\text{max}} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times (2 \times 10^6) \times (0.01)^2$

$U_{\text{max}} = 10^6 \times 10^{-4} = 100$ J.

The maximum kinetic energy is also equal to the maximum potential energy, which is $100$ J.

Answer: maximum potential energy is $100$ J.

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle starts oscillating simple harmonically from its equilibrium position with time period T. The ratio of kinetic energy to potential energy of the particle at time $t = T/12$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:4$
- **(B)** $2:1$
- **(C)** $3:1$
- **(D)** $4:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of displacement for a particle starting from the equilibrium position is given by $x = A \sin(\omega t)$.

Given $t = \dfrac{T}{12}$ and $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$, the displacement is:

$x = A \sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{T} \times \dfrac{T}{12}\right) = A \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \dfrac{A}{2}$

The potential energy (PE) of the particle is:

$PE = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 \left(\dfrac{A}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{8} m \omega^2 A^2$

The total energy (TE) of the particle is:

$TE = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 A^2$

The kinetic energy (KE) is:

$KE = TE - PE = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 A^2 - \dfrac{1}{8} m \omega^2 A^2 = \dfrac{3}{8} m \omega^2 A^2$

The ratio of kinetic energy to potential energy is:

$\dfrac{KE}{PE} = \dfrac{\dfrac{3}{8} m \omega^2 A^2}{\dfrac{1}{8} m \omega^2 A^2} = \dfrac{3}{1}$

Answer: $3:1$

</details>
