### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ball released from height $(h)$ which makes perfectly elastic collision with ground. The frequency of periodic vibratory motion is ($g$ = acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Time taken by the ball to fall from height $h$ is given by $h = \dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$, which gives $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$.

Since the collision is perfectly elastic, the ball rebounds with the same speed and reaches the same height $h$. The time taken to ascend is also $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$.

The total time period of the periodic motion is $T = 2t = 2\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$.

The frequency of the periodic vibratory motion is $f = \dfrac{1}{T} = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The ratio of the frequencies of two simple pendulums is $4: 3$ at the same place. The ratio of their respective lengths is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3: 4$
- **(B)** $4: 3$
- **(C)** $9: 16$
- **(D)** $16: 9$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $9: 16$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The frequency of a simple pendulum is given by $n = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{l}}$.

Since the pendulums are at the same place, $g$ is constant. Thus, $n \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{l}}$, which implies $l \propto \dfrac{1}{n^2}$.

The ratio of their lengths is $\dfrac{l_1}{l_2} = \left(\dfrac{n_2}{n_1}\right)^2$.

Given $\dfrac{n_1}{n_2} = \dfrac{4}{3}$, we get $\dfrac{l_1}{l_2} = \left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 = \dfrac{9}{16}$.

Answer: $9: 16$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A mass $m$ is suspended from a spring of negligible mass. The spring is pulled a little and then released, so that mass executes S.H.M. of time period T . If the mass is increased by $\mathrm{m}_0$, the periodic time becomes $\frac{5 \mathrm{~T}}{4}$. The ratio $\frac{\mathrm{m}_0}{m}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{3}{4}$
- **(B)** $\frac{4}{3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{9}{16}$
- **(D)** $\frac{16}{9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{9}{16}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of a spring-mass system is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$.

When the mass is increased by $m_0$, the new time period is $T' = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m + m_0}{k}}$.

Given that $T' = \dfrac{5T}{4}$, we have:

$2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m + m_0}{k}} = \dfrac{5}{4} \left( 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}} \right)$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$\dfrac{m + m_0}{k} = \dfrac{25}{16} \dfrac{m}{k}$

$m + m_0 = \dfrac{25}{16} m$

$m_0 = \dfrac{25}{16} m - m = \dfrac{9}{16} m$

$\dfrac{m_0}{m} = \dfrac{9}{16}$

Answer: $\frac{9}{16}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle executing S.H.M. has velocities ' $\mathrm{V}_1$ ' and ' $\mathrm{V}_2$ ' at distances ' $x_1$ ' and ' $x_2$ ' respectively, from the mean position. Its frequency is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{V_1^2-V_2^2}{x_1^2-x_2^2}}$
- **(B)** $2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{x_1^2-x_2^2}{V_1^2-V_2^2}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{V_2^2-V_1^2}{x_1^2-x_2^2}}$
- **(D)** $2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{x_1^2-x_2^2}{V_2^2-V_1^2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{V_2^2-V_1^2}{x_1^2-x_2^2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The velocity of a particle executing S.H.M. at a distance $x$ from the mean position is given by:

$V = \omega \sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$V^2 = \omega^2 (A^2 - x^2)$

For the two given positions, we can write:

$V_1^2 = \omega^2 (A^2 - x_1^2)$

$V_2^2 = \omega^2 (A^2 - x_2^2)$

Subtracting the first equation from the second gives:

$V_2^2 - V_1^2 = \omega^2 (A^2 - x_2^2) - \omega^2 (A^2 - x_1^2)$

$V_2^2 - V_1^2 = \omega^2 (x_1^2 - x_2^2)$

$\omega^2 = \dfrac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{x_1^2 - x_2^2}$

$\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{x_1^2 - x_2^2}}$

Since the angular frequency $\omega = 2 \pi f$, the frequency $f$ is:

$f = \dfrac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{x_1^2 - x_2^2}}$

Answer: $\frac{1}{2 \pi} \sqrt{\frac{V_2^2-V_1^2}{x_1^2-x_2^2}}$

</details>

A piece of wood has length, breadth and height, ' $a$ ', 'b' and ' $c$ ' respectively. Its relative density is ' $d$ '. It is floating in water such that the side ' $a$ ' is vertical. It is pushed down a little and released. The time period of S.H.M. executed by it is ( $g=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{a b c}{g}}$
- **(B)** $2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{b c}{d g}}$
- **(C)** $2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{g}{d a}}$
- **(D)** $\quad 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{ad}}{\mathrm{g}}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\quad 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{ad}}{\mathrm{g}}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Mass of the wooden block is $M = a b c d \rho_{w}$, where $\rho_{w}$ is the density of water.

When the block is pushed down by a small distance $x$, the additional volume submerged is $b c x$.

The restoring force acting on the block is the additional buoyant force:

$F = - (b c x) \rho_{w} g$

The acceleration of the block is:

$\alpha = \dfrac{F}{M} = \dfrac{- b c x \rho_{w} g}{a b c d \rho_{w}} = - \dfrac{g}{a d} x$

Comparing this with the standard equation of S.H.M., $\alpha = - \omega^2 x$, we get:

$\omega^2 = \dfrac{g}{a d} \Rightarrow \omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{a d}}$

The time period of S.H.M. is:

$T = \dfrac{2 \pi}{\omega} = 2 \pi \sqrt{\dfrac{a d}{g}}$

### Q12. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

### Q50. MHT CET 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The periodic time of a particle doing simple hormonic motion is $4 \mathrm{~s}$. The time taken by it to go from its mean position to half the maximum displacement (amplitude) is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \mathrm{~s}$
- **(B)** $1 \mathrm{~s}$
- **(C)** $\frac{2}{3} \mathrm{~s}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{3} s$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{1}{3} s$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$y=A \sin \left(\frac{2 \pi}{T}\right) t$
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\Rightarrow & \frac{A}{2}=A \sin \left(\frac{2 \pi}{4}\right) t \\
\Rightarrow & \frac{\pi t}{2}=\frac{\pi}{6} \\
\Rightarrow & t=\frac{1}{3} \mathrm{~s}
\end{array}
$$

</details>

### Q51. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A hollow sphere is filled with water through the small hole in it. It is then hung by a long thread and made to oscillate. As the water slowly flow out of the hole at the bottom, the period of oscillation will

**Options:**

- **(A)** continuously decrease
- **(B)** continuously increase
- **(C)** first decrease then increase
- **(D)** first increase then decrease

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) first decrease then increase**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of the pendulum
$$
\begin{aligned}
& & T &=2 \pi \sqrt{l / g} \\
\Rightarrow & & T & \propto \sqrt{l}
\end{aligned}
$$
Initially the centre of mass of the sphere is at the centre of the sphere. As the water slowly flows out of the hole at the bottom, the CM of the liquid (hollow sphere) first goes on downward and then upward. Hence, the effective length of the pendulum first increases and then decreases.

</details>

### Q52. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The graph between the time period and the length of a simple pendulum is

**Options:**

- **(A)** straight line
- **(B)** curve
- **(C)** ellipse
- **(D)** parabola

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) parabola**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of a simple pendulum is
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/mht_cet/laqbZI6CxkmVlhLg5pvmgiHuzk06WjrC4ArR4KxQvJo.original.fullsize.png">

$$
T=2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}
$$
Here, $l$ is the length of the pendulum. On squaring both sides
$$
\begin{aligned}
& T^{2}=\frac{4 \pi^{2} l}{g} \\
\Rightarrow \quad & T^{2} \propto l
\end{aligned}
$$
So, the graph between time period $T$ and length $l$ of the pendulum is a parabola.

</details>
