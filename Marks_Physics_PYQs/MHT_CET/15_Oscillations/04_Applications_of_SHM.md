### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The bob of a simple pendulum is a hollow sphere with a small hole in the bottom. It is filled with sand and set into oscillation. Its periodic time (T) is measured at regular intervals. As the sand starts coming out we find that.

**Options:**

- **(A)** T increases with time
- **(B)** T does not change with time
- **(C)** T first decreases and then increases
- **(D)** T first increases and then decreases

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) T first increases and then decreases**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of a simple pendulum is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$, where $l$ is the effective length of the pendulum, measured from the point of suspension to the center of mass of the bob.

Initially, when the hollow sphere is completely filled with sand, the center of mass is at the center of the sphere.

As the sand starts leaking out from the small hole at the bottom, the center of mass of the remaining sand and the sphere shifts downwards. This increases the effective length $l$ of the pendulum. Since $T \propto \sqrt{l}$, the time period $T$ initially increases.

When the sand continues to drain and the sphere becomes completely empty, the center of mass shifts back to the geometric center of the hollow sphere. This decreases the effective length $l$ back to its original value, causing the time period $T$ to decrease back to its initial value.

Therefore, the time period $T$ first increases and then decreases.

Answer: T first increases and then decreases

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two simple pendulums having lengths $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ with negligible string mass undergo angular displacement $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ from their mean positions respectively. If the angular acceleration of both pendulums are same, then which equation is correct ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\theta_1 \ell_2^2 = \theta_2 \ell_1^2$
- **(B)** $\theta_1 \ell_1 = \theta_2 \ell_2$
- **(C)** $\theta_1 \ell_1^2 = \theta_2 \ell_2^2$
- **(D)** $\theta_1 \ell_2 = \theta_2 \ell_1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\theta_1 \ell_2 = \theta_2 \ell_1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a simple pendulum, the restoring torque is $\tau = -mg\ell \sin\theta$.

For small angular displacements, $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, so $\tau = -mg\ell\theta$.

The moment of inertia of the pendulum about the point of suspension is $I = m\ell^2$.

The angular acceleration $\alpha$ is given by $\alpha = \dfrac{\tau}{I} = \dfrac{-mg\ell\theta}{m\ell^2} = -\dfrac{g}{\ell}\theta$.

The magnitude of the angular acceleration is $|\alpha| = \dfrac{g}{\ell}\theta$.

Given that the angular accelerations of both pendulums are the same, we have $|\alpha_1| = |\alpha_2|$.

$\dfrac{g}{\ell_1}\theta_1 = \dfrac{g}{\ell_2}\theta_2$

$\theta_1 \ell_2 = \theta_2 \ell_1$

Answer: $\theta_1 \ell_2 = \theta_2 \ell_1$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A body of mass $64$ g is made to oscillate turn by turn on two different springs A and B. Spring A and B has force constant $4$ N/m and $16$ N/m respectively. If $T_1$ and $T_2$ are the period of oscillations of springs A and B respectively, then $(T_1 + T_2)/(T_1 - T_2)$ will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:2$
- **(B)** $1:3$
- **(C)** $3:1$
- **(D)** $2:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of oscillation of a spring-mass system is given by $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$.

For spring A, $T_1 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k_1}}$

For spring B, $T_2 = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k_2}}$

Taking the ratio of the time periods, we get:

$\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{k_2}{k_1}}$

Given $k_1 = 4$ N/m and $k_2 = 16$ N/m:

$\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{16}{4}} = 2$

This implies $T_1 = 2T_2$.

Now, evaluating the required expression:

$\dfrac{T_1 + T_2}{T_1 - T_2} = \dfrac{2T_2 + T_2}{2T_2 - T_2} = \dfrac{3T_2}{T_2} = \dfrac{3}{1}$

Thus, the ratio is $3:1$.

Answer: $3:1$

- **(B)** Amplitude of B is smaller than A

- **(D)** Amplitude of A is twice that of B

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Amplitude of B is smaller than A**

**Detailed Solution:**

The vibrational energy of a simple pendulum in simple harmonic motion is given by $E = \dfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 A^2$.

For a simple pendulum, the angular frequency is $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{g}{L}}$.

Substituting $\omega$ into the energy equation, we get $E = \dfrac{1}{2} m \left(\dfrac{g}{L}\right) A^2$.

Given that the vibrational energies are equal ($E_1 = E_2$) and the masses are equal ($M_1 = M_2$):

$\dfrac{1}{2} M_1 \dfrac{g}{L_1} A_1^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} M_2 \dfrac{g}{L_2} A_2^2$

$\dfrac{A_1^2}{L_1} = \dfrac{A_2^2}{L_2}$

Since $L_1 = 2L_2$, we can substitute this into the equation:

$\dfrac{A_1^2}{2L_2} = \dfrac{A_2^2}{L_2}$

$A_1^2 = 2A_2^2 \Rightarrow A_1 = \sqrt{2} A_2$

Thus, $A_1 > A_2$, which means the amplitude of pendulum B is smaller than that of pendulum A.

Answer: Amplitude of B is smaller than A

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The period of oscillation of a simple pendulum is $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$ where $l$ is about $100$ cm and has $1$ mm accuracy. The period is about $2$ s. The time of $100$ oscillations is measured by a stopwatch of least count $0.1$ s. The percentage error in '$g$' is (gravitation acceleration)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.1\%$
- **(B)** $1\%$
- **(C)** $0.2\%$
- **(D)** $0.8\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.2\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The formula for the time period of a simple pendulum is given by $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$

Squaring both sides and rearranging for $g$, we get:

$g = \dfrac{4\pi^2 l}{T^2}$

The percentage error in $g$ is given by:

$\dfrac{\Delta g}{g} \times 100 = \left( \dfrac{\Delta l}{l} + 2 \dfrac{\Delta T}{T} \right) \times 100$

Given that $l = 100$ cm and $\Delta l = 1$ mm $= 0.1$ cm, the percentage error in length is:

$\dfrac{\Delta l}{l} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.1}{100} \times 100 = 0.1\%$

The total time for $100$ oscillations is $t = 100 \times 2 = 200$ s.

The error in measuring the total time is the least count of the stopwatch, so $\Delta t = 0.1$ s.

The relative error in the time period $T$ is equal to the relative error in the total time $t$:

$\dfrac{\Delta T}{T} = \dfrac{\Delta t}{t} = \dfrac{0.1}{200}$

The percentage error contributed by the time period measurement is:

$2 \dfrac{\Delta T}{T} \times 100 = 2 \times \dfrac{0.1}{200} \times 100 = 0.1\%$

Adding the percentage errors, the total percentage error in $g$ is:

$\dfrac{\Delta g}{g} \times 100 = 0.1\% + 0.1\% = 0.2\%$

Answer: $0.2\%$

</details>

- **(D)** $2$ s

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2$ s**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of a simple pendulum in a stationary lift is given by

$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}} = \sqrt{5}$ s

$g_{eff} = g + a = g + \dfrac{g}{4} = \dfrac{5g}{4}$

The new time period $T'$ is

$T' = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g_{eff}}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{\dfrac{5g}{4}}}$

$T' = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \times 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$

Substituting the value of $T$, we get

$T' = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \times \sqrt{5} = 2$ s

Answer: $2$ s

$v_{max}^2 = \omega^2 A^2 = \dfrac{g}{L} A^2$

$T_{max} = mg + \dfrac{m}{L} \left(\dfrac{g}{L} A^2\right)$

$T_{max} = mg + mg \left(\dfrac{A}{L}\right)^2$
