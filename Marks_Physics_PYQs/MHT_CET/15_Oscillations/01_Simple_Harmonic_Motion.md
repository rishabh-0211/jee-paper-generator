### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A spring of force constant $180$ N/m is loaded with a mass $0.20$ kg. The amplitude of oscillations is $4$ cm. When mass comes to equillibrium position, its velocity in m/s is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $12$
- **(B)** $1.2$
- **(C)** $0.12$
- **(D)** $0.012$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular frequency of the spring-mass system is given by $\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$.

Substituting the given values, $k = 180$ N/m and $m = 0.20$ kg:

$\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{180}{0.20}} = \sqrt{900} = 30$ rad/s.

The velocity of the mass at the equilibrium position is its maximum velocity, which is given by $v = A\omega$.

Given the amplitude $A = 4$ cm $= 0.04$ m:

$v = 0.04 \times 30 = 1.2$ m/s.

Answer: $1.2$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A simple pendulum performs simple harmonic motion about $x = 0$ with an amplitude 'A' and angular frequency $\omega$. The speed of pendulum at $x = \dfrac{A}{8}$ will be.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{61}\, A\omega}{8}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{62}\, A\omega}{8}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{63}\, A\omega}{8}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{65}\, A\omega}{8}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{\sqrt{63}\, A\omega}{8}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The velocity of a particle executing simple harmonic motion at a distance $x$ from the mean position is given by $v = \omega \sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$.

Substituting $x = \dfrac{A}{8}$:

$v = \omega \sqrt{A^2 - \left(\dfrac{A}{8}\right)^2}$

$v = \omega \sqrt{A^2 - \dfrac{A^2}{64}}$

$v = \omega \sqrt{\dfrac{63A^2}{64}}$

$v = \dfrac{\sqrt{63}\, A\omega}{8}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\sqrt{63}\, A\omega}{8}$

</details>

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

A horizontal spring executes S.H.M. with amplitude $A_1$ when mass $m$ is attached to it. When it passes through mean position another mass $m$ is placed on it. Both masses move together with amplitude $A_2$. The ratio $\dfrac{A_1}{A_2}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the spring constant be $k$. The velocity of the mass $m$ at the mean position is maximum and is given by $v_1 = A_1 \omega_1 = A_1 \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$.

When another mass $m$ is gently placed on it at the mean position, the net external force in the horizontal direction is zero. By conservation of linear momentum:

$m v_1 = (m + m) v_2$

$v_2 = \dfrac{v_1}{2}$

The new system has a total mass of $2m$, so its new angular frequency is $\omega_2 = \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{2m}}$.

The new amplitude $A_2$ is related to the new maximum velocity $v_2$ at the mean position by $v_2 = A_2 \omega_2$.

Substituting the values of $v_2$ and $\omega_2$:

$\dfrac{v_1}{2} = A_2 \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{2m}}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} A_1 \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}} = A_2 \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{2m}}$

$\dfrac{A_1}{2} = \dfrac{A_2}{\sqrt{2}}$

$\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2}$

Answer: $\sqrt{2}$

</details>

### Q14. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

**Correct Option:** **(D) $F / \sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the spring constant of each identical spring be $k$.

In the given arrangement, when the mass $m$ is displaced by a distance $x$ from its equilibrium position, one spring is stretched by $x$ and the other is compressed by $x$. Both springs exert a restoring force in the same direction.

The total restoring force is $F_{net} = -kx - kx = -2kx$.

Thus, the effective spring constant of the system is $k_{eff} = 2k$ (the springs are in parallel).

The initial frequency of oscillation is:
$F = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k_{eff}}{m}} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{2k}{m}}$

When one spring is removed, the mass is attached to only one spring. The new effective spring constant is $k'_{eff} = k$.

The new frequency of oscillation is:
$F' = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k'_{eff}}{m}} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$

$\dfrac{F'}{F} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}}{\dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{2k}{m}}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$\Rightarrow F' = \dfrac{F}{\sqrt{2}}$

Answer: $F / \sqrt{2}$

### Q15. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

A body has time period $T_1$ under the action of one force and $T_2$ under the action of another force. The square of the time period when both the forces are acting in the same direction is

The time periods are $T_1 = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k_1}}$ and $T_2 = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k_2}}$.

From these, we get $k_1 = \dfrac{4\pi^2 m}{T_1^2}$ and $k_2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2 m}{T_2^2}$.

When both forces act in the same direction, the net restoring force is $F = F_1 + F_2 = -(k_1 + k_2)x$.

The effective force constant is $k_{eff} = k_1 + k_2$.

The new time period $T$ is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k_{eff}}}$, which implies $k_{eff} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 m}{T^2}$.

Substituting the values of $k_1$, $k_2$, and $k_{eff}$:

$\dfrac{4\pi^2 m}{T^2} = \dfrac{4\pi^2 m}{T_1^2} + \dfrac{4\pi^2 m}{T_2^2}$

$\dfrac{1}{T^2} = \dfrac{1}{T_1^2} + \dfrac{1}{T_2^2} = \dfrac{T_1^2 + T_2^2}{T_1^2 T_2^2}$

$T^2 = \dfrac{T_1^2 T_2^2}{T_1^2 + T_2^2}$

### Q149. MHT CET 2007

Sound waves are not transmitted a long distance because

**Options:**

- **(B)** they have constant frequency

- **(D)** velocity of sound waves is very less

<details>

**Correct Option:** **(A) they are absorbed by the atmosphere**

**Detailed Solution:**

There are several factors which affect the propagation of sound. These are geometric spreading, atmospheric effect and surface effects.
