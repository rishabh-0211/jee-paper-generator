### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The relationship between the magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) and magnetic permeability ($\mu$) of a material is ($\mu_0$ and $\mu_r$ is the permeability of free space and relative permeability respectively)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\chi = \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0} - 1$
- **(B)** $\chi = \dfrac{\mu_r}{\mu_0} + 1$
- **(C)** $\chi = \mu_r + 1$
- **(D)** $\chi = 1 - \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\chi = \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0} - 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic permeability of a material is given by $\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r$, where $\mu_0$ is the permeability of free space and $\mu_r$ is the relative permeability.

The relative permeability is related to the magnetic susceptibility $\chi$ by the equation $\mu_r = 1 + \chi$.

Substituting $\mu_r = \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0}$ into the equation, we get:

$\dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0} = 1 + \chi$

$\chi = \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0} - 1$

Answer: $\chi = \dfrac{\mu}{\mu_0} - 1$

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

' $\chi_1{ }^{\prime},{ }^{\prime} \chi_2{ }^{\prime}$ and ' $\chi_3{ }^{\prime}$ are susceptibilities of a paramagnetic material at temperatures ' $\mathrm{T}_1{ }^{\prime} \mathrm{K}$, ' $\mathrm{T}_2{ }^{\prime} \mathrm{K}$ and ' $\mathrm{T}_3{ }^{\prime} \mathrm{K}$ respectively, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\chi_1: \chi_2=\mathrm{T}_2: \mathrm{T}_1, \chi_2: \chi_3=\mathrm{T}_3: \mathrm{T}_2$
- **(B)** $\chi_1: \chi_2=\mathrm{T}_1: \mathrm{T}_2, \chi_2: \chi_3=\mathrm{T}_3: \mathrm{T}_2$
- **(C)** $\chi_1: \chi_2=\mathrm{T}_1: \mathrm{T}_2, \chi_2: \chi_3=\mathrm{T}_2: \mathrm{T}_3$
- **(D)** $\chi_1: \chi_2=\mathrm{T}_2: \mathrm{T}_1, \chi_2: \chi_3=\mathrm{T}_2: \mathrm{T}_3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\chi_1: \chi_2=\mathrm{T}_2: \mathrm{T}_1, \chi_2: \chi_3=\mathrm{T}_3: \mathrm{T}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Curie's law for paramagnetic materials, the magnetic susceptibility $\chi$ is inversely proportional to the absolute temperature $T$.

$\chi \propto \dfrac{1}{T}$

$\chi = \dfrac{C}{T}$

For temperatures $T_1$, $T_2$, and $T_3$, the susceptibilities are:

$\chi_1 = \dfrac{C}{T_1}$

$\chi_2 = \dfrac{C}{T_2}$

$\chi_3 = \dfrac{C}{T_3}$

Taking the ratio of $\chi_1$ and $\chi_2$:

$\dfrac{\chi_1}{\chi_2} = \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} \Rightarrow \chi_1 : \chi_2 = T_2 : T_1$

Taking the ratio of $\chi_2$ and $\chi_3$:

$\dfrac{\chi_2}{\chi_3} = \dfrac{T_3}{T_2} \Rightarrow \chi_2 : \chi_3 = T_3 : T_2$

Answer: $\chi_1: \chi_2=\mathrm{T}_2: \mathrm{T}_1, \chi_2: \chi_3=\mathrm{T}_3: \mathrm{T}_2$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two bar magnets $A$ and $B$ are geometrically similar but the magnetic moment of $A$ is twice that of $B$. $T_1$ is the time period of oscillation when their like poles are kept together. When unlike poles are kept together, the time period of oscillation is $\mathrm{T}_2$. The ratio $\mathrm{T}_1: \mathrm{T}_2$ will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 3$
- **(B)** $1: 2$
- **(C)** $1: \sqrt{3}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{3}: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1: \sqrt{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the magnetic moment of magnet $B$ be $M$. Then the magnetic moment of magnet $A$ is $2M$.

Since the magnets are geometrically similar, their moments of inertia are equal. Let $I_A = I_B = I$.

When like poles are kept together, the effective magnetic moment is $M_1 = M_A + M_B = 2M + M = 3M$.

The effective moment of inertia is $I_1 = I_A + I_B = 2I$.

The time period of oscillation is $T_1 = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{I_1}{M_1 B_H}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{2I}{3M B_H}}$.

When unlike poles are kept together, the effective magnetic moment is $M_2 = M_A - M_B = 2M - M = M$.

The effective moment of inertia remains $I_2 = I_A + I_B = 2I$.

The time period of oscillation is $T_2 = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{I_2}{M_2 B_H}} = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{2I}{M B_H}}$.

Taking the ratio of $T_1$ and $T_2$:

$\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{M_2}{M_1}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{M}{3M}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$

Thus, $T_1 : T_2 = 1 : \sqrt{3}$.

Answer: $1: \sqrt{3}$

</details>

where $I=$ moment of inertia of magnet $=\frac{m L^{2}}{12}(m$, being the mass of magnet $)$ $M=$ pole strength $\times L$ and $H=$ horizontal component of earth's magnetic field. When the three equal parts of magnet are placed on one another with their like poles together, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\prime} &=\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{m}{3}\right) \times\left(\frac{L}{3}\right)^{2} \times 3 \\
&=\frac{1}{12} \frac{m L^{2}}{9}=\frac{I}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$
and $\quad M^{\prime}=$ pole strength $\times \frac{L}{3} \times 3=M$
Hence, $T^{\prime}=2 \pi \sqrt{\left(\frac{I / 9}{M H}\right)}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Rightarrow \quad T^{\prime} &=\frac{1}{3} \times T \\
T^{\prime} &=\frac{2}{3} \mathrm{~s}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q62. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If a source emitting waves of frequency $f$ moves towards an observer with a velocity $\frac{v}{4}$ and the observer moves away from the source with a velocity $v / 6$, the apparent frequency as heard by the observer will be $(v=$ velocity of sound)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{14}{15} f$
- **(B)** $\frac{14}{9} f$
- **(C)** $\frac{10}{9} f$
- **(D)** $\frac{2}{3} f$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{10}{9} f$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When source and observer both are moving in the same direction and observer is ahead of source, then apparent frequency
$$
f^{\prime}=\frac{v-v_{o}}{v-v_{s}} f
$$
$$
=\frac{v-\frac{v}{6}}{v-\frac{v}{4}}=\frac{\frac{5 v}{6}}{\frac{3 v}{4}} f=\frac{10}{9} f
$$

</details>
