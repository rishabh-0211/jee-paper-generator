Soft iron is used in making an electromagnet because

**Options:**

- **(B)** It has high coercivity
- **(C)** It has high retentivity
- **(D)** It has low retentivity

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) It has low retentivity**

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The product of magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) and absolute temperature (T) is constant for a

**Options:**

- **(A)** diamagnetic substance.
- **(B)** paramagnetic substance.
- **(C)** ferromagnetic substance.
- **(D)** paramagnetic as well as ferromagnetic substance.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) paramagnetic substance.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Curie's law, the magnetic susceptibility ($\chi$) of a paramagnetic substance is inversely proportional to its absolute temperature ($T$).

$\chi \propto \dfrac{1}{T}$

$\chi = \dfrac{C}{T}$

$\chi T = C$

where $C$ is Curie's constant. Thus, the product of magnetic susceptibility and absolute temperature is constant for a paramagnetic substance.

For diamagnetic substances, susceptibility is independent of temperature. For ferromagnetic substances, susceptibility follows the Curie-Weiss law ($\chi = \dfrac{C}{T - T_c}$) above the Curie temperature.

Answer: paramagnetic substance.

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Magnetic hysteresis is exhibited by magnetic materials which are

**Options:**

- **(A)** only paramagnetic
- **(B)** only diamagnetic
- **(C)** only ferromagnetic
- **(D)** both para and ferromagnetic

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) only ferromagnetic**

**Detailed Solution:**

Magnetic hysteresis is the phenomenon where the magnetic induction ($B$) lags behind the magnetizing field ($H$). This property is a characteristic feature of ferromagnetic materials, as they retain some magnetization even after the external magnetic field is removed (retentivity) and require a reverse magnetic field to be completely demagnetized (coercivity).

Diamagnetic and paramagnetic materials do not retain magnetization in the absence of an external magnetic field and therefore do not exhibit magnetic hysteresis.

Answer: only ferromagnetic

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The gyromagnetic ratio and Bohr magneton are given respectively by [Given $\rightarrow \mathrm{e}=$ charge on electron, $\mathrm{m}=$ mass of electron, $\mathrm{h}=$ Planck's constant].

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{e}}{2 \mathrm{~m}}, \dfrac{\mathrm{eh}}{4 \pi \mathrm{~m}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{eh}}{4 \pi \mathrm{~m}}, \dfrac{\mathrm{e}}{2 \mathrm{~m}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2 \mathrm{~m}}{\mathrm{e}}, \dfrac{4 \pi \mathrm{~m}}{\text { eh }}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{4 \pi \mathrm{~m}}{\mathrm{eh}}, \dfrac{2 \mathrm{~m}}{\mathrm{e}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{\mathrm{e}}{2 \mathrm{~m}}, \dfrac{\mathrm{eh}}{4 \pi \mathrm{~m}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The gyromagnetic ratio is defined as the ratio of the magnetic dipole moment to the angular momentum of a revolving electron.

Magnetic moment $M = I A = \dfrac{e}{T} \pi r^2 = \dfrac{e v}{2 \pi r} \pi r^2 = \dfrac{e v r}{2}$

Angular momentum $L = m v r$

Gyromagnetic ratio $= \dfrac{M}{L} = \dfrac{\dfrac{e v r}{2}}{m v r} = \dfrac{e}{2m}$

The Bohr magneton is the magnetic moment of an electron in the first Bohr orbit of a hydrogen atom.

From Bohr's quantization rule, the angular momentum in the first orbit is $L = \dfrac{h}{2\pi}$

Bohr magneton $\mu_B = \dfrac{e}{2m} L = \dfrac{e}{2m} \left(\dfrac{h}{2\pi}\right) = \dfrac{eh}{4\pi m}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\mathrm{e}}{2 \mathrm{~m}}, \dfrac{\mathrm{eh}}{4 \pi \mathrm{~m}}$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A ferromagnetic material is heated above its curie temperature. The correct statement from the following is that

**Options:**

- **(A)** ferromagnetic domains are perfectly arranged.
- **(B)** ferromagnetic domains become random.
- **(C)** ferromagnetic domains are not influenced.
- **(D)** ferromagnetic material changes itself into diamagnetic material.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) ferromagnetic domains become random.**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a ferromagnetic material is heated above its Curie temperature, the thermal energy becomes sufficient to overcome the exchange interaction that keeps the magnetic dipoles aligned within the domains.

As a result, the ordered arrangement of magnetic moments is disrupted, and the ferromagnetic domains become randomly oriented. The material loses its spontaneous magnetization and transitions into a paramagnetic state.

Answer: ferromagnetic domains become random.

</details>

### Q21. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Nickel shows ferromagnetic property at room temperature. If the temperature is increased beyond Curie temperature, then it will show

**Options:**

- **(A)** paramagnetism
- **(B)** anti-ferromagnetism
- **(C)** no magnetic property
- **(D)** diamagnetism

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) paramagnetism**

**Detailed Solution:**

Nickel exhibits ferromagnetism because of a quantum physical effect called exchange coupling in which the electron spins of one atom interact with those of neighbouring atoms. The result is alignment of the magnetic dipole moments of the atoms, inspite of the randomizing tendency of atomic collisions. This persistent alignment is what gives ferromagnetic materials their permanent magnetism. If the temperature of a ferromagnetic material is raised above a certain critical value, called the Curie temperature, the exchange coupling ceases to be effective. Most such materials then become simply paramagnetic; that is, the dipoles still tend to align with an external field but much more weakly, and thermal agitation can now more easily disrupt the alignment.

</details>
