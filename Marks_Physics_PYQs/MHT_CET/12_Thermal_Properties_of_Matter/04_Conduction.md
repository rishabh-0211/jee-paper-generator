### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two rods A and B are of equal lengths. Their ends are kept between the same temperatures and their area cross-sections are $A_1$ and $A_2$, thermal conductivities are $K_1$ and $K_2$. The rate of heat transmission in the two rods will be equal if

**Options:**

- **(A)** $K_1 A_2 = K_2 A_1$
- **(B)** $K_1 A_1 = K_2 A_2$
- **(C)** $K_1 A_1^2 = K_2 A_2^2$
- **(D)** $K_1 = K_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $K_1 A_1 = K_2 A_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rate of heat transmission through a rod is given by $H = \dfrac{K A \Delta T}{L}$, where $K$ is the thermal conductivity, $A$ is the area of cross-section, $\Delta T$ is the temperature difference, and $L$ is the length of the rod.

For rod A, the rate of heat transmission is $H_1 = \dfrac{K_1 A_1 \Delta T}{L}$.

For rod B, the rate of heat transmission is $H_2 = \dfrac{K_2 A_2 \Delta T}{L}$.

Given that the rate of heat transmission is equal for both rods, $H_1 = H_2$.

$\dfrac{K_1 A_1 \Delta T}{L} = \dfrac{K_2 A_2 \Delta T}{L}$

Since the lengths and temperature differences are the same, they cancel out, giving $K_1 A_1 = K_2 A_2$.

Answer: $K_1 A_1 = K_2 A_2$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Three rods made of the same material and the same cross-sectional areas have been joined as shown in the figure. Each rod is of the same length. The temperature of the junction is
<img style="width: 300px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/12_may_shift_1__q6_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $60^\circ\text{C}$
- **(B)** $45^\circ\text{C}$
- **(C)** $30^\circ\text{C}$
- **(D)** $20^\circ\text{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $60^\circ\text{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the temperature of the junction be $T$.

In steady state, the net heat current at the junction is zero. Let $R$ be the thermal resistance of each rod. Since all three rods have the same material, length, and cross-sectional area, their thermal resistances are equal ($R = \dfrac{L}{kA}$).

Applying the principle of continuity of heat flow at the junction:

$\dfrac{T - 0}{R} + \dfrac{T - 90}{R} + \dfrac{T - 90}{R} = 0$

Multiplying by $R$ on both sides, we get:

$T + (T - 90) + (T - 90) = 0$

$3T - 180 = 0$

$3T = 180$

$T = 60^\circ\text{C}$

Answer: $60^\circ\text{C}$

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two rods of same length, radius and material transfer a given amount of heat in 't' second when they are joined as shown in fig (1). But when they are joined as shown in fig (2) then they will transfer same heat in same condition in time
<img style="width: 300px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/19_april_shift_1__q7_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2t$
- **(B)** $3t$
- **(C)** $4t$
- **(D)** $6t$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4t$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the thermal resistance of each rod be $R$.

In figure (1), the two rods are connected in parallel. The equivalent thermal resistance is:
$R_p = \dfrac{R \times R}{R + R} = \dfrac{R}{2}$

The amount of heat transferred in time $t$ is:
$Q = \dfrac{\Delta T}{R_p} t = \dfrac{2 \Delta T}{R} t$

In figure (2), the two rods are connected in series. The equivalent thermal resistance is:
$R_s = R + R = 2R$

Let the time taken to transfer the same amount of heat $Q$ under the same temperature difference $\Delta T$ be $t'$. Then:
$Q = \dfrac{\Delta T}{R_s} t' = \dfrac{\Delta T}{2R} t'$

Equating the two expressions for $Q$:
$\dfrac{2 \Delta T}{R} t = \dfrac{\Delta T}{2R} t'$

$2t = \dfrac{t'}{2}$

$t' = 4t$

Answer: $4t$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a composite slab there are two materials having coefficients of thermal conductivity K and 2K, thickness x and 4x repectively. The temperature of the two outer surfaces of a composite slab are $T_2$ and $T_1$ $(T_2 > T_1)$. $T_2$ is on side K and $T_1$ is on side 2K. The rate of heat transfer through the slab in a steady state is $\left[\dfrac{A(T_2 - T_1)K}{x}\right] \cdot f$, where f is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{1}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The thermal resistance of a slab is given by $R = \dfrac{L}{KA}$, where $L$ is the thickness, $K$ is the thermal conductivity, and $A$ is the area of cross-section.

For the first material, the thermal resistance is $R_1 = \dfrac{x}{KA}$.

For the second material, the thermal resistance is $R_2 = \dfrac{4x}{2KA} = \dfrac{2x}{KA}$.

Since the two materials form a composite slab in series, the equivalent thermal resistance is:

$R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 = \dfrac{x}{KA} + \dfrac{2x}{KA} = \dfrac{3x}{KA}$

The rate of heat transfer through the slab in a steady state is given by:

$H = \dfrac{T_2 - T_1}{R_{eq}} = \dfrac{T_2 - T_1}{\dfrac{3x}{KA}} = \dfrac{KA(T_2 - T_1)}{3x}$

This can be rewritten as:

$H = \left[\dfrac{A(T_2 - T_1)K}{x}\right] \cdot \dfrac{1}{3}$

Comparing this with the given expression $H = \left[\dfrac{A(T_2 - T_1)K}{x}\right] \cdot f$, we get:

$f = \dfrac{1}{3}$

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{3}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Consider two rods 1 and 2 of same length. They have different specific heats $(C_1, C_2)$, thermal conductivities $(K_1, K_2)$ and area of cross-section $(A_1, A_2)$ respectively. Both the rods have temperatures $(T_1, T_2)$ at their ends. If their rate of loss of heat due to conduction is equal, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $A_1 K_2 = A_2 K_1$
- **(B)** $A_1 K_1 = A_2 K_2$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{A_1 K_1}{C_1} = \dfrac{A_2 K_2}{C_2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{A_1 K_2}{C_1} = \dfrac{A_2 K_1}{C_2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $A_1 K_1 = A_2 K_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rate of heat flow (or rate of loss of heat due to conduction) through a rod is given by Fourier's law of heat conduction:

$\dfrac{dQ}{dt} = \dfrac{KA(T_1 - T_2)}{L}$

For the first rod, the rate of heat flow is:

$\left(\dfrac{dQ}{dt}\right)_1 = \dfrac{K_1 A_1 (T_1 - T_2)}{L}$

For the second rod, the rate of heat flow is:

$\left(\dfrac{dQ}{dt}\right)_2 = \dfrac{K_2 A_2 (T_1 - T_2)}{L}$

It is given that the rate of loss of heat due to conduction is equal for both rods. Equating the two rates:

$\dfrac{K_1 A_1 (T_1 - T_2)}{L} = \dfrac{K_2 A_2 (T_1 - T_2)}{L}$

Since the length $L$ and the temperature difference $(T_1 - T_2)$ are the same for both rods, they cancel out from both sides:

$K_1 A_1 = K_2 A_2$

Specific heat is not involved in the steady-state rate of heat conduction.

Answer: $A_1 K_1 = A_2 K_2$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Three identical heat conducting rods are connected in series as shown in the figure. The rods on the side have thermal conductivity $2K$ while that in the middle has thermal conductivity $K$. The left end of the combination is maintained at temperature $3T$ and the right end at $T$. The rods are thermally insulated from outside. In steady state, temperature at the left junction is $T_1$ and that at the right junctions is $T_2$. The ratio $T_1/T_2$ is
<img style="width: 300px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/11_april_shift_1__q45_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{5}{3}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{4}{5}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{5}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the length of each rod be $L$ and the area of cross-section be $A$.

In steady state, the rate of heat flow is the same through all three rods:

$\dfrac{dQ}{dt} = \dfrac{2KA(3T - T_1)}{L} = \dfrac{KA(T_1 - T_2)}{L} = \dfrac{2KA(T_2 - T)}{L}$

Cancelling $\dfrac{KA}{L}$ from all terms, we get:

$2(3T - T_1) = T_1 - T_2 = 2(T_2 - T)$

From the first and third parts of the equality:

$2(3T - T_1) = 2(T_2 - T)$

$3T - T_1 = T_2 - T$

$T_1 + T_2 = 4T \quad \dots (1)$

From the second and third parts of the equality:

$T_1 - T_2 = 2(T_2 - T)$

$T_1 - 3T_2 = -2T \quad \dots (2)$

Subtracting equation (2) from equation (1):

$(T_1 + T_2) - (T_1 - 3T_2) = 4T - (-2T)$

$4T_2 = 6T \Rightarrow T_2 = \dfrac{3T}{2}$

Substituting $T_2$ in equation (1):

$T_1 + \dfrac{3T}{2} = 4T \Rightarrow T_1 = \dfrac{5T}{2}$

The required ratio is:

$\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{5T}{2}}{\dfrac{3T}{2}} = \dfrac{5}{3}$

Answer: $\dfrac{5}{3}$

</details>

In an adiabatic change, the pressure and temperature of a monoatomic gas are related with relation $p \propto T^{C}$, where $C$ is equal to

$$
For adiabatic process, Poisson's equation is given by
$$
p V^{\gamma}=\text { constant }
$$
Ideal gas relation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
& p V &=R T \\
\Rightarrow & V &=\frac{R T}{p}
\end{aligned}
$$
From Eqs. (i) and (ii), we get
$$
p\left(\frac{R T}{p}\right)^{\gamma}=\text { constant }

$$

$$
