### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A sheet of solid material having area $A$ and coefficient of linear expansion $\alpha$. The mass of sheet is $m$ and specific heat capacity is $s$. If $dQ$ is the amount of heat given to the sheet then change in its area is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{A(2\alpha)ms}{\Delta Q}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{A(2\alpha)\Delta Q}{ms}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{A\alpha \Delta Q}{ms}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{A\alpha ms}{\Delta Q}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{A(2\alpha)\Delta Q}{ms}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The heat supplied to the sheet is given by the relation:

$\Delta Q = ms \Delta T$

$\Delta T = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{ms}$

The change in area of the sheet due to thermal expansion is:

$\Delta A = A \beta \Delta T$

For an isotropic material, the coefficient of superficial expansion is $\beta = 2\alpha$.

Substituting the values of $\beta$ and $\Delta T$, we get:

$\Delta A = A(2\alpha) \left( \dfrac{\Delta Q}{ms} \right)$

$\Delta A = \dfrac{A(2\alpha)\Delta Q}{ms}$

Answer: $\dfrac{A(2\alpha)\Delta Q}{ms}$

</details>

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the diameter of the wooden wheel, $D = 1.0$ m.

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A metal rod (A) of length $20$ cm expands by $0.075$ cm when its temperature changes from $0^\circ$C to $100^\circ$C. Another metal rod (B) of different metal of same length expands by $0.045$ cm for same temperature change. A third rod of same length is composed of 2 parts, one of metal A and another of metal B. For same temperature change, this rod expands by $0.060$ cm. The portion made of metal A has the length

**Options:**

- **(A)** $12$ cm
- **(B)** $10$ cm
- **(C)** $15$ cm
- **(D)** $18$ cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $10$ cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the length of the portion made of metal A be $x$ cm. The length of the portion made of metal B will be $(20 - x)$ cm.

For rod A of length $20$ cm, the expansion is $\Delta L_A = \alpha_A \times 20 \times \Delta T = 0.075$ cm.

For rod B of length $20$ cm, the expansion is $\Delta L_B = \alpha_B \times 20 \times \Delta T = 0.045$ cm.

For the composite rod, the total expansion is the sum of the expansions of its individual parts:

$\Delta L = \alpha_A x \Delta T + \alpha_B (20 - x) \Delta T = 0.060$ cm.

Substituting $\alpha_A \Delta T = \dfrac{0.075}{20}$ and $\alpha_B \Delta T = \dfrac{0.045}{20}$:

$x \left( \dfrac{0.075}{20} \right) + (20 - x) \left( \dfrac{0.045}{20} \right) = 0.060$

Multiplying the entire equation by $20$:

$0.075 x + 0.045 (20 - x) = 1.2$

$0.075 x + 0.9 - 0.045 x = 1.2$

$0.030 x = 0.3$

$x = 10$ cm.

Answer: $10$ cm

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two rods of different materials have lengths ' $\ell_1$ ' and ' $\ell_2$ ' whose coefficient of linear expansions are ' $\alpha_1$ ' and ' $\alpha_2$ ' respectively. If the difference between the two lengths is independent of temperature then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\alpha_1^2 \ell_1=\alpha_2^2 \ell_2$
- **(B)** $\frac{\ell_1}{\ell_2}=\frac{\alpha_2}{\alpha_1}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\ell_1}{\ell_2}=\frac{\alpha_1}{\alpha_2}$
- **(D)** $\ell_1^2 \alpha_2=\ell_2^2 \alpha_1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{\ell_1}{\ell_2}=\frac{\alpha_2}{\alpha_1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial lengths of the two rods be $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ at temperature $T$.

When the temperature is increased by $\Delta T$, the new lengths are:
$\ell_1' = \ell_1(1 + \alpha_1 \Delta T)$
$\ell_2' = \ell_2(1 + \alpha_2 \Delta T)$

The difference between their lengths at the new temperature is:
$\ell_1' - \ell_2' = \ell_1(1 + \alpha_1 \Delta T) - \ell_2(1 + \alpha_2 \Delta T)$
$\ell_1' - \ell_2' = (\ell_1 - \ell_2) + (\ell_1 \alpha_1 - \ell_2 \alpha_2)\Delta T$

For the difference in lengths to be independent of temperature, the change in difference must be zero. Thus, the coefficient of $\Delta T$ must be zero:
$\ell_1 \alpha_1 - \ell_2 \alpha_2 = 0$
$\ell_1 \alpha_1 = \ell_2 \alpha_2$
$\dfrac{\ell_1}{\ell_2} = \dfrac{\alpha_2}{\alpha_1}$

Answer: $\frac{\ell_1}{\ell_2}=\frac{\alpha_2}{\alpha_1}$

</details>

### Q14. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

A cylindrical rod is having temperatures $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ at its ends. The rate of heat flow is $\mathrm{Q} / \mathrm{S}$. All the linear dimensions of the rod are doubled by keeping the temperature constant. The new rate of flow of heat is

**Options:**

- **(B)** 2 Q
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}$
- **(D)** $\quad \frac{\mathrm{Q}}{4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 2 Q**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rate of heat flow through a cylindrical rod is given by Fourier's law of heat conduction:

$H = \dfrac{KA(\theta_1 - \theta_2)}{L}$

For a cylindrical rod of radius $r$, the cross-sectional area is $A = \pi r^2$. Thus, the initial rate of heat flow is:

$Q = \dfrac{K(\pi r^2)(\theta_1 - \theta_2)}{L}$

When all linear dimensions of the rod are doubled, the new length becomes $L' = 2L$ and the new radius becomes $r' = 2r$. The new cross-sectional area is:

$A' = \pi (2r)^2 = 4\pi r^2 = 4A$

The new rate of heat flow $H'$ is:

$H' = \dfrac{K(4\pi r^2)(\theta_1 - \theta_2)}{2L}$

$H' = 2 \left[ \dfrac{K(\pi r^2)(\theta_1 - \theta_2)}{L} \right]$

$H' = 2Q$

It is observed that the difference between their length's remains constant at all temperatures. The ratio $\mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{A}}: \mathrm{L}_{\mathrm{B}}$ is given by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\alpha_A}{\alpha_B}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\alpha_B}{\alpha_A}$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial lengths of rod A and rod B be $L_A$ and $L_B$ respectively at a given temperature.

$L_A' = L_A(1 + \alpha_A \Delta T)$

$$

\end{aligned}

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\mathrm{T}_2}{\mathrm{~T}_1}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{V}_1}{\mathrm{~V}_2}\right)^{\gamma-1}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{V}_1}{\mathrm{~V}_2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} \\

\therefore \quad \frac{\mathrm{T}_2}{\mathrm{~T}_1} & =\left(\frac{\mathrm{L}_1}{\mathrm{~L}_2}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

$$

### Q40. MHT CET 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

With an increase in temperature, surface tension of liquid (except molten copper and cadmium)

**Options:**

- **(A)** increases
- **(B)** remain same
- **(C)** decreases
- **(D)** first decreases then increases

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) decreases**

**Detailed Solution:**

The surface tension of liquid decreases with rise of temperature. The surface tension of liquid is zero at its boiling point and it vanishes at critical temperature. $\quad$ At $\quad$ critical $\quad$ temperature intermolecular forces for liquid and gases becomes equal and liquid can expand without any restriction. For small temperature differences, the variation in surface tension with temperature is linear and is given by relation
$$
T_{t}=T_{0}(1-\alpha t)
$$
where $T_{t}, T_{0}$ are the surface tension at $t^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and $0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ respectively and $\alpha$ is the temperature coefficient of surface tension.

</details>
