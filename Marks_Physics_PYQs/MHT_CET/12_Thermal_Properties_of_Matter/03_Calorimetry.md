### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

$540$ gram of ice at $0^\circ$C is mixed with $540$ gram of water at $90^\circ$C. The final temperature of the mixture will be [Latent heat of fusion of water = 80 cal/g]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10^\circ$C
- **(B)** $15^\circ$C
- **(C)** $5^\circ$C
- **(D)** $30^\circ$C

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5^\circ$C**

**Detailed Solution:**

The heat required to completely melt the ice at $0^{\circ}\text{C}$ is $Q_1 = m_i L$.

$Q_1 = 540 \times 80 = 43200 \text{ cal}$

The maximum heat released by the hot water if it cools down to $0^{\circ}\text{C}$ is $Q_2 = m_w s \Delta T$.

$Q_2 = 540 \times 1 \times (90 - 0) = 48600 \text{ cal}$

Since $Q_2 > Q_1$, all the ice will melt and the final temperature $T$ of the mixture will be greater than $0^{\circ}\text{C}$.

According to the principle of calorimetry, the heat gained by the ice equals the heat lost by the water.

$m_i L + m_i s (T - 0) = m_w s (90 - T)$

$540 \times 80 + 540 \times 1 \times T = 540 \times 1 \times (90 - T)$

Dividing both sides by $540$:

$80 + T = 90 - T$

$2T = 10$

$T = 5^{\circ}\text{C}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A piece of metal of 850 K is dropped into 1 kg of water at 300 K. If equilibrium temperature of water is 350 K then the heat capacity of the metal expressed in $\dfrac{J}{K}$ is $\left(\text{Sp. heat of water} = 1\ \dfrac{\text{cal}}{\text{g}^\circ\text{C}}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $420$
- **(B)** $240$
- **(C)** $100$
- **(D)** $24$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $420$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the principle of calorimetry, heat lost by the metal is equal to the heat gained by the water.

Heat gained by water is $Q = m_w s_w \Delta T_w$

Given mass of water $m_w = 1\text{ kg} = 1000\text{ g}$

Specific heat of water $s_w = 1\text{ cal/g}^\circ\text{C} = 4.2\text{ J/g}^\circ\text{C}$

Change in temperature of water $\Delta T_w = 350 - 300 = 50\text{ K}$

$Q = 1000 \times 4.2 \times 50 = 210000\text{ J}$

Heat lost by the metal is $Q = C \Delta T_m$

Change in temperature of the metal $\Delta T_m = 850 - 350 = 500\text{ K}$

Equating the heat lost to the heat gained:

$C \times 500 = 210000$

$C = \dfrac{210000}{500} = 420\text{ J/K}$

Answer: $420$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

$15$ g of ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ is added to a vessel containing water at $40^\circ\text{C}$. The mass of water and water equivalent of the vessel is $60$ g. Assuming that negligible heat is taken from the surroundings, the final temperature of the mixture will be
[ $L_{\text{ice}} = 80\,\text{cal/g}$ , $S_{\text{water}} = 1\,\text{cal/g}$ ]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30^\circ\text{C}$
- **(B)** $22^\circ\text{C}$
- **(C)** $16^\circ\text{C}$
- **(D)** $10^\circ\text{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $16^\circ\text{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the final temperature of the mixture be $T^\circ\text{C}$.

According to the principle of calorimetry, heat lost by the water and vessel equals the heat gained by the ice.

Heat lost by water and vessel = $m_{\text{water}} S_{\text{water}} (40 - T)$

Heat gained by ice = $m_{\text{ice}} L_{\text{ice}} + m_{\text{ice}} S_{\text{water}} (T - 0)$

Equating the two:

$60 \times 1 \times (40 - T) = 15 \times 80 + 15 \times 1 \times T$

$2400 - 60T = 1200 + 15T$

$75T = 1200$

$T = \dfrac{1200}{75} = 16^\circ\text{C}$

Answer: $16^\circ\text{C}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A calorimeter contains 10 g of water at $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. The temperature fall to $15^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ in 10 min . When calorimeter contains 20 g of water at $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, it takes 15 min . for the temperature to become $15^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. The water equivalent at the calorimeter is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 50 g
- **(B)** 25 g
- **(C)** 10 g
- **(D)** 5 g

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 10 g**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $W$ be the water equivalent of the calorimeter.

According to Newton's law of cooling, the rate of heat loss depends only on the temperature difference between the body and the surroundings. Since the temperature falls from $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ to $15^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ in both cases, the average rate of heat loss is the same.

Heat lost in the first case:
$Q_1 = (m_1 + W) c \Delta \theta = (10 + W) c (20 - 15) = 5(10 + W) c$

Rate of heat loss in the first case:
$R_1 = \dfrac{Q_1}{t_1} = \dfrac{5(10 + W) c}{10} = \dfrac{(10 + W) c}{2}$

Heat lost in the second case:
$Q_2 = (m_2 + W) c \Delta \theta = (20 + W) c (20 - 15) = 5(20 + W) c$

Rate of heat loss in the second case:
$R_2 = \dfrac{Q_2}{t_2} = \dfrac{5(20 + W) c}{15} = \dfrac{(20 + W) c}{3}$

Equating the rates of heat loss ($R_1 = R_2$):
$\dfrac{(10 + W) c}{2} = \dfrac{(20 + W) c}{3}$

$3(10 + W) = 2(20 + W)$

$30 + 3W = 40 + 2W$

$W = 10 \text{ g}$

Answer: 10 g

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A stationary object at $4^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and weighing 3.5 kg falls from a height of 2000 m on snow mountain at $0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. If the temperature of the object just before hitting the snow is $0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and the object comes to rest immediately then the quantity of ice that melts is
(Acceleration due to gravity $=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$, Latent heat of ice $\left.=3.5 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{kg}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2 gram
- **(B)** 20 gram
- **(C)** 200 gram
- **(D)** 2 kg

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 200 gram**

**Detailed Solution:**

The potential energy of the object at a height $h$ is converted into kinetic energy just before it hits the snow. Upon impact, this kinetic energy is entirely converted into heat energy which melts the ice.

The potential energy of the object is given by:
$E = mgh$

Substituting the given values:
$E = 3.5 \times 10 \times 2000 = 70000 \text{ J}$

Since the object's temperature just before hitting the snow is $0^{\circ}\text{C}$, it does not release any additional heat due to a change in its own temperature. The heat $Q$ produced upon impact is equal to the mechanical energy lost:
$Q = 70000 \text{ J}$

Let $M$ be the mass of the ice that melts. The heat required to melt the ice is:
$Q = M \times L$

Substituting the values of $Q$ and the latent heat of ice $L$:
$70000 = M \times 3.5 \times 10^5$

$M = \dfrac{70000}{3.5 \times 10^5} = \dfrac{7 \times 10^4}{35 \times 10^4} = \dfrac{1}{5} \text{ kg}$

Converting the mass into grams:
$M = 0.2 \text{ kg} = 200 \text{ g}$

Answer: 200 gram

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A lead bullet moving with velocity ' $v$ ' strikes a wall and stops. If $50 \%$ of its energy is converted into heat, then the increase in temperature is ( $\mathrm{s}=$ specific heat of lead)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{v^2 s}{2 J}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{v}^2}{4 \mathrm{~sJ}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{v}^2 \mathrm{~s}}{\mathrm{~J}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{2 \mathrm{v}^2}{\mathrm{~Js}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{\mathrm{v}^2}{4 \mathrm{~sJ}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial kinetic energy of the bullet is given by $K = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.

Since $50\%$ of its kinetic energy is converted into heat, the mechanical energy converted into heat is:

$W = \dfrac{50}{100} \times \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}mv^2$

The heat energy required to raise the temperature of the bullet by $\Delta T$ is $Q = ms\Delta T$.

Using the mechanical equivalent of heat $W = JQ$, we get:

$\dfrac{1}{4}mv^2 = J(ms\Delta T)$

$\Delta T = \dfrac{v^2}{4sJ}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\mathrm{v}^2}{4 \mathrm{~sJ}}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two rods of same length & material transfer a given amount of heat in 12 s when they are joined end to end. But when they are joined length wise parallel to each other they will transfer same amount of heat in same condition in time

**Options:**

- **(A)** 24 s
- **(B)** 3 s
- **(C)** 1.5 s
- **(D)** 48 s

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 3 s**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the thermal resistance of each rod be $R$.

When the rods are joined end to end (in series), the equivalent thermal resistance is $R_s = R + R = 2R$.

The time taken to transfer a given amount of heat $Q$ under a temperature difference $\Delta T$ is given by $t_s = \dfrac{Q \cdot R_s}{\Delta T} = \dfrac{Q(2R)}{\Delta T} = 12$ s.

When the rods are joined lengthwise parallel to each other, the equivalent thermal resistance is $R_p = \dfrac{R \cdot R}{R + R} = \dfrac{R}{2}$.

The time taken to transfer the same amount of heat $Q$ under the same conditions is $t_p = \dfrac{Q \cdot R_p}{\Delta T} = \dfrac{Q(R/2)}{\Delta T}$.

Taking the ratio of the times:

$\dfrac{t_p}{t_s} = \dfrac{R_p}{R_s} = \dfrac{R/2}{2R} = \dfrac{1}{4}$

$t_p = \dfrac{t_s}{4} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$ s.

Answer: 3 s

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A monoatomic gas is heated at constant pressure. The percentage of total heat used for doing external work is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30 \%$
- **(B)** $40 \%$
- **(C)** $50 \%$
- **(D)** $60 \%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $40 \%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a monoatomic gas, the molar heat capacity at constant pressure is $C_P = \dfrac{5}{2}R$.

The total heat supplied at constant pressure is $\Delta Q = nC_P\Delta T = \dfrac{5}{2}nR\Delta T$.

The external work done by the gas is $\Delta W = P\Delta V = nR\Delta T$.

The fraction of total heat used for doing external work is $\dfrac{\Delta W}{\Delta Q} = \dfrac{nR\Delta T}{\dfrac{5}{2}nR\Delta T} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.

Percentage of heat used for external work $= \dfrac{2}{5} \times 100\% = 40\%$.

Answer: $40 \%$

</details>
