### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The length of mercury column in a mercury-in-glass thermometer is $H_1$ mm at the ice point and $H_2$ mm at the steam point. When the length is $H$ mm, the temperature $T$ is given in $^\circ$C by ($H_1 < H_2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{100(H - H_1)}{(H_2 - H_1)}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{100(H_2 - H)}{(H_2 - H_1)}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{100(H_2 - H_1)}{(H - H_1)}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{100(H_2 - H)}{(H - H_1)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{100(H - H_1)}{(H_2 - H_1)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The temperature $T$ on a linear scale is given by the relation:

$T = \dfrac{X - X_0}{X_{100} - X_0} \times 100$

where $X$ is the value of the thermometric property at temperature $T$, $X_0$ is the value at the ice point ($0^\circ$C), and $X_{100}$ is the value at the steam point ($100^\circ$C).

Given that the thermometric property is the length of the mercury column:

$X = H$

$X_0 = H_1$

$X_{100} = H_2$

Substituting these values into the formula, we get:

$T = \dfrac{H - H_1}{H_2 - H_1} \times 100$

$T = \dfrac{100(H - H_1)}{H_2 - H_1}$

Answer: $\dfrac{100(H - H_1)}{(H_2 - H_1)}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the two temperatures on Fahrenheit scale differ by $54^\circ\text{F}$, then the difference in temperature on the Celsius scale is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30^\circ\text{C}$
- **(B)** $35^\circ\text{C}$
- **(C)** $40^\circ\text{C}$
- **(D)** $45^\circ\text{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $30^\circ\text{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The relation between the Fahrenheit and Celsius temperature scales is given by:

$F = \dfrac{9}{5}C + 32$

Taking the difference for two temperatures, we get:

$\Delta F = \dfrac{9}{5}\Delta C$

Given that the difference on the Fahrenheit scale is $\Delta F = 54^{\circ}\text{F}$, substituting this value yields:

$54 = \dfrac{9}{5}\Delta C$

$\Delta C = 54 \times \dfrac{5}{9}$

$\Delta C = 30^{\circ}\text{C}$

The difference in temperature on the Celsius scale is $30^{\circ}\text{C}$.

Answer: $30^\circ\text{C}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A celsius and a Fahrenheit thermometer are dipped in boiling water. The temperature of water is lowered until the Farenheit thermometer shows $140^\circ$. What is the fall in temperature on the Celsius thermometer?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $60^\circ$ C
- **(B)** $40^\circ$ C
- **(C)** $50^\circ$ C
- **(D)** $80^\circ$ C

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $40^\circ$ C**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial temperature of boiling water is $100^\circ$ C, which corresponds to $212^\circ$ F.

The final temperature is given as $140^\circ$ F.

The relationship between the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales is given by:

$\dfrac{C}{5} = \dfrac{F - 32}{9}$

Substituting $F = 140$:

$\dfrac{C}{5} = \dfrac{140 - 32}{9} = \dfrac{108}{9} = 12$

$C = 60^\circ$ C

The fall in temperature on the Celsius thermometer is the difference between the initial and final temperatures:

$\Delta C = 100^\circ \text{ C} - 60^\circ \text{ C} = 40^\circ \text{ C}$

Answer: $40^\circ$ C

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2023 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A metal rod cools at the rate of $4{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C} / \mathrm{min}$ when its temperature is $90^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and the rate of $1{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C} / \mathrm{min}$ when its temperature is $30^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. The temperature of the surrounding is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(B)** $15^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(C)** $10^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(D)** $5^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $10^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Newton's law of cooling,
$\begin{array}{ll} 
& \frac{\mathrm{R}_1}{\mathrm{R}_2}=\frac{\theta_1-\theta_0}{\theta_2-\theta_0} \\
\therefore \quad & \frac{4}{1}=\frac{90-\theta_0}{30-\theta_0} \\
\therefore \quad & 120-90=4 \theta_0-\theta_0 \\
\therefore \quad & 3 \theta_0=30
\end{array}$
$\therefore \quad$ Temperature of the surroundings is,
$\theta_0=10^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2023 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A sphere and a cube, both of copper have equal volumes and are black. They are allowed to cool at same temperature and in same atmosphere. The ratio of their rate of loss of heat will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 1$
- **(B)** $\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}$
- **(C)** $\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{4 \pi}{3}: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Volumes of the cube and the sphere are equal
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore & \mathrm{a}^3=\frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{r}^3 \\
\therefore & \mathrm{a}=\left(\frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{r}^3\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}
\end{array}
$$

Rate of loss of radiation is proportional to the surface area of the object.
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \quad \frac{Q_{\text {sphere }}}{Q_{\text {cube }}}=\frac{4 \pi \mathrm{r}^2}{6 \mathrm{a}^2} \\
& \therefore \quad \frac{\mathrm{Q}_{\text {sphere }}}{\mathrm{Q}_{\text {cube }}}=\frac{4 \pi \mathrm{r}^2}{6\left(\frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{r}^3\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}} \\
& \therefore \quad \frac{\mathrm{Q}_{\text {sphere }}}{\mathrm{Q}_{\text {cube }}}=\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2023 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A composite slab consists of two materials having coefficient of thermal conductivity $K$ and $2 \mathrm{~K}$, thickness $\mathrm{x}$ and $4 \mathrm{x}$ respectively. The temperature of the two outer surfaces of a composite slab are $\mathrm{T}_2$ and $\mathrm{T}_1\left(\mathrm{~T}_2>\mathrm{T}_1\right)$. The rate of heat transfer through the slab in a steady state is $\left[\frac{\mathrm{A}\left(\mathrm{T}_2-\mathrm{T}_1\right) \mathrm{K}}{\mathrm{x}}\right] \cdot \mathrm{f}$ where ' $\mathrm{f}$ ' is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $\frac{2}{3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{1}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{array}{ll} 
& \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\mathrm{R}_1+\mathrm{R}_2 \\
\because & \mathrm{R}_1=\frac{\mathrm{x}}{\mathrm{KA}}, \mathrm{R}_2=\frac{4 \mathrm{x}}{2 \mathrm{KA}} \\
\therefore & \mathrm{R}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{\mathrm{x}}{\mathrm{KA}}+\frac{2 \mathrm{x}}{\mathrm{KA}}=\frac{3 \mathrm{x}}{\mathrm{KA}}
\end{array}
$$
Rate of heat transfer of composite slab is given by,
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \quad \frac{\mathrm{dQ}}{\mathrm{dt}}=\frac{\mathrm{T}_2-\mathrm{T}_1}{\mathrm{R}_{e \mathrm{q}}}=\frac{\mathrm{KA}\left(\mathrm{T}_2-\mathrm{T}_1\right)}{3 \mathrm{x}} \\
& \therefore \quad \mathrm{f}=\frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2021 (20 Sep Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

On an imaginary linear scale of temperature (called ' $\mathrm{W}$ ' scale) the freezing and boiling points of water are $39^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$ and $239^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$ respectively. The temperature on the new scale corresponding to $39^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ temperature on Celsius scale will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $139^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$
- **(B)** $78^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$
- **(C)** $117^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$
- **(D)** $200^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $117^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In Celsius scale the freezing and boiling point are $0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ and $100^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. In the given imaginary scale the freezing and boiling points are $39^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$ and $239^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$. Hence, we can write
$$
\frac{\mathrm{C}-0}{100}=\frac{\mathrm{W}-39}{200}
$$
For $\mathrm{C}=39^{\circ}, \frac{39}{100}=\frac{\mathrm{W}-39}{200}$
Solving, $\mathrm{W}=117$

</details>
