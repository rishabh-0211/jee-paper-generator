### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Temperature of two stars are in the ratio $3:2$. If wavelength of maximum intensity of first star is $4200\,\text{Å}$, the corresponding wavelength for second star is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2100\,\text{Å}$
- **(B)** $3000\,\text{Å}$
- **(C)** $6300\,\text{Å}$
- **(D)** $7000\,\text{Å}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $6300\,\text{Å}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Wien's displacement law, the wavelength corresponding to maximum intensity is inversely proportional to the absolute temperature.

$\lambda_{m} \propto \dfrac{1}{T}$

$\lambda_{m1} T_1 = \lambda_{m2} T_2$

Given $\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and $\lambda_{m1} = 4200\,\text{\AA}$

$\lambda_{m2} = \lambda_{m1} \times \dfrac{T_1}{T_2}$

$\lambda_{m2} = 4200 \times \dfrac{3}{2} = 6300\,\text{\AA}$

Answer: $6300\,\text{Å}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

Two bodies 'X' and 'Y' at temperatures $T_1$ K and $T_2$ K respectively have the same dimensions. Their emissivities $e_1$ and $e_2$ are in the ratio of $1:6$. If their emissive powers are same, the relation between their temperatures is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $T_1:T_2 = 4^{1/4}:1$
- **(B)** $T_1:T_2 = 5^{1/4}:1$

<details>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $T_1:T_2 = 6^{1/4}:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The emissive power of a body is given by the Stefan-Boltzmann law:

$E = e \sigma T^4$

$\dfrac{T_1^4}{T_2^4} = 6$

$\dfrac{T_1}{T_2} = 6^{1/4}$

$T_1 : T_2 = 6^{1/4} : 1$

Answer: $T_1:T_2 = 6^{1/4}:1$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A perfectly black body radiates power at temperature $T_1$K. If it is to radiate 16 times this power, its temperature should be increased by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(16T_1)K$
- **(B)** $(4T_1)K$
- **(C)** $(2T_1)K$
- **(D)** $(T_1)K$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $(T_1)K$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the Stefan-Boltzmann law, the power radiated by a perfectly black body is given by $P = \sigma A T^4$.

Let the initial power be $P_1$ at temperature $T_1$.

$P_1 = \sigma A T_1^4$

Let the final temperature be $T_2$ for power $P_2 = 16 P_1$.

$P_2 = \sigma A T_2^4$

Substituting $P_2 = 16 P_1$, we get:

$16 \sigma A T_1^4 = \sigma A T_2^4$

$T_2^4 = 16 T_1^4$

$T_2 = 2 T_1$

The increase in temperature is $\Delta T = T_2 - T_1 = 2 T_1 - T_1 = T_1$.

Therefore, the temperature should be increased by $T_1$ K.

Answer: $(T_1)K$

</details>
