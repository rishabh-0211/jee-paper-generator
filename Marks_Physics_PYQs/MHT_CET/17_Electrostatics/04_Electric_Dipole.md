### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Polar molecules are those in which

**Options:**

- **(A)** they have symmetric shape.
- **(B)** centre of mass of positive charges does not coincide with centre of mass of negative charges.
- **(C)** they have no permanent dipole moments.
- **(D)** centre of mass of positive charges coincide with centre of mass of negative charges.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) centre of mass of positive charges does not coincide with centre of mass of negative charges.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In polar molecules, the centre of mass of positive charges (protons) does not coincide with the centre of mass of negative charges (electrons). This separation of charges results in a net permanent electric dipole moment.

Answer: centre of mass of positive charges does not coincide with centre of mass of negative charges.

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A dipole of electric dipole moment $\vec{P}$ is placed in an electric field of strength $\vec{E}$. The angle between $\vec{P}$ and $\vec{E}$ for which the potential energy of the electric dipole is maximum, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\cos^{-1}(1)$
- **(B)** $\cos^{-1}(-1)$
- **(C)** $\sin^{-1}(1)$
- **(D)** $\sin^{-1}(-1)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\cos^{-1}(-1)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The potential energy $U$ of an electric dipole in a uniform electric field is given by

$U = -\vec{P} \cdot \vec{E} = -PE \cos \theta$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the dipole moment $\vec{P}$ and the electric field $\vec{E}$.

For the potential energy to be maximum, the value of $\cos \theta$ must be minimum.

The minimum value of $\cos \theta$ is $-1$.

$\cos \theta = -1$

$\theta = \cos^{-1}(-1)$

This corresponds to an angle of $180^{\circ}$, which is the position of unstable equilibrium.

Answer: $\cos^{-1}(-1)$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two electric dipoles of moment $P$ and $8P$ are placed in opposite directions on a line at a distance of $24$ cm. The electric field will be zero at the point between the dipoles whose distance from the dipole of moment $P$ will be $x_1$. When $8P$ is replaced by $27P$ keeping all other quantities the same, the distance $x_1$ now becomes $x_2$. The difference $|x_2 - x_1|$ is ( All the distances are measured from the centers of the dipole.)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2$ cm
- **(B)** $4$ cm
- **(C)** $6$ cm
- **(D)** $8$ cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2$ cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the distance of the null point from the dipole of moment $P$ be $x$. The distance from the second dipole is $24 - x$.

The electric field due to a short dipole on its axis at a distance $r$ is given by $E = \dfrac{2kP}{r^3}$.

For the net electric field to be zero, the magnitudes of the electric fields due to both dipoles must be equal.

Case 1: Dipoles are $P$ and $8P$
$\dfrac{2kP}{x_1^3} = \dfrac{2k(8P)}{(24 - x_1)^3}$

Taking the cube root on both sides:
$\dfrac{1}{x_1} = \dfrac{2}{24 - x_1}$

$24 - x_1 = 2x_1 \Rightarrow 3x_1 = 24 \Rightarrow x_1 = 8$ cm

Case 2: Dipole $8P$ is replaced by $27P$
$\dfrac{2kP}{x_2^3} = \dfrac{2k(27P)}{(24 - x_2)^3}$

Taking the cube root on both sides:
$\dfrac{1}{x_2} = \dfrac{3}{24 - x_2}$

$24 - x_2 = 3x_2 \Rightarrow 4x_2 = 24 \Rightarrow x_2 = 6$ cm

The difference is $|x_2 - x_1| = |6 - 8| = 2$ cm.

Answer: $2$ cm

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An electric dipole of length $3\text{cm}$ is placed with its axis making an angle of $60^\circ$ with a uniform electric field of $5 \times 10^4$ N/C. It experiences a torque of $9\sqrt{3}$ Nm. The magnitude of charge on the dipole is $(\sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.6 \times 10^{-2}$ C
- **(B)** $1.2 \times 10^{-2}$ C
- **(C)** $1.8 \times 10^{-2}$ C
- **(D)** $2.4 \times 10^{-2}$ C

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.2 \times 10^{-2}$ C**

**Detailed Solution:**

The torque experienced by an electric dipole in a uniform electric field is given by $\tau = pE \sin\theta$

Since the dipole moment is $p = q \times d$, where $d$ is the length of the dipole, the equation becomes:

$\tau = q \times d \times E \sin\theta$

Given $\tau = 9\sqrt{3}$ Nm, $d = 3 \times 10^{-2}$ m, $E = 5 \times 10^4$ N/C, and $\theta = 60^\circ$. Substituting these values:

$9\sqrt{3} = q \times (3 \times 10^{-2}) \times (5 \times 10^4) \times \sin 60^\circ$

$9\sqrt{3} = q \times 1500 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

$18 = 1500 q$

$q = \dfrac{18}{1500} = 1.2 \times 10^{-2}$ C

Answer: $1.2 \times 10^{-2}$ C

</details>

An electric dipole of length $0.5\ \mu\text{m}$ is placed with its axis making an angle of $30^\circ$ with uniform electric field $10^4\ \text{V/m}$. If it experiences a torque of $5 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{Nm}$, the magnitude of the charge on the dipole is ($\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1\ \mu\text{C}$
- **(B)** $1.5\ \mu\text{C}$
- **(C)** $2\ \mu\text{C}$
- **(D)** $2.5\ \mu\text{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2\ \mu\text{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The torque experienced by an electric dipole in a uniform electric field is given by

$\tau = pE \sin\theta$

where $p = qd$ is the dipole moment, $q$ is the magnitude of the charge, and $d$ is the length of the dipole.

Substituting $p$ in the torque equation gives

$\tau = q d E \sin\theta$

Rearranging for $q$ yields

$q = \dfrac{\tau}{d E \sin\theta}$

Substituting the given values $\tau = 5 \times 10^{-9}\ \text{Nm}$, $d = 0.5 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}$, $E = 10^4\ \text{V/m}$, and $\theta = 30^\circ$:

$q = \dfrac{5 \times 10^{-9}}{0.5 \times 10^{-6} \times 10^4 \times \sin 30^\circ}$

$q = \dfrac{5 \times 10^{-9}}{0.5 \times 10^{-2} \times 0.5}$

$q = \dfrac{5 \times 10^{-9}}{0.25 \times 10^{-2}}$

$q = 20 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{C}$

$q = 2 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{C} = 2\ \mu\text{C}$

Answer: $2\ \mu\text{C}$

</details>
