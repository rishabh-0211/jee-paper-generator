### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Three equal $(+q)$ charges are placed in air at the vertices of equilateral triangle. If the side of equilateral triangle is $l$, the net potential energy of the system is $\left[K = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{kq^2}{l}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{2kq^2}{l}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{3kq^2}{l}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{4kq^2}{l}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{3kq^2}{l}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The potential energy of a system of point charges is the sum of the potential energies of all pairs of charges.

For three charges $q_1, q_2, q_3$ placed at the vertices of a triangle, the total potential energy $U$ is given by:

$U = U_{12} + U_{23} + U_{31}$

Given $q_1 = q_2 = q_3 = +q$ and the distance between any two charges is $l$.

$U = \dfrac{k q \cdot q}{l} + \dfrac{k q \cdot q}{l} + \dfrac{k q \cdot q}{l}$

$U = \dfrac{3kq^2}{l}$

Answer: $\dfrac{3kq^2}{l}$

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two spheres A and B of radius $a$ and $b$ respectively are at the same electric potential. The ratio of the surface charge densities of A to that of B is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{a^2}{b^2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{b^2}{a^2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{b}{a}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{a}{b}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{b}{a}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The electric potential of a sphere of radius $R$ and charge $Q$ is given by $V = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0} \dfrac{Q}{R}$.

Substituting the charge in terms of surface charge density $Q = \sigma (4\pi R^2)$, we get $V = \dfrac{\sigma R}{\epsilon_0}$.

Since both spheres A and B are at the same electric potential, $V_A = V_B$.

$\dfrac{\sigma_A a}{\epsilon_0} = \dfrac{\sigma_B b}{\epsilon_0}$

$\sigma_A a = \sigma_B b$

$\dfrac{\sigma_A}{\sigma_B} = \dfrac{b}{a}$

Answer: $\dfrac{b}{a}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A semicircular arc of radius '$R$' is charged uniformly and has charge per unit length as $\lambda$. The electric potential $V$ at the centre of curvature is [$\varepsilon_0$ = permittivity of vacuum]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon_0}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\lambda}{2\varepsilon_0}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\lambda}{8\varepsilon_0}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\lambda}{\varepsilon_0}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon_0}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Consider a small element of length $dl$ on the semicircular arc. The charge on this element is $dq = \lambda dl$.

The electric potential at the centre of curvature due to this small element is given by:

$dV = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \dfrac{dq}{R} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \dfrac{\lambda dl}{R}$

Integrating this expression over the entire length of the semicircular arc to find the total potential at the centre:

$V = \int dV = \int \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \dfrac{\lambda dl}{R}$

Since $\lambda$, $R$, and $\varepsilon_0$ are constants, they can be taken out of the integral:

$V = \dfrac{\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R} \int dl$

The total length of the semicircular arc is $\int dl = \pi R$.

Substituting this into the equation for $V$:

$V = \dfrac{\lambda}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R} (\pi R) = \dfrac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon_0}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\lambda}{4\varepsilon_0}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

- **(C)** $\dfrac{Q}{(r + r_1)}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{Qr_1}{(r + r_1)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{Qr_1}{(r + r_1)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When the two conducting spheres are placed in contact, charge flows until their potentials become equal.

Let the final charge on the larger sphere of radius $r$ be $Q_1$ and on the smaller sphere of radius $r_1$ be $Q_2$.

By conservation of charge:
$Q_1 + Q_2 = Q$

Equating the final potentials of the two spheres:
$\dfrac{k Q_1}{r} = \dfrac{k Q_2}{r_1}$

Substituting $Q_1$ into the charge conservation equation:
$Q_2 \dfrac{r}{r_1} + Q_2 = Q$

$Q_2 \left( \dfrac{r + r_1}{r_1} \right) = Q$

$Q_2 = \dfrac{Q r_1}{r + r_1}$

Answer: $\dfrac{Qr_1}{(r + r_1)}$

</details>

If charge $+q$ is taken from one point to another over an equipotential surface, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** work done on the charge continuously increases.
- **(B)** work is done by the charge.
- **(C)** work done on the charge is constant.
- **(D)** no work is done.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) no work is done.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work done in moving a charge $q$ from point $A$ to point $B$ is given by $W = q(V_B - V_A)$.

Substituting this into the work equation gives $W = q \times 0 = 0$.

Hence, no work is done in moving a charge over an equipotential surface.
