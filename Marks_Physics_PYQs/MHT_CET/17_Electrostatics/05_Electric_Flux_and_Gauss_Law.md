### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The electric flux entering and leaving an enclosed surface are $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ respectively. The electric charge inside the surface will be ($\varepsilon_0$ = permittivity of vacuum)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\phi_2 - \phi_1}{\varepsilon_0}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\phi_1 + \phi_2}{\varepsilon_0}$
- **(C)** $\varepsilon_0(\phi_1 - \phi_2)$
- **(D)** $\varepsilon_0(\phi_2 - \phi_1)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\varepsilon_0(\phi_2 - \phi_1)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Gauss's law, the net electric flux through a closed surface is equal to the net charge enclosed divided by $\varepsilon_0$.

Net electric flux $\Phi_{net} = \Phi_{leaving} - \Phi_{entering}$

Given that the flux entering is $\phi_1$ and the flux leaving is $\phi_2$.

$\Phi_{net} = \phi_2 - \phi_1$

From Gauss's law, $\Phi_{net} = \dfrac{q_{enclosed}}{\varepsilon_0}$

$\phi_2 - \phi_1 = \dfrac{q_{enclosed}}{\varepsilon_0}$

$q_{enclosed} = \varepsilon_0(\phi_2 - \phi_1)$

Answer: $\varepsilon_0(\phi_2 - \phi_1)$

</details>

$\Phi = al^2$

**Detailed Solution:**

By convention, the electric flux entering a closed surface is considered negative, and the flux leaving the surface is considered positive.

Given that the flux entering is $\phi_1$ and the flux leaving is $\phi_2$, the net electric flux through the enclosed surface is:

$\Phi_{net} = \phi_2 - \phi_1$

According to Gauss's law, the net electric flux through a closed surface is equal to the net charge enclosed divided by the permittivity of free space $\varepsilon_0$:

$\Phi_{net} = \dfrac{q_{enclosed}}{\varepsilon_0}$

Substituting the value of net flux:

$\phi_2 - \phi_1 = \dfrac{q_{enclosed}}{\varepsilon_0}$

$q_{enclosed} = \varepsilon_0(\phi_2 - \phi_1)$

Answer: $\varepsilon_0\left(\phi_2-\phi_1\right)$

In spherical coordinates, for a potential depending only on $r$, the Laplacian is:
