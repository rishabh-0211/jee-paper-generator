An electric charge of magnitude '$2e$' and mass '$2m$' is moving in an electric field 'E'. The acceleration imparted to the charge is

The electric field intensity at a point near and outside the surface of a charged conductor of any shape is $E_1$. The electric field intensity due to a uniformly charged infinite thin plane sheet having the same surface charge density is $E_2$. The relation between $E_1$ and $E_2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2E_1 = E_2$
- **(B)** $E_1 = E_2$
- **(C)** $E_1 = 2E_2$

**Correct Option:** **(C) $E_1 = 2E_2$**

$E_1 = \dfrac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}$

$E_1 = 2E_2$

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An uniformly charged thin spherical shell of radius 'R' has uniform surface charge density '$\sigma$'. It is made of two hemispherical identical shells held together by pressing them with force 'F' as shown. F is proportional to
[$\epsilon_0$ = permittivity of free space]
<img style="width: 200px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/18_april_shift_2__q32_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_0}\,\dfrac{\sigma^2}{R^2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_0}\,\dfrac{\sigma^2}{R}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_0}\,\sigma^2 R^2$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_0}\,\sigma^2 R$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_0}\,\sigma^2 R^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The electrostatic pressure $P$ on the surface of a uniformly charged thin spherical shell is given by:

$P = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2\epsilon_0}$

Consider a small area element $dA$ on the surface of one of the hemispheres. The outward electrostatic force on this element is:

$dF = P dA = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{2\epsilon_0} dA$

By symmetry, the net force on the hemisphere will be directed along the axis perpendicular to its circular base. The component of the force along this axis is:

$dF_{\perp} = dF \cos\theta = P dA \cos\theta$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the radially outward normal and the axis of symmetry.

Integrating this over the entire surface of the hemisphere gives the total repulsive force:

$F_{rep} = \int P dA \cos\theta = P \int dA \cos\theta$

The term $\int dA \cos\theta$ represents the projected area of the hemisphere onto the plane of its base, which is simply the area of a circle of radius $R$:

$\int dA \cos\theta = \pi R^2$

Substituting this back, we get the net repulsive force:

$F_{rep} = \left(\dfrac{\sigma^2}{2\epsilon_0}\right) (\pi R^2) = \dfrac{\pi \sigma^2 R^2}{2\epsilon_0}$

To hold the two hemispheres together, the external applied force $F$ must balance this electrostatic repulsion:

$F = \dfrac{\pi \sigma^2 R^2}{2\epsilon_0}$

From this expression, it is clear that the force $F$ is proportional to $\dfrac{\sigma^2 R^2}{\epsilon_0}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{\epsilon_0}\,\sigma^2 R^2$

</details>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The electric lines of force pass through the conductor.**

Electric lines of force originate from a positive charge and terminate on a negative charge.
