### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Three concentric metallic spherical shells of radii $R$, $3R$ and $9R$ are given charges $Q_1$, $Q_2$ and $Q_3$, respectively. It is found that surface charge densities on the outer surfaces of the shells are equal. Then the ratio of charges given to shells $Q_1 : Q_2 : Q_3$, is (Neglect the induction effects)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 : 4 : 16$
- **(B)** $1 : 3 : 9$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{3} : 3 : 27$
- **(D)** $1 : \dfrac{1}{3} : 9$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{1}{3} : 3 : 27$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the uniform surface charge density on the outer surfaces of the shells be $\sigma$.

The surface area of a sphere of radius $r$ is given by $A = 4\pi r^2$.

The surface areas of the three given concentric shells are:

$A_1 = 4\pi R^2$

$A_2 = 4\pi (3R)^2 = 36\pi R^2$

$A_3 = 4\pi (9R)^2 = 324\pi R^2$

Since induction effects are to be neglected, the charge given to each shell is assumed to reside entirely on its own outer surface.

The charge on each shell can be calculated as $Q = \sigma A$.

$Q_1 = \sigma A_1 = \sigma (4\pi R^2)$

$Q_2 = \sigma A_2 = \sigma (36\pi R^2)$

$Q_3 = \sigma A_3 = \sigma (324\pi R^2)$

The ratio of the charges is:

$Q_1 : Q_2 : Q_3 = 4\pi R^2 \sigma : 36\pi R^2 \sigma : 324\pi R^2 \sigma$

$Q_1 : Q_2 : Q_3 = 1 : 9 : 81$

Comparing this with the given options, we can rewrite the ratio by dividing each term by $3$:

$Q_1 : Q_2 : Q_3 = \dfrac{1}{3} : \dfrac{9}{3} : \dfrac{81}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3} : 3 : 27$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two charged particles each having a charge '$+q$' and mass 'm' are kept at a distance 'd'. If they are in equilibrium under the gravitational force and the electric force between them, then the ratio $\dfrac{q}{m}$ (specific charge) of each particle is ($G$- Universal gravitation constant, $\varepsilon_0$ = permittivity of free space)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $G/4\pi\varepsilon_0$
- **(B)** $4\pi\varepsilon_0 G$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0 G}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi\varepsilon_0}{G}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0 G}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the particles to be in equilibrium, the repulsive electrostatic force between them must be balanced by the attractive gravitational force.

The electrostatic force is given by Coulomb's law:

$F_e = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \dfrac{q^2}{d^2}$

The gravitational force is given by Newton's law of gravitation:

$F_g = G \dfrac{m^2}{d^2}$

Equating the magnitudes of the two forces:

$\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \dfrac{q^2}{d^2} = G \dfrac{m^2}{d^2}$

Canceling $d^2$ from both sides and rearranging the terms:

$\dfrac{q^2}{m^2} = 4\pi\varepsilon_0 G$

Taking the square root on both sides gives the specific charge:

$\dfrac{q}{m} = \sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0 G}$

Answer: $\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0 G}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two charged identical metal spheres A and B repel each other with a force $3 \times 10^{-5}$ N. Another identical uncharged sphere C is touched with A and then placed at the mid-point between A and B. Net force on C is $x \times 10^{-5}$ N. The value of $x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $1.5$
- **(D)** $3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial charge on spheres A and B be $q$ and the distance between them be $r$.

Initial force of repulsion between A and B is $F = \dfrac{k q^2}{r^2} = 3 \times 10^{-5}$ N.

When identical uncharged sphere C touches A, the charge is shared equally.

Charge on A becomes $q_A = \dfrac{q}{2}$.

Charge on C becomes $q_C = \dfrac{q}{2}$.

Charge on B remains $q_B = q$.

Sphere C is placed at the midpoint between A and B, so the distance of C from both A and B is $\dfrac{r}{2}$.

Force on C due to A is $F_{CA} = \dfrac{k q_A q_C}{\left(\dfrac{r}{2}\right)^2} = \dfrac{k \left(\dfrac{q}{2}\right) \left(\dfrac{q}{2}\right)}{\dfrac{r^2}{4}} = \dfrac{k q^2}{r^2} = F$.

Force on C due to B is $F_{CB} = \dfrac{k q_B q_C}{\left(\dfrac{r}{2}\right)^2} = \dfrac{k (q) \left(\dfrac{q}{2}\right)}{\dfrac{r^2}{4}} = \dfrac{2 k q^2}{r^2} = 2F$.

Since A and B have the same sign of charge, the forces $F_{CA}$ and $F_{CB}$ act in opposite directions.

Net force on C is $F_{net} = F_{CB} - F_{CA} = 2F - F = F$.

Therefore, $F_{net} = 3 \times 10^{-5}$ N.

Comparing with $x \times 10^{-5}$ N, we get $x = 3$.

Answer: $3$

</details>

- **(C)** $x = 3L$
- **(D)** $x = L$

**Correct Option:** **(A) $x = 2L$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the proton be placed at a coordinate $x$ on the x-axis.

For the proton to be in equilibrium, the net electric field at its position must be zero.

Since $q_1$ and $q_2$ have opposite signs, the equilibrium point must lie outside the region between the two charges. Furthermore, since $|q_1| > |q_2|$, the point must be closer to $q_2$, which means it lies to the right of $q_2$ ($x > L$).

Equating the magnitudes of the electric fields produced by $q_1$ and $q_2$ at position $x$:

$\dfrac{k|q_1|}{x^2} = \dfrac{k|q_2|}{(x - L)^2}$

$\dfrac{k(8q)}{x^2} = \dfrac{k(2q)}{(x - L)^2}$

$\dfrac{2}{x} = \dfrac{1}{x - L}$

$2(x - L) = x$

$2x - 2L = x$

$x = 2L$

Thus, the proton should be placed at $x = 2L$.
