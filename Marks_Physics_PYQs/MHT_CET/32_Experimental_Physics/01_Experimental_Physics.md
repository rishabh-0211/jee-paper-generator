### Q1. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A simple pendulum has a periodic time ' $\mathrm{T}_1$ ' when it is on the surface of earth of radius ' $R$ '. Its periodic time is ' $T_2$ ' when it is taken to a height ' $R$ ' above the earth's surface. The value of $\frac{T_2}{T_1}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** 1
- **(C)** 2
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

The periodic time of a simple pendulum is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$.

On the surface of the earth, the acceleration due to gravity is $g_1 = \dfrac{GM}{R^2}$ and the periodic time is $T_1 = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g_1}}$.

At a height $h = R$ above the earth's surface, the distance from the center of the earth is $r = R + h = 2R$.

The acceleration due to gravity at this height is $g_2 = \dfrac{GM}{(2R)^2} = \dfrac{g_1}{4}$.

The periodic time at this height is $T_2 = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g_2}}$.

Taking the ratio of the periodic times:

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \sqrt{\dfrac{g_1}{g_2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{g_1}{g_1 / 4}} = \sqrt{4} = 2$.

Answer: 2

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A meter scale is supported on a wedge at its centre of gravity. A body of weight ' $w$ ' is suspended from the 20 cm mark and another weight of 25 gram is suspended from 74 cm mark balances it and the meter scale remains perfectly horizontal. Neglecting the weight of the meter scale, the weight of the body is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 33 gram wt.
- **(B)** 30 gram wt.
- **(C)** 20 gram wt.
- **(D)** 15 gram wt.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 20 gram wt.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The meter scale is supported at its centre of gravity, which is at the $50\text{ cm}$ mark.

The distance of the weight $w$ from the pivot is $50 - 20 = 30\text{ cm}$.

The distance of the $25\text{ g}$ weight from the pivot is $74 - 50 = 24\text{ cm}$.

For the meter scale to remain perfectly horizontal, the net torque about the pivot must be zero. Equating the anticlockwise torque to the clockwise torque:

$w \times 30 = 25 \times 24$

$w = \dfrac{25 \times 24}{30}$

$w = 20\text{ gram wt.}$

Answer: 20 gram wt.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two resistance $\mathrm{X}$ and $\mathrm{Y}$ are connected in the two gaps of a meterbridge and the null points is obtained at $20 \mathrm{~cm}$ from zero end. When the resistance of $20 \Omega$ is connected in series with the smaller of the two resistance $\mathrm{X}$ and $\mathrm{Y}$, the null point shifts to $40 \mathrm{~cm}$ from left end. The value of smaller resistance in ohm is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6$
- **(B)** $9$
- **(C)** $12$
- **(D)** $15$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $12$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a meterbridge, $\frac{\mathrm{X}}{\mathrm{Y}}=\frac{l}{100-l}$
In the first case, $l=20 \mathrm{~cm}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{X}}{\mathrm{Y}} & =\frac{20}{100-20}=\frac{20}{80}=\frac{1}{4} \\
\therefore \quad 4 \mathrm{X} & =\mathrm{Y}
\end{aligned}
$$

In the second case, $l^{\prime}=40 \mathrm{~cm}$
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore & \frac{\mathrm{X}^{\prime}}{\mathrm{Y}^{\prime}}=\frac{40}{100-40}= \\
& \text { But, } \mathrm{X}^{\prime}=\mathrm{X}+2 \\
\therefore \quad & \frac{\mathrm{X}+20}{\mathrm{Y}}=\frac{2}{3} \\
\therefore \quad & \frac{\mathrm{X}+20}{4 \mathrm{X}}=\frac{2}{3} \\
\therefore \quad & 8 \mathrm{X}=3 \mathrm{X}+60 \\
\therefore \quad & \mathrm{X}=12 \Omega
\end{array}
$$

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In meter bridge experiment, null point was obtained at a distance ' $l$ ' from left end. The values of resistances in the left and right gap are doubled and then interchanged. The new position of the null point is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(100-l)$
- **(B)** $(100-2 l)$
- **(C)** $(100-3 l)$
- **(D)** $\left(100-\frac{l}{2}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $(100-l)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& \text { For a Metre bridge, } \\
& \frac{\mathrm{X}}{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{l_{\mathrm{X}}}{l_{\mathrm{R}}}
\end{aligned}$
After doubling and interchanging the left and right gap resistances, the ratio of the resistances do not change.
This means the null point does not change.
$\therefore \quad$ The new position will remain at $(100-l)$.

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2020 (15 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A glass convex lens is of refractive index $1 \cdot 55$ with both faces of same radius of curvature. What will be the radius of curvature if focal length is to be $20 \mathrm{~cm}$ ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $22 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(B)** $21 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(C)** $18 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(D)** $20 \mathrm{~cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $22 \mathrm{~cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Lens maker's formula,
\(\frac{1}{\mathrm{f}}=(\mu-1)\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_{1}}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_{2}}\right)\)
Here, \(\mathrm{f}=20 \mathrm{~cm}, \mu=1.55, \mathrm{R}_{1}=\mathrm{R}, \mathrm{R}_{2}=-\mathrm{R}\)
\(\begin{aligned}
&\frac{1}{20}=(1.55-1)\left(\frac{1}{R}-\frac{1}{(-R)}\right) \text { or } \frac{1}{20}=0.55 \times \frac{2}{R} \\
&\Rightarrow R=1.1 \times 20=22 \mathrm{~cm}
\end{aligned}\)

</details>
