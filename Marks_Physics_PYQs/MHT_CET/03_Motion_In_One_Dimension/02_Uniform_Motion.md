### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body moving with uniform acceleration travel $16$ m in the third second of its motion and $24$ m in the fifth second of its motion. The velocity of the body in $10$ second is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $42$ m/s
- **(B)** $46$ m/s
- **(C)** $50$ m/s
- **(D)** $56$ m/s

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $46$ m/s**

**Detailed Solution:**

The distance traveled by a body in the $n$-th second is given by the formula:

$S_n = u + \dfrac{a}{2}(2n - 1)$

For the 3rd second ($n = 3$):

$S_3 = u + \dfrac{a}{2}(2 \times 3 - 1) = u + \dfrac{5a}{2} = 16$

For the 5th second ($n = 5$):

$S_5 = u + \dfrac{a}{2}(2 \times 5 - 1) = u + \dfrac{9a}{2} = 24$

Subtracting the first equation from the second equation:

$\dfrac{9a}{2} - \dfrac{5a}{2} = 24 - 16$

$2a = 8 \Rightarrow a = 4$ m/s$^2$

Substituting $a = 4$ into the first equation:

$u + \dfrac{5 \times 4}{2} = 16$

$u + 10 = 16 \Rightarrow u = 6$ m/s

The velocity of the body at the end of $10$ seconds is given by:

$v = u + at$

$v = 6 + 4 \times 10 = 46$ m/s

Answer: $46$ m/s

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A car passes three points A, B and C at $3$ m/s, $6$ m/s and $9$ m/s respectively in a straight line with uniform acceleration. If distance $AB = 60$ m then find the distance BC.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $100$ m
- **(B)** $75$ m
- **(C)** $200$ m
- **(D)** $125$ m

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $100$ m**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the equation of motion $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$ for the interval AB:

$v_B^2 - v_A^2 = 2a(AB)$

$6^2 - 3^2 = 2a(60)$

$36 - 9 = 120a$

$27 = 120a \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{27}{120} = \dfrac{9}{40}$ m/s$^2$

Applying the same equation for the interval BC:

$v_C^2 - v_B^2 = 2a(BC)$

$9^2 - 6^2 = 2 \times \dfrac{9}{40} \times BC$

$81 - 36 = \dfrac{9}{20} \times BC$

$45 = \dfrac{9}{20} \times BC$

$BC = \dfrac{45 \times 20}{9} = 100$ m

Answer: $100$ m

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If a ball is thrown vertically upwards with speed ' $u$ ', the distance covered by it during the last ' $t$ ' second of its ascent is ( $g=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2} g t^2$
- **(B)** ut
- **(C)** $(u+g t) t$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{ut}-\frac{1}{2} g \mathrm{t}^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{1}{2} g t^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the total time of ascent be $T$. At the highest point, the final velocity is $0$.

Using $v = u - gT$, we get $T = \dfrac{u}{g}$.

The distance covered in the last $t$ seconds of ascent is the distance covered from time $(T-t)$ to $T$.

By the symmetry of motion under gravity, the distance covered in the last $t$ seconds of ascent is exactly equal to the distance covered in the first $t$ seconds of free fall from the highest point.

For a freely falling body from the highest point, the initial velocity is $0$.

The distance covered in time $t$ is given by $s = 0 + \dfrac{1}{2}gt^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$.

Answer: $\frac{1}{2} g t^2$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A car moving at a speed ' $V$ ' is stopped in a certain distance when the brakes produce a deceleration 'a'. If the speed of the car is 'nv', what must be the deceleration of the car to stop it in the same distance?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\mathrm{n}} \cdot \mathrm{a}$
- **(B)** n.a
- **(C)** $\mathrm{n}^2 \cdot \mathrm{a}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{n}^3 \cdot \mathrm{a}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\mathrm{n}^2 \cdot \mathrm{a}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the third equation of motion, $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$.

For the first case, the final velocity is zero, initial velocity is $V$, and deceleration is $a$.

$0 = V^2 - 2as \Rightarrow s = \dfrac{V^2}{2a}$

For the second case, the initial velocity is $nV$, final velocity is zero, and let the new deceleration be $a'$.

$0 = (nV)^2 - 2a's \Rightarrow s = \dfrac{n^2 V^2}{2a'}$

Since the stopping distance $s$ is the same in both cases, we equate the two expressions for $s$:

$\dfrac{V^2}{2a} = \dfrac{n^2 V^2}{2a'}$

$a' = n^2 a$

Answer: $\mathrm{n}^2 \cdot \mathrm{a}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body starts from rest and moves with a uniform acceleration. The ratio of the distance covered by the body in the $\mathrm{n}^{\text {th }}$ second of its motion to the total distance travelled in n second is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{2}{n^2}-\frac{1}{n}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n^2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{2}{n}-\frac{1}{n^2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2}{n^2}+\frac{1}{n}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{2}{n}-\frac{1}{n^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial velocity be $u = 0$ and the uniform acceleration be $a$.

The distance covered by the body in the $n^{\text{th}}$ second is given by:
$S_n = u + \dfrac{a}{2}(2n - 1)$

Since $u = 0$, we get:
$S_n = \dfrac{a}{2}(2n - 1)$

The total distance travelled by the body in $n$ seconds is given by:
$S = un + \dfrac{1}{2}an^2$

Since $u = 0$, we get:
$S = \dfrac{1}{2}an^2$

The required ratio is:
$\dfrac{S_n}{S} = \dfrac{\dfrac{a}{2}(2n - 1)}{\dfrac{1}{2}an^2}$

$\dfrac{S_n}{S} = \dfrac{2n - 1}{n^2} = \dfrac{2}{n} - \dfrac{1}{n^2}$

Answer: $\frac{2}{n}-\frac{1}{n^2}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two bodies A and B move in same straight line starting from same position. Body A moves with constant velocity 'u' and body B moves with constant acceleration ' $a$ '. When their velocities become equal, the distance between them is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{u}}{2 \mathrm{a}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{u^2}{4 a}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{u}^2}{\mathrm{a}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{u}^2}{2 \mathrm{a}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\mathrm{u}^2}{2 \mathrm{a}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the bodies start at $t=0$ from the same position.

Velocity of body A at time $t$ is $v_A = u$.

Assuming body B starts from rest, its velocity at time $t$ is $v_B = at$.

The time when their velocities become equal is given by $v_A = v_B$.

$u = at \Rightarrow t = \dfrac{u}{a}$

Distance traveled by body A in time $t$ is $x_A = ut = u \left(\dfrac{u}{a}\right) = \dfrac{u^2}{a}$.

Distance traveled by body B in time $t$ is $x_B = \dfrac{1}{2}at^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}a \left(\dfrac{u}{a}\right)^2 = \dfrac{u^2}{2a}$.

The distance between them at this instant is $x_A - x_B = \dfrac{u^2}{a} - \dfrac{u^2}{2a} = \dfrac{u^2}{2a}$.

Answer: $\frac{\mathrm{u}^2}{2 \mathrm{a}}$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A body travelling with uniform acceleration crosses two points A and B with velocities $20 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ and $30 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ respectively. The speed of the body at mid point of A and B is (nearly)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $25 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(B)** $25.5 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(C)** $24 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(D)** $10 \sqrt{6} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $25.5 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $v_A$ and $v_B$ be the velocities at points A and B respectively.

Let $s$ be the distance between A and B, and $a$ be the uniform acceleration.

Using the third equation of motion for the journey from A to B:

$v_B^2 = v_A^2 + 2as \Rightarrow as = \dfrac{v_B^2 - v_A^2}{2}$

Let $v_M$ be the velocity at the midpoint. The distance from A to the midpoint is $\dfrac{s}{2}$.

$v_M^2 = v_A^2 + 2a\left(\dfrac{s}{2}\right) = v_A^2 + as$

Substituting the value of $as$:

$v_M^2 = v_A^2 + \dfrac{v_B^2 - v_A^2}{2} = \dfrac{v_A^2 + v_B^2}{2}$

$v_M = \sqrt{\dfrac{v_A^2 + v_B^2}{2}}$

Given $v_A = 20 \text{ m/s}$ and $v_B = 30 \text{ m/s}$:

$v_M = \sqrt{\dfrac{20^2 + 30^2}{2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{400 + 900}{2}} = \sqrt{650}$

Since $25.5^2 = 650.25$, we get $v_M \approx 25.5 \text{ m/s}$.

Answer: $25.5 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ball is released from the top of a tower of height H m. It takes T second to reach the ground. The height of the ball from the ground after $\frac{T}{4}$ second is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{13 \mathrm{H}}{14}$
- **(B)** $\frac{15 \mathrm{H}}{16}$
- **(C)** $\frac{11 \mathrm{H}}{12}$
- **(D)** $\frac{9 \mathrm{H}}{10}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{15 \mathrm{H}}{16}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the second equation of motion for a freely falling body, the total height of the tower is given by

$H = \dfrac{1}{2} g T^2$

The distance fallen by the ball from the top of the tower in time $t = \dfrac{T}{4}$ is

$h = \dfrac{1}{2} g \left( \dfrac{T}{4} \right)^2$

$h = \dfrac{1}{16} \left( \dfrac{1}{2} g T^2 \right)$

Substituting $H = \dfrac{1}{2} g T^2$, we get

$h = \dfrac{H}{16}$

The height of the ball from the ground after $\dfrac{T}{4}$ seconds is

$H' = H - h$

$H' = H - \dfrac{H}{16} = \dfrac{15H}{16}$

Answer: $\frac{15 \mathrm{H}}{16}$

**Options:**

- **(B)** 8 cm
- **(C)** 10 cm
- **(D)** 12 cm

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 10 cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the first part of the penetration:

$0 = \dfrac{v^2}{4} + 2\left(-\dfrac{v^2}{80}\right)x$

- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{P}^{2}}{\mu \mathrm{gM}^{2}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{P}^{2}}{2 \mu \mathrm{m}^{2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{\mathrm{P}^{2}}{2 \mu \mathrm{gM}^{2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial velocity $\mathrm{u}=\frac{\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{m}}$
Final velocity $\mathrm{v}=0$ (as the vehicle must stop)
Force of friction $=\mu \mathrm{mg}$
(where $\mathrm{g}$ is acceleration due to gravity)
Acceleration due to friction $=-\frac{\mu \mathrm{mg}}{\mathrm{m}}=-\mu \mathrm{g}$
(-ve sign shows that it is retardation )
Using the kinematic expression
$$
\mathrm{v}^{2}=\mathrm{u}^{2}=2 \mathrm{as}
$$
and inserting various values we get stopping distance $s$
$$
\begin{array}{l}
(0)^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{p}^{2}}{\mathrm{~m}^{2}}=2(-\mu \mathrm{g}) \mathrm{s} \\
\Rightarrow \mathrm{s}=\frac{\mathrm{p}^{2}}{2 \mu^{2} \mu \mathrm{g}}
\end{array}
$$

### Q21. MHT CET 2020 (16 Oct Shift 1)

A moving body is covering distances which are proportional to square of the time. Then the acceleration of the body is

**Options:**

- **(A)** decreasing.
- **(B)** Constant but not Zero
- **(C)** Zero
- **(D)** Increasing

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Constant but not Zero**

**Detailed Solution:**

$s \propto t^{2}$
$s=k t^{2}$
$\frac{d s}{d t}=2 k t$
$\frac{d^{2} s}{d t^{2}}=2 k$

The distance $x$ covered in time $t$ by a body having initial velocity $u$ and having constant acceleration $a$ is given by $x=u t+\frac{1}{2} a t^{2} .$ This result follows from

- **(B)** Newton's second law

The equation of motion, $x=u t+\frac{1}{2} a t^{2}$ does not follow any of the Newton's laws of motion.
