### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A juggler throws balls into air. He throws one ball whenever the previous one is at its highest point. If he throws 4 balls in one second, the height from which each ball returns is nearly [acceleration due to gravity is $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30 \text{ cm}$
- **(B)** $40 \text{ cm}$
- **(C)** $50 \text{ cm}$
- **(D)** $60 \text{ cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $30 \text{ cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The juggler throws 4 balls in one second, so the time interval between two consecutive throws is $\Delta t = \dfrac{1}{4} = 0.25 \text{ s}$.

Since a new ball is thrown when the previous one is at its highest point, the time taken by each ball to reach its maximum height is $t = 0.25 \text{ s}$.

At the maximum height, the vertical velocity becomes zero. Using the equation of motion for the upward journey:

$h = \dfrac{1}{2} g t^2$

Substituting the given values ($g = 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$ and $t = 0.25 \text{ s}$):

$h = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times (0.25)^2$

$h = 4.9 \times 0.0625$

$h = 0.30625 \text{ m}$

$h = 30.625 \text{ cm}$

The height from which each ball returns is nearly $30 \text{ cm}$.

Answer: $30 \text{ cm}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ball is released from height 'h' which makes perfectly elastic collision with ground. The frequency of periodic vibratory motion is (g=acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Time taken by the ball to fall from height $h$ to the ground is given by $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$.

Since the collision is perfectly elastic, the ball rebounds with the same speed and reaches the same height $h$. The time taken to go up is also $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$.

The total time period of one complete oscillation is $T = 2t = 2\sqrt{\dfrac{2h}{g}}$.

The frequency of the periodic motion is $f = \dfrac{1}{T} = \dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{g}{2h}}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A stone falls from the top of a tower of height $100$ m and at the same time another stone is projected vertically upwards from the ground with a velocity $25$ m/s. The two stones meet after

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2$ s
- **(B)** $5$ s
- **(C)** $4$ s
- **(D)** $3$ s

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4$ s**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the two stones meet after time $t$.

For the stone falling from the top of the tower:
Displacement downwards, $h_1 = \dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$

For the stone projected upwards from the ground:
Displacement upwards, $h_2 = 25t - \dfrac{1}{2}gt^2$

When the two stones meet, the sum of their displacements is equal to the height of the tower.
$h_1 + h_2 = 100$

$\dfrac{1}{2}gt^2 + 25t - \dfrac{1}{2}gt^2 = 100$

$25t = 100$

$t = 4$ s

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Spherical insulating ball and a spherical metallic ball of same size and mass are dropped from the same height. Choose the correct statement out of the following {Assume negligible air friction}

**Options:**

- **(A)** Time taken by them to reach the earth's surface will be independent of the properties of their materials.
- **(B)** Insulating ball will reach the earth's surface earlier than the metal ball.
- **(C)** Both will reach the earth's surface simultaneously.
- **(D)** Metal ball will reach the earth's surface earlier than the insulating ball.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Insulating ball will reach the earth's surface earlier than the metal ball.**

**Detailed Solution:**

As the metallic ball falls through the Earth's magnetic field, it cuts the magnetic field lines, which induces an electromotive force (EMF) in it.

Since the ball is a conductor, this induced EMF generates eddy currents within the metallic ball.

According to Lenz's law, these eddy currents oppose the motion that causes them, creating an upward magnetic retarding force.

As a result, the net downward acceleration of the metallic ball becomes less than the acceleration due to gravity $g$.

The insulating ball is a non-conductor, so no eddy currents are induced in it. It falls with the full acceleration $g$.

Because the insulating ball has a greater downward acceleration, it will reach the Earth's surface earlier than the metallic ball.

Answer: Insulating ball will reach the earth's surface earlier than the metal ball.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2023 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A small steel ball is dropped from a height of $1.5 \mathrm{~m}$ into a glycerine jar. The ball reaches the bottom of the jar 1.5 second after it was dropped. If the retardation is $2.66 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$, the height of the glycerine in the jar is about (acceleration due to gravity $g=9.8 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^2$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $7.0 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(B)** $7.5 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(C)** $5.5 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(D)** $3.2 \mathrm{~m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5.5 \mathrm{~m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The velocity of the ball after it has been dropped from height till it reaches the glycerine surface is
$\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{i}}^2=0+2 \mathrm{gh} \quad \quad \ldots .\left(\because \mathrm{v}^2-\mathrm{u}^2=2 \mathrm{gh}\right)$
$v_i^2=2 \times 9.8 \times 1.5$
$v_i^2=29.4$
The velocity of the ball after it enters glycerine is $v_f^2=v_i^2-2 g h$
$0=29.4-(2 \times 2.66 \times h)$
$\therefore \quad \mathrm{h}=\frac{29.4}{2 \times 2.66}=5.5 \mathrm{~m}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2023 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ball is projected vertically upwards from ground. It reaches a height ' $h$ ' in time $t_1$, continues its motion and then takes a time $t_2$ to reach ground. The height $h$ in terms of $g, t_1$ and $t_2$ is $(g=$ acceleration due to gravity)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2} \frac{\mathrm{gt}_1}{\mathrm{t}_2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{gt}_1 \mathrm{t}_2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{g} \mathrm{t}_1 \mathrm{t}_2$
- **(D)** $2 \mathrm{gt}_1 \mathrm{t}_2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{gt}_1 \mathrm{t}_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We know,
$$
\mathrm{S}=\mathrm{ut}+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{at}^2
$$

The total time required for the ball to go up and reach the ground is $t=t_1+t_2$, and the total displacement is zero.
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \quad 0=\mathrm{u}\left(\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{t}_2\right)+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~g}\left(\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{t}_2\right)^2 \\
& \therefore \quad \mathrm{u}=\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~g}\left(\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{t}_2\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

The displacement in time $t_1$ is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{h} & =\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~g}\left(\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{t}_2\right) \mathrm{t}_1-\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{gt}_1^2 \\
\mathrm{~h} & =\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{gt}_1\left(\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{t}_2-\mathrm{t}_1\right) \\
\therefore \quad \mathrm{h} & =\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{gt}_1 \mathrm{t}_2
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

- **(B)** $8 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$

$\begin{aligned}

& u=8 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}$

### Q8. MHT CET 2023 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body is released from the top of a tower ' $\mathrm{H}$ ' metre high. It takes $t$ second to reach the ground. The height of the body $\frac{t}{2}$ second after release is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{H}}{2}$ metre from ground
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{H}}{4}$ metre from ground
- **(C)** $3 \frac{\mathrm{H}}{4}$ metre from ground
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{H}}{6}$ metre from ground

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3 \frac{\mathrm{H}}{4}$ metre from ground**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the body be at $\mathrm{x}$ from the top after $\frac{\mathrm{t}}{2} \mathrm{~s}$.
$\begin{aligned}
\therefore \quad \mathrm{x} & =\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~g} \frac{\mathrm{t}^2}{4}=\frac{\mathrm{gt}^2}{8}.... (i) \\
\mathrm{H} & =\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{gt}^2 .... (ii)
\end{aligned}$
Eliminating $\mathrm{t}$ from (i) and (ii), we get $\frac{8 \mathrm{x}}{\mathrm{g}}=\frac{2 \mathrm{H}}{\mathrm{g}} \Rightarrow \mathrm{x}=\frac{\mathrm{H}}{4}$
$\therefore \quad$ Height of the body from the ground $=\mathrm{H}-\frac{\mathrm{H}}{4}=\frac{3 \mathrm{H}}{4}$ metres

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2021 (21 Sep Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body at rest falls through a height ' $h$ ' with velocity ' $\mathrm{V}$ '. If it has to fall down further for its velocity to become three times, the distance travelled in that interval is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 8 h
- **(B)** 6 h
- **(C)** 4 h
- **(D)** 12 h

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 8 h**

**Detailed Solution:**

The body acquired velocity $\mathrm{V}$ when it falls through a height $\mathrm{h}$, starting from rest.
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \mathrm{V}^2=2 \mathrm{gh} \\
& \therefore \mathrm{h}=\frac{\mathrm{V}^2}{2 \mathrm{~g}}
\end{aligned}
$$
If it falls further and attains velocity $3 \mathrm{~V}$ and if the total height through which it falls is h', then
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left(3 \mathrm{~V}^2\right)=2 \mathrm{gh}^{\prime} \\
& \therefore 9 \mathrm{~V}^2=2 \mathrm{gh}^{\prime} \\
& \therefore \mathrm{h}^{\prime}=\frac{9 \mathrm{~V}^2}{2 \mathrm{~g}}=9 \mathrm{~h} \\
& \therefore \mathrm{h}^{\prime}-\mathrm{h}=9 \mathrm{~h}-\mathrm{h}=8 \mathrm{~h}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
