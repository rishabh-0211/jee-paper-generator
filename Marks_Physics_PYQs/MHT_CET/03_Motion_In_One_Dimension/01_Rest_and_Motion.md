### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The minute hand of a wall-clock is $10$ cm long. The average velocity of the tip of the minute hand between $6.00$ am to $7.30$ am is

**Options:**

- **(A)** zero
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{270}$ cm/s
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{180}$ cm/s
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{90}$ cm/s

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{1}{270}$ cm/s**

**Detailed Solution:**

Length of the minute hand, $R = 10$ cm.

At $6.00$ am, the minute hand is at the $12$ mark.

At $7.30$ am, the minute hand is at the $6$ mark.

The displacement of the tip of the minute hand is the straight line distance between these two positions, which is the diameter of the circular path.

Displacement $= 2R = 2 \times 10 = 20$ cm.

The time elapsed between $6.00$ am and $7.30$ am is $1.5$ hours.

Time $= 1.5 \times 60 \times 60 = 5400$ s.

Average velocity $= \dfrac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}} = \dfrac{20}{5400} = \dfrac{1}{270}$ cm/s.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{270}$ cm/s

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A person travelling in a straight line moves with uniform velocity $V_1$ for a distance $x$ and with a uniform velocity $V_2$ for the next $\dfrac{3x}{2}$ distance. The average velocity in this motion is $\dfrac{50}{7}$ m/s. If $V_1$ is $5$ m/s then $V_2$ (in m/s) is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5$
- **(B)** $15$
- **(C)** $10$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $10$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Total distance travelled is $d = x + \dfrac{3x}{2} = \dfrac{5x}{2}$

Total time taken is $t = t_1 + t_2 = \dfrac{x}{V_1} + \dfrac{3x}{2V_2}$

Average velocity $V_{avg} = \dfrac{d}{t} = \dfrac{\dfrac{5x}{2}}{\dfrac{x}{V_1} + \dfrac{3x}{2V_2}}$

Given $V_{avg} = \dfrac{50}{7}$ m/s and $V_1 = 5$ m/s:

$\dfrac{50}{7} = \dfrac{\dfrac{5}{2}}{\dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{3}{2V_2}}$

$\dfrac{10}{7} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2}}{\dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{3}{2V_2}}$

$\dfrac{10}{7} \left( \dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{3}{2V_2} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\dfrac{2}{7} + \dfrac{15}{7V_2} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\dfrac{15}{7V_2} = \dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{2}{7} = \dfrac{3}{14}$

$V_2 = \dfrac{15 \times 14}{7 \times 3} = 10$ m/s

Answer: $10$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A student runs along a circular track of diameter $80$ m. The distance travelled and the magnitude of displacement of the student when he covers $\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^{th}$ of the circle is (in m)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $60\pi,\,40\sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** $40\pi,\,60\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $120\pi,\,80\sqrt{2}$
- **(D)** $80\pi,\,120\sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $60\pi,\,40\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Radius of the circular track $R = \dfrac{80}{2} = 40$ m.

Distance travelled by the student in covering $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the circle is given by $d = \dfrac{3}{4} \times 2\pi R$.

$d = \dfrac{3}{4} \times 2\pi \times 40 = 60\pi$ m.

The angle between the initial and final position vectors from the center is $90^{\circ}$ since $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of the circle is covered.

Magnitude of displacement is the straight line distance between the initial and final positions, which is $\sqrt{R^2 + R^2} = \sqrt{2}R$.

Displacement $= 40\sqrt{2}$ m.

Answer: $60\pi,\,40\sqrt{2}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

At any time ' $t$ ', the co-ordinates of moving particle are $x=a t^2$ and $\mathrm{y}=\mathrm{bt}^2$. The speed of the particle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 t \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$
- **(B)** $2 t \sqrt{a^2-b^2}$
- **(C)** $2 t(a+b)$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 \mathrm{t}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{a}^2+\mathrm{b}^2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2 t \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $x = a t^2$ and $y = b t^2$.

The velocity components along the X and Y axes are:

$v_x = \dfrac{dx}{dt} = \dfrac{d}{dt}(a t^2) = 2at$

$v_y = \dfrac{dy}{dt} = \dfrac{d}{dt}(b t^2) = 2bt$

The speed of the particle is the magnitude of the velocity vector:

$v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}$

$v = \sqrt{(2at)^2 + (2bt)^2}$

$v = \sqrt{4a^2t^2 + 4b^2t^2}$

$v = 2t\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$

Answer: $2 t \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$

</details>

- **(C)** $5: 9$
- **(D)** $7: 4$

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5: 9$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The area under the velocity-time graph gives the displacement of the body when considered with the appropriate sign, and the distance travelled when absolute values of the areas are taken.

Let us calculate the area of each segment from the given graph:
Area from $t = 0$ to $t = 2$ s: $A_1 = 3 \times 2 = 6$ m

Area from $t = 3$ to $t = 5$ s: $A_3 = 2 \times 2 = 4$ m
Area from $t = 5$ to $t = 6$ s: $A_4 = -2 \times 1 = -2$ m

$\text{Distance} = 6 + 2 + 4 + 2 + 4 = 18$ m

$\text{Ratio} = \dfrac{10}{18} = \dfrac{5}{9}$

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The co-ordinates of a moving particle at any time ' t ' are given by $x=\alpha \mathrm{t}^3$ and $y=\beta \mathrm{t}^3$ where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are constants. The speed of the particle at time ' $t$ ' is given by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $t \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}$
- **(B)** $3 \mathrm{t} \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}$
- **(C)** $t^2 \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}$
- **(D)** $3 \mathrm{t}^2 \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $3 \mathrm{t}^2 \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The position coordinates of the particle are given by $x = \alpha t^3$ and $y = \beta t^3$.

The velocity components along the x and y axes are:
$v_x = \dfrac{dx}{dt} = \dfrac{d}{dt}(\alpha t^3) = 3\alpha t^2$
$v_y = \dfrac{dy}{dt} = \dfrac{d}{dt}(\beta t^3) = 3\beta t^2$

The speed of the particle is the magnitude of the resultant velocity:
$v = \sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}$

Substituting the values of $v_x$ and $v_y$:
$v = \sqrt{(3\alpha t^2)^2 + (3\beta t^2)^2}$
$v = \sqrt{9\alpha^2 t^4 + 9\beta^2 t^4}$
$v = \sqrt{9t^4(\alpha^2 + \beta^2)}$
$v = 3t^2 \sqrt{\alpha^2 + \beta^2}$

Answer: $3 \mathrm{t}^2 \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

Two cars start from a point at the same time in a straight line and their positions are represented by $\mathrm{x}_1(\mathrm{t})=\mathrm{at}+\mathrm{bt}^2$ and $\mathrm{x}_2(\mathrm{t})=\mathrm{Ft}-\mathrm{t}^2$. At what time do the cars have the same velocity?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{a+F}{2(b-1)}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{F}}{1+\mathrm{b}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{F}}{2(1+\mathrm{b})}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{F}-\mathrm{a}}{2(1+\mathrm{b})}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\mathrm{F}-\mathrm{a}}{2(1+\mathrm{b})}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The position of the first car is given by $x_1(t) = at + bt^2$.

The velocity of the first car is $v_1(t) = \dfrac{dx_1}{dt} = a + 2bt$.

The position of the second car is given by $x_2(t) = Ft - t^2$.

The velocity of the second car is $v_2(t) = \dfrac{dx_2}{dt} = F - 2t$.

Equating the velocities of the two cars:

$v_1(t) = v_2(t)$

$a + 2bt = F - 2t$

$2bt + 2t = F - a$

$2t(b + 1) = F - a$

$t = \dfrac{F - a}{2(1 + b)}$

Answer: $\frac{\mathrm{F}-\mathrm{a}}{2(1+\mathrm{b})}$

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A vehicle runs on a straight road of length ' L '. It travels half the distance with speed V and the remaining distance with speed $\dfrac{\mathrm{V}}{3}$. Its average speed is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{3 \mathrm{~V}}{2}$
- **(B)** V
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{V}}{2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{2 \mathrm{~V}}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{\mathrm{V}}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Total distance is $L$.

Time taken to travel the first half of the distance is $t_1 = \dfrac{L/2}{V} = \dfrac{L}{2V}$.

Time taken to travel the remaining half of the distance is $t_2 = \dfrac{L/2}{V/3} = \dfrac{3L}{2V}$.

Total time taken is $t = t_1 + t_2 = \dfrac{L}{2V} + \dfrac{3L}{2V} = \dfrac{4L}{2V} = \dfrac{2L}{V}$.

Average speed is given by $\dfrac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}} = \dfrac{L}{\dfrac{2L}{V}} = \dfrac{V}{2}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{\mathrm{V}}{2}$

</details>
