### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A beam of light is travelling from region I to region IV. The refractive indices in regions I, II, III and IV are $n$, $\dfrac{n}{2}$, $\dfrac{n}{3}$ and $\dfrac{n}{4}$ respectively. The angle of incidence for which the beam just misses entering region IV is [$\sin 90^\circ = 1$]

<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/may/watermarked_images/20_may_shift_1__q18_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sin^{-1}(1)$
- **(B)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- **(C)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)$
- **(D)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Snell's law for multiple parallel interfaces, the product of the refractive index and the sine of the angle made with the normal remains constant across all regions:

$n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2 = n_3 \sin \theta_3 = n_4 \sin \theta_4$

For the beam to just miss entering region IV, it must undergo total internal reflection at the boundary between region III and region IV. This means the angle of refraction in region IV is $90^\circ$.

Equating the terms for region I and region IV, we get:

$n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_4 \sin 90^\circ$

Given that $n_1 = n$, $\theta_1 = \theta$, and $n_4 = \dfrac{n}{4}$:

$n \sin \theta = \dfrac{n}{4} \times 1$

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{1}{4}$

$\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$

Answer: $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For the same angle of incidence, the angles of refraction in media A, B, C and D are $50^\circ$, $40^\circ$, $30^\circ$ and $20^\circ$ respectively. The speed of light is minimum in medium

**Options:**

- **(A)** A
- **(B)** B
- **(C)** C
- **(D)** D

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) D**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Snell's law, $\mu = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$

The refractive index is also given by $\mu = \dfrac{c}{v}$

Equating the two expressions, we get $\dfrac{c}{v} = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$

$v = c \dfrac{\sin r}{\sin i}$

For a given angle of incidence $i$, the speed of light $v$ is directly proportional to $\sin r$.

Since the sine function is increasing in the interval $(0^\circ, 90^\circ)$, the minimum value of $r$ will result in the minimum speed of light.

The minimum angle of refraction is $20^\circ$, which corresponds to medium D.

Therefore, the speed of light is minimum in medium D.

Answer: D

</details>

- **(C)** $12$ cm

**Detailed Solution:**

The height of the empty part of the container is $H - h$.

The apparent depth of the water when viewed from the top is $h_{app} = \dfrac{h}{\mu}$.

$\dfrac{h}{\mu} = 21 - h$

$\dfrac{3h}{4} = 21 - h$

$\dfrac{3h}{4} + h = 21$

$\dfrac{7h}{4} = 21$

Answer: $12$ cm

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the angle of incidence be $i$ and the angle of refraction be $r$.

Given that the angle of incidence is twice the angle of refraction:

$i = 2r$

According to Snell's law:

$\sin(i) = n \sin(r)$

Substituting $i = 2r$ into the equation:

$\sin(2r) = n \sin(r)$

Using the double angle formula $\sin(2r) = 2 \sin(r) \cos(r)$:

$2 \sin(r) \cos(r) = n \sin(r)$

Assuming $r \neq 0$, dividing both sides by $\sin(r)$ gives:

$2 \cos(r) = n$

$\cos(r) = \dfrac{n}{2}$

$r = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)$

Since the angle of incidence is $i = 2r$, we get:

$i = 2\cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)$

Answer: $2\cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)$

### Q11. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A monochromatic ray of light is incident normally on a thin prism of refracting angle A . The ray is deviated through an angle $(1.15)^{\circ}$ in passing through the prism. The ray reflected internally from the second face emerges from the first face making an angle of $(6.3)^{\circ}$ with the incident ray. The refractive index of the prism is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1.625
- **(B)** 1.575
- **(C)** 1.525
- **(D)** 1.515

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 1.575**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the refracting angle of the thin prism be $A$ and its refractive index be $\mu$.

Since the monochromatic ray is incident normally on the first face, the angle of incidence is $i_1 = 0^{\circ}$, and the angle of refraction is $r_1 = 0^{\circ}$.
The ray strikes the second face at an angle of incidence $r_2 = A$.
For the ray passing through the prism, the angle of emergence $e$ is given by Snell's law:
$\sin e = \mu \sin A$
For a thin prism, angles are small, so $e \approx \mu A$.
The deviation $\delta$ of the emergent ray is:
$\delta = e - A = (\mu - 1)A$
Given that $\delta = 1.15^{\circ}$, we have:
$(\mu - 1)A = 1.15^{\circ}$  ... (1)

Now, consider the ray that is internally reflected from the second face.
The angle of incidence at the second face is $A$, so the angle of reflection is also $A$.
The reflected ray makes an angle of $2A$ with the normal to the first face (which is the direction of the incident ray).
This ray now strikes the first face from inside the prism at an angle of incidence $2A$.
Let the new angle of emergence be $e'$. Using Snell's law at the first face:
$\sin e' = \mu \sin(2A)$
For small angles, $e' \approx \mu(2A) = 2\mu A$.
This emergent ray makes an angle $e'$ with the normal to the first face. Since the incident ray is along the normal, the angle between the emergent ray and the incident ray is $e'$.
Given that this angle is $6.3^{\circ}$, we have:
$2\mu A = 6.3^{\circ}$
$\mu A = 3.15^{\circ}$  ... (2)

Substituting $\mu A = 3.15^{\circ}$ into equation (1):
$\mu A - A = 1.15^{\circ}$
$3.15^{\circ} - A = 1.15^{\circ}$
$A = 2.0^{\circ}$

Now, substituting the value of $A$ back into equation (2):
$\mu (2.0^{\circ}) = 3.15^{\circ}$
$\mu = \dfrac{3.15}{2.0} = 1.575$

Answer: 1.575

</details>
