### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An observer looks at a distant tree of height $10$ m with a telescope of magnifying power of $20$. To the observer, the tree appears

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$ times taller.
- **(B)** $10$ times nearer.
- **(C)** $20$ times nearer.
- **(D)** $20$ times taller.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $20$ times nearer.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnifying power of a telescope is defined as the ratio of the angle subtended by the image at the eye to the angle subtended by the object at the unaided eye.

$m = \dfrac{\beta}{\alpha} = 20$

This means the angle subtended by the image is $20$ times the angle subtended by the actual object. For a distant object, the angular size is inversely proportional to its distance from the observer ($\theta \approx \dfrac{h}{d}$). 

Since the telescope increases the angular size by a factor of $20$ without changing the actual linear height of the tree, the brain interprets this increased angular size as the object being at $\dfrac{1}{20}$th of its original distance. 

Therefore, the tree appears $20$ times nearer to the observer.

Answer: $20$ times nearer.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A simple microscope is used to see the object first in blue light and then in red light. Due to the change from blue to red light, what is the effect on its magnifying power?

**Options:**

- **(A)** decreases
- **(B)** increases
- **(C)** remains constant
- **(D)** first increase and then decreases

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) decreases**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnifying power of a simple microscope is given by $M = 1 + \dfrac{D}{f}$ or $M = \dfrac{D}{f}$.

The focal length of a lens is related to the refractive index by the lens maker's formula: $\dfrac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right)$.

According to Cauchy's relation, the refractive index decreases with an increase in wavelength: $\mu = A + \dfrac{B}{\lambda^2}$.

Since the wavelength of red light is greater than that of blue light ($\lambda_{\text{red}} > \lambda_{\text{blue}}$), the refractive index for red light is less than that for blue light ($\mu_{\text{red}} < \mu_{\text{blue}}$).

Consequently, the focal length of the lens is greater for red light ($f_{\text{red}} > f_{\text{blue}}$).

Since magnifying power is inversely proportional to focal length, the magnifying power decreases when the light is changed from blue to red ($M_{\text{red}} < M_{\text{blue}}$).

Answer: decreases

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The length of the compound microscope is 15 cm . The magnifying power for relaxed eye is 25 . If the focal length of eye lens is 6 cm then the object distance for objective lens will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1.3 cm
- **(B)** 1.5 cm
- **(C)** 1.7 cm
- **(D)** 1.9 cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 1.5 cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a compound microscope in normal adjustment (relaxed eye), the length of the microscope tube is given by:

$L = v_o + f_e$

Substituting the given values $L = 15$ cm and $f_e = 6$ cm:

$15 = v_o + 6$

$v_o = 9$ cm

The magnifying power of a compound microscope for a relaxed eye is given by:

$M = \dfrac{v_o}{u_o} \times \dfrac{D}{f_e}$

where $D$ is the least distance of distinct vision ($D = 25$ cm). Substituting the known values:

$25 = \dfrac{9}{u_o} \times \dfrac{25}{6}$

$1 = \dfrac{9}{6 u_o}$

$u_o = \dfrac{9}{6} = 1.5$ cm

Answer: 1.5 cm

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A person is observing a bacteria through a compound microscope. For better analysis and to improve the resolving power he should

**Options:**

- **(A)** increase the wavelength of light.

- **(C)** decrease the focal length of the eye-piece.

The resolving power of a compound microscope is given by:

$\text{Resolving Power} = \dfrac{2 \mu \sin \theta}{\lambda}$

where $\mu$ is the refractive index of the medium between the object and the objective lens, $\theta$ is the semi-vertical angle of the cone of light entering the objective, and $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light.

To improve the resolving power, one must either decrease the wavelength of light $\lambda$, increase the refractive index of the medium $\mu$, or increase the diameter of the objective lens to increase $\theta$.

Therefore, increasing the refractive index of the medium between the object and the objective lens improves the resolving power.

Answer: increase the refractive index of the medium between the object and objective lens.
