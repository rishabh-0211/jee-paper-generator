### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If a parallel beam of light is incident on spherical mirrors then after reflection they pass through focal point. If F is the focal length and R is the radius of curvature of mirror then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $R = F$
- **(B)** $R = 2F$
- **(C)** $R = F/2$
- **(D)** $R = F/4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $R = 2F$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a spherical mirror, the focal length $F$ is defined as the distance from the pole of the mirror to the principal focus.

For paraxial rays (rays close to the principal axis), the relationship between the focal length $F$ and the radius of curvature $R$ is given by $F = \dfrac{R}{2}$.

Rearranging this relation, we get $R = 2F$.

Answer: $R = 2F$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

The image of an object approaching a convex mirror of radius of curvature 20 m along its optical axis is observed to move from $25 / 3 \mathrm{~m}$ to $50 / 7 \mathrm{~m}$ in 30 second. The speed of the object in $\mathrm{km} / \mathrm{hr}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 5
- **(B)** 2.5
- **(C)** 4
- **(D)** 3

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 3**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a convex mirror, the focal length is $f = \dfrac{R}{2} = \dfrac{20}{2} = +10 \text{ m}$.

Using the mirror formula, $\dfrac{1}{v} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}$

For the first position of the image, $v_1 = \dfrac{25}{3} \text{ m}$:

$\dfrac{1}{u_1} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{3}{25} = \dfrac{5 - 6}{50} = -\dfrac{1}{50}$

$u_1 = -50 \text{ m}$

For the second position of the image, $v_2 = \dfrac{50}{7} \text{ m}$:

$\dfrac{1}{u_2} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{7}{50} = \dfrac{5 - 7}{50} = -\dfrac{2}{50} = -\dfrac{1}{25}$

$u_2 = -25 \text{ m}$

The distance moved by the object is $\Delta u = |-25 - (-50)| = 25 \text{ m}$.

The speed of the object is $v_{obj} = \dfrac{\Delta u}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{25}{30} = \dfrac{5}{6} \text{ m/s}$.

Converting the speed to km/hr:

$v_{obj} = \dfrac{5}{6} \times \dfrac{18}{5} = 3 \text{ km/hr}$

Answer: 3

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A concave mirror of focal length ' $f$ ' produces an image ' $n$ ' time the size of the object. If the image is real, then the distance of the object from the mirror is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(\mathrm{n}-1) \mathrm{f}$
- **(B)** $\left(\dfrac{n-1}{n}\right) \mathrm{f}$
- **(C)** $\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{n}+1}{\mathrm{n}}\right) \mathrm{f}$
- **(D)** $(\mathrm{n}+1) \mathrm{f}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{n}+1}{\mathrm{n}}\right) \mathrm{f}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a concave mirror, the focal length is taken as $-f$ and the object distance as $-u$ (where $f$ and $u$ are positive distances).

Since the image is real, it is inverted, so the magnification $m$ is negative. Thus, $m = -n$.

The magnification formula in terms of focal length and object distance is given by:

$m = \dfrac{f}{f - u}$

Applying the sign convention:

$-n = \dfrac{-f}{-f - (-u)}$

$-n = \dfrac{-f}{-f + u}$

$-n(-f + u) = -f$

$nf - nu = -f$

$nu = nf + f$

$nu = (n + 1)f$

$u = \left(\dfrac{n + 1}{n}\right)f$

The distance of the object from the mirror is $\left(\dfrac{n + 1}{n}\right)f$.

Answer: $\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{n}+1}{\mathrm{n}}\right) \mathrm{f}$

</details>
