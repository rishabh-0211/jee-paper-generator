### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An equilateral prism deviates a ray through $45^\circ$ for the two angles of incidence differing by $20^\circ$. One of the angle of incidence is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $62.5^\circ$
- **(B)** $32.5^\circ$
- **(C)** $52.5^\circ$
- **(D)** $22.5^\circ$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $62.5^\circ$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a prism, the angle of deviation $\delta$ is given by the relation:

$\delta = i + e - A$

where $i$ is the angle of incidence, $e$ is the angle of emergence, and $A$ is the angle of the prism.

For a given deviation, the two possible angles of incidence correspond to $i$ and $e$. Let these be $i_1$ and $i_2$.

Given that the prism is equilateral, $A = 60^\circ$. The deviation is $\delta = 45^\circ$.

Substituting these values, we get:

$i_1 + i_2 = 45^\circ + 60^\circ = 105^\circ$

It is also given that the two angles of incidence differ by $20^\circ$:

$i_1 - i_2 = 20^\circ$

Adding the two equations:

$2i_1 = 125^\circ \Rightarrow i_1 = 62.5^\circ$

Subtracting the two equations:

$2i_2 = 85^\circ \Rightarrow i_2 = 42.5^\circ$

Thus, one of the angles of incidence is $62.5^\circ$.

Answer: $62.5^\circ$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A thin glass prism has refractive index $1.5$. The correct relation between the angle of minimum deviation $(\delta m)$ and angle of refraction $(r)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\delta m = \dfrac{r}{2}$
- **(B)** $\delta m = 2r$
- **(C)** $\delta m = r$
- **(D)** $\delta m = \dfrac{3r}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\delta m = r$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a thin prism, the angle of minimum deviation is given by $\delta_m = (\mu - 1)A$.

At the position of minimum deviation, the angle of refraction $r$ inside the prism is $r = \dfrac{A}{2}$, which gives $A = 2r$.

Given the refractive index $\mu = 1.5$.

Substituting the values into the deviation formula, we get:

$\delta_m = (1.5 - 1)(2r)$

$\delta_m = 0.5 \times 2r$

$\delta_m = r$

Answer: $\delta m = r$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The prism has refracting angle A. The second refracting surface of the prism is silvered. Light ray falling on first refracting surface with angle of incidence $2A$, reaches the second surface and returns back through same path due to reflection at the silvered surface. The refractive index of the material of prism is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \sin A$
- **(B)** $2 \cos A$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{2} \sin A$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{2} \cos A$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2 \cos A$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a prism, the angle of the prism is given by $A = r_1 + r_2$.

Since the light ray retraces its path after reflection from the silvered second surface, it must strike the second surface normally. Thus, the angle of incidence at the second surface is $r_2 = 0$.

Substituting $r_2 = 0$ into the prism formula gives $r_1 = A$.

Applying Snell's law at the first refracting surface:

$\sin i = \mu \sin r_1$

Given the angle of incidence $i = 2A$, we have:

$\sin(2A) = \mu \sin A$

Using the trigonometric identity $\sin(2A) = 2 \sin A \cos A$:

$2 \sin A \cos A = \mu \sin A$

$\mu = 2 \cos A$

Answer: $2 \cos A$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The angle of minimum deviation produced by a thin prism in air is $\delta_1$. What will be the minimum deviation ($\delta_2$) if the prism is immersed in liquid ?
$\left[ {}^a n_g = \dfrac{3}{2}, \ {}^a n_\ell = \dfrac{1}{3} \right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\delta_2 = 3\,\delta_1$
- **(B)** $\delta_2 = 5\,\delta_1$
- **(C)** $\delta_2 = 7\,\delta_1$
- **(D)** $\delta_2 = 9\,\delta_1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\delta_2 = 7\,\delta_1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a thin prism, the angle of minimum deviation is given by $\delta = (n_{rel} - 1)A$, where $n_{rel}$ is the refractive index of the prism relative to the surrounding medium and $A$ is the angle of the prism.

When the prism is in air, the deviation $\delta_1$ is:
$\delta_1 = ({}^a n_g - 1)A$
Substituting ${}^a n_g = \dfrac{3}{2}$:
$\delta_1 = \left(\dfrac{3}{2} - 1\right)A = \dfrac{A}{2}$

When the prism is immersed in the liquid, the relative refractive index of the glass with respect to the liquid is:
${}^\ell n_g = \dfrac{{}^a n_g}{{}^a n_\ell} = \dfrac{\dfrac{3}{2}}{\dfrac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{9}{2}$

The new deviation $\delta_2$ in the liquid is:
$\delta_2 = ({}^\ell n_g - 1)A$
$\delta_2 = \left(\dfrac{9}{2} - 1\right)A = \dfrac{7A}{2}$

Taking the ratio of $\delta_2$ to $\delta_1$:
$\dfrac{\delta_2}{\delta_1} = \dfrac{\dfrac{7A}{2}}{\dfrac{A}{2}} = 7$

$\delta_2 = 7\delta_1$

Answer: $\delta_2 = 7\,\delta_1$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A glass prism ' $A$ ' deviates the red and blue rays through $10^{\circ}$ and $12^{\circ}$ respectively. A second prism ' B ' deviates them through $8^{\circ}$ and $10^{\circ}$ respectively. The ratio of their dispersive powers is (A to B)

**Options:**

- **(A)** 9:13
- **(B)** $4: 5$
- **(C)** $9: 11$
- **(D)** $8: 9$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $9: 11$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The dispersive power of a prism is given by $\omega = \dfrac{\delta_b - \delta_r}{\delta_y}$

The mean deviation $\delta_y$ is the average of the deviations of the blue and red rays: $\delta_y = \dfrac{\delta_b + \delta_r}{2}$

For prism A:
$\delta_b = 12^{\circ}$, $\delta_r = 10^{\circ}$
$\delta_{yA} = \dfrac{12^{\circ} + 10^{\circ}}{2} = 11^{\circ}$
$\omega_A = \dfrac{12^{\circ} - 10^{\circ}}{11^{\circ}} = \dfrac{2}{11}$

For prism B:
$\delta_b = 10^{\circ}$, $\delta_r = 8^{\circ}$
$\delta_{yB} = \dfrac{10^{\circ} + 8^{\circ}}{2} = 9^{\circ}$
$\omega_B = \dfrac{10^{\circ} - 8^{\circ}}{9^{\circ}} = \dfrac{2}{9}$

The ratio of their dispersive powers is:
$\dfrac{\omega_A}{\omega_B} = \dfrac{\dfrac{2}{11}}{\dfrac{2}{9}} = \dfrac{9}{11}$

### Q11. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ray of light is incident on a medium of refractive index ' $\mu$ ' at an angle of incidence ' $i$ ': On refraction in the medium ' $\delta$ ' is the angle of deviation. Then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{\mu}=\cos \delta-\dfrac{\sin \delta}{\tan i}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{\mu}=\sin \delta-\dfrac{\cos \delta}{\tan i}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{\mu}=\cos \delta-\sin \delta \cdot \tan i$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{\mu}=\sin \delta-\cos \delta \cdot \tan i$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{1}{\mu}=\cos \delta-\dfrac{\sin \delta}{\tan i}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By Snell's law, we have $\sin i = \mu \sin r$, which gives $\dfrac{1}{\mu} = \dfrac{\sin r}{\sin i}$.

The angle of deviation is $\delta = i - r$, which implies $r = i - \delta$.

Substituting this into the expression for $\dfrac{1}{\mu}$:

$\dfrac{1}{\mu} = \dfrac{\sin(i - \delta)}{\sin i}$

Expanding the numerator using the identity $\sin(A - B) = \sin A \cos B - \cos A \sin B$:

$\dfrac{1}{\mu} = \dfrac{\sin i \cos \delta - \cos i \sin \delta}{\sin i}$

Dividing each term by $\sin i$:

$\dfrac{1}{\mu} = \cos \delta - \cot i \sin \delta$

$\dfrac{1}{\mu} = \cos \delta - \dfrac{\sin \delta}{\tan i}$

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{\mu}=\cos \delta-\dfrac{\sin \delta}{\tan i}$
