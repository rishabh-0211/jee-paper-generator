### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The focal length of a glass convex lens in air is $f_a$ and that in water is $f_w$. The refractive index from air to glass is $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and that from air to water is $\dfrac{4}{3}$. The ratio $f_a:f_w$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:2$
- **(B)** $1:3$
- **(C)** $1:4$
- **(D)** $1:8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1:4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the Lens Maker's formula for the lens in air:

$\dfrac{1}{f_a} = (\mu_g - 1)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right)$

Substituting $\mu_g = \dfrac{3}{2}$:

$\dfrac{1}{f_a} = \left(\dfrac{3}{2} - 1\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right)$

For the lens in water, the relative refractive index is $\dfrac{\mu_g}{\mu_w}$:

$\dfrac{1}{f_w} = \left(\dfrac{\mu_g}{\mu_w} - 1\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right)$

Substituting $\mu_g = \dfrac{3}{2}$ and $\mu_w = \dfrac{4}{3}$:

$\dfrac{1}{f_w} = \left(\dfrac{3/2}{4/3} - 1\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right) = \left(\dfrac{9}{8} - 1\right)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{8}\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right)$

Dividing the equation for $f_w$ by the equation for $f_a$:

$\dfrac{f_w}{f_a} = \dfrac{1/2}{1/8} = 4$

$\Rightarrow \dfrac{f_a}{f_w} = \dfrac{1}{4}$

The ratio $f_a : f_w$ is $1 : 4$.

Answer: $1:4$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The radii of curvature of both the surfaces of a convex lens are equal. One of the surfaces is made plane by grinding. The new focal length and power of the lens, respectively, are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{f}{2}$ and $2P$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{2} f$ and $\dfrac{P}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(C)** $2f$ and $\dfrac{P}{2}$
- **(D)** $f$ and $P$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2f$ and $\dfrac{P}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the initial equiconvex lens, the radii of curvature are $R_1 = R$ and $R_2 = -R$.

Using the lens maker's formula, the initial focal length $f$ is given by

$\dfrac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1) \left( \dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2} \right) = (\mu - 1) \left( \dfrac{1}{R} - \dfrac{1}{-R} \right) = \dfrac{2(\mu - 1)}{R}$

The initial power is $P = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{2(\mu - 1)}{R}$.

When one surface is made plane by grinding, its radius of curvature becomes infinity. Let the new radii be $R_1' = R$ and $R_2' = \infty$.

The new focal length $f'$ is

$\dfrac{1}{f'} = (\mu - 1) \left( \dfrac{1}{R} - \dfrac{1}{\infty} \right) = \dfrac{\mu - 1}{R}$

Comparing the two equations, we get

$\dfrac{1}{f'} = \dfrac{1}{2} \left( \dfrac{2(\mu - 1)}{R} \right) = \dfrac{1}{2f}$

$\Rightarrow f' = 2f$

The new power $P'$ is

$P' = \dfrac{1}{f'} = \dfrac{1}{2f} = \dfrac{P}{2}$

Answer: $2f$ and $\dfrac{P}{2}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1/3$ cm and $1/5$ cm
- **(B)** $1/5$ cm and $1/7$ cm
- **(C)** $1/3$ cm and $1/7$ cm
- **(D)** $1/6$ cm and $1/9$ cm

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1/5$ cm and $1/7$ cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the Lens Maker's formula, the power of a lens is given by:

$P = (\mu - 1) \left( \dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2} \right)$

For the initial biconvex lens, $R_1 = \dfrac{1}{6}$ cm and $R_2 = -\dfrac{1}{6}$ cm. Substituting these values:

$P = (\mu - 1) (6 - (-6)) = 12(\mu - 1)$

For the new biconvex lens, the power remains the same. Let its radii of curvature be $R_1'$ and $R_2'$ (where $R_1'$ is positive and $R_2'$ is negative). Let their magnitudes be $r_1$ and $r_2$.

$P' = (\mu - 1) \left( \dfrac{1}{r_1} + \dfrac{1}{r_2} \right)$

Equating the powers $P = P'$:

$\dfrac{1}{r_1} + \dfrac{1}{r_2} = 12$

Checking the given options for the sum of the reciprocals of the radii:

For $1/3$ cm and $1/5$ cm: $3 + 5 = 8 \neq 12$

For $1/5$ cm and $1/7$ cm: $5 + 7 = 12$

For $1/3$ cm and $1/7$ cm: $3 + 7 = 10 \neq 12$

For $1/6$ cm and $1/9$ cm: $6 + 9 = 15 \neq 12$

The combination of $1/5$ cm and $1/7$ cm satisfies the condition.

Answer: $1/5$ cm and $1/7$ cm

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An achromatic convergent doublet of two lenses in contact has a power of $+2D$. The convex lens has power $+5D$. What is the ratio of the dispersive powers of the convergent and divergent lenses?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2:5$
- **(B)** $3:5$
- **(C)** $5:2$
- **(D)** $5:3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3:5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Power of the combination, $P = +2D$

Power of the convex (convergent) lens, $P_1 = +5D$

Power of the divergent lens, $P_2 = P - P_1 = 2 - 5 = -3D$

For an achromatic doublet, the condition is $\dfrac{\omega_1}{f_1} + \dfrac{\omega_2}{f_2} = 0$

Since $P = \dfrac{1}{f}$, we have $\omega_1 P_1 + \omega_2 P_2 = 0$

Substituting the values of $P_1$ and $P_2$:

$\omega_1 (5) + \omega_2 (-3) = 0$

$5 \omega_1 = 3 \omega_2$

$\dfrac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \dfrac{3}{5}$

The ratio of the dispersive powers is $3:5$.

Answer: $3:5$

The combined focal length is $F$ when a convex lens of focal length $F_1$ and concave lens of focal length $F_2$ is combined. The combination of these lenses will behave like a concave lens, if

**Options:**

- **(A)** $F_1 > F_2$
- **(B)** $F_1 < F_2$
- **(C)** $F_1 = F_2$
- **(D)** $F \leq F_2$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

$\dfrac{1}{F_1} - \dfrac{1}{F_2} < 0$

$F_1 > F_2$

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A convex lens of focal length $F$ produces a real image $'n'$ times the size of the object. The image distance is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{F}{(n+1)}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{F}{(n-1)}$
- **(C)** $F(n - 1)$
- **(D)** $F(n + 1)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $F(n + 1)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a real image formed by a convex lens, the magnification $m$ is negative. Thus, $m = -n$.

The magnification is also given by $m = \dfrac{v}{u}$, which implies $\dfrac{v}{u} = -n \Rightarrow u = -\dfrac{v}{n}$.

Using the thin lens formula:

$\dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}$

Substituting $u = -\dfrac{v}{n}$ and $f = F$:

$\dfrac{1}{v} - \left(-\dfrac{n}{v}\right) = \dfrac{1}{F}$

$\dfrac{1 + n}{v} = \dfrac{1}{F}$

$v = F(n + 1)$

Answer: $F(n + 1)$

</details>

The given bi-convex lens can be considered as a combination of two plano-convex lenses placed in contact with their flat surfaces facing each other.

Refractive index, $n_1 = 1.5$
Radius of curvature of the first surface, $R_1 = +14$ cm
Radius of curvature of the second surface, $R_2 = \infty$
Using the lens maker's formula, its focal length $f_1$ is:

$\dfrac{1}{F} = \dfrac{1}{f_1} + \dfrac{1}{f_2}$
$\dfrac{1}{F} = \dfrac{1}{28} + \dfrac{1}{70} = \dfrac{5 + 2}{140} = \dfrac{7}{140} = \dfrac{1}{20} \text{ cm}^{-1}$

### Q13. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When a beam of white light is allowed to pass through convex lens parallel to principle axis, the different colors of light converge at different point on the principle axis after refraction. This is called

**Options:**

- **(A)** scattering
- **(B)** chromatic aberration
- **(C)** spherical aberration
- **(D)** polarisation

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) chromatic aberration**

**Detailed Solution:**

The refractive index of the material of a lens varies with the wavelength of light. According to Cauchy's formula, the refractive index is different for different colors of white light.

The focal length of a lens is given by the lens maker's formula: $\dfrac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right)$.

Since $\mu$ is different for different colors, the focal length $f$ will also be different for different colors. As a result, when a beam of white light passes through a convex lens parallel to the principal axis, the different colors converge at different points on the principal axis.

This inability of a lens to focus all colors of light at a single point is known as chromatic aberration.

Answer: chromatic aberration

</details>
