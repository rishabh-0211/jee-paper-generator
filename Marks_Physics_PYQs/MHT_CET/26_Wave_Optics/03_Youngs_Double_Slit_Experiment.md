### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the Young's double slit experiment, when a glass plate of thickness 't' is introduced in the path of one of the slits, the intensity at the position where the central maximum occurred previously remains unchanged. The minimum thickness of glass plate is [refractive index of glass plate = 1.5, wavelength of light in vacuum is $\lambda$]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2\lambda$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{2\lambda}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\lambda}{3}$
- **(D)** $\lambda$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2\lambda$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Before introducing the glass plate, the central maximum has zero path difference and maximum intensity.

When a glass plate of thickness $t$ and refractive index $\mu$ is introduced in one of the paths, the path difference at the original central position becomes $\Delta x = (\mu - 1)t$.

For the intensity at this position to remain unchanged, it must continue to be a maximum.

The condition for maximum intensity (constructive interference) is $\Delta x = n\lambda$, where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

For the minimum thickness of the glass plate, we take $n = 1$.

$(\mu - 1)t = \lambda$

Substituting the given refractive index $\mu = 1.5$:

$(1.5 - 1)t = \lambda$

$0.5t = \lambda$

$t = 2\lambda$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

At two points $P$ and $Q$ on the screen in Young's double slit experiment, waves from slits $S_1$ and $S_2$ have a path difference of zero and $\dfrac{\lambda}{4}$ respectively. The ratio of intensities at $P$ and $Q$ will be $\left(\cos 0^\circ = 1,\,\cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3:2$
- **(B)** $2:1$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{2}:1$
- **(D)** $4:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The phase difference $\phi$ is related to the path difference $\Delta x$ by the formula $\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x$.

The intensity at any point on the screen is given by $I = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\phi}{2}\right)$.

At point $P$, the path difference is $\Delta x_P = 0$.

$\phi_P = 0$

$I_P = I_{max} \cos^2(0) = I_{max}$

At point $Q$, the path difference is $\Delta x_Q = \dfrac{\lambda}{4}$.

$\phi_Q = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \times \dfrac{\lambda}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

$I_Q = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = I_{max} \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 = \dfrac{I_{max}}{2}$

The ratio of intensities at $P$ and $Q$ is:

$\dfrac{I_P}{I_Q} = \dfrac{I_{max}}{\dfrac{I_{max}}{2}} = \dfrac{2}{1}$

Answer: $2:1$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In Young's experiment, one of the slit is covered with a transparent sheet of thickness $3.6 \times 10^{-3}$ cm due to which position of central image shifts to a position originally occupied by $30^{th}$ bright fringe. The refractive index of the sheet is ($\lambda = 6000\ \text{\AA}$)

- **(C)** $1.3$
- **(D)** $1.7$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1.5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The shift in the central fringe due to the introduction of a transparent sheet of thickness $t$ and refractive index $\mu$ is given by:

$\Delta y = \dfrac{D}{d}(\mu - 1)t$

The position of the $n^{th}$ bright fringe is given by:

$y_n = n\dfrac{D\lambda}{d}$

Given that the central fringe shifts to the position of the $30^{th}$ bright fringe, we have $\Delta y = y_{30}$:

$\dfrac{D}{d}(\mu - 1)t = 30\dfrac{D\lambda}{d}$

$(\mu - 1)t = 30\lambda$

$\mu - 1 = \dfrac{30 \times 6000 \times 10^{-8}}{3.6 \times 10^{-3}}$

$\mu - 1 = \dfrac{1.8 \times 10^{-3}}{3.6 \times 10^{-3}}$

$\mu - 1 = 0.5$

$\mu = 1.5$

### Q9. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In double slit experiment, the two slits are $1$ mm apart and the screen is placed $1$ m away. A monochromatic light of wavelength $500$ nm is used. What will be the width of each slit for obtaining ten maxima of double slit within the central maxima of single slit pattern?

- **(B)** $0.1$ mm
- **(C)** $0.5$ mm
- **(D)** $0.02$ mm

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular width of the central maximum in a single slit diffraction pattern is given by $\theta_0 = \dfrac{2\lambda}{a}$, where $a$ is the width of the slit.

The angular separation between two consecutive maxima (fringe width) in a double slit interference pattern is given by $\theta = \dfrac{\lambda}{d}$, where $d$ is the distance between the two slits.

The number of interference maxima $N$ that can be accommodated within the central diffraction maximum is the ratio of their angular widths:

$N = \dfrac{\theta_0}{\theta} = \dfrac{\dfrac{2\lambda}{a}}{\dfrac{\lambda}{d}} = \dfrac{2d}{a}$

Given that $N = 10$ and $d = 1$ mm, substituting these values we get:

$10 = \dfrac{2 \times 1}{a}$

The ratio of intensity at the centre of a bright fringe to the intensity at a point distant $\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{th}$ of the distance between two successive bright fringes will be $(\cos 0^\circ = 1, \cos 90^\circ = 0)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $3$
- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The distance between two successive bright fringes is the fringe width, $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$.

The distance of the given point from the centre of the bright fringe is $y = \dfrac{\beta}{4}$.

The path difference at this point is $\Delta x = \dfrac{y d}{D} = \dfrac{\beta d}{4 D} = \dfrac{\lambda}{4}$.

The corresponding phase difference is $\Delta \phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \left(\dfrac{\lambda}{4}\right) = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

The intensity at any point in the interference pattern is given by $I = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\Delta \phi}{2}\right)$.

At the centre of a bright fringe, $\Delta \phi = 0$, so the intensity is $I_0 = I_{max} \cos^2(0) = I_{max}$.

At the given point, the intensity is $I = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\pi/2}{2}\right) = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = I_{max} \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 = \dfrac{I_{max}}{2}$.

The required ratio of the intensity at the centre of the bright fringe to the intensity at the given point is:

$\dfrac{I_0}{I} = \dfrac{I_{max}}{\dfrac{I_{max}}{2}} = 2$

</details>

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the intensity of light from the two slits be $I_1 = I$ and $I_2 = 2I$.

The maximum intensity in the interference pattern is given by:
$I_{max} = (\sqrt{I_1} + \sqrt{I_2})^2 = (\sqrt{I} + \sqrt{2I})^2 = I(1 + \sqrt{2})^2 = I(3 + 2\sqrt{2})$

The minimum intensity is given by:
$I_{min} = (\sqrt{I_2} - \sqrt{I_1})^2 = (\sqrt{2I} - \sqrt{I})^2 = I(\sqrt{2} - 1)^2 = I(3 - 2\sqrt{2})$

The ratio of maximum to minimum intensity is:
$\dfrac{I_{max}}{I_{min}} = \dfrac{3 + 2\sqrt{2}}{3 - 2\sqrt{2}}$

Rationalizing the denominator:

**Correct Option:** **(A) He (molecular mass 4)**

**Detailed Solution:**

Average speed $\left(v_{\mathrm{av}}\right)$ of gas molecules is inversely proportional to the square root of the absolute temperature $(T)$ of the gas
$$
v_{\mathrm{av}}=\sqrt{\frac{8 R T}{\pi M}}
$$
where $R$ is gas constant and $M$ the molecular weight. Given, $v_{1}=v, M_{1}=64, v_{2}=4 v$
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{M_{2}}{M_{1}}} \\
\frac{v}{4 v}=\sqrt{\frac{M_{2}}{64}}
\end{array}
$$
$$
M_{2}=\frac{64}{16}=4
$$
Hence, the gas is helium (molecular mass 4).
Note On the basis of the concept that speed is inversely proportional to molecular weight, one can separate two gases from the mixture. If we enclose the mixture in a porous pot and place it in vacuum, then the lighter gas due to its larger molecular velocity, will diffuse more rapidly from the walls of the vessel than the heavier gas. This method is used to separate the fissionable uranium $\left(\mathrm{U}^{235}\right)$ from the natural uranium.
