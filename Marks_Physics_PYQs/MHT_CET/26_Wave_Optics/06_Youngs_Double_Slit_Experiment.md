### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In Young's double slit experiment, the separation between the slits is decreased to $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{th}$ of its original distance and the distance between the slits and the screen is doubled. The fringe width

**Options:**

- **(A)** remains unchanged.
- **(B)** becomes $2$ times the original.
- **(C)** becomes $3$ times the original.
- **(D)** becomes $6$ times the original.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) becomes $6$ times the original.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fringe width in Young's double slit experiment is given by $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$

Given that the new distance between the slits and the screen is $D' = 2D$

The new separation between the slits is $d' = \dfrac{d}{3}$

The new fringe width is $\beta' = \dfrac{\lambda D'}{d'}$

Substituting the values, we get $\beta' = \dfrac{\lambda (2D)}{\dfrac{d}{3}} = 6 \dfrac{\lambda D}{d} = 6\beta$

Therefore, the fringe width becomes $6$ times the original.

Answer: becomes $6$ times the original.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If Young's double slit experiment is performed in water

**Options:**

- **(A)** the fringe width will decrease
- **(B)** the fringe width will increase
- **(C)** the fringe width will remain unchanged
- **(D)** the fringe width first decreases and then increases

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) the fringe width will decrease**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fringe width in Young's double slit experiment is given by $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$

When the apparatus is immersed in water, the wavelength of light changes to $\lambda' = \dfrac{\lambda}{\mu}$, where $\mu$ is the refractive index of water.

Since $\mu > 1$, the new wavelength $\lambda'$ is less than $\lambda$.

The new fringe width becomes $\beta' = \dfrac{\lambda' D}{d} = \dfrac{\lambda D}{\mu d} = \dfrac{\beta}{\mu}$

Since $\beta' < \beta$, the fringe width will decrease.

Answer: the fringe width will decrease

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A double slit experiment is immersed in water of refractive index $1.33$. The slit separation is $1$ mm, distance between slit and screen is $1.33$ m. The slits are illuminated by a light of wavelength $6300\,\text{\AA}$. The fringewidth is $x \times 10^{-4}\,m$. The value of $'x'$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5.8$
- **(B)** $8.6$
- **(C)** $4.9$
- **(D)** $6.3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $6.3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:
Refractive index of water, $\mu = 1.33$
Slit separation, $d = 1 \times 10^{-3}$ m
Distance to screen, $D = 1.33$ m
Wavelength of light in vacuum, $\lambda = 6300 \times 10^{-10}$ m $= 6.3 \times 10^{-7}$ m

The wavelength of light in water is given by:
$\lambda' = \dfrac{\lambda}{\mu}$

The fringe width in the medium is:
$\beta = \dfrac{\lambda' D}{d} = \dfrac{\lambda D}{\mu d}$

Substituting the given values:
$\beta = \dfrac{6.3 \times 10^{-7} \times 1.33}{1.33 \times 10^{-3}}$

$\beta = 6.3 \times 10^{-4}$ m

Comparing with $x \times 10^{-4}$ m, we get $x = 6.3$.

Answer: $6.3$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In Young's double slit experiment, the fringe width is $2.4$ mm with light of wavelength $9000$ A.U. If the light of wavelength $5400$ A.U. is used, with no other change in the experimental set up. The change in fringe width is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.86$ mm
- **(B)** $0.96$ mm
- **(C)** $0.86$ cm
- **(D)** $0.96$ cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.96$ mm**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fringe width in Young's double slit experiment is given by $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$.

Since $D$ and $d$ are constant, $\beta \propto \lambda$.

$\dfrac{\beta_2}{\beta_1} = \dfrac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}$

Substituting the given values:

$\beta_2 = 2.4 \times \dfrac{5400}{9000} = 2.4 \times \dfrac{3}{5} = 1.44$ mm

The change in fringe width is $\Delta \beta = \beta_1 - \beta_2 = 2.4 - 1.44 = 0.96$ mm.

Answer: $0.96$ mm

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a biprism experiment, a steady interference pattern is observed on the screen kept at a distance of 100 cm using a light of wavelength $5000$ Å. Without changing the distance between the virtual images of the slit, the source of light is replaced by a source of wavelength $6400$ Å. Now, to reduce the fringe width by $20\%$ of its initial value, the screen should be moved

**Options:**

- **(A)** towards the source by $37.5$ cm
- **(B)** towards the source by $62.5$ cm
- **(C)** away from the source by $62.5$ cm
- **(D)** away from the source by $37.5$ cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) towards the source by $37.5$ cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial fringe width is given by $\beta_1 = \dfrac{\lambda_1 D_1}{d}$

The final fringe width is given by $\beta_2 = \dfrac{\lambda_2 D_2}{d}$

It is given that the fringe width is reduced by $20\%$ of its initial value, so:

$\beta_2 = \beta_1 - 0.2\beta_1 = 0.8\beta_1$

Substituting the expressions for fringe widths:

$\dfrac{\lambda_2 D_2}{d} = 0.8 \dfrac{\lambda_1 D_1}{d}$

$D_2 = \dfrac{0.8 \lambda_1 D_1}{\lambda_2}$

Given $\lambda_1 = 5000$ \AA, $\lambda_2 = 6400$ \AA, and $D_1 = 100$ cm:

$D_2 = \dfrac{0.8 \times 5000 \times 100}{6400}$

$D_2 = \dfrac{400000}{6400} = 62.5$ cm

The displacement of the screen is $\Delta D = D_1 - D_2 = 100 - 62.5 = 37.5$ cm.

Since $D_2 < D_1$, the screen should be moved towards the source by $37.5$ cm.

Answer: towards the source by $37.5$ cm

</details>
