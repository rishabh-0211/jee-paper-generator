### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The interference pattern is obtained with two coherent light sources of intensity ($I_1$ and $I_2$) ratio '$\beta$'. In the interference pattern, the ratio $\left(\dfrac{I_{max}+I_{min}}{I_{max}-I_{min}}\right)$ will be $\left(\beta = \dfrac{I_2}{I_1}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\beta+1}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{\beta}}{(\beta+1)^2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\beta+1}{2\sqrt{\beta}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{(\beta+1)^2}{2(\sqrt{\beta})}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{\beta+1}{2\sqrt{\beta}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The maximum and minimum intensities in an interference pattern are given by:

$I_{max} = (\sqrt{I_1} + \sqrt{I_2})^2 = I_1 + I_2 + 2\sqrt{I_1 I_2}$

$I_{min} = (\sqrt{I_1} - \sqrt{I_2})^2 = I_1 + I_2 - 2\sqrt{I_1 I_2}$

Adding the two equations:

$I_{max} + I_{min} = 2(I_1 + I_2)$

Subtracting the two equations:

$I_{max} - I_{min} = 4\sqrt{I_1 I_2}$

Taking the ratio:

$\dfrac{I_{max}+I_{min}}{I_{max}-I_{min}} = \dfrac{2(I_1 + I_2)}{4\sqrt{I_1 I_2}} = \dfrac{I_1 + I_2}{2\sqrt{I_1 I_2}}$

Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $I_1$:

$\dfrac{I_{max}+I_{min}}{I_{max}-I_{min}} = \dfrac{1 + \dfrac{I_2}{I_1}}{2\sqrt{\dfrac{I_2}{I_1}}}$

Substituting $\beta = \dfrac{I_2}{I_1}$:

$\dfrac{I_{max}+I_{min}}{I_{max}-I_{min}} = \dfrac{\beta + 1}{2\sqrt{\beta}}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\beta+1}{2\sqrt{\beta}}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a biprism experiment, using a light of wavelength $4800 \text{ Å}$, a steady interference pattern was obtained on a screen $1 \text{ m}$ away from the source. Now the source is replaced by another of wavelength $6400 \text{ Å}$ and the screen is moved away from it by $20 \text{ cm}$. The fringewidth will

**Options:**

- **(A)** decrease by 40%.
- **(B)** decrease by 60%.
- **(C)** increase by 60%.
- **(D)** increase by 40%.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) increase by 60%.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fringe width in a biprism experiment is given by $\beta = \dfrac{\lambda D}{d}$

Initial fringe width is $\beta_1 = \dfrac{\lambda_1 D_1}{d}$

Final fringe width is $\beta_2 = \dfrac{\lambda_2 D_2}{d}$

Taking the ratio of the two fringe widths:

$\dfrac{\beta_2}{\beta_1} = \dfrac{\lambda_2 D_2}{\lambda_1 D_1}$

Substituting the given values $\lambda_1 = 4800 \text{ \AA}$, $D_1 = 1 \text{ m}$, $\lambda_2 = 6400 \text{ \AA}$, and $D_2 = 1 + 0.2 = 1.2 \text{ m}$:

$\dfrac{\beta_2}{\beta_1} = \dfrac{6400 \times 1.2}{4800 \times 1} = \dfrac{4}{3} \times 1.2 = 1.6$

Percentage change in fringe width is given by:

$\dfrac{\beta_2 - \beta_1}{\beta_1} \times 100 = \left( \dfrac{\beta_2}{\beta_1} - 1 \right) \times 100$

$\Rightarrow (1.6 - 1) \times 100 = 60\%$

The fringe width increases by $60\%$.

Answer: increase by 60%.

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a biprism experiment, fifth dark fringe is obtained at a point. A thin transparent film of refractive index '$\mu$' is placed in one of the interfering paths. Now $7^{th}$ bright fringe is obtained at the same point. If '$\lambda$' is the wavelength of light used, the thickness of film is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.5(\mu - 1)\lambda$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1.5\,\lambda}{(\mu-1)}$
- **(C)** $2.5(\mu - 1)\lambda$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{2.5\,\lambda}{(\mu-1)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{2.5\,\lambda}{(\mu-1)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The path difference for the $n^{th}$ dark fringe is given by $\Delta x = (2n - 1)\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$.

For the $5^{th}$ dark fringe ($n = 5$), the initial path difference at the point is:
$\Delta x_1 = (2(5) - 1)\dfrac{\lambda}{2} = 4.5\lambda$

When a thin transparent film of thickness $t$ and refractive index $\mu$ is introduced in one of the interfering paths, the optical path changes by $(\mu - 1)t$.

The new fringe at the same point is the $7^{th}$ bright fringe. The path difference for the $n^{th}$ bright fringe is $\Delta x = n\lambda$.

For the $7^{th}$ bright fringe ($n = 7$), the new path difference is:
$\Delta x_2 = 7\lambda$

The change in path difference is equal to the additional optical path introduced by the film:
$\Delta x_2 - \Delta x_1 = (\mu - 1)t$

$7\lambda - 4.5\lambda = (\mu - 1)t$

$2.5\lambda = (\mu - 1)t$

$t = \dfrac{2.5\lambda}{\mu - 1}$

Answer: $\dfrac{2.5\,\lambda}{(\mu-1)}$

For obtaining maximum contrast between the bright and dark fringes in an interference pattern, the intensities of the two light coherent sources should be

- **(D)** in the ratio $2 : 1$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

For $I_{min} = 0$, we must have $\sqrt{I_1} = \sqrt{I_2}$, which implies $I_1 = I_2$.

$\dfrac{I_{\text{max}}}{I_{\text{min}}} = \left( \dfrac{a_1 + a_2}{a_1 - a_2} \right)^2$

### Q41. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The cell has an emf of $2 \mathrm{~V}$ and the internal resistance of this cell is $0.1 \Omega$, it is connected to resistance of $3.9 \Omega$, the voltage across the cell will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.95 \mathrm{~V}$
- **(B)** $1.5 \mathrm{~V}$
- **(C)** $2 \mathrm{~V}$
- **(D)** $1.8 \mathrm{~V}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1.95 \mathrm{~V}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Key Idea When cell is giving current then the potential difference across its plates is less than its emf as potential drop across internal resistance occurs.
When a cell of $\operatorname{emf} E$ is connected to a resistance of $3.9 \Omega$, then the emf $E$ of the cell remains constant, while voltage $V$ goes on decreasing on taking more and more current from the cell.
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/mht_cet/eIPIdK2OYfPKZbkCfF3MEFVPILKnAXhrl_p2dzlz3v8.original.fullsize.png">

$$
\therefore \quad V=E-i r
$$
where $r$ is internal resistance.
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\text { Also current } & i=\frac{E}{R+r} \\
\therefore & V=E-\left(\frac{E}{R+r}\right) r
\end{array}
$$
Putting the numerical values, we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
E=2 V, r &=0.1 \Omega, R=3.9 \Omega \\
V &=2-\left(\frac{2}{3.9+0.1}\right) \times 0.1 \\
V &=1.95 \mathrm{volt}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
