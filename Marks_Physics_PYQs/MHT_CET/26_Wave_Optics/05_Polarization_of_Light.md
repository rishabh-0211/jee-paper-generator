### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Three polaroid sheets are co-axially placed such that pass axis of polaroids $2$ and $3$ make $30^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ with pass axis of sheet. If $I_0$ is the intensity of unpolarised light entering first sheet, the intensity of the emergent light through $3^{rd}$ sheet is

**Options:**

- **(A)** zero
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3I_0}{32}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{3I_0}{8}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{3I_0}{16}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{3I_0}{32}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Intensity of unpolarised light entering the first polaroid is $I_0$.

Intensity of light emerging from the first polaroid is $I_1 = \dfrac{I_0}{2}$.

The angle between the pass axis of the first and second polaroid is $\theta_1 = 30^\circ$.

Using Malus's law, the intensity of light emerging from the second polaroid is:

$I_2 = I_1 \cos^2(30^\circ) = \dfrac{I_0}{2} \times \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{I_0}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3I_0}{8}$

The angle between the pass axis of the first and third polaroid is $90^\circ$. Therefore, the angle between the pass axis of the second and third polaroid is $\theta_2 = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$.

The intensity of light emerging from the third polaroid is:

$I_3 = I_2 \cos^2(60^\circ) = \dfrac{3I_0}{8} \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{3I_0}{8} \times \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3I_0}{32}$

Answer: $\dfrac{3I_0}{32}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Unpolarized light with intensity $I_0$ passes through three polaroids such that the transmission axis of the last polaroid is crossed with the first. If the intensity of emerging light is $I_1\left(I_1=\dfrac{3}{32}I_0\right)$. The angle between the first two polaroids and the angle for which the transmitted intensity is maximum are respectively $\{$given, $\cos(90)=0\}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $45^\circ$
- **(B)** $\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $90^\circ$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $45^\circ$
- **(D)** $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $90^\circ$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $45^\circ$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the intensity of unpolarized light be $I_0$.

Intensity after passing through the first polaroid is $I_1' = \dfrac{I_0}{2}$.

Let $\theta$ be the angle between the transmission axes of the first and second polaroids. The intensity after the second polaroid is $I_2' = \dfrac{I_0}{2} \cos^2 \theta$.

Since the third polaroid is crossed with the first, the angle between the second and third polaroids is $90^\circ - \theta$. The intensity after the third polaroid is $I_1 = I_2' \cos^2(90^\circ - \theta) = \dfrac{I_0}{2} \cos^2 \theta \sin^2 \theta$.

Using the identity $\sin 2\theta = 2 \sin \theta \cos \theta$, we get $I_1 = \dfrac{I_0}{8} \sin^2 2\theta$.

Given $I_1 = \dfrac{3}{32} I_0$, we have:

$\dfrac{I_0}{8} \sin^2 2\theta = \dfrac{3}{32} I_0$

$\sin^2 2\theta = \dfrac{3}{4}$

$\sin 2\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

$\theta = \dfrac{1}{2} \sin^{-1} \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

For maximum transmitted intensity, $\sin^2 2\theta$ must be maximum, which is $1$.

$\sin^2 2\theta = 1 \Rightarrow 2\theta = 90^\circ \Rightarrow \theta = 45^\circ$.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $45^\circ$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

'n' polarizing sheets are arranged such that each makes an angle $45^\circ$ with the preceding sheet. An unpolarized light of intensity $I$ is incident into this arrangement. The output intensity is found to be $I/64$. The value of $n$ will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $6$
- **(C)** $5$
- **(D)** $4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $6$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial intensity of the unpolarized light be $I$.

When unpolarized light passes through the first polarizing sheet, its intensity becomes half. Thus, the intensity after the first sheet is:
$I_1 = \dfrac{I}{2}$

For the subsequent sheets, the light is already polarized. According to Malus's Law, when polarized light passes through a polarizer at an angle $\theta$, the transmitted intensity is multiplied by $\cos^2 \theta$.

Since each of the remaining $(n-1)$ sheets makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the preceding one, the intensity after passing through the $n$-th sheet is:
$I_n = I_1 (\cos^2 45^\circ)^{n-1}$

Substituting $I_1 = \dfrac{I}{2}$ and $\cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$:
$I_n = \dfrac{I}{2} \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2(n-1)}$

$I_n = \dfrac{I}{2} \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{n-1} = \dfrac{I}{2^n}$

We are given that the final output intensity is $\dfrac{I}{64}$. Therefore:
$\dfrac{I}{2^n} = \dfrac{I}{64}$

$2^n = 64$

$2^n = 2^6 \Rightarrow n = 6$

Answer: $6$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Choose the correct statement from the following.
Brewster's angle for a transparent medium is

**Options:**

- **(A)** different for light of different colours
- **(B)** same for lights of different colours
- **(C)** independent of refractive index of the medium
- **(D)** different for lights of same colour

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) different for light of different colours**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Brewster's law, the tangent of the polarizing angle (Brewster's angle) is equal to the refractive index of the medium.

$\tan \theta_B = \mu$

The refractive index $\mu$ of a transparent medium depends on the wavelength of the incident light according to Cauchy's relation:

$\mu = A + \dfrac{B}{\lambda^2} + \dots$

Since different colours of light have different wavelengths $\lambda$, the refractive index $\mu$ is different for different colours. Consequently, Brewster's angle $\theta_B$ is different for light of different colours.

Answer: different for light of different colours

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A ray of light is incident of the surface of a glass plate at an angle of incidence equal to Brewster's angle $\Phi$. If $\mu$ denotes the refractive index of glass w.r.t. air, then what will be the angle between reflected and refracted rays?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $90^\circ - \sin^{-1}(\sin \Phi / \mu)$
- **(B)** $90^\circ$
- **(C)** $\sin^{-1}(\mu \cos \Phi)$
- **(D)** $90^\circ + \Phi$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $90^\circ$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Brewster's law, when a ray of light is incident at Brewster's angle $\Phi$, the refractive index of the medium is given by $\mu = \tan \Phi$.

By Snell's law, $\mu = \dfrac{\sin \Phi}{\sin r}$, where $r$ is the angle of refraction.

Equating the two expressions for $\mu$, we get:

$\dfrac{\sin \Phi}{\cos \Phi} = \dfrac{\sin \Phi}{\sin r}$

$\sin r = \cos \Phi = \sin(90^\circ - \Phi)$

$\Rightarrow r = 90^\circ - \Phi$

The angle of reflection is equal to the angle of incidence, so the angle of reflection is $\Phi$.

The angle between the reflected and refracted rays is $180^\circ - (\Phi + r)$.

Substituting the value of $r$, we get:

$180^\circ - (\Phi + 90^\circ - \Phi) = 90^\circ$

Thus, the reflected and refracted rays are mutually perpendicular.

Answer: $90^\circ$

</details>
