### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

According to Huygens principle, a wave front propagates through a medium by

**Options:**

- **(A)** pushing particles of the medium
- **(B)** propagating through medium with speed of light
- **(C)** carrying particles of same phase along with it
- **(D)** creating secondary wavelets which forms a new wave front

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) creating secondary wavelets which forms a new wave front**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Huygens principle, every point on a given wave front acts as a fresh source of new disturbance, called secondary wavelets. These secondary wavelets travel in all directions with the velocity of the wave in that medium.

A surface touching these secondary wavelets tangentially in the forward direction at any instant gives the new wave front at that instant.

Thus, a wave front propagates through a medium by creating secondary wavelets which form a new wave front.

Answer: creating secondary wavelets which forms a new wave front

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A Parallel beam of monochromatic light is incident on a plane reflecting surface. If width of the plane incident and reflected plane wavefront is represented by $AB$ and $CD$ respectively then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $AB > CD$
- **(B)** $AB < CD$
- **(C)** $AB = CD$
- **(D)** $AB >> CD$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $AB = CD$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the width of the beam on the plane reflecting surface be $l$.

Let the angle of incidence be $i$ and the angle of reflection be $r$.

The width of the incident wavefront is given by $AB = l \cos i$.

The width of the reflected wavefront is given by $CD = l \cos r$.

According to the law of reflection, the angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection, so $i = r$.

Therefore, $\cos i = \cos r$, which implies $AB = CD$.

Answer: $AB = CD$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A plane wavefront of width $x$, is incident on an air-water interface and the corresponding refracted wavefront has a width y as shown in figure. The refreactive index of air with respect to water in terms of distances w and z is
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/17_april_shift_2__q29_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{w}{z}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{z}{w}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{w}{z}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{z}{w}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{w}{z}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the length of the interface between the incident and refracted wavefronts be $L$.

From the geometry of the given figure, the angle of incidence $i$ is the angle between the incident wavefront and the interface. Similarly, the angle of refraction $r$ is the angle between the refracted wavefront and the interface.

In the right-angled triangle in air, we have:
$\sin i = \dfrac{z}{L}$

In the right-angled triangle in water, we have:
$\sin r = \dfrac{w}{L}$

According to Snell's law:
$\mu_a \sin i = \mu_w \sin r$

Substituting the values of $\sin i$ and $\sin r$:
$\mu_a \left(\dfrac{z}{L}\right) = \mu_w \left(\dfrac{w}{L}\right)$

$\mu_a z = \mu_w w$

The refractive index of air with respect to water is given by the ratio of the refractive index of air to the refractive index of water:
$\mu_{aw} = \dfrac{\mu_a}{\mu_w} = \dfrac{w}{z}$

Answer: $\dfrac{w}{z}$

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

According to corpuscular theory of light, Which is NOT the property of light?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The velocity of light does not change after reflection
- **(B)** Light travels in a straight line
- **(C)** The velocity of light in air is greater than in glass
- **(D)** The velocity of light changes after refraction

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) The velocity of light in air is greater than in glass**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Newton's corpuscular theory of light, light consists of tiny, perfectly elastic particles called corpuscles that travel in straight lines.

During reflection, the corpuscles undergo perfectly elastic collisions with the reflecting surface, meaning their speed remains unchanged.

To explain refraction, the theory assumes that corpuscles experience an attractive force when approaching a denser medium. This attractive force increases the normal component of their velocity, leading to the conclusion that the speed of light is greater in a denser medium (like glass) than in a rarer medium (like air).

Therefore, the statement that the velocity of light in air is greater than in glass contradicts the corpuscular theory.

Answer: The velocity of light in air is greater than in glass

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ray of light from a monochromatic point source of light is incident at a point on the screen. If a thin mica film of thickness ' $t$ ' and refractive index ' $n$ ' is introduced in its path, then the optical path

**Options:**

- **(A)** is decreased by $(\mathrm{n}-1) \mathrm{t}$.
- **(B)** is increased by $(\mathrm{n}+1) \mathrm{t}$.
- **(C)** is not affected.
- **(D)** is increased by $(\mathrm{n}-1) \mathrm{t}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) is increased by $(\mathrm{n}-1) \mathrm{t}$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The optical path of a light ray traveling a distance $t$ in a medium of refractive index $n$ is $nt$.

Before the introduction of the mica film, the light ray traveled the same distance $t$ in air, so the optical path was $t$.

The change in the optical path is the difference between the new optical path and the old optical path.

Change in optical path $= nt - t = (n - 1)t$.

Since $n > 1$, the optical path is increased by $(n - 1)t$.

Answer: is increased by $(\mathrm{n}-1) \mathrm{t}$.

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

According to Huygen's wave theory of light, which one of the following statements is <u>not</u> correct?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Different colours of light are due to different wavelengths of waves.
- **(B)** Different colours of light are due to different sizes of the corpuscles.
- **(C)** Speed of light in denser medium is less than that in rarer medium.
- **(D)** It can explain laws of reflection and refraction.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Different colours of light are due to different sizes of the corpuscles.**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Huygen's wave theory of light, light travels in the form of waves. The different colours of light are due to different wavelengths of these waves.

The statement that different colours of light are due to different sizes of corpuscles is a postulate of Newton's corpuscular theory, not Huygen's wave theory.

Huygen's theory successfully explains the laws of reflection and refraction, and it correctly predicts that the speed of light in a denser medium is less than that in a rarer medium.

Answer: Different colours of light are due to different sizes of the corpuscles.

### Q7. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A wavefront is a surface

**Options:**

- **(A)** perpendicular to the direction of propagation of light.
- **(B)** parallel to the direction of propagation of light.
- **(C)** without any specific orientation with direction of propagation of light.
- **(D)** which has nothing to do with intensity of light.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) perpendicular to the direction of propagation of light.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A wavefront is defined as the continuous locus of all particles of a medium which are vibrating in the same phase.

According to Huygens' principle, the direction of propagation of light (a light ray) is always perpendicular to the wavefront at any given point.

Therefore, a wavefront is a surface that is perpendicular to the direction of propagation of light.

Answer: perpendicular to the direction of propagation of light.

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When wavefronts pass from denser medium to rarer medium, the distance between successive wavefronts

**Options:**

- **(A)** increases.
- **(B)** may increase or decrease.
- **(C)** decreases.
- **(D)** remains unchanged.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) increases.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The distance between successive wavefronts represents the wavelength $\lambda$ of the wave.

When a wave passes from a denser medium to a rarer medium, its speed $v$ increases.

The frequency $f$ of the wave remains unchanged during refraction.

Using the relation $v = f \lambda$, since $v$ increases and $f$ is constant, the wavelength $\lambda$ must increase.

Therefore, the distance between successive wavefronts increases.

Answer: increases.

</details>
