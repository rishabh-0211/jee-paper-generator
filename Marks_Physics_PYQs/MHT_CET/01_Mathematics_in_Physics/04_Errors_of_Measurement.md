### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A wire has mass $(0.4 \pm 0.002)$ g, radius $(0.6 \pm 0.006)$ mm and length $(8 \pm 0.04)$ cm. The percentage error in the measurement of its density is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2\%$
- **(B)** $2.55\%$
- **(C)** $3\%$
- **(D)** $3.55\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The density of the wire is given by $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V} = \dfrac{m}{\pi r^2 l}$

The maximum percentage error in density is

$\dfrac{\Delta \rho}{\rho} \times 100 = \left( \dfrac{\Delta m}{m} + 2 \dfrac{\Delta r}{r} + \dfrac{\Delta l}{l} \right) \times 100$

Substituting the given values:

$\dfrac{\Delta m}{m} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.002}{0.4} \times 100 = 0.5\%$

$\dfrac{\Delta r}{r} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.006}{0.6} \times 100 = 1\%$

$\dfrac{\Delta l}{l} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.04}{8} \times 100 = 0.5\%$

Therefore, the percentage error in density is

$\dfrac{\Delta \rho}{\rho} \times 100 = 0.5\% + 2(1\%) + 0.5\% = 3\%$

Answer: $3\%$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The resistance of a conductor is $R = V/I$ where voltage $V = 100 \pm 5$ V and current $I = 10 \pm 0.2$ A. The error in the calculation of $R$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5\%$
- **(B)** $7\%$
- **(C)** $5.2\%$
- **(D)** $2.5\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $7\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $V = 100 \pm 5$ V and $I = 10 \pm 0.2$ A.

The percentage error in voltage $V$ is $\dfrac{\Delta V}{V} \times 100 = \dfrac{5}{100} \times 100 = 5\%$.

The percentage error in current $I$ is $\dfrac{\Delta I}{I} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.2}{10} \times 100 = 2\%$.

The maximum percentage error in $R$ is the sum of the percentage errors in $V$ and $I$.

$\dfrac{\Delta R}{R} \times 100 = \left( \dfrac{\Delta V}{V} + \dfrac{\Delta I}{I} \right) \times 100 = 5\% + 2\% = 7\%$.

- **(D)** $8\%$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

The formula for the time period of a simple pendulum is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{L}{g}}$.

Squaring both sides and rearranging for $g$, we get:

$g = \dfrac{4\pi^2 L}{T^2}$

The maximum percentage error in the measurement of $g$ is:

$\dfrac{\Delta g}{g} \times 100 = \left( \dfrac{\Delta L}{L} + 2 \dfrac{\Delta T}{T} \right) \times 100$

Given the measured value of length $L = 10 \text{ cm}$ and accuracy $\Delta L = 1 \text{ mm} = 0.1 \text{ cm}$.

The percentage error in length is:

$\dfrac{\Delta L}{L} \times 100 = \dfrac{0.1}{10} \times 100 = 1\%$

The time for $100$ oscillations is $t = 50 \text{ s}$ and the resolution of the watch is $\Delta t = 1 \text{ s}$.

Since $T = \dfrac{t}{n}$, the relative error in $T$ is the same as the relative error in $t$:

$\dfrac{\Delta T}{T} = \dfrac{\Delta t}{t} = \dfrac{1}{50}$

The percentage error in time period is:

$\dfrac{\Delta T}{T} \times 100 = \dfrac{1}{50} \times 100 = 2\%$

Substituting these values into the error equation for $g$:

$\dfrac{\Delta g}{g} \times 100 = 1\% + 2(2\%) = 1\% + 4\% = 5\%$

Answer: $5\%$
