Let $\vec{P} = \hat{i}+\hat{j}+\hat{k}$ and $\vec{Q} = -\hat{i}-\hat{j}-\hat{k}$. The angle between $(\vec{P}-\vec{Q})$ and $\vec{P}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $90^\circ$
- **(B)** $30^\circ$
- **(C)** $60^\circ$
- **(D)** $0^\circ$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The resultant $\vec{R}$ of $\vec{P}$ and $\vec{Q}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{P}$. Also $|\vec{P}| = |\vec{R}|$. The angle between $\vec{P}$ and $\vec{Q}$ is
($\tan 45^\circ = 1$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $\vec{R} = \vec{P} + \vec{Q}$ and $\vec{R}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{P}$.

$\vec{R} \cdot \vec{P} = 0$

$(\vec{P} + \vec{Q}) \cdot \vec{P} = 0$

$P^2 + PQ \cos\theta = 0$

$Q \cos\theta = -P$

Also given $|\vec{R}| = |\vec{P}|$, which implies $R^2 = P^2$.

$P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ \cos\theta = P^2$

$Q^2 + 2PQ \cos\theta = 0$

Substituting $Q \cos\theta = -P$ into the above equation:

$Q^2 + 2P(-P) = 0$

$Q^2 = 2P^2 \Rightarrow Q = \sqrt{2}P$

Substituting $Q = \sqrt{2}P$ in $Q \cos\theta = -P$:

$\sqrt{2}P \cos\theta = -P$

$\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

Answer: $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

</details>

- **(C)** $3B$
- **(D)** $4B$

Given that the resultant of $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ is $\vec{C}$, we have:

$\vec{C} = \vec{A} + \vec{B}$

The magnitude of $\vec{C}$ is given by:

$C^2 = A^2 + B^2 + 2AB \cos \theta$

When the magnitude of $\vec{B}$ is doubled, the new resultant vector is $\vec{R} = \vec{A} + 2\vec{B}$.

It is given that $\vec{R}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{A}$, so their dot product must be zero:

$\vec{R} \cdot \vec{A} = 0$

$(\vec{A} + 2\vec{B}) \cdot \vec{A} = 0$

$2AB \cos \theta = -A^2$

$C^2 = A^2 + B^2 + (-A^2)$

$C^2 = B^2$

$C = B$

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Let $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ are two non-zero vectors of different magnitude. Which one of the following is the correct equation ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = -\vec{B} \cdot \vec{A}$
- **(B)** $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = \vec{B} \times \vec{A}$
- **(C)** $\vec{A} + \vec{B} = \vec{B} + \vec{A}$
- **(D)** $\vec{A} - \vec{B} = \vec{B} - \vec{A}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\vec{A} + \vec{B} = \vec{B} + \vec{A}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Vector addition follows the commutative law, which means the order of addition does not change the result.

$\vec{A} + \vec{B} = \vec{B} + \vec{A}$

The dot product is commutative, so $\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A}$.

The cross product is anti-commutative, so $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = -\vec{B} \times \vec{A}$.

Vector subtraction is not commutative, so $\vec{A} - \vec{B} = -(\vec{B} - \vec{A})$.

Answer: $\vec{A} + \vec{B} = \vec{B} + \vec{A}$

</details>
