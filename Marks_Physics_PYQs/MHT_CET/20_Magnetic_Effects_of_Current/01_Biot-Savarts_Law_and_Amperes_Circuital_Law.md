### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Consider a circular loop (radius $R$) carrying current $I$, the magnetic field produced by it at a point on the axis at a distance $z$ from the centre of the loop is $\dfrac{xR^2}{(z^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}$. The value of $x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mu_0 I$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field at a point on the axis of a circular loop of radius $R$ carrying current $I$, at a distance $z$ from its centre is given by Biot-Savart's law as:

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(z^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}$

Comparing this with the given expression $B = \dfrac{xR^2}{(z^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}$, we get:

$x = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2}$

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The magnitude of magnetic induction at a point on the axis at a large distance $r$ from a centre of a circular coil of $n$ turns and area $A$ carrying current $i$ is ($\mu_0$ = permeability of free space)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 nA}{4\pi i r^3}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{2\mu_0 niA}{4\pi r^3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2\mu_0 ni}{4\pi A r^3}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 niA}{4\pi r^3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{2\mu_0 niA}{4\pi r^3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field on the axis of a circular coil of $n$ turns and radius $R$ at a distance $r$ from its centre is given by

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n i R^2}{2(R^2 + r^2)^{3/2}}$

For a large distance $r \gg R$, the expression simplifies to

$B \approx \dfrac{\mu_0 n i R^2}{2 r^3}$

The area of the circular coil is $A = \pi R^2$, which gives $R^2 = \dfrac{A}{\pi}$. Substituting this into the equation for $B$, we get

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n i \left(\dfrac{A}{\pi}\right)}{2 r^3} = \dfrac{\mu_0 n i A}{2 \pi r^3}$

Multiplying the numerator and denominator by $2$, we obtain

$B = \dfrac{2\mu_0 n i A}{4\pi r^3}$

Alternatively, the coil acts as a magnetic dipole with magnetic moment $M = n i A$. The magnetic field on the axis of a dipole at a large distance $r$ is

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{2M}{r^3} = \dfrac{2\mu_0 n i A}{4\pi r^3}$

Answer: $\dfrac{2\mu_0 niA}{4\pi r^3}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Six very long insulated copper wires are bound together to form a cable. The currents carried by the wires are $i_1 = 10$A, $i_2 = -13$A, $i_3 = 10$A, $i_4 = 7$A, $i_5 = -12$A and $i_6 = 18$ A. The magnetic induction at a perpendicular distance of $10$ cm from the cable is $(\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ SI units$)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $40$ $\mu$T
- **(B)** $375$ $\mu$T
- **(C)** $30$ $\mu$T
- **(D)** $35$ $\mu$T

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $40$ $\mu$T**

**Detailed Solution:**

The net current in the cable is the algebraic sum of the currents in the individual wires.

$I = i_1 + i_2 + i_3 + i_4 + i_5 + i_6$

$I = 10 - 13 + 10 + 7 - 12 + 18 = 20$ A

The magnetic induction $B$ at a perpendicular distance $r$ from a long straight cable carrying current $I$ is given by Ampere's Law:

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$

Given $r = 10$ cm $= 0.1$ m and $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ T m/A, substituting the values:

$B = \dfrac{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 20}{2\pi \times 0.1}$

$B = \dfrac{2 \times 10^{-7} \times 20}{0.1}$

$B = 400 \times 10^{-7}$ T

$B = 40 \times 10^{-6}$ T $= 40$ $\mu$T

Answer: $40$ $\mu$T

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A current carrying circular coil of radius 'R' produces magnetic field $B_1$ at an axial point P at a distance 'x' from its centre and $B_2$ at point Q placed at its centre respectively . If $B_2 = 8B_1$, the value of x is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3R$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{3}\,R$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{R}{2\sqrt{3}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{2R}{\sqrt{3}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{3}\,R$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Magnetic field at an axial point P at a distance $x$ from the centre of a circular coil of radius $R$ is given by:

$B_1 = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$

Magnetic field at the centre Q of the coil is given by:

$B_2 = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$

Given that $B_2 = 8B_1$, substituting the expressions for $B_1$ and $B_2$:

$\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R} = 8 \left( \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}} \right)$

$\dfrac{1}{R} = \dfrac{8R^2}{(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$

$(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2} = 8R^3$

Taking the cube root on both sides:

$(R^2 + x^2)^{1/2} = 2R$

Squaring both sides:

$R^2 + x^2 = 4R^2$

$x^2 = 3R^2$

$x = \sqrt{3}R$

Answer: $\sqrt{3}\,R$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two circular coils X (smaller) and Y (bigger), each having a single turn, carry equal currents in the same direction and subtend the same angle at point 'O' along the axis of the coil. The distances of the centers of coil to point 'O' are $d$ and $\left(\dfrac{d}{2}\right)$ for coil Y and X respectively. The radii of coils Y and X are $(2r)$ and $(r)$ respectively. The magnetic induction due to the bigger coil at point 'O' is $B_y$ and that due to smaller coil X at point 'O' is $B_x$. ($d \gg r$) The relation between $B_x$ and $B_y$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $B_y = B_x$
- **(B)** $B_y = 2B_x$
- **(C)** $B_x = 2B_y$
- **(D)** $B_x = 4B_y$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $B_x = 2B_y$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field on the axis of a circular coil of radius $R$ at a distance $x$ from its center is given by:

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$

For coil Y (bigger coil):
Radius $R_y = 2r$, distance $x_y = d$.

$B_y = \dfrac{\mu_0 I (2r)^2}{2((2r)^2 + d^2)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{4\mu_0 I r^2}{2(4r^2 + d^2)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{2\mu_0 I r^2}{(4r^2 + d^2)^{3/2}}$

For coil X (smaller coil):
Radius $R_x = r$, distance $x_x = \dfrac{d}{2}$.

$B_x = \dfrac{\mu_0 I r^2}{2\left(r^2 + \left(\dfrac{d}{2}\right)^2\right)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I r^2}{2\left(\dfrac{4r^2 + d^2}{4}\right)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{8\mu_0 I r^2}{2(4r^2 + d^2)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{4\mu_0 I r^2}{(4r^2 + d^2)^{3/2}}$

Comparing the expressions for $B_x$ and $B_y$, we get:

$B_x = 2B_y$

Answer: $B_x = 2B_y$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two wires with current 3A and 1.5A are enclosed in a circular loop P. Third parallel wire with current 1A is situated outside the loop as shown. All the wires are perpendicular to the plane of the circular loop. The value of $\oint \vec{B} \cdot \vec{dl}$ around the loop is ($\mu_0 = $ permeability of free space)
<img style="width: 230px" src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mht_cet_pyqs_2026/april/watermarked_images/17_april_shift_2__q36_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5.5\,\mu_0$
- **(B)** $2.5\,\mu_0$
- **(C)** $1.5\,\mu_0$
- **(D)** $0.5\,\mu_0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.5\,\mu_0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Ampere's circuital law, the line integral of the magnetic field $\vec{B}$ around any closed loop is equal to $\mu_0$ times the net current enclosed by the loop:

$\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enclosed}}$

From the given figure, the loop P encloses two wires carrying currents $3\text{ A}$ (upwards) and $1.5\text{ A}$ (downwards). The third wire carrying $1\text{ A}$ is outside the loop and does not contribute to the enclosed current.

Taking the upward direction as positive, the net enclosed current is:

$I_{\text{enclosed}} = 3 - 1.5 = 1.5\text{ A}$

Therefore, the value of the integral is:

$\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 (1.5) = 1.5\mu_0$

Answer: $1.5\,\mu_0$

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The magnetic field at a distance 'r' from a long straight wire carrying current I is 0.4 tesla. The magnetic field at a distance 2r will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0 \cdot 1$ tesla
- **(B)** $0 \cdot 2$ tesla
- **(C)** $0 \cdot 8$ tesla
- **(D)** $1 \cdot 6$ tesla

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0 \cdot 2$ tesla**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field due to a long straight wire carrying current $I$ at a distance $r$ is given by $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2 \pi r}$.

This implies that $B \propto \dfrac{1}{r}$.

Let the magnetic field at distance $r$ be $B_1 = 0.4$ T.

At distance $2r$, the magnetic field $B_2$ will be:

$\dfrac{B_2}{B_1} = \dfrac{r}{2r} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$B_2 = \dfrac{B_1}{2} = \dfrac{0.4}{2} = 0.2$ T.

Answer: $0 \cdot 2$ tesla

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A circular loop of radius R is carrying current I. The ratio of magnetic field at the center of circular loop and at a distance R from the center of the loop on its axis is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 : \sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** $1 : 3\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{8} : 1$
- **(D)** $3\sqrt{2} : 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{8} : 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field at the center of a circular loop of radius $R$ carrying current $I$ is given by:

$B_{center} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$

The magnetic field on the axis of the circular loop at a distance $x$ from the center is given by:

$B_{axis} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$

Substituting $x = R$, we get:

$B_{axis} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(R^2 + R^2)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(2R^2)^{3/2}} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I R^2}{2(2\sqrt{2}R^3)} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4\sqrt{2}R}$

The ratio of the magnetic fields is:

$\dfrac{B_{center}}{B_{axis}} = \dfrac{\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R}}{\dfrac{\mu_0 I}{4\sqrt{2}R}} = \dfrac{4\sqrt{2}}{2} = 2\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{8}$

The required ratio is $\sqrt{8} : 1$.

### Q11. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A current ' i ' is flowing in a conductor PQRST as shown in figure. The radius of curved path QRS is ' r ' and length of straight portion PQ and ST is very large. The magnetic field at the centre [O] of the curved part is ($\mu_0=$ permeability of free space)
<img src="https://cdn.quizrr.in/question-assets/mhtcet/mhtcet2025/LYKp8bsH5FlGij2mvgexCag7hjDTG9AtWXi9gNaPQ98.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{i}}{4 \pi \mathrm{r}}\left(\frac{3 \pi}{2}+1\right)(-\widehat{\mathrm{k}})$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{i}}{4 \pi \mathrm{r}}\left(\frac{3 \pi}{2}+1\right) \widehat{\mathrm{k}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{i}}{4 \pi \mathrm{r}}\left[\frac{3 \pi}{2}-1\right](-\widehat{\mathrm{k}})$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{i}}{4 \pi \mathrm{r}}\left[\frac{3 \pi}{2}-1\right] \hat{\mathrm{k}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{i}}{4 \pi \mathrm{r}}\left(\frac{3 \pi}{2}+1\right) \widehat{\mathrm{k}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The conductor can be divided into three parts: the semi-infinite straight wire PQ, the circular arc QRS, and the semi-infinite straight wire ST.

1. Magnetic field due to the straight wire PQ at O:
The extended line of the wire PQ passes through the center O. Therefore, the angle between the current element $d\vec{l}$ and the position vector $\vec{r}$ is zero. Thus, the magnetic field produced by this segment at O is zero.
$\vec{B}_{PQ} = 0$

2. Magnetic field due to the circular arc QRS at O:
The arc QRS subtends an angle of $360^\circ - 90^\circ = 270^\circ = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ radians at the center O. The current flows in a counterclockwise direction. According to the right-hand thumb rule, the magnetic field at O is directed outwards, i.e., in the $+\hat{k}$ direction.
$\vec{B}_{QRS} = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} \theta \, \hat{k} = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} \left(\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right) \hat{k}$

3. Magnetic field due to the semi-infinite straight wire ST at O:
The wire ST is a semi-infinite straight wire starting from S and extending to infinity. The perpendicular distance from O to the wire is $r$. The point O lies exactly above the starting point S of the wire. The angles subtended by the ends of the wire at O are $\theta_1 = 90^\circ$ (for the end at infinity) and $\theta_2 = 0^\circ$ (for the end at S). The current flows to the right, so by the right-hand thumb rule, the magnetic field at O is directed outwards ($+\hat{k}$).
$\vec{B}_{ST} = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} (\sin 90^\circ + \sin 0^\circ) \, \hat{k} = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} (1 + 0) \, \hat{k} = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} \hat{k}$

Total magnetic field at O is the vector sum of the magnetic fields due to all three parts:
$\vec{B}_{net} = \vec{B}_{PQ} + \vec{B}_{QRS} + \vec{B}_{ST}$

$\vec{B}_{net} = 0 + \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} \left(\dfrac{3\pi}{2}\right) \hat{k} + \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} \hat{k}$

$\vec{B}_{net} = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{4\pi r} \left(\dfrac{3\pi}{2} + 1\right) \hat{k}$

Answer: $\frac{\mu_0 \mathrm{i}}{4 \pi \mathrm{r}}\left(\frac{3 \pi}{2}+1\right) \widehat{\mathrm{k}}$

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

An element $\overrightarrow{\Delta \ell}=\Delta \mathrm{x} \hat{\mathrm{i}}$ is placed at the origin and carries a current of 10A. The magnitude of magnetic field on the Y axis at a distance of 0.5 m if $\Delta \mathrm{x}=1 \mathrm{~cm}$ is $\left(\frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi}=10^{-7}\right.$ SI unit $)\left(\sin 90^{\circ}=1\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{~T}$
- **(B)** $10^{-8} \mathrm{~T}$
- **(C)** $4 \times 10^{-8} \mathrm{~T}$
- **(D)** $2 \times 10^{-8} \mathrm{~T}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4 \times 10^{-8} \mathrm{~T}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Biot-Savart law, the magnitude of the magnetic field due to a current element is given by:

$dB = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{I dl \sin\theta}{r^2}$

Given:
$I = 10$ A
$dl = \Delta x = 1$ cm $= 10^{-2}$ m
$r = 0.5$ m
$\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7}$ T m/A

Since the current element is along the X-axis and the point is on the Y-axis, the angle between them is $\theta = 90^\circ$.

Substituting the values into the formula:

$dB = 10^{-7} \times \dfrac{10 \times 10^{-2} \times \sin 90^\circ}{(0.5)^2}$

$dB = 10^{-7} \times \dfrac{10^{-1}}{0.25}$

$dB = \dfrac{10^{-8}}{0.25}$

$dB = 4 \times 10^{-8}$ T

Answer: $4 \times 10^{-8} \mathrm{~T}$

</details>
