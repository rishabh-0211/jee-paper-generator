### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A circular coil having n turns, each of radius 'r' carries a current I. The magnetic field at a distance $x = \sqrt{8}\, r$ from its centre along the axis of the coil is ($\mu_0$ = permeability of free space)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 nI}{51r}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 nI}{52r}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 nI}{53r}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\mu_0 nI}{54r}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{\mu_0 nI}{54r}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field at a distance $x$ from the centre of a circular coil of $n$ turns and radius $r$ along its axis is given by

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n I r^2}{2(r^2 + x^2)^{3/2}}$

Given $x = \sqrt{8} r$, substituting this value in the formula gives

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n I r^2}{2(r^2 + (\sqrt{8} r)^2)^{3/2}}$

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n I r^2}{2(r^2 + 8r^2)^{3/2}}$

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n I r^2}{2(9r^2)^{3/2}}$

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n I r^2}{2(27r^3)}$

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 n I}{54r}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\mu_0 nI}{54r}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two circular coils A and B are made from same wire but the radius of coil A is twice that of coil B. If the magnetic field at their centres is same then the ratio potential difference applied across them is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3:1$
- **(B)** $4:1$
- **(C)** $6:1$
- **(D)** $2:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field at the centre of a circular coil of $N$ turns and radius $R$ is given by:

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 N I}{2 R}$

From this, the current $I$ in the coil is:

$I = \dfrac{2 R B}{\mu_0 N}$

The resistance of the coil is given by $R' = \rho \dfrac{l}{a}$, where $l$ is the total length of the wire and $a$ is its cross-sectional area. Since the coil has $N$ turns of radius $R$, the length is $l = N(2\pi R)$.

$R' = \rho \dfrac{N(2\pi R)}{a}$

The potential difference $V$ applied across the coil is:

$V = I R' = \left(\dfrac{2 R B}{\mu_0 N}\right) \left(\rho \dfrac{N(2\pi R)}{a}\right)$

$V = \dfrac{4 \pi \rho B R^2}{\mu_0 a}$

Since both coils are made from the same wire, $\rho$ and $a$ are constant. We are also given that the magnetic field $B$ at their centres is the same. Therefore, the potential difference is directly proportional to the square of the radius:

$V \propto R^2$

Given that the radius of coil A is twice that of coil B ($R_A = 2R_B$):

$\dfrac{V_A}{V_B} = \left(\dfrac{R_A}{R_B}\right)^2 = (2)^2 = 4$

The ratio of the potential difference applied across them is $4:1$.

Answer: $4:1$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two parallel long wires carry currents $i_1$ and $i_2$ with $i_1 > i_2$. When the currents are in the same direction, the magnetic field at a point midway between the wires is $10\ \mu$T. If the direction of $i_2$ is reversed then the field becomes $30\ \mu$T. The ratio $\dfrac{i_1}{i_2}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $3$
- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the distance between the two parallel wires be $2d$. The magnetic field at the midpoint due to a long straight wire carrying current $i$ is given by $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 i}{2\pi d}$.

When the currents are in the same direction, the magnetic fields produced by the two wires at the midpoint are in opposite directions. Since $i_1 > i_2$, the net magnetic field is:

$B_1 = \dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi d}(i_1 - i_2) = 10\ \mu\text{T}$

When the direction of $i_2$ is reversed, the currents are in opposite directions, and their magnetic fields at the midpoint add up. The net magnetic field becomes:

$B_2 = \dfrac{\mu_0}{2\pi d}(i_1 + i_2) = 30\ \mu\text{T}$

Dividing the second equation by the first equation, we get:

$\dfrac{i_1 + i_2}{i_1 - i_2} = \dfrac{30}{10} = 3$

$i_1 + i_2 = 3i_1 - 3i_2$

$2i_1 = 4i_2$

$\dfrac{i_1}{i_2} = 2$

Answer: $2$

</details>

**Options:**

- **(B)** $4 : 1$
- **(C)** $6 : 1$
- **(D)** $2 : 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4 : 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the resistance per unit length of the wire be $\lambda$.

For a circular coil of radius $R$ having a single turn, the length of the wire is $2\pi R$.

The resistance of the coil is given by:
$R_c = \lambda (2\pi R)$

The magnetic field at the centre of a circular coil carrying current $I$ is:
$B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2R}$

Using Ohm's law, the current $I$ can be written in terms of the applied potential difference $V$ as:
$I = \dfrac{V}{R_c} = \dfrac{V}{\lambda (2\pi R)}$

Substituting this into the magnetic field formula, we get:
$B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{2R} \left( \dfrac{V}{\lambda 2\pi R} \right) = \dfrac{\mu_0 V}{4\pi \lambda R^2}$

We are given that the magnetic field at the centre of both coils is the same ($B_A = B_B$):
$\dfrac{\mu_0 V_A}{4\pi \lambda R_A^2} = \dfrac{\mu_0 V_B}{4\pi \lambda R_B^2}$

$\Rightarrow \dfrac{V_A}{V_B} = \left( \dfrac{R_A}{R_B} \right)^2$

Given that the radius of coil $A$ is twice that of coil $B$ ($R_A = 2R_B$), we have $\dfrac{R_A}{R_B} = 2$.

$\Rightarrow \dfrac{V_A}{V_B} = (2)^2 = 4$

Therefore, the ratio of the potential difference applied across them is $4 : 1$.

Answer: $4 : 1$

</details>
