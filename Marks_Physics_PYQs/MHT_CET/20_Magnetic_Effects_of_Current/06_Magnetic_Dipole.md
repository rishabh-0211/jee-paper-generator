### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A magnetic dipole lying parallel to a magnetic field requires '$W$' units of work to turn it through $30^\circ$. The torque required to keep this needle in this position is
$\left[\sin 30^\circ = \cos 60^\circ = 0.5, \sin 60^\circ = \cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \sin 0^\circ = \cos 90^\circ = 1\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{3}W$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}W$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{W}{(\sqrt{3}-1)}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{W}{(2-\sqrt{3})}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{W}{(2-\sqrt{3})}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work done in rotating a magnetic dipole in a uniform magnetic field from an initial angle $\theta_1$ to a final angle $\theta_2$ is given by:

$W = MB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$

Given that the dipole is initially parallel to the magnetic field, $\theta_1 = 0^\circ$. It is turned through an angle $\theta_2 = 30^\circ$. Substituting these values:

$W = MB(\cos 0^\circ - \cos 30^\circ)$

$W = MB\left(1 - \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = MB\left(\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$

The torque required to hold the magnetic dipole at an angle $\theta$ is given by:

$\tau = MB\sin\theta$

At $\theta = 30^\circ$, the torque is:

$\tau = MB\sin 30^\circ = MB\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

From this, we can write $MB = 2\tau$. Substituting this into the expression for work done:

$W = 2\tau \left(\dfrac{2 - \sqrt{3}}{2}\right)$

$W = \tau(2 - \sqrt{3})$

$\tau = \dfrac{W}{2 - \sqrt{3}}$

Answer: $\dfrac{W}{(2-\sqrt{3})}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The work done in turning a magnet of magnetic moment ' M ' by an angle of $90^{\circ}$ from the meridian is ' n ' times the corresponding work done to turn it through an angle of $60^{\circ}$ where the value of ' $n$ ' is $\left(\cos 90^{\circ}=0, \cos 60^{\circ}=0.5\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0.5
- **(B)** 2
- **(C)** 0.25
- **(D)** 1

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work done in rotating a magnet of magnetic moment $M$ in a uniform magnetic field $B$ from an initial angle $\theta_1$ to a final angle $\theta_2$ is given by:

$W = MB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$

Initially, the magnet is in the magnetic meridian, so $\theta_1 = 0^{\circ}$.

Work done in turning the magnet by $90^{\circ}$ ($\theta_2 = 90^{\circ}$):

$W_1 = MB(\cos 0^{\circ} - \cos 90^{\circ}) = MB(1 - 0) = MB$

Work done in turning the magnet by $60^{\circ}$ ($\theta_2 = 60^{\circ}$):

$W_2 = MB(\cos 0^{\circ} - \cos 60^{\circ}) = MB(1 - 0.5) = 0.5 MB$

Given that $W_1 = n W_2$:

$MB = n(0.5 MB)$

$n = \dfrac{1}{0.5} = 2$

Answer: 2

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A magnetic needle of magnetic moment $6 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{Am}^2$ and moment of inertia $9.6 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~kg} \mathrm{~m}^2$ performs simple harmonic motion in a magnetic field of 0.01 T . Time taken to complete 10 oscillations is [Take $\pi=3 \cdot 14$ ]

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0.2512 s
- **(B)** 2.512 s
- **(C)** 25.12 s
- **(D)** $251 \cdot 2 \mathrm{~s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 25.12 s**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period of oscillation of a magnetic needle in a uniform magnetic field is given by the formula:

$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{I}{MB}}$

Substituting the given values:

$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{9.6 \times 10^{-5}}{6 \times 10^{-2} \times 0.01}}$

$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{9.6 \times 10^{-5}}{6 \times 10^{-4}}}$

$T = 2\pi \sqrt{0.16}$

$T = 2\pi \times 0.4 = 0.8\pi \text{ s}$

The time taken to complete 10 oscillations is:

$t = 10T = 10 \times 0.8\pi = 8\pi \text{ s}$

Using $\pi = 3.14$:

$t = 8 \times 3.14 = 25.12 \text{ s}$

Answer: 25.12 s

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

At certain place a magnet makes 30 oscillations per minute. At another place if the magnetic induction is increased by two times the magnetic induction at first place, then the time period of same magnet will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \mathrm{~s}$
- **(B)** $2 \sqrt{3} \mathrm{~s}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \mathrm{~s}$
- **(D)** $\quad \sqrt{3} \mathrm{~s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \mathrm{~s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Frequency at the first place, $f_1 = \dfrac{30}{60} = \dfrac{1}{2} \text{ Hz}$

Time period at the first place, $T_1 = \dfrac{1}{f_1} = 2 \text{ s}$

Let the magnetic induction at the first place be $B_1$.

Since the magnetic induction at the second place is increased by two times the magnetic induction at the first place, $B_2 = B_1 + 2B_1 = 3B_1$.

The time period of a magnet is given by $T = 2\pi \sqrt{\dfrac{I}{MB}}$, which implies $T \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{B}}$.

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \sqrt{\dfrac{B_1}{B_2}}$

$T_2 = 2 \sqrt{\dfrac{B_1}{3B_1}} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \text{ s}$

Answer: $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \mathrm{~s}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2020 (15 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A circular and a square coil is prepared from two identical metal wires and a current is passed through them. Ratio of magnetic dipole moment associated with circular coil to that with square coil is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\pi}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{4}{\pi}$
- **(C)** $\pi$
- **(D)** $\frac{2}{\pi}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{4}{\pi}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let I be the length of metal wire.
When wire is bent into a circular coil of radius \(r\), then
\(r=\frac{l}{2 \pi}\)
\(\therefore\) Area, \(\mathrm{A}=\pi\left(\frac{l}{2 \pi}\right)^{2}=\pi \frac{l^{2}}{4 \pi^{2}}\)
\(\therefore\) Magnetic dipole moment associated with circular coil,
\(\mu_{\mathrm{c}}-\mathrm{iA}=\mathrm{i} \pi\left(\frac{l^{2}}{4 \pi^{2}}\right)\)
\(\mu_{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{\mathrm{i \textrm {i } ^ { 2 }}}{4 \pi} \quad \ldots . .(\mathrm{i})\)
When metal wire Is bent into a square coil then side of square
\(\mathrm{a}=\frac{l}{4}\)
\(\therefore\) Area, \(\mathrm{A}=\mathrm{a}^{2}=\frac{l^{2}}{16}\)
\(\therefore\) Magnetic dipole moment associated with square coil,
\(\mu_{\mathrm{s}}=\mathrm{i} \mathrm{A}=\mathrm{i} \frac{l^{2}}{16}\)
\(\begin{aligned}
&\therefore \frac{\mu_{\mathrm{c}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}}=\frac{\frac{\mathrm{i} l^{2}}{4 \pi}}{\frac{\mathrm{i} l^{2}}{16}} \\
&=\frac{16}{4 \pi}=\frac{4}{\pi}
\end{aligned}\)
Hence, the ratio of magnetic dipole moment of circular coil and square coil is \(4: \pi\).

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A circular loop and a square loop are formed from the same wire and the same current is passed through them. Find the ratio of their dipole moments.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4 \pi$
- **(B)** $\frac{4}{\pi}$
- **(C)** $\frac{2}{\pi}$
- **(D)** $2 \pi$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{4}{\pi}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Suppose, the length of wire is $l$. When, wire formed in circular loop then radius of loop,
$$
r=\frac{l}{2 \pi}
$$
$[\because l=2 \pi r]$
Magnet dipole, $M_{1}=i A$
$$
\begin{array}{l}
=i \pi r^{2}=i \pi \times\left(\frac{l}{2 \pi}\right)^{2} \\
=i \times \frac{l^{2}}{4 \pi}
\end{array}
$$
When, wire formed in square loop then the side of loop,
$$
a=\frac{l}{4}
$$
Magnet dipole, $M_{2}=I A$
$$
\begin{aligned}
&=i \times a^{2} \\
&=i \times \frac{l^{2}}{16} \\
\frac{M_{1}}{M_{2}} &=\frac{4}{\pi}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
