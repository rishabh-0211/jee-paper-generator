### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A rectangular coil of $n$ turns has area $\vec{A}$ and it is placed in uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$. If the current passing through the coil is 'I' then the torque acting on the coil is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{I}{n}(\vec{A}\times\vec{B})$
- **(B)** $nI(\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B})$
- **(C)** $nI(\vec{A}\times\vec{B})$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{n}{I}(\vec{A}\cdot\vec{B})$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $nI(\vec{A}\times\vec{B})$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic dipole moment of a coil of $n$ turns, area $\vec{A}$, and carrying current $I$ is given by $\vec{M} = nI\vec{A}$.

The torque $\vec{\tau}$ acting on a magnetic dipole placed in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ is given by $\vec{\tau} = \vec{M} \times \vec{B}$.

Substituting the value of $\vec{M}$, we get $\vec{\tau} = nI(\vec{A} \times \vec{B})$.

Answer: $nI(\vec{A}\times\vec{B})$

</details>

$\dfrac{F}{L} = \dfrac{\mu_0 (I) \left(\dfrac{I}{3}\right)}{2\pi d} = \dfrac{\mu_0 I^2}{6\pi d}$

Answer: $\dfrac{\mu_0 I^2}{6\pi d}$

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

- **(B)** $40^\circ$
- **(C)** $45^\circ$
- **(D)** $50^\circ$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $40^\circ$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a coil in a radial magnetic field, the deflecting torque is given by

$\tau_{d} = I A_{eff} B$

The restoring torque provided by the suspension fibre is

$\tau_{r} = C \theta$

In equilibrium, $\tau_{d} = \tau_{r}$

$I A_{eff} B = C \theta$

$\theta = \dfrac{I A_{eff} B}{C}$

Substituting the given values:

$\theta = \dfrac{250 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.04 \times 0.02}{5 \times 10^{-9}}$

$\theta = \dfrac{2 \times 10^{-7}}{5 \times 10^{-9}}$

$\theta = 40^{\circ}$

The magnetic field is given as $\vec{B} = B(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k})$.

$\vec{F} = I[L\hat{i} \times B(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} - 2\hat{k})]$

Two long conductors separated by a distance 'd' carry currents ' $\mathrm{I}_1$ ' and ' $\mathrm{I}_2$ ' in the same directions. They exert a force ' F ' on each other. The distance between them is increased to ' 3 d '. If new repulsive force of magnitude '2/3 F' is found between these conductors, the required change in the magnitude and direction of one of the currents in the conductor is respectively [length of the conductors is constant]

**Options:**

- **(A)** same, reversed.
- **(B)** twice, reversed.
- **(C)** thrice, same.
- **(D)** twice, same.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) twice, reversed.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The initial force between the two long straight conductors carrying currents in the same direction is attractive and its magnitude is given by:

$F = \dfrac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2 L}{2 \pi d}$

When the distance is increased to $3d$, the new force is given as repulsive. For the force to become repulsive, the direction of one of the currents must be reversed.

Let the new magnitude of the changed current be $I_2'$. The new force magnitude is:

$F' = \dfrac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2' L}{2 \pi (3d)}$

Given that $F' = \dfrac{2}{3} F$, we can substitute the expressions for $F$ and $F'$:

$\dfrac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2' L}{2 \pi (3d)} = \dfrac{2}{3} \left( \dfrac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2 L}{2 \pi d} \right)$

$\dfrac{I_2'}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3} I_2$

$I_2' = 2 I_2$

Thus, the magnitude of one of the currents must be made twice its initial value, and its direction must be reversed.

Answer: twice, reversed.

</details>

### Q44. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A charge $q$ is placed at the corner of a cube of side $a$. The electric flux through the cube is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{q}{\varepsilon_{0}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{q}{3 \varepsilon_{0}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{q}{6 \varepsilon_{0}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{q}{8 \varepsilon_{0}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{q}{8 \varepsilon_{0}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to Gauss's law, the electric flux through a closed surface is equal to $\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}$ times the net charge enclosed by the surface. Since, $q$ is the charge enclosed by the surface, then the electric flux $\phi=\frac{q}{\varepsilon_{0}}$. If charge $q$ is placed at a corner of cube, it will be divided into 8 such cubes. Therefore, electric flux through the cube is
$$
\phi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{q}{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)
$$

</details>
