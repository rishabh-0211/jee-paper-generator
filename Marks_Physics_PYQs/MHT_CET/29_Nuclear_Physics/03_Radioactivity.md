### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In an alpha decay, the daughter nucleus produced is itself unstable and undergoes further decay. If the number of parent and daughter nuclei at time $t$ are $N_p$ and $N_d$ respectively and their decay constants are $\lambda_p$ and $\lambda_d$ respectively. What condition needs to be satisfied in order for $N_d$ to remain constant?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $N_p N_d = \lambda_d \lambda_p$
- **(B)** $(N_p)/(N_d) = (\lambda_p)/(\lambda_d)$
- **(C)** $2N_p \lambda_d = 3N_d \lambda_p$
- **(D)** $N_p \lambda_p = N_d \lambda_d$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $N_p \lambda_p = N_d \lambda_d$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The rate of change of the number of daughter nuclei is given by the difference between its rate of formation and its rate of decay.

$\dfrac{dN_d}{dt} = \lambda_p N_p - \lambda_d N_d$

For the number of daughter nuclei $N_d$ to remain constant, its rate of change must be zero.

$\dfrac{dN_d}{dt} = 0$

$\lambda_p N_p - \lambda_d N_d = 0$

$\lambda_p N_p = \lambda_d N_d$

Answer: $N_p \lambda_p = N_d \lambda_d$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A radioactive nucleus X with a half-life $T$, decays into a nucleus Y. At $t = 0$, there is no nucleus Y. After some time '$t$', the ratio of the number of Y to that of X is $0.4$. Then, '$t$' is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $T\log(1.4)$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{T\log(1.4)}{\log(2)}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{T\log(2)}{2\log(1.4)}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{T}{\log(2)}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{T\log(1.4)}{\log(2)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the initial number of nuclei of X be $N_0$.

At time $t$, the number of nuclei of X is $N_X$ and the number of nuclei of Y is $N_Y = N_0 - N_X$.

Given $\dfrac{N_Y}{N_X} = 0.4$

$\dfrac{N_0 - N_X}{N_X} = 0.4$

$\dfrac{N_0}{N_X} - 1 = 0.4$

$\dfrac{N_0}{N_X} = 1.4$

$N_X = \dfrac{N_0}{1.4}$

Using the radioactive decay law, $N_X = N_0 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{\dfrac{t}{T}}$

$\dfrac{N_0}{1.4} = N_0 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{\dfrac{t}{T}}$

$1.4 = 2^{\dfrac{t}{T}}$

Taking logarithm on both sides:

$\log(1.4) = \dfrac{t}{T} \log(2)$

$t = \dfrac{T \log(1.4)}{\log(2)}$

Answer: $\dfrac{T\log(1.4)}{\log(2)}$

</details>

**Detailed Solution:**

Activity is given by $A = \lambda N$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $0.75\log_e 2$**

Given initial activity $A_0 = 16000$ disintegrations per minute, final activity $A = 2000$ disintegrations per minute, and time $t = 4$ minutes.

Substituting these values into the equation:

$2000 = 16000 e^{-\lambda (4)}$

$\dfrac{2000}{16000} = e^{-4\lambda}$

$\log_e\left(\dfrac{1}{8}\right) = -4\lambda$

$\log_e(2^{-3}) = -4\lambda$

$-3 \log_e 2 = -4\lambda$

$\lambda = \dfrac{3}{4} \log_e 2$

$\lambda = 0.75 \log_e 2$

In radioactive decay process, the negatively charged emitted $\beta$ -particles are

**Options:**

- **(A)** the electrons present inside the nucleus
- **(B)** the electrons produced as a result of the decay of neutrons inside the nucleus
- **(C)** the electrons produced as a result of collisions between atoms
- **(D)** the electrons orbiting around the nucleus

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) the electrons produced as a result of the decay of neutrons inside the nucleus**

**Detailed Solution:**

Beta decay can involve the emission of either electrons or positrons. The electrons or positrons emitted in a $\beta$ -decay do not exist inside the nucleus. They are only created at the time of emission, just as photons are created when an atom makes a transition from higher to a lower energy state. In negative $\beta$ -decay a neutron in the nucleus is transformed into a proton, an electron and an antineutrino. Hence, in radioactive decay process, the negatively charged emitted \beta-particles are the electrons produced as a result of the decay of neutrons present inside the nucleus.

</details>
