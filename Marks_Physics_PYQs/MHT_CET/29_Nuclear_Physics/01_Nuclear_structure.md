### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The ratio of the density of oxygen nucleus $({}^{16}_{8}\text{O})$ and helium nucleus $({}^{4}_{2}\text{He})$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 : 1$
- **(B)** $2 : 1$
- **(C)** $4 : 1$
- **(D)** $8 : 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1 : 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The radius of a nucleus with mass number $A$ is given by $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$.

The volume of the nucleus is $V = \dfrac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = \dfrac{4}{3} \pi R_0^3 A$.

The mass of the nucleus is $M \approx A m$, where $m$ is the average mass of a nucleon.

The density of the nucleus is $\rho = \dfrac{M}{V} = \dfrac{A m}{\dfrac{4}{3} \pi R_0^3 A} = \dfrac{m}{\dfrac{4}{3} \pi R_0^3}$.

Since the density is independent of the mass number $A$, it is constant for all nuclei.

Therefore, the ratio of the density of the oxygen nucleus to the helium nucleus is $1 : 1$.

Answer: $1 : 1$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A radioactive element A decays into radioactive element C by the following processes in succession.
$\mathrm{A} \rightarrow \mathrm{~B}+{ }_2 \mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{e}}^4 ; \mathrm{B} \rightarrow \mathrm{C}+2 \mathrm{e}^{-}$
Then elements

**Options:**

- **(A)** $A$ and $B$ are isobars.
- **(B)** $A$ and $C$ are isobars.
- **(C)** $A$ and $C$ are isotopes.
- **(D)** $A$ and $B$ are isotopes.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $A$ and $C$ are isotopes.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the atomic number and mass number of element $\mathrm{A}$ be $Z$ and $A_m$ respectively. So, $\mathrm{A}$ can be represented as ${}_{Z}\mathrm{A}^{A_m}$.

In the first decay process, $\mathrm{A}$ emits an alpha particle (${}_{2}\mathrm{He}^{4}$):
${}_{Z}\mathrm{A}^{A_m} \rightarrow {}_{Z-2}\mathrm{B}^{A_m-4} + {}_{2}\mathrm{He}^{4}$
Thus, element $\mathrm{B}$ has atomic number $Z-2$ and mass number $A_m-4$.

In the second decay process, $\mathrm{B}$ emits two beta particles ($2 {}_{-1}\mathrm{e}^{0}$):
${}_{Z-2}\mathrm{B}^{A_m-4} \rightarrow {}_{Z}\mathrm{C}^{A_m-4} + 2 {}_{-1}\mathrm{e}^{0}$
Thus, element $\mathrm{C}$ has atomic number $Z$ and mass number $A_m-4$.

Comparing elements $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{C}$, both have the same atomic number $Z$ but different mass numbers ($A_m$ and $A_m-4$). Elements with the same atomic number but different mass numbers are called isotopes.

Therefore, $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{C}$ are isotopes.

Answer: $A$ and $C$ are isotopes.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2021 (22 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A nucleus breaks into two nuclear parts, which have their velocity ratio $2: 1$. The ratio of their nuclear radii will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{2^{1 / 3}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{1}{2^{1 / 3}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

By law of conservation of momentum, the two parts will have equal and opposite momentum, assuming the nucleus was initially at rest.
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \mathrm{m}_1 \mathrm{v}_1=\mathrm{m}_2 \mathrm{v}_2 \text { or } \frac{\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2}=\frac{\mathrm{v}_2}{\mathrm{v}_1}=\frac{1}{2} \\
& \frac{\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{r}_1}{\mathrm{r}_2}\right)^3=\frac{1}{2} \\
& \therefore \frac{\mathrm{r}_1}{\mathrm{r}_2}=\frac{1}{2^{1 / 3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A particle having almost zero mass and exactly zero charge is

**Options:**

- **(A)** positron
- **(B)** electron
- **(C)** neutron
- **(D)** neutrino

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) neutrino**

**Detailed Solution:**

We know that the neutrino is the particle which has almost zero mass and exactly zero charge. Positron has the charge $+e$ and mass $m_{e}$ [equal to electron mass].
Electron is a particle which has $-e$ charge and $9.1 \times 10^{-31}$ kg mass.
Neutron is a particle which has zero charge and $1838 \mathrm{~m}_{e}$ mass.
So, the required answer is neutrino.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Two nuclei have their mass numbers in the ratio of $1: 3$. The ratio of their nuclear densities would be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 3$
- **(B)** $3: 1$
- **(C)** $(3)^{1 / 3}: 1$
- **(D)** $1: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Density of nuclear matter is independent of mass number, so the required ratio is $1: 1$. 
Alternative
$$
A_{1}: A_{2}=1: 3
$$
Their radii will be in the ratio
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{0} A_{1}^{1 / 3}: R_{0} A_{2}^{1 / 3}=1: 3^{1 / 3} \\
\text { Density }=\frac{A}{\frac{4}{3} \pi R^{3}} \\
\therefore \rho_{A_{1}}: \rho_{A_{2}}=\frac{1}{\frac{4}{3} \pi R_{0}^{3} \cdot 1^{3}}=\frac{3}{\frac{4}{3} \pi R_{0}^{3}\left(3^{1 / 3}\right)^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$
Their nuclear densities will be the same.

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The momentum of a photon of energy $1 \mathrm{MeV}$ in $\mathrm{kg}-\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s}$, will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.33 \times 10^{6}$
- **(B)** $7 \times 10^{-24}$
- **(C)** $10^{-22}$
- **(D)** $5 \times 10^{-22}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $5 \times 10^{-22}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Energy of photon is given by
$$
E=\frac{h c}{\lambda}
$$
where $h$ is the Planck's constant, $c$ the velocity of light and $\lambda$ its wavelength. de-Broglie wavelength is given by
$$
\lambda=\frac{h}{p}
$$
where $p$ is being momentum of photon. From Eqs. (i) and (ii), we get
$$
E=\frac{h c}{h / p}=p c
$$
Or $p=E / c$
$$
\text { Given, } \begin{aligned}
&=1 \mathrm{MeV}=1 \times 10^{6} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \mathrm{~J}, \\
c &=3 \times 10^{8} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, after putting numerical values, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
p &=\frac{1 \times 10^{6} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}}{3 \times 10^{8}} \mathrm{~kg}-\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s} \\
&=5 \times 10^{-22} \mathrm{~kg}-\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
