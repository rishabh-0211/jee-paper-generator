### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which one of the following is possible nuclear reaction?

**Options:**

- **(A)** ${}_5\text{B}^{10} + {}_2\text{He}^4 \rightarrow {}_7\text{N}^{13} + {}_1\text{H}^1$
- **(B)** ${}_{11}\text{Na}^{23} + {}_1\text{H}^1 \rightarrow {}_{10}\text{N}^{20} + {}_2\text{He}^4$
- **(C)** ${}_{93}\text{Np}^{239} \rightarrow {}_{94}\text{Pu}^{239} + {}_{-1}\text{e}^0$
- **(D)** ${}_7\text{N}^{11} + {}_1\text{H}^1 \rightarrow {}_6\text{C}^{12} + {}_{-1}\text{e}^0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) ${}_{93}\text{Np}^{239} \rightarrow {}_{94}\text{Pu}^{239} + {}_{-1}\text{e}^0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a nuclear reaction to be possible, both the mass number ($A$) and the atomic number ($Z$) must be conserved.

Checking the given options:

For ${}_5\text{B}^{10} + {}_2\text{He}^4 \rightarrow {}_7\text{N}^{13} + {}_1\text{H}^1$:
LHS: $Z = 5 + 2 = 7$
RHS: $Z = 7 + 1 = 8$
Since $7 \neq 8$, the atomic number is not conserved.

For ${}_{11}\text{Na}^{23} + {}_1\text{H}^1 \rightarrow {}_{10}\text{N}^{20} + {}_2\text{He}^4$:
LHS: $Z = 11 + 1 = 12$, $A = 23 + 1 = 24$
RHS: $Z = 10 + 2 = 12$, $A = 20 + 4 = 24$
Although $Z$ and $A$ are mathematically balanced, the chemical symbol for $Z=10$ is Neon ($\text{Ne}$), not Nitrogen ($\text{N}$).

For ${}_{93}\text{Np}^{239} \rightarrow {}_{94}\text{Pu}^{239} + {}_{-1}\text{e}^0$:
LHS: $Z = 93$, $A = 239$
RHS: $Z = 94 - 1 = 93$, $A = 239 + 0 = 239$
Both the atomic number and mass number are conserved. This is a valid $\beta^{-}$ decay reaction.

For ${}_7\text{N}^{11} + {}_1\text{H}^1 \rightarrow {}_6\text{C}^{12} + {}_{-1}\text{e}^0$:
LHS: $Z = 7 + 1 = 8$
RHS: $Z = 6 - 1 = 5$
Since $8 \neq 5$, the atomic number is not conserved.

Answer: ${}_{93}\text{Np}^{239} \rightarrow {}_{94}\text{Pu}^{239} + {}_{-1}\text{e}^0$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (03 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the uranium radioactive series, the initial nucleus is ${ }_{92}^{238} \mathrm{U}$ and that the final nucleus is ${ }_{82}^{206} \mathrm{~Pb}$. When uranium nucleus decays into lead, the number of $\alpha$-particles and $\beta$-particles emitted are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4 \alpha, 5 \beta$
- **(B)** $5 \alpha, 3 \beta$
- **(C)** $6 \alpha, 7 \beta$
- **(D)** $8 \alpha, 6 \beta$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $8 \alpha, 6 \beta$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the number of $\alpha$-particles emitted be $x$ and the number of $\beta$-particles emitted be $y$.

The nuclear reaction can be written as:

${}_{92}^{238}\mathrm{U} \rightarrow {}_{82}^{206}\mathrm{Pb} + x({}_{2}^{4}\mathrm{He}) + y({}_{-1}^{0}\mathrm{e})$

Equating the mass numbers on both sides:

$238 = 206 + 4x$

$4x = 32 \Rightarrow x = 8$

Equating the atomic numbers on both sides:

$92 = 82 + 2x - y$

Substituting $x = 8$:

$92 = 82 + 16 - y$

$92 = 98 - y \Rightarrow y = 6$

Thus, $8$ $\alpha$-particles and $6$ $\beta$-particles are emitted.

Answer: $8 \alpha, 6 \beta$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2020 (19 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A shell of mass 'M' initially at rest suddenly explodes in three fragments. Two of
these fragments are of mass 'M $/ 4$ ' each, which move with velocities $3 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$ and
$4 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$ respectively in mutually perpendicular directions. The magnitude of velocity of the third fragment is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$
- **(B)** $2 \cdot 5 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$
- **(C)** $1 \cdot 5 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$
- **(D)** $2 \cdot 0 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2 \cdot 5 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The mass $M$ is initially at rest, hence its initial momentum is zero. By law or conservation of momentum, the net momentum of the three pieces should be zero. The moments of the two pieces are
$\mathrm{p}_{1}=\frac{3 \mathrm{M}}{4} \text { and } \mathrm{p}_{2}=\frac{5 \mathrm{M}}{4}$
Three are at right angles to each other.
Their resultant will be $p=\sqrt{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}}$
$\begin{array}{l}
=\sqrt{\left(\frac{3 M}{4}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{4 M}{4}\right)^{2}} \\
=\frac{M}{4} \sqrt{(3)^{2}+(4)^{2}} \\
=\frac{M}{4} \sqrt{25}=\frac{5 M}{4}
\end{array}$
The momentum of the third piece will be equal and opposite to this.
The mass of the third piece will be $\frac{M}{2}$
If its velocity is $\mathrm{V}$, then
$\begin{array}{l}
\frac{M}{2} \cdot V=\frac{5 M}{4} \\
\therefore V=2.5 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}
\end{array}$

</details>
