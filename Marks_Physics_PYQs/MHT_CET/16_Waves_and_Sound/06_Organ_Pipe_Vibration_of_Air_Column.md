### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A pipe open at both ends has length $1$ m. The air column in a pipe cannot resonate for a frequency (Neglect end correction, speed of sound in air $= 340$ m/s)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $510$ Hz
- **(B)** $340$ Hz
- **(C)** $170$ Hz
- **(D)** $85$ Hz

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $85$ Hz**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a pipe open at both ends, the resonant frequencies are given by $f_n = \dfrac{nv}{2L}$, where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

Given $v = 340$ m/s and $L = 1$ m.

The fundamental frequency is $f_1 = \dfrac{340}{2 \times 1} = 170$ Hz.

The possible resonant frequencies are integral multiples of the fundamental frequency, i.e., $170$ Hz, $340$ Hz, $510$ Hz, $\dots$

Since $85$ Hz is not an integral multiple of $170$ Hz, the air column cannot resonate at this frequency.

Answer: $85$ Hz

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A pipe open at both ends has a frequency $f$ in air. The pipe is dipped vertically in water, so that its $(1/3)$ part of its length is in water. The fundamental frequency of the air column is now $xf$, the value of $x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{3}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fundamental frequency of a pipe open at both ends of length $L$ is given by $f = \dfrac{v}{2L}$.

When the pipe is dipped vertically in water such that $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of its length is submerged, the length of the air column becomes $L' = L - \dfrac{L}{3} = \dfrac{2L}{3}$.

The pipe now acts as a closed pipe at one end (the water surface acts as a rigid boundary). The fundamental frequency of a closed pipe of length $L'$ is $f' = \dfrac{v}{4L'}$.

Substituting $L' = \dfrac{2L}{3}$, we get:

$f' = \dfrac{v}{4 \left( \dfrac{2L}{3} \right)} = \dfrac{3v}{8L}$

Since $f = \dfrac{v}{2L}$, we can rewrite $f'$ as:

$f' = \dfrac{3}{4} \left( \dfrac{v}{2L} \right) = \dfrac{3}{4} f$

Comparing this with $f' = xf$, we get $x = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{3}{4}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An organ pipe $P_1$ closed at one end and vibrating in its first overtone and other pipe $P_2$ open at both ends vibrating in its third overtone are in resonance with a given tuning fork. The ratio of length of $P_1$ and $P_2$ will be

- **(A)** $3:8$
- **(B)** $3:4$
- **(C)** $3:2$
- **(D)** $3:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $3:8$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For an organ pipe closed at one end, the frequency of the first overtone (which corresponds to the third harmonic) is given by $f_1 = \dfrac{3v}{4L_1}$.

For an organ pipe open at both ends, the frequency of the third overtone (which corresponds to the fourth harmonic) is given by $f_2 = \dfrac{4v}{2L_2}$.

Since both pipes are in resonance with the same tuning fork, their frequencies must be equal:

$f_1 = f_2$

$\dfrac{3v}{4L_1} = \dfrac{4v}{2L_2}$

$\dfrac{3}{4L_1} = \dfrac{2}{L_2}$

$\dfrac{L_1}{L_2} = \dfrac{3}{8}$

The ratio of the lengths of $P_1$ and $P_2$ is $3:8$.

Answer: $3:8$

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In an experiment with a closed organ pipe, it is filled with water by $\left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^{th}$ of its volume. The frequency of the fundamental note will change by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $25\%$
- **(B)** $20\%$
- **(C)** $-20\%$
- **(D)** $-25\%$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $25\%$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the original length of the air column in the closed organ pipe be $L$.

The fundamental frequency of the closed organ pipe is given by:
$f = \dfrac{v}{4L}$

When the pipe is filled with water by $\dfrac{1}{5}$ of its volume, the water occupies a length of $\dfrac{L}{5}$ at the closed end. The new length of the air column becomes:
$L' = L - \dfrac{L}{5} = \dfrac{4L}{5}$

The new fundamental frequency is:
$f' = \dfrac{v}{4L'} = \dfrac{v}{4\left(\dfrac{4L}{5}\right)} = \dfrac{5}{4}\left(\dfrac{v}{4L}\right) = \dfrac{5}{4}f$

The change in frequency is:
$\Delta f = f' - f = \dfrac{5}{4}f - f = \dfrac{1}{4}f$

The percentage change in the frequency is:
$\dfrac{\Delta f}{f} \times 100\% = \dfrac{1}{4} \times 100\% = 25\%$

Answer: $25\%$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two closed organ pipes A and B have the same length and A is wider than B. They resonate in the fundamental mode at frequencies $n_A$ and $n_B$ respectively, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $n_A = n_B$
- **(B)** $n_A > n_B$
- **(C)** $n_A < n_B$
- **(D)** either (2) or (3) depending on the ratio of their diameters

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $n_A < n_B$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fundamental frequency of a closed organ pipe is given by $n = \dfrac{v}{4(L + e)}$

where $v$ is the speed of sound, $L$ is the length of the pipe, and $e$ is the end correction.

The end correction $e$ is directly proportional to the radius $r$ of the pipe ($e = 0.6r$).

Since pipe A is wider than pipe B, its radius is larger, which implies $e_A > e_B$.

Therefore, the effective length of pipe A is greater than that of pipe B, i.e., $(L + e_A) > (L + e_B)$.

As frequency is inversely proportional to the effective length, we get $n_A < n_B$.

Answer: $n_A < n_B$

</details>

$n_1 = \dfrac{v}{4L_1} \Rightarrow L_1 = \dfrac{v}{4n_1}$

The fundamental frequency of the open pipe is given by:
$n_2 = \dfrac{v}{2L_2} \Rightarrow L_2 = \dfrac{v}{2n_2}$

When the two pipes are joined end to end, the new pipe formed is closed at one end and open at the other. The total length of this new closed pipe is $L = L_1 + L_2$.

$n = \dfrac{v}{4L}$

$n = \dfrac{1}{\dfrac{1}{n_1} + \dfrac{2}{n_2}}$

$n = \dfrac{n_1 n_2}{n_2 + 2n_1}$

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An organ pipe closed at one end has fundamental frequency of $1500$ Hz. The maximum number of overtones generated by this pipe a normal person can hear is (neglect end connection)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $13$
- **(C)** $6$
- **(D)** $9$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $6$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fundamental frequency of the closed organ pipe is $f_0 = 1500$ Hz.

For a closed organ pipe, only odd harmonics are present. The frequency of the $n$-th overtone is given by:

$f_n = (2n + 1)f_0$

The maximum audible frequency for a normal human ear is $20000$ Hz. Therefore, the frequency of the $n$-th overtone must satisfy:

$(2n + 1)f_0 \le 20000$

Substituting $f_0 = 1500$ Hz:

$(2n + 1)(1500) \le 20000$

$2n + 1 \le \dfrac{20000}{1500}$

$2n + 1 \le 13.33$

$2n \le 12.33$

$n \le 6.16$

Since the number of overtones $n$ must be an integer, the maximum possible value for $n$ is $6$.

Thus, a normal person can hear a maximum of $6$ overtones.

Answer: $6$

</details>

$n_{1}$ is the frequency of the pipe closed at one end and $n_{2}$ is the frequency of the pipe open at both ends. If both are joined end to end, find the fundamental frequency of closed pipe so formed

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{n_{1} n_{2}}{n_{2}+2 n_{1}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{n_{1} n_{2}}{2 n_{2}+n_{1}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{n_{1}+2 n_{2}}{n_{2} n_{1}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 n_{1}+n_{2}}{n_{2} n_{1}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{n_{1} n_{2}}{n_{2}+2 n_{1}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Frequency of closed pipe, $n_{1}=\frac{v}{4 l_{1}} \Rightarrow l_{1}=\frac{v}{4 n_{1}}$
Frequency of open pipe, $n_{2}=\frac{v}{2 l_{1}} \Rightarrow l_{2}=\frac{v}{2 n_{2}}$
when both pipe are joined then length of closed pipe
$$
\begin{array}{c}
l=l_{1}+l_{2} \\
\frac{v}{4 n}=\frac{v}{4 n_{1}}+\frac{v}{2 n_{2}}
\end{array}
$$
or $\quad \frac{1}{2 n}=\frac{1}{2 n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}$
or
$$
\frac{1}{2 n}=\frac{n_{2}+2 n_{1}}{2 n_{1} n_{2}}
$$
or
$$
n=\frac{n_{1} n_{2}}{n_{2}+2 n_{1}}
$$

</details>

### Q109. MHT CET 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the fundamental mode, time taken by the wave to reach the closed end of the air filled pipe is $0.01 \mathrm{~s}$. The fundamental frequency is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 25
- **(B)** $12.5$
- **(C)** 20
- **(D)** 15

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 25**

**Detailed Solution:**

In the fundamental mode,
Frequency, $n=\frac{v}{\lambda}$
$$
\begin{array}{l}
n=\frac{v}{4 l} \\
n=\frac{l}{t \times 4 l} \quad\left[\because v=\frac{l}{t}\right] \\
n=\frac{1}{0.01 \times 4} \\
n=25
\end{array}
$$

</details>

### Q111. MHT CET 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a resonance pipe the first and second resonances are obtained at depths $22.7 \mathrm{~cm}$ and $70.2 \mathrm{~cm}$ respectively. What will be the end correction ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.05 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(B)** $115.5 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(C)** $92.5 \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(D)** $113.5 \mathrm{~cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1.05 \mathrm{~cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For end correction $x$,
$$
\frac{l_{2}+x}{l_{1}+x}=\frac{3 \lambda / 4}{\lambda / 4}=3
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x &=\frac{l_{2}-3 l_{1}}{2} \\
&=\frac{70.2-3 \times 22.7}{2}=1.05 \mathrm{~cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
