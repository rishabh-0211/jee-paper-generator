### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two monoatomic ideal gases '1' and '2' of molecular masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ respectively are enclosed in separate containers kept at the same temperature. The ratio of the speed of sound in gas '1' to that in gas '2' is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_1}{m_2}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{m_1}{m_2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The speed of sound in an ideal gas is given by $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma R T}{M}}$, where $M$ is the molar mass of the gas.

Since $M = m N_A$, where $m$ is the molecular mass and $N_A$ is Avogadro's number, the speed of sound can be written as $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma R T}{m N_A}}$.

Both gases are monoatomic, so they have the same adiabatic index $\gamma$. They are also at the same temperature $T$.

Therefore, $v \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{m}}$.

The ratio of the speed of sound in gas '1' to that in gas '2' is $\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}}$.

Answer: $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The ratio of the speed of sound in helium gas to that in nitrogen gas at $295$ K is ( Ratio of specific heats $\gamma$ for helium is $\dfrac{5}{3}$ and that for nitrogen is $\dfrac{7}{5}$. Molecular weight for helium and nitrogen are $4$ and $28$ respectively.)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{7}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{5}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{5}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The speed of sound in a gas is given by $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma R T}{M}}$.

For helium, $\gamma_{He} = \dfrac{5}{3}$ and $M_{He} = 4$.

For nitrogen, $\gamma_{N_2} = \dfrac{7}{5}$ and $M_{N_2} = 28$.

The ratio of the speed of sound in helium to that in nitrogen at the same temperature $T$ is:

$\dfrac{v_{He}}{v_{N_2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma_{He}}{\gamma_{N_2}} \times \dfrac{M_{N_2}}{M_{He}}}$

Substituting the given values:

$\dfrac{v_{He}}{v_{N_2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{5/3}{7/5} \times \dfrac{28}{4}}$

$\dfrac{v_{He}}{v_{N_2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{25}{21} \times 7} = \sqrt{\dfrac{25}{3}} = \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}$

Answer: $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3}}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The enclosed air inside a closed box of rigid walls has pressure $P_1$. The density of air inside this box is constant. On heating the gas, the pressure of the enclosed air is increased from $P_1$ to $P_2$. Now it is observed that the sound travels $1.4$ times faster than at pressure $P_1$. The ratio $P_2$ by $P_1$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.25$
- **(B)** $1.96$
- **(C)** $1.5$
- **(D)** $1.4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.96$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The speed of sound in a gas is given by $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma P}{\rho}}$.

Since the box has rigid walls, the volume is constant. The mass of the enclosed air is also constant, which means the density $\rho$ remains constant.

Thus, the speed of sound is directly proportional to the square root of pressure, $v \propto \sqrt{P}$.

$\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = \sqrt{\dfrac{P_2}{P_1}}$

Given that $v_2 = 1.4 v_1$, we have:

$1.4 = \sqrt{\dfrac{P_2}{P_1}}$

Squaring both sides gives:

$\dfrac{P_2}{P_1} = (1.4)^2 = 1.96$

Answer: $1.96$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

What is the effect of humidity on sound waves when humidity increases?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Speed of sound waves is more.
- **(B)** Speed of sound waves is less.
- **(C)** Speed of sound waves remains same.
- **(D)** Speed of sound waves becomes zero.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Speed of sound waves is more.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The speed of sound in a gas is given by $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma R T}{M}}$, where $M$ is the molar mass of the gas.

Dry air consists mainly of nitrogen ($N_2$, molar mass $28$ g/mol) and oxygen ($O_2$, molar mass $32$ g/mol), giving an average molar mass of approximately $28.97$ g/mol.

Water vapor ($H_2O$) has a molar mass of $18$ g/mol. When humidity increases, water vapor displaces heavier nitrogen and oxygen molecules, which decreases the average molar mass $M$ of the air.

Since the speed of sound is inversely proportional to the square root of the molar mass ($v \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$), a decrease in molar mass leads to an increase in the speed of sound.

Thus, when humidity increases, the speed of sound waves is more.

Answer: Speed of sound waves is more.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The ratio of the velocity of sound in hydrogen gas $\left(\gamma=\frac{7}{5}\right)$ to that in helium gas $\left(\gamma=\frac{5}{3}\right)$ at the same temperature is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 1$
- **(B)** $7 ; 3$
- **(C)** $21: 25$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{42}: 5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{42}: 5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The velocity of sound in a gas is given by the formula:

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma R T}{M}}$

For hydrogen gas ($H_2$):
$\gamma_1 = \dfrac{7}{5}$
$M_1 = 2$

For helium gas ($He$):
$\gamma_2 = \dfrac{5}{3}$
$M_2 = 4$

The ratio of the velocity of sound in hydrogen to that in helium at the same temperature is:

$\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma_1}{\gamma_2} \times \dfrac{M_2}{M_1}}$

Substituting the given values:

$\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{7/5}{5/3} \times \dfrac{4}{2}}$

$\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{21}{25} \times 2}$

$\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{42}{25}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{42}}{5}$

The ratio is $\sqrt{42} : 5$.

Answer: $\sqrt{42}: 5$

### Q6. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A sound of frequency $480 \mathrm{~Hz}$ is emitted from the stringed instrument. The velocity of sound in air is $320 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$. After completing 180 vibrations, the distance covered by a wave is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $60 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(B)** $90 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(C)** $120 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(D)** $180 \mathrm{~m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $120 \mathrm{~m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given: $\mathrm{v}=320 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}, \mathrm{f}=480 \mathrm{~Hz}, \mathrm{~N}=180$
$\mathrm{v}=\mathrm{f} \lambda$
$\therefore \quad \lambda=\frac{\mathrm{v}}{\mathrm{f}}$
Substituting the values, we get
$\lambda=\frac{320}{480}=\frac{2}{3}$
$\therefore \quad$ The total distance covered after 180 vibrations is
$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{D}=\mathrm{N} \times \lambda \\
& \mathrm{D}=180 \times \frac{2}{3} \\
& \mathrm{D}=120 \mathrm{~m}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

At what temperature will the speed of sound be nearly 1.5 times its value at N.T.P.?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $136^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(B)** $614^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(C)** $341^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$
- **(D)** $409^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $341^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Speed of sound depends on $T$ temperature, $M$ molar mass and $\gamma$ gas exponent:
$\begin{aligned}

& \Rightarrow \frac{v^{\prime}}{v}=\sqrt{\frac{T^{\prime}}{T}}=1.5 \\
& \Rightarrow \sqrt{T^{\prime}}=1.5 \sqrt{(273)} \\
& T^{\prime}=2.25 \times 273 \mathrm{~K}=614 \mathrm{~K}
\end{aligned}$
On writing in degree Celsius:
$T^{\prime}=341^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$

### Q10. MHT CET 2022 (05 Aug Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

For a particular sound wave propagating in air, a path difference between two points is $0.54 \mathrm{~m}$ which is equivalent to phase difference of $(1.87 \pi)$. If the velocity of sound wave in air is $330 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$, the frequency of this wave is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $660 \mathrm{~Hz}$.
- **(B)** $550 \mathrm{~Hz}$.
- **(C)** $110 \mathrm{~Hz}$
- **(D)** 367 Hz.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $550 \mathrm{~Hz}$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The correct option is (B).
Concept: Path difference and phase difference are related by,
$\frac{2 \pi \mathrm{X}}{\lambda}=\phi$
where $\mathrm{x}$ is the path difference, $\phi$ the phase difference and $\lambda$ the wavelength.
The velocity of a wave is given by: $v=\lambda f$, where $f$ is the frequency.
Combining the above two equations:
$f=\frac{\phi v}{2 \pi x}$
Given, $x=0.54 \mathrm{~m}, \phi=(1.8 \pi)$ and $\mathrm{v}=330 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$, therefore,
$\mathrm{f}=\frac{(1.8 \pi)(330 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s})}{2 \pi(0.54 \mathrm{~m})}=550 \mathrm{~Hz}$

</details>
