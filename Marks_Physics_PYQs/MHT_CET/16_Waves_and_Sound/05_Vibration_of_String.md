### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two vibrating metal wires of the same material of length 'L' and '3L' and have radii '2r' and 'r' respectively. They are stretched under same tension. Both the wires vibrate in their fundamental modes. The ratio $\dfrac{n_1}{n_2}$ is [$n_1$ and $n_2$ are the frequencies of first and second wire respectively]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:1$
- **(B)** $1.5:1$
- **(C)** $2:1$
- **(D)** $2.5:1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.5:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fundamental frequency of a stretched wire is given by $n = \dfrac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\mu}}$

The linear mass density $\mu = \pi r^2 \rho$, where $\rho$ is the density of the material.

Substituting $\mu$, we get $n = \dfrac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi r^2 \rho}} = \dfrac{1}{2Lr} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}$

Since the tension $T$ and density $\rho$ are the same for both wires, $n \propto \dfrac{1}{Lr}$

For the first wire, $L_1 = L$ and $r_1 = 2r$, so $n_1 \propto \dfrac{1}{L(2r)} = \dfrac{1}{2Lr}$

For the second wire, $L_2 = 3L$ and $r_2 = r$, so $n_2 \propto \dfrac{1}{(3L)r} = \dfrac{1}{3Lr}$

Taking the ratio of the frequencies:

$\dfrac{n_1}{n_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2Lr}}{\dfrac{1}{3Lr}} = \dfrac{3}{2} = 1.5$

The ratio is $1.5:1$.

Answer: $1.5:1$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

There are two wires vibrating with same frequency of $500$ Hz. Tension in one wire is increased by 'x' % so that $5$ beats per second are heard. The value of 'x' in % is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5$
- **(B)** $4$
- **(C)** $3$
- **(D)** $2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The frequency of a vibrating string is given by $f = \dfrac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\mu}}$

This implies $f \propto \sqrt{T}$

For small changes in tension, the fractional change in frequency is given by:

$\dfrac{\Delta f}{f} = \dfrac{1}{2} \dfrac{\Delta T}{T}$

Given the initial frequency $f = 500$ Hz and the beat frequency $\Delta f = 5$ Hz, the fractional change in frequency is:

$\dfrac{\Delta f}{f} = \dfrac{5}{500} = \dfrac{1}{100}$

Substituting this into the relation:

$\dfrac{1}{100} = \dfrac{1}{2} \left(\dfrac{x}{100}\right)$

$\dfrac{x}{100} = \dfrac{2}{100}$

$x = 2$

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

A sonometer wire resonates with a given tuning fork, forming standing waves with five antinodes between the two bridges, when a mass of $9$ kg is suspended from the wire. When this mass is replaced by mass $M$, the wire resonates with the same tuning fork, forming three antinodes for the same positions of the bridges. The value of $M$ (in kg) is

- **(C)** $\dfrac{25}{2}$
- **(D)** $25$

**Detailed Solution:**

The frequency of a stretched string is given by $f = \dfrac{p}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\mu}}$

where $p$ is the number of antinodes (loops), $L$ is the length of the wire, $T$ is the tension, and $\mu$ is the linear mass density.

For the first case, $p_1 = 5$ and $T_1 = 9g$.

$f = \dfrac{5}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{9g}{\mu}}$

For the second case, $p_2 = 3$ and $T_2 = Mg$.

$f = \dfrac{3}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{Mg}{\mu}}$

$\dfrac{5}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{9g}{\mu}} = \dfrac{3}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{Mg}{\mu}}$

$5 \sqrt{9} = 3 \sqrt{M}$

$5 \times 3 = 3 \sqrt{M}$

$\sqrt{M} = 5$

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two uniform strings A and B made of steel are vibrating under the same tension. If the first overtone of A is equal to the second overtone of B and if the radius of A is twice that of B, the ratio of the lengths of the strings is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2:1$
- **(B)** $3:4$
- **(C)** $3:2$
- **(D)** $1:3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1:3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The frequency of the $n$-th harmonic of a stretched string is given by:

$f = \dfrac{n}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\mu}}$

The linear mass density $\mu$ can be written in terms of the radius $r$ and density $\rho$ of the string as $\mu = \pi r^2 \rho$. Substituting this into the frequency formula, we get:

$f = \dfrac{n}{2Lr} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}$

For string A, the first overtone corresponds to the second harmonic ($n_A = 2$).
For string B, the second overtone corresponds to the third harmonic ($n_B = 3$).

Since both strings are made of steel and are under the same tension, $\rho$ and $T$ are constant for both. We are given that the frequencies are equal ($f_A = f_B$) and $r_A = 2r_B$.

Equating the frequencies:

$\dfrac{2}{2 L_A r_A} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}} = \dfrac{3}{2 L_B r_B} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}$

$\dfrac{1}{L_A r_A} = \dfrac{3}{2 L_B r_B}$

Substituting $r_A = 2r_B$:

$\dfrac{1}{L_A (2r_B)} = \dfrac{3}{2 L_B r_B}$

$\dfrac{1}{2 L_A} = \dfrac{3}{2 L_B}$

$\dfrac{L_A}{L_B} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

The ratio of the lengths of the strings is $1:3$.

Answer: $1:3$

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

The linear mass density $m$ can be expressed in terms of diameter $d$ and density $\rho$ as:

$m = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{length}} = \dfrac{\text{volume} \times \rho}{L} = \dfrac{(\pi r^2 L) \rho}{L} = \pi \left(\dfrac{d}{2}\right)^2 \rho = \dfrac{\pi d^2 \rho}{4}$

$n = \dfrac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{4T}{\pi d^2 \rho}} = \dfrac{1}{Ld} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}$

$n_A = \dfrac{1}{Ld} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}$

$n_B = \dfrac{1}{(2L)(3d)} \sqrt{\dfrac{2T}{\pi (2\rho)}}$

$n_B = \dfrac{1}{6Ld} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}$

Taking the ratio of $n_B$ to $n_A$:

$\dfrac{n_B}{n_A} = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{6Ld} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}}{\dfrac{1}{Ld} \sqrt{\dfrac{T}{\pi \rho}}} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

### Q14. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a sonometer experiment, the fundamental frequency of vibration of wire is '$n_1$' when wire is stretched by hanging a metal bob. If the bob is completely immersed in water, the frequency of vibration of wire becomes '$n_2$'. The relative density of the metal of the bob is

**Options:**

When the bob is in air, the tension is $T_1 = V \rho g$, where $V$ is the volume and $\rho$ is the density of the bob.

$n_1 = \dfrac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{V \rho g}{m}}$

When the bob is completely immersed in water, the tension becomes $T_2 = V \rho g - V \rho_w g = V(\rho - \rho_w)g$, where $\rho_w$ is the density of water.

$n_2 = \dfrac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\dfrac{V(\rho - \rho_w)g}{m}}$

$\dfrac{n_1^2}{n_2^2} = \dfrac{d}{d - 1}$

$n_1^2 d - n_1^2 = n_2^2 d$

$d(n_1^2 - n_2^2) = n_1^2$

$d = \dfrac{n_1^2}{n_1^2 - n_2^2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{n_1^2}{n_1^2 - n_2^2}$
