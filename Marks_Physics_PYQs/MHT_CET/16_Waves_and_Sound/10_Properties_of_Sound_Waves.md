### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The 'Loudness' and 'Pitch' of sound respectively are the human perception to

**Options:**

- **(A)** intensity of sound and frequency of sound.
- **(B)** intensity of sound and velocity of sound.
- **(C)** frequency of sound and velocity of sound.
- **(D)** frequency of sound and intensity of sound.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) intensity of sound and frequency of sound.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Loudness is the physiological sensation of sound which is directly related to the intensity of the sound wave. A higher intensity sound is perceived as louder.

Pitch is the characteristic of sound that determines its shrillness or flatness, and it is directly related to the frequency of the sound wave. A higher frequency sound is perceived as having a higher pitch.

Therefore, loudness and pitch are the human perceptions of the intensity of sound and the frequency of sound, respectively.

Answer: intensity of sound and frequency of sound.

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which one of the following statements regarding pitch of sound is <em>'WRONG'</em> ?
(A) Pitch refers to sharpness of sound.
(B) Tone refers to the single frequency of that wave.
(C) High pitch sound need not be louder.
(D) In general male sound is sharper than that of a female.

**Options:**

- **(A)** D
- **(B)** A
- **(C)** C
- **(D)** B

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) D**

**Detailed Solution:**

Pitch is the characteristic of sound that determines its shrillness or sharpness, which depends on the frequency of the sound wave. A higher frequency corresponds to a higher pitch and a sharper sound.

A sound of a single frequency is called a tone.

Pitch and loudness are independent characteristics; pitch depends on frequency, whereas loudness depends on the amplitude of the sound wave. Thus, a high pitch sound need not be louder.

In general, the vocal cords of females are shorter and vibrate at a higher frequency compared to males. Therefore, a female's voice has a higher pitch and is sharper than a male's voice.

Statement D claims that male sound is sharper than that of a female, which is incorrect.

Answer: D

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A musical instrument 'P' produce sound waves of frequency ' n ' and amplitude 'A'. Another musical instrument 'Q' produces sound waves of frequency ' $\frac{n}{4}$ '. The waves produced by ' $P$ ' and ' $Q$ ' have equal energies. If the amplitude of waves produced by ' $P$ ' is ' $A_P$ ', the amplitude of waves produced by 'Q' will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \mathrm{~A}_p$
- **(B)** $4 \mathrm{~A}_p$
- **(C)** $6 A_p$
- **(D)** $9 A_p$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4 \mathrm{~A}_p$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The energy of a sound wave is directly proportional to the square of its frequency and the square of its amplitude.

$E \propto n^2 A^2$

Given that the energies of the waves produced by 'P' and 'Q' are equal:

$E_P = E_Q$

$n_P^2 A_P^2 = n_Q^2 A_Q^2$

Substituting the given values $n_P = n$ and $n_Q = \dfrac{n}{4}$:

$n^2 A_P^2 = \left(\dfrac{n}{4}\right)^2 A_Q^2$

$n^2 A_P^2 = \dfrac{n^2}{16} A_Q^2$

$A_Q^2 = 16 A_P^2$

$A_Q = 4 A_P$

Answer: $4 \mathrm{~A}_p$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

How many times more intense is a 60 dB sound than a 30 dB sound?

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** 4
- **(C)** 1000
- **(D)** 10000

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 1000**

**Detailed Solution:**

The loudness of sound in decibels is given by $L = 10 \log_{10} \left( \dfrac{I}{I_0} \right)$.

For the first sound, $L_1 = 60$ dB:

$60 = 10 \log_{10} \left( \dfrac{I_1}{I_0} \right) \Rightarrow \dfrac{I_1}{I_0} = 10^6$

For the second sound, $L_2 = 30$ dB:

$30 = 10 \log_{10} \left( \dfrac{I_2}{I_0} \right) \Rightarrow \dfrac{I_2}{I_0} = 10^3$

Taking the ratio of the two intensities:

$\dfrac{I_1}{I_2} = \dfrac{10^6}{10^3} = 1000$

Thus, a 60 dB sound is 1000 times more intense than a 30 dB sound.

Answer: 1000

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2023 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Sound waves of frequency $600 \mathrm{~Hz}$ fall normally on a perfectly reflecting wall. The shortest distance from the wall at which all particles will have maximum amplitude of vibration is (speed of sound $=300 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$ )

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{4} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{8} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(C)** $\frac{3}{8} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(D)** $\frac{7}{8} \mathrm{~m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{8} \mathrm{~m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The maximum displacement of the wave will occur at antinode of the wave. The first antinode will be a point where, $\mathrm{d}=\frac{\lambda}{4}$
The wavelength is given by,
$\lambda=\frac{\mathrm{v}}{\mathrm{f}}=\frac{300}{600}$
So, $d=\frac{0.5}{4}=\frac{1}{8} m$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The gases carbon-monoxide (CO) and nitrogen at the same temperature have kinetic energies $E_{1}$ and $E_{2}$ respectively. Then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $E_{1}=E_{2}$
- **(B)** $E_{1}>E_{2}$
- **(C)** $E_{1} < E_{2}$
- **(D)** $E_{1}$ and $E_{2}$ cannot be compared

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $E_{1}=E_{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The gases carbon monoxide (CO) and nitrogen $\left(\mathrm{N}_{2}\right)$ are diatomic, so both have equal kinetic energy $\frac{5}{2} k T$, ie, $E_{1}=E_{2}$.

</details>
