### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The amplitude of a sound wave at a distance $r$ from a point source is $A$. The amplitude at a distance $2r$ from the point source is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{A}{4}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{A}{2}$
- **(C)** $A$
- **(D)** $2A$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{A}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a point source, the intensity $I$ of a sound wave at a distance $r$ is inversely proportional to the square of the distance.

$I \propto \dfrac{1}{r^2}$

The intensity is also directly proportional to the square of the amplitude $A$.

$I \propto A^2$

From the above two relations, we get:

$A^2 \propto \dfrac{1}{r^2} \Rightarrow A \propto \dfrac{1}{r}$

Let the amplitude at distance $r$ be $A_1 = A$ and at distance $2r$ be $A_2$.

$\dfrac{A_2}{A_1} = \dfrac{r}{2r} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$A_2 = \dfrac{A}{2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{A}{2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two points separated by $6$ cm on a string has phase difference of $60^\circ$. If frequency of oscillation is $500$ Hz, the velocity of the wave in $\dfrac{m}{s}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $200$
- **(B)** $180$
- **(C)** $100$
- **(D)** $80$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $180$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The relation between phase difference $\Delta \phi$ and path difference $\Delta x$ is given by:

$\Delta \phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x$

Given $\Delta \phi = 60^\circ = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\Delta x = 6$ cm $= 0.06$ m, substituting these values:

$\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \times 0.06$

$\lambda = 6 \times 0.06 = 0.36$ m

The velocity of the wave is given by:

$v = f \lambda$

$v = 500 \times 0.36 = 180$ m/s

Answer: $180$

</details>

### Q52. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two periodic waves of intensities $I_{1}$ and $I_{2}$ pass through a region at the same time in the same direction. The sum of the maximum and minimum intensities is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $I_{1}+I_{2}$
- **(B)** $\left(\sqrt{I_{1}}+\sqrt{I_{2}}\right)^{2}$
- **(C)** $\left(\sqrt{I_{1}}-\sqrt{I_{2}}\right)^{2}$
- **(D)** $2\left(I_{1}+I_{2}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2\left(I_{1}+I_{2}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Resultant intensity of two periodic waves is given by
$$
I=I_{1}+I_{2}+2 \sqrt{I_{1} I_{2}} \cos \delta
$$
where $\delta$ is the phase difference between the waves.
For maximum intensity,
$$
\delta=2 n \pi ; \quad n=0,1,2, \ldots \text { etc. }
$$
Therefore, for zero order maxima, $\cos \delta=1$
$$
I_{\max }=I_{1}+I_{2}+2 \sqrt{I_{1} I_{2}}=\left(\sqrt{I_{1}}+\sqrt{I_{2}}\right)^{2}
$$
For minimum intensity,
$$
\delta=(2 n-1) \pi ; n=1,2, \ldots \text { etc }
$$
Therefore, for Ist order minima, $\cos \delta=-1$
$$
\begin{array}{c}
I_{\min }=I_{1}+I_{2}-2 \sqrt{I_{1} I_{2}} \\
=\left(\sqrt{I_{1}}-\sqrt{I_{2}}\right)^{2} \\
\text { Therefore, } I_{\max }+I_{\min }=\left(\sqrt{I_{1}}+\sqrt{I_{2}}\right)^{2} \\
\quad+\left(\sqrt{I_{1}}-\sqrt{I_{2}}\right)^{2} \\
=2\left(I_{1}+I_{2}\right)
\end{array}
$$

</details>
