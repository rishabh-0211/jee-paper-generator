### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

For a stationary wave, $y = 10 \cos(\pi x/15)\sin(48\pi t)$ cm, the distance between a node and the successive antinode is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30 \text{ cm}$
- **(B)** $22.5 \text{ cm}$
- **(C)** $15 \text{ cm}$
- **(D)** $7.5 \text{ cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $7.5 \text{ cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Comparing the given equation $y = 10 \cos(\pi x/15)\sin(48\pi t)$ with the standard equation of a stationary wave $y = A \cos(kx)\sin(\omega t)$, we get:

$k = \dfrac{\pi}{15}$

Since $k = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}$, we have:

$\dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} = \dfrac{\pi}{15}$

$\lambda = 30 \text{ cm}$

The distance between a node and the successive antinode is $\dfrac{\lambda}{4}$.

Distance $= \dfrac{30}{4} = 7.5 \text{ cm}$

Answer: $7.5 \text{ cm}$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In case of stationary wave pattern, which of the following statement is CORRECT ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** The distance between consecutive antinodes is equal to the wavelength.
- **(B)** In a pipe open at both ends when an air column is vibrated, only even harmonics are present.
- **(C)** When an air column is vibrated in a pipe closed at one end, all harmonics are present.
- **(D)** In the case of a stretched string, when vibrated frequency of the first overtone is same as the second harmonic.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) In the case of a stretched string, when vibrated frequency of the first overtone is same as the second harmonic.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The distance between consecutive antinodes in a stationary wave is $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$, not $\lambda$.

In a pipe open at both ends, all harmonics (both even and odd) are present, with frequencies $f_n = n \dfrac{v}{2L}$ where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

In a pipe closed at one end, only odd harmonics are present, with frequencies $f_n = (2n-1) \dfrac{v}{4L}$ where $n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$

In a stretched string fixed at both ends, the frequencies of vibration are $f_n = n \dfrac{v}{2L}$. The fundamental frequency is $f_1$ (first harmonic). The first overtone corresponds to the next higher frequency, which is $f_2 = 2f_1$. This is exactly the second harmonic. Thus, the frequency of the first overtone is the same as the second harmonic.

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In a stationary wave, all the particles

**Options:**

- **(A)** except at node vibrate in S.H.M. of same period but of different amplitudes
- **(B)** vibrate in S.H.M. of same period and amplitude
- **(C)** except at nodes vibrate in S.H.M. of same period and same amplitude
- **(D)** vibrate in S.H.M. of different periods and different amplitudes

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) except at node vibrate in S.H.M. of same period but of different amplitudes**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of a stationary wave is given by $y = 2A \sin(kx) \cos(\omega t)$.

This can be written as $y = A' \cos(\omega t)$, where $A' = 2A \sin(kx)$ is the amplitude of the particle at position $x$.

The amplitude $A'$ depends on the position $x$, meaning different particles vibrate with different amplitudes.

The time-dependent part $\cos(\omega t)$ is the same for all particles, indicating that they all vibrate in Simple Harmonic Motion (S.H.M.) with the same angular frequency $\omega$ and the same period $T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}$.

At the nodes, $\sin(kx) = 0$, so the amplitude is zero and the particles do not vibrate.

Therefore, all particles except those at the nodes vibrate in S.H.M. with the same period but different amplitudes.

Answer: except at node vibrate in S.H.M. of same period but of different amplitudes

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Two progressive waves $Y_1=\sin 2 \pi\left(\frac{t}{0.4}-\frac{x}{4}\right)$ and $\mathrm{Y}_2=\sin 2 \pi\left(\frac{\mathrm{t}}{0.4}+\frac{\mathrm{x}}{4}\right)$ superpose to form a standing wave. ' $x$ ' and ' $y$ ' are in SI system. Amplitude of the particle at $x=0.5 \mathrm{~m}$ is $\left[\sin 45^{\circ}=\cos 45^{\circ}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{3} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(B)** $3 \sqrt{3} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{2} \mathrm{~m}$
- **(D)** $2 \sqrt{2} \mathrm{~m}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{2} \mathrm{~m}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given progressive waves are:

$Y_1 = \sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{0.4} - \dfrac{x}{4}\right)$

$Y_2 = \sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{0.4} + \dfrac{x}{4}\right)$

According to the principle of superposition, the resultant standing wave is given by:

$Y = Y_1 + Y_2$

$Y = \sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{0.4} - \dfrac{x}{4}\right) + \sin 2\pi\left(\dfrac{t}{0.4} + \dfrac{x}{4}\right)$

Using the trigonometric identity $\sin A + \sin B = 2 \sin\left(\dfrac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\dfrac{A-B}{2}\right)$:

$Y = 2 \sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi t}{0.4}\right) \cos\left(-\dfrac{2\pi x}{4}\right)$

$Y = 2 \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{2}\right) \sin\left(\dfrac{2\pi t}{0.4}\right)$

The amplitude of the standing wave at any position $x$ is given by the magnitude of the position-dependent part:

$A = \left| 2 \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{2}\right) \right|$

Substituting $x = 0.5 \text{ m}$:

$A = \left| 2 \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi \times 0.5}{2}\right) \right|$

$A = 2 \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$

Since $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \cos 45^{\circ} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$:

$A = 2 \times \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2} \text{ m}$

Answer: $\sqrt{2} \mathrm{~m}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Stationary wave is produced along the stretched string of length 80 cm . The resonant frequencies of string are $90 \mathrm{~Hz}, 150 \mathrm{~Hz}$ and 210 Hz . The speed of transverse wave in the string is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $45 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(B)** $75 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(C)** $48 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(D)** $80 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $48 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a stretched string fixed at both ends, the resonant frequencies are integral multiples of the fundamental frequency $f_0$.

The given resonant frequencies are $90 \mathrm{~Hz}$, $150 \mathrm{~Hz}$, and $210 \mathrm{~Hz}$. The fundamental frequency $f_0$ must be the highest common factor (HCF) of these frequencies.

$\text{HCF}(90, 150, 210) = 30 \mathrm{~Hz}$

Thus, the fundamental frequency is $f_0 = 30 \mathrm{~Hz}$.

The formula for the fundamental frequency of a stretched string is:

$f_0 = \dfrac{v}{2L}$

where $v$ is the speed of the transverse wave and $L$ is the length of the string.

Given $L = 80 \mathrm{~cm} = 0.8 \mathrm{~m}$, we can substitute the values into the formula:

$30 = \dfrac{v}{2 \times 0.8}$

$30 = \dfrac{v}{1.6}$

$v = 30 \times 1.6 = 48 \mathrm{~m/s}$

Answer: $48 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A stationary wave is formed having 3 nodes along the length of the string 90 cm . The wavelength of the wave is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 60 cm
- **(B)** 75 cm
- **(C)** 90 cm
- **(D)** 30 cm

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 90 cm**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a stationary wave on a string, the distance between two consecutive nodes is $\dfrac{\lambda}{2}$.

Given that there are 3 nodes along the length of the string, the number of loops (segments) formed between these nodes is $3 - 1 = 2$.

The total length of the string $L$ is equal to the sum of the lengths of these loops:

$L = 2 \times \dfrac{\lambda}{2} = \lambda$

Given $L = 90$ cm, we get:

$\lambda = 90$ cm

Answer: 90 cm

</details>

### Q23. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 1)

**Correct Option:** **(D) $0.025$ kg-wt.**

**Detailed Solution:**

For Melde's experiment. $\mathrm{TP}^{2}=$ constant where $\mathrm{T}$ is the tension and $\mathrm{P}$ is the number of loops.
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\therefore \mathrm{T}_{1} \mathrm{P}_{1}{ }^{2}=\mathrm{T}_{2} \mathrm{P}_{2}{ }^{2} \\
\therefore \mathrm{T}_{1}(4)^{2}=\left(\mathrm{T}_{1}-0.009\right)(5)^{2} \\
