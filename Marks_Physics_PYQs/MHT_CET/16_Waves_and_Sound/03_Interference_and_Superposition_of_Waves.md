### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two simple harmonic sound waves travelling in the same direction are given by $y_1(x,t) = A \sin(0.5\pi x - 100\pi t)$ and $y_2(x,t) = A \sin(0.46\pi x - 92\pi t)$
The number of times, a stationary listener can hear sound of maximum intensity in one second is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $4$
- **(C)** $6$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Comparing the given wave equations with the standard wave equation $y = A \sin(kx - \omega t)$, we get the angular frequencies of the two waves as:

$\omega_1 = 100\pi$

$\omega_2 = 92\pi$

The frequencies of the two waves are:

$f_1 = \dfrac{\omega_1}{2\pi} = \dfrac{100\pi}{2\pi} = 50$ Hz

$f_2 = \dfrac{\omega_2}{2\pi} = \dfrac{92\pi}{2\pi} = 46$ Hz

The number of times a stationary listener hears sound of maximum intensity in one second is equal to the beat frequency.

Beat frequency $= |f_1 - f_2|$

Beat frequency $= |50 - 46| = 4$ Hz

Thus, the listener hears sound of maximum intensity $4$ times in one second.

Answer: $4$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two sound waves each of wavelength $\lambda$ and same amplitude $A$ interfere at point $Q$. If the path difference is $\dfrac{\lambda}{4}$, the amplitude of the resultant wave at point $Q$ is $\left[\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1, \cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 0\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $A$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{2}A$
- **(C)** $3A$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{3}A$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sqrt{2}A$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The phase difference $\phi$ is related to the path difference $\Delta x$ by the formula:

$\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x$

Substituting $\Delta x = \dfrac{\lambda}{4}$:

$\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \times \dfrac{\lambda}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

The resultant amplitude $A_R$ of two interfering waves of amplitudes $A_1$ and $A_2$ is given by:

$A_R = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1A_2\cos\phi}$

Given $A_1 = A_2 = A$ and $\phi = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$:

$A_R = \sqrt{A^2 + A^2 + 2A^2\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)}$

Since $\cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 0$:

$A_R = \sqrt{2A^2} = \sqrt{2}A$

Answer: $\sqrt{2}A$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

When two sound waves having amplitude ' 3 ' and ' 5 ' units are superimposed then the ratio of maximum to minimum intensity of wave produced is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 16:1
- **(B)** 2:1
- **(C)** 5:3
- **(D)** 4:1

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 16:1**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the amplitudes of the two sound waves be $A_1 = 5$ units and $A_2 = 3$ units.

The maximum amplitude when the waves are superimposed is $A_{max} = A_1 + A_2 = 5 + 3 = 8$ units.

The minimum amplitude is $A_{min} = A_1 - A_2 = 5 - 3 = 2$ units.

The intensity of a wave is directly proportional to the square of its amplitude, $I \propto A^2$.

The ratio of maximum to minimum intensity is given by:

$\dfrac{I_{max}}{I_{min}} = \dfrac{(A_{max})^2}{(A_{min})^2}$

$\dfrac{I_{max}}{I_{min}} = \dfrac{8^2}{2^2} = \dfrac{64}{4} = \dfrac{16}{1}$

The ratio of maximum to minimum intensity is $16:1$.

Answer: 16:1

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two sound waves each of wavelength ' $\lambda$ ' and having the same amplitude ' A ' from two source ' $\mathrm{S}_1$ ' and ' $\mathrm{S}_2$ ' interfere at a point P . If the path difference, $\mathrm{S}_2 \mathrm{P}-\mathrm{S}_1 \mathrm{P}=\lambda / 3$ then the amplitude of resultant wave at point ' P ' will be $\left[\cos \left(120^{\circ}\right)=-0.5\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** A
- **(B)** $\quad 2 \mathrm{~A}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{A}}{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{3 \mathrm{~A}}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) A**

**Detailed Solution:**

The phase difference $\phi$ is related to the path difference $\Delta x$ by the relation:

$\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x$

Given $\Delta x = \dfrac{\lambda}{3}$, we get:

$\phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \times \dfrac{\lambda}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = 120^{\circ}$

The resultant amplitude $A_R$ of two interfering waves is given by:

$A_R = \sqrt{A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1A_2 \cos \phi}$

Substituting $A_1 = A_2 = A$ and $\phi = 120^{\circ}$:

$A_R = \sqrt{A^2 + A^2 + 2A^2 \cos 120^{\circ}}$

Since $\cos 120^{\circ} = -0.5$, we have:

$A_R = \sqrt{2A^2 + 2A^2 (-0.5)}$

$A_R = \sqrt{2A^2 - A^2} = \sqrt{A^2} = A$

Answer: A

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equations of two waves are given as
$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{y}_1=\mathrm{asin}\left(\omega \mathrm{t}+\phi_1\right) \\
& \mathrm{y}_2=\operatorname{asin}\left(\omega \mathrm{t}+\phi_2\right)
\end{aligned}$
If amplitude and time period of resultant wave is same as the individual waves, then $\left(\phi_1-\phi_2\right)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-1}{2}\right)$
- **(B)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-1}{4}\right)$
- **(C)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{6}\right)$
- **(D)** $\quad \cos ^{-1}\left(-\dfrac{1}{8}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\cos ^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-1}{2}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equations of the two waves are $y_1 = a \sin(\omega t + \phi_1)$ and $y_2 = a \sin(\omega t + \phi_2)$.

The amplitude of the resultant wave is given by $A_R = \sqrt{a_1^2 + a_2^2 + 2a_1 a_2 \cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2)}$.

Given that $a_1 = a$, $a_2 = a$, and $A_R = a$.

Substituting these values, we get $a = \sqrt{a^2 + a^2 + 2a^2 \cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2)}$.

Squaring both sides, $a^2 = 2a^2 + 2a^2 \cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2)$.

Dividing by $a^2$, $1 = 2 + 2 \cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2)$.

$2 \cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2) = -1$

$\cos(\phi_1 - \phi_2) = \dfrac{-1}{2}$

$\phi_1 - \phi_2 = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-1}{2}\right)$

Answer: $\cos ^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-1}{2}\right)$

</details>
