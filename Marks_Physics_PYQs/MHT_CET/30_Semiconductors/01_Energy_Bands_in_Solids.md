### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In the energy band diagram of a p-type semiconductor, the "Acceptor Energy Level" is located

**Options:**

- **(A)** exactly in the middle of the forbidden gap
- **(B)** inside the conduction band
- **(C)** just below the conduction band
- **(D)** just above the valence band

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) just above the valence band**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a p-type semiconductor, trivalent impurity atoms are added, which act as acceptors. These acceptor atoms create a discrete energy level known as the acceptor energy level.

This acceptor energy level is located just above the valence band in the energy band diagram. Because it is very close to the valence band, electrons from the valence band can easily be thermally excited into the acceptor level at room temperature, leaving behind holes in the valence band which contribute to electrical conduction.

Answer: just above the valence band

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In semiconductors, at room temperature the

**Options:**

- **(A)** valance band is partially empty and the conduction band is partially filled.
- **(B)** valance band is completely filled and the conduction band is partially filled.
- **(C)** valance band is completely filled.
- **(D)** conduction band is completely empty.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) valance band is partially empty and the conduction band is partially filled.**

**Detailed Solution:**

At absolute zero temperature ($0$ K), the valence band of a semiconductor is completely filled and the conduction band is completely empty.

At room temperature, some electrons in the valence band gain sufficient thermal energy to overcome the forbidden energy gap and jump into the conduction band.

As a result, the valence band becomes partially empty (due to the creation of holes) and the conduction band becomes partially filled with electrons.

Answer: valance band is partially empty and the conduction band is partially filled.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

There is no hole current in good conductors because they

**Options:**

- **(A)** have large forbidden energy gap.
- **(B)** have overlapping valence and conduction bands.
- **(C)** are full of electrons.
- **(D)** have no valence band.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) have overlapping valence and conduction bands.**

**Detailed Solution:**

In good conductors, the valence band and the conduction band overlap each other. There is no forbidden energy gap between them.

Because of this overlapping, a large number of free electrons are available for conduction at room temperature. The concept of holes as charge carriers arises in semiconductors where an electron leaves a vacancy in the valence band after crossing the energy gap. Since there is no band gap in conductors, the current is entirely due to the flow of free electrons, and there is no hole current.

Answer: have overlapping valence and conduction bands.

</details>

In energy band diagram of insulators, a band gap and the conduction band is respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** very high, empty
- **(B)** very low, empty
- **(C)** very high, completely filed
- **(D)** very low, partially filled

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In semiconductors at room temperature,

**Options:**

- **(A)** the valence band is completely filled and the conduction band partially filled.
- **(B)** the valence band is completely filled.
- **(C)** the conduction band is completely filled.
- **(D)** the valence band is partially empty and the conduction band partially filled.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) the valence band is partially empty and the conduction band partially filled.**

**Detailed Solution:**

At absolute zero temperature ($0\text{ K}$), the valence band of a semiconductor is completely filled and the conduction band is completely empty.

At room temperature, some electrons in the valence band acquire sufficient thermal energy to jump across the energy gap into the conduction band. This leaves behind vacant energy states (holes) in the valence band.

Therefore, at room temperature, the valence band becomes partially empty and the conduction band becomes partially filled.

Answer: the valence band is partially empty and the conduction band partially filled.

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2021 (21 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a pure silicon, number of electrons and holes per unit volume are $1.6 \times 10^{16} \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$. If silicon is doped with Boron in a way that on doping hole density increases to $4 \times 10^{22} \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$. Then electron density in doped semiconductor will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.4 \times 10^{-9} \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$
- **(B)** $6.4 \times 10^9 \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$
- **(C)** $6.4 \times 10^{-10} \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$
- **(D)** $6.4 \times 10^{10} \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $6.4 \times 10^9 \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{i}}=1.6 \times 10^{16} \mathrm{~m}^{-3}, \mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{h}}=4 \times 10^{22} \mathrm{~m}^{-3} \\
& \mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{e}} \mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{h}}=\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{i}}^2 \\
& \therefore \mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{e}}=\frac{\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{i}}^2}{\mathrm{n}_{\mathrm{h}}}=\frac{\left(1.6 \times 10^{16}\right)^2}{4 \times 10^{22}}=\frac{1.6 \times 1.6 \times 10^{32}}{4 \times 10^{22}} \\
& =6.4 \times 10^9 \mathrm{~m}^{-3}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A solid reflects incident light and its electrical conductivity decreases with temperature. The binding in this solid is

**Options:**

- **(A)** ionic
- **(B)** covalent
- **(C)** metallic
- **(D)** molecular

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) metallic**

**Detailed Solution:**

Metals reflect incident light by the vibrations of free electrons under the influence of electric field of incident wave. The conductivity of metals decreases with increase of temperature due to increases in random motion of free electrons. The bonding is therefore metallic.
