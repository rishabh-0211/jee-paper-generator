### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In an intrinsic semiconductor, at any ordinary temperature, the correct relation between the number of electrons per unit volume ($n_e$) and number of holes per unit volume ($n_h$) is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $n_e = n_h$
- **(B)** $n_e > n_h$
- **(C)** $n_e < n_h$
- **(D)** $n_e = n_h = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $n_e = n_h$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In an intrinsic semiconductor, charge carriers are generated due to thermal excitation.

When an electron jumps from the valence band to the conduction band, it leaves behind a vacancy in the valence band, which acts as a hole.

Since electrons and holes are always created in pairs, the number of electrons per unit volume is exactly equal to the number of holes per unit volume.

Therefore, $n_e = n_h$.

Answer: $n_e = n_h$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Pure silicon crystal at $300$K has equal electron and hole concentration $2 \times 10^{16}$ per m$^3$. Doping by indium increases hole concentration to $4 \times 10^{22}$ per m$^3$. Electron concentration in doped silicon is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5 \times 10^9/m^3$
- **(B)** $10^{10}/m^3$
- **(C)** $8 \times 10^{10}/m^3$
- **(D)** $10^9/m^3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $10^{10}/m^3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the mass action law for semiconductors:

$n_e n_h = n_i^2$

Given $n_i = 2 \times 10^{16} / m^3$ and $n_h = 4 \times 10^{22} / m^3$.

Substituting the values:

$n_e (4 \times 10^{22}) = (2 \times 10^{16})^2$

$n_e (4 \times 10^{22}) = 4 \times 10^{32}$

$n_e = \dfrac{4 \times 10^{32}}{4 \times 10^{22}}$

$n_e = 10^{10} / m^3$

Answer: $10^{10}/m^3$

</details>

### Q11. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

When a small amount of impurity atoms are added to a semiconductor then generally its resistivity

**Options:**

- **(A)** increases
- **(B)** decreases
- **(C)** does not change
- **(D)** may increase or decrease depending upon the percentage of doping

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) decreases**

**Detailed Solution:**

When a small amount of impurity atoms is added to a semiconductor, the process is known as doping.

Doping significantly increases the number of charge carriers (either free electrons or holes, depending on the type of impurity) in the semiconductor.

The conductivity of a semiconductor is given by $\sigma = e(n_e \mu_e + n_h \mu_h)$, where $n_e$ and $n_h$ are the number densities of electrons and holes respectively.

Since the number of charge carriers increases, the conductivity of the semiconductor increases.

Resistivity is the reciprocal of conductivity ($\rho = \dfrac{1}{\sigma}$).

Therefore, the resistivity of the semiconductor decreases.

Answer: decreases

</details>

### Q12. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A pure silicon crystal at temperature $300$ K has electron and hole concentration ($n_i$) $10^{16}$ per m$^3$ each and $10^{21}$ phosphorus atoms ($n_e$) are added per cubic metre. The new hole concentration in silicon crystal is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10^{19}$ per m$^3$
- **(B)** $10^{21}$ per m$^3$
- **(C)** $10^5$ per m$^3$
- **(D)** $10^{11}$ per m$^3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $10^{11}$ per m$^3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to the mass action law for semiconductors:

$n_e n_h = n_i^2$

Given the intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i = 10^{16}$ m$^{-3}$.

Phosphorus is a pentavalent impurity, which acts as a donor. Since the number of donor atoms added is much greater than the intrinsic concentration, the new electron concentration is approximately equal to the donor concentration:

$n_e \approx N_D = 10^{21}$ m$^{-3}$

Substituting the values into the mass action law to find the new hole concentration $n_h$:

$n_h = \dfrac{n_i^2}{n_e}$

$n_h = \dfrac{(10^{16})^2}{10^{21}}$

$n_h = \dfrac{10^{32}}{10^{21}} = 10^{11}$ m$^{-3}$

Answer: $10^{11}$ per m$^3$

</details>

At absolute zero temperature ($0\text{ K}$), the valence band of a semiconductor is completely filled and the conduction band is completely empty.

At room temperature, the available thermal energy is sufficient to excite some electrons from the valence band across the forbidden energy gap into the conduction band.

As a result, the conduction band becomes partially filled with these excited electrons, and the valence band becomes partially empty due to the creation of holes left behind by the electrons.

Therefore, at room temperature, the conduction band is partially filled and the valence band is partially empty.

Answer: the conduction band is partially filled and the valence band is partially empty.

</details>
