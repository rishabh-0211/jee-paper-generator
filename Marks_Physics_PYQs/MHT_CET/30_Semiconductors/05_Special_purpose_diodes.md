### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A LED emits visible light when its

**Options:**

- **(A)** junction is hot.
- **(B)** holes and electrons recombine.
- **(C)** depletion region widens.
- **(D)** junction is reverse biased.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) holes and electrons recombine.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A Light Emitting Diode (LED) is a heavily doped p-n junction diode that operates in forward bias.

When the diode is forward biased, electrons from the n-region and holes from the p-region cross the junction and recombine with each other.

During this recombination process, the energy difference between the conduction band and the valence band is released in the form of photons.

If the semiconductor material has a band gap in the range of $1.8 \text{ eV}$ to $3.0 \text{ eV}$, the emitted photons fall in the visible light spectrum.

Thus, an LED emits visible light when holes and electrons recombine.

Answer: holes and electrons recombine.

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The material used for solar cell should have band gap
(A) equal to zero.

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following statement is NOT correct in the case of LED?
(A) It is a heavily doped p-n junction diode.
(B) It emits light only when it is forward biased.
(C) It emits light only when it is reverse biased.
(D) The energy of the light emitted is equal to or slightly less than the energy gap of the semiconductor used.

**Options:**

- **(A)** A
- **(B)** B
- **(C)** C
- **(D)** D

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) C**

**Detailed Solution:**

A Light Emitting Diode (LED) is a heavily doped p-n junction diode which under forward bias emits spontaneous radiation.

When the diode is forward biased, electrons are sent from n $\rightarrow$ p (where they are minority carriers) and holes are sent from p $\rightarrow$ n (where they are minority carriers). At the junction boundary, the concentration of minority carriers increases compared to the equilibrium concentration. Thus, at the junction boundary on either side of the junction, excess minority carriers are there which recombine with majority carriers near the junction.

On recombination, the energy is released in the form of photons. Photons with energy equal to or slightly less than the band gap are emitted.

Therefore, an LED emits light only when it is forward biased. The statement that it emits light only when it is reverse biased is incorrect.

Answer: C

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Photodiode is a device

**Options:**

- **(A)** in which photocurrent is dependent on reverse bias.
- **(B)** in which photocurrent is independent of incident radiation.
- **(C)** which is always operated in forward bias.
- **(D)** which is always operated in reverse bias.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) which is always operated in reverse bias.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A photodiode is a p-n junction fabricated from a photosensitive semiconductor and provided with a transparent window to allow light to fall on its junction.

It is always operated under reverse bias. When the photodiode is illuminated with light (photons) with energy $h\nu$ greater than the energy gap $E_g$ of the semiconductor, electron-hole pairs are generated due to the absorption of photons.

Operating it in reverse bias makes it easier to observe changes in current with light intensity, because the fractional change in minority carrier current is much larger than the fractional change in majority carrier current (which would dominate in forward bias).

The photocurrent is directly proportional to the intensity of incident radiation and is largely independent of the reverse bias voltage.

Answer: which is always operated in reverse bias.

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** equal to zero.
- **(B)** less than $1.0 \mathrm{~eV}$ (non-zero).

### Q8. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

For detecting light intensity we use

**Options:**

- **(B)** photodiode in forwarded bias.

- **(D)** LED in forward bias.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) photodiode in reverse bias.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A photodiode is a semiconductor device used to detect optical signals or light intensity. It is always operated in reverse bias. ### Q9. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In the working of photodiode, the reverse current depends on

**Options:**

- **(A)** concentration of majority carriers.
- **(B)** concentration of minority carriers.
- **(C)** applied voltage.
- **(D)** recombination of holes and electrons.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) concentration of minority carriers.**

**Detailed Solution:**

A photodiode is operated in reverse bias mode. In reverse bias, the current that flows through the p-n junction is the reverse saturation current, which is primarily due to the drift of minority charge carriers across the junction.

When light of suitable frequency falls on the photodiode, additional electron-hole pairs are generated in or near the depletion region. This increases the concentration of minority carriers, which in turn increases the reverse current (photocurrent).

Therefore, the reverse current in a photodiode depends on the concentration of minority carriers.

Answer: concentration of minority carriers.

</details>
