### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

Two loudspeakers ($L_{1}$ and $L_{2}$) are placed with a separation of 10 m, as shown in figure. Both speakers are fed with an audio input signal of same frequency with constant volume. A voice recorder, initially at point $A$, at equidistance to both loud speakers, is moved by 25 m along the line $A B$ while monitoring the audio signal. The measured signal was found to undergo 10 cycles of minima and maxima during the movement. The frequency of the input signal is $\_\_\_\_$ Hz (Speed of sound in air is $324 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ and $\sqrt{5}=2.23$) 
 <img style="width:300px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/22S1_q_48_1.png">

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **600**

**Detailed Solution:**

Point B will $10^{\text {th }}$ maxima
$\Delta x=L_2 B-L_1 B$
$L_1 B=\sqrt{20^2+40^2}=20 \sqrt{5} \mathrm{~m}=44.6 \mathrm{~m}$
$L_2 B=\sqrt{40^2+30^2}=50 \mathrm{~m}$
$\Delta x=50-44.6=5.4 \mathrm{~m}$
$\Delta x=n \lambda$
$5.4=10 \times \lambda$
$\lambda=0.54 \mathrm{~m}$
$V=f \lambda$
$f=\frac{324}{0.54}=600 \mathrm{~Hz}$

</details>

### Q8. JEE Main 2012 (12 May Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A wave represented by the equation $y_1=a \cos$ $(k x-\omega t)$ is superimposed with another wave to form a stationary wave such that the point $x-0$ is node. The equation for the other wave is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $a \cos (k x-\omega t+\pi)$
- **(B)** $a \cos (k x+\omega t+\pi)$
- **(C)** $a \cos \left(k x+\omega t+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$
- **(D)** $a \cos \left(k x-\omega t+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $a \cos (k x+\omega t+\pi)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since the point $x=0$ is a node and reflection is taking place from point $x=0$. This means that reflection must be taking place from the fixed end and hence the reflected ray must suffer an additional phase change of $\pi$ or a path change of $\frac{\lambda}{2}$.
So, if $y_{\text {incident }}=a \cos (k x-\omega t)$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Rightarrow y_{\text {incident }} & =a \cos (-k x-\omega t+\pi) \\
& =-a \cos (\omega t+k x)
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence equation for the other wave
$$
y=a \cos (k x+\omega t+\pi)
$$

</details>
