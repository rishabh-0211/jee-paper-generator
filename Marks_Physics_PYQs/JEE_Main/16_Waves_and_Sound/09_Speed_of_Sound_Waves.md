### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The speed of a longitudinal wave in a metallic bar is $400 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$. If the density and Young's modulus of the bar material are increased by $0.5 \%$ and $1 \%$, respectively then the speed of the wave is changed approximately to $\_\_\_\_$ $\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{s}$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 398
- **(B)** 401
- **(C)** 399
- **(D)** 402

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 401**

**Detailed Solution:**

The speed of a longitudinal wave in a solid is given by $v = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}}$ where $Y$ is Young's modulus and $\rho$ is density. 
Initial condition: $v_0 = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}} = 400$ m/s 
After changes: $\rho' = 1.005\rho$ (0.5% increase) and $Y' = 1.01Y$ (1% increase) 
New speed: $v' = \sqrt{\frac{Y'}{\rho'}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.01Y}{1.005\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.01}{1.005}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}}$ 
$\frac{v'}{v_0} = \sqrt{\frac{1.01}{1.005}} = \sqrt{1.00498...} \approx 1.00249$ 
$v' = 400 \times 1.00249 \approx 401$ m/s

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

The velocity of sound in air is doubled when the temperature is raised from $0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ to $\alpha^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. The value of $\alpha$ is $\_\_\_\_$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **819**

**Detailed Solution:**

The velocity of sound in air depends on temperature as $v \propto \sqrt{T}$ (absolute temperature).
If the velocity doubles:
$\frac{v_2}{v_1} = 2 = \sqrt{\frac{T_2}{T_1}}$.
Squaring: $\frac{T_2}{T_1} = 4$.
With $T_1 = 0°C = 273.15$ K and $T_2 = 273.15 + \alpha$ K:
$\frac{273.15 + \alpha}{273.15} = 4$, giving $273.15 + \alpha = 1092.6$ K.
Therefore $\alpha = 819.45°C \approx 819°C$.

</details>

The ratio of the speed of sound through the gases $\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{He}}: \mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{CH}_4}: \mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{CO}_2}$ is given by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\frac{7}{5}}: \sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}: \sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\frac{5}{3}}: \sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}: \sqrt{\frac{7}{5}}$

From any of the displacement equation
