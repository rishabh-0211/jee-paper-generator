### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Figure represents the extension ($\Delta l$) of a wire of length $1$ meter, suspended from the ceiling of the room at one end with a load $W$ connected to the other end. If the cross-sectional area of the wire is $10^{-5}$ m$^2$ then the Young's modulus of the wire is __________ N/m$^2$.
<img style="width: 250px" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/06APRS2__q33_d1.jpg" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.0 \times 10^{11}$
- **(B)** $2.0 \times 10^{10}$
- **(C)** $1.0 \times 10^{10}$
- **(D)** $2.0 \times 10^{11}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.0 \times 10^{10}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

From the given graph, we can observe that for a load $W = 20$ N, the extension is $\Delta l = 2 \times 10^{-4}$ m.

The formula for Young's modulus $Y$ is:

$Y = \dfrac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}} = \dfrac{W / A}{\Delta l / L} = \dfrac{W L}{A \Delta l}$

Given:
Length of the wire, $L = 1$ m
Cross-sectional area, $A = 10^{-5}$ m$^2$

Substituting the values into the formula:

$Y = \dfrac{20 \times 1}{10^{-5} \times 2 \times 10^{-4}}$

$Y = \dfrac{20}{2 \times 10^{-9}}$

$Y = 10 \times 10^9 = 1.0 \times 10^{10}$ N/m$^2$

Answer: $1.0 \times 10^{10}$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The two wires $A$ and $B$ of equal cross-section but of different materials are joined together. The ratio of Young's modulus of wire $A$ and wire $B$ is $20/11$. When the joined wire is kept under certain tension the elongations in the wires $A$ and $B$ are equal. If the length of wire $A$ is $2.2$ m, then the length of wire $B$ is _______ m.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.1$
- **(B)** $2.22$
- **(C)** $1.21$
- **(D)** $4.44$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.21$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given that the wires have equal cross-sectional area $A$ and are under the same tension $F$, the elongation $\Delta L$ is given by:

$\Delta L = \dfrac{F L}{A Y}$

Since the elongations in both wires are equal ($\Delta L_A = \Delta L_B$):

$\dfrac{F L_A}{A Y_A} = \dfrac{F L_B}{A Y_B}$

$\dfrac{L_A}{Y_A} = \dfrac{L_B}{Y_B}$

$L_B = L_A \times \dfrac{Y_B}{Y_A}$

Substituting the given values $L_A = 2.2$ m and $\dfrac{Y_A}{Y_B} = \dfrac{20}{11}$:

$L_B = 2.2 \times \dfrac{11}{20}$

$L_B = \dfrac{24.2}{20} = 1.21$ m

Answer: $1.21$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A lift of mass $1600$ kg is supported by thick iron wire. If the maximum stress which the wire can withstand is $4 \times 10^8$ N/m$^2$ and its radius is $4$ mm, then maximum acceleration the lift can take is _______ m/s$^2$. (take $g = 10$ m/s$^2$ and $\pi = 3.14$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.56$
- **(B)** $3.89$
- **(C)** $4.32$
- **(D)** $5.16$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2.56$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Maximum stress $\sigma_{max} = 4 \times 10^8$ N/m$^2$

Radius of wire $r = 4 \times 10^{-3}$ m

Mass of lift $m = 1600$ kg

Maximum tension the wire can withstand is given by:

$T_{max} = \sigma_{max} \times \pi r^2$

$T_{max} = 4 \times 10^8 \times 3.14 \times (4 \times 10^{-3})^2$

$T_{max} = 4 \times 10^8 \times 3.14 \times 16 \times 10^{-6} = 20096$ N

When the lift accelerates upwards with maximum acceleration $a$, the tension in the wire is:

$T_{max} = m(g + a)$

$20096 = 1600(10 + a)$

$10 + a = \dfrac{20096}{1600} = 12.56$

$a = 12.56 - 10 = 2.56$ m/s$^2$

Answer: $2.56$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two wires as shown in the figure below, made of steel and have breaking stress of $12 \times 10^8$ N/m$^2$. Area of cross-section of upper wire is $0.008$ cm$^2$ and of lower wire is $0.004$ cm$^2$. The maximum mass that can be added to pan without breaking any wire is _____ kg.
(take $g = 10$ m/s$^2$)
<img style="width: 200px" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/05APRS1__q43_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $56$
- **(B)** $38$
- **(C)** $96$
- **(D)** $5.6$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $38$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the maximum mass that can be added to the pan be $m$.

The breaking stress for both wires is $\sigma_{max} = 12 \times 10^8 \text{ N/m}^2$.

For the upper wire:
Area of cross-section, $A_u = 0.008 \text{ cm}^2 = 8 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}^2$
Maximum tension the upper wire can withstand is:
$T_{u,max} = \sigma_{max} \times A_u = (12 \times 10^8) \times (8 \times 10^{-7}) = 960 \text{ N}$
The total mass supported by the upper wire is $(30 + 10 + m) = (40 + m) \text{ kg}$.
Tension in the upper wire, $T_u = (40 + m)g = (40 + m) \times 10$
To avoid breaking the upper wire:
$(40 + m) \times 10 \le 960$
$40 + m \le 96 \implies m \le 56 \text{ kg}$

For the lower wire:
Area of cross-section, $A_l = 0.004 \text{ cm}^2 = 4 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}^2$
Maximum tension the lower wire can withstand is:
$T_{l,max} = \sigma_{max} \times A_l = (12 \times 10^8) \times (4 \times 10^{-7}) = 480 \text{ N}$
The total mass supported by the lower wire is $(10 + m) \text{ kg}$.
Tension in the lower wire, $T_l = (10 + m)g = (10 + m) \times 10$
To avoid breaking the lower wire:
$(10 + m) \times 10 \le 480$
$10 + m \le 48 \implies m \le 38 \text{ kg}$

To ensure neither wire breaks, the added mass $m$ must satisfy both conditions. Therefore, the maximum mass that can be added is the smaller of the two values:
$m = 38 \text{ kg}$

Answer: $38$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The Young's modulus of steel wire of radius $r$ and length $L$ is $Y$. If the radius $r$ and length $L$ of the wire are doubled then the value of $Y$

**Options:**

- **(A)** increases by two times
- **(B)** reduces by half
- **(C)** remains unchanged
- **(D)** becomes one fourth

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) remains unchanged**

**Detailed Solution:**

Young's modulus $Y$ is an intrinsic property of the material of the wire.

It depends only on the nature of the material and its temperature, and is independent of the dimensions of the wire such as length $L$ and radius $r$.

Therefore, when the radius and length of the steel wire are doubled, its Young's modulus remains unchanged.

Answer: remains unchanged

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A metal string $A$ is suspended from a rigid support and its free end is attached to a block of mass $M$. Second block having mass $2M$ is suspended at the bottom of the first block using a string $B$. The area of cross sections of strings $A$ and $B$ are same. The ratio of lengths of strings of $A$ to $B$ is $2$ and the ratio of their Young's moduli $(Y_A/Y_B)$ is $0.5$. The ratio of elongations in $A$ to $B$ is ______.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $4$
- **(C)** $8$
- **(D)** $6$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $6$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Tension in string $B$ is due to the block of mass $2M$:

$T_B = 2Mg$

Tension in string $A$ is due to both blocks of mass $M$ and $2M$:

$T_A = Mg + 2Mg = 3Mg$

The elongation in a string is given by Hooke's law as $\Delta L = \dfrac{TL}{AY}$.

The ratio of elongations in string $A$ to string $B$ is:

$\dfrac{\Delta L_A}{\Delta L_B} = \left(\dfrac{T_A}{T_B}\right) \left(\dfrac{L_A}{L_B}\right) \left(\dfrac{A_B}{A_A}\right) \left(\dfrac{Y_B}{Y_A}\right)$

Given that $\dfrac{L_A}{L_B} = 2$, $\dfrac{A_B}{A_A} = 1$, and $\dfrac{Y_A}{Y_B} = 0.5 \Rightarrow \dfrac{Y_B}{Y_A} = 2$.

Substituting the values into the ratio equation:

$\dfrac{\Delta L_A}{\Delta L_B} = \left(\dfrac{3Mg}{2Mg}\right) \times (2) \times (1) \times (2)$

$\dfrac{\Delta L_A}{\Delta L_B} = \dfrac{3}{2} \times 4 = 6$

Answer: $6$

</details>

### Q7. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A string $A$ of length $0.314$ m and Young's modulus $2 \times 10^{10}$ N/m$^2$ is connected to another string $B$ of length and Young's modulus both twice of those of $A$. This series combination of strings is then suspended from a rigid support and its free end is fixed to a load of mass $0.8$ kg. The net change in length of the combination is _____ mm. (radius of both the strings is $0.2$ mm and acceleration due to gravity $= 10$ m/s$^2$) (Mass of both strings is to be neglected as compared to the mass of load)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $1.9$
- **(D)** $1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:
Length of string A, $L_A = 0.314$ m
Young's modulus of string A, $Y_A = 2 \times 10^{10}$ N/m$^2$
Radius of both strings, $r = 0.2$ mm $= 2 \times 10^{-4}$ m
Length of string B, $L_B = 2L_A = 0.628$ m
Young's modulus of string B, $Y_B = 2Y_A = 4 \times 10^{10}$ N/m$^2$
Mass of load, $m = 0.8$ kg
Acceleration due to gravity, $g = 10$ m/s$^2$

Tension in both strings, $T = mg = 0.8 \times 10 = 8$ N

Cross-sectional area of both strings:
$A = \pi r^2 = 3.14 \times (2 \times 10^{-4})^2 = 3.14 \times 4 \times 10^{-8}$ m$^2$

Elongation in string A:
$\Delta L_A = \dfrac{T L_A}{A Y_A}$
$\Delta L_A = \dfrac{8 \times 0.314}{3.14 \times 4 \times 10^{-8} \times 2 \times 10^{10}}$
$\Delta L_A = \dfrac{8 \times 0.314}{8 \times 3.14 \times 10^2} = \dfrac{0.1}{10^2} = 10^{-3}$ m $= 1$ mm

Elongation in string B:
$\Delta L_B = \dfrac{T L_B}{A Y_B}$
$\Delta L_B = \dfrac{8 \times (2 \times 0.314)}{A \times (2 Y_A)} = \dfrac{T L_A}{A Y_A} = 1$ mm

Net change in length of the combination:
$\Delta L = \Delta L_A + \Delta L_B = 1 + 1 = 2$ mm

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q8. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

A uniform wire of length $l$ of weight $w$ is suspended from the roof with a weight of $W$ at the other end. The stress in the wire at $\dfrac{l}{3}$ distance from the top is $\left(\dfrac{W}{A} + \dfrac{2}{\gamma}\dfrac{w}{A}\right)$, where, $A$ is the cross sectional area of the wire. The value of $\gamma$ is _______.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **3**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $T$ be the tension in the wire at a distance $\dfrac{l}{3}$ from the top.

The length of the wire below this point is $l - \dfrac{l}{3} = \dfrac{2l}{3}$.

The weight of this lower part of the wire is $\dfrac{w}{l} \times \dfrac{2l}{3} = \dfrac{2w}{3}$.

The total downward force (tension $T$) at this point is the sum of the weight $W$ attached at the bottom and the weight of the lower part of the wire:

$T = W + \dfrac{2w}{3}$

The stress at this point is given by the tension divided by the cross-sectional area $A$:

$\text{Stress} = \dfrac{T}{A} = \dfrac{W + \dfrac{2w}{3}}{A} = \dfrac{W}{A} + \dfrac{2}{3}\dfrac{w}{A}$

Comparing this with the given expression for stress, $\dfrac{W}{A} + \dfrac{2}{\gamma}\dfrac{w}{A}$, we get:

$\gamma = 3$

Answer: $3$

</details>

### Q9. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

The diameter of a wire measured by a screw gauge of least count $0.001$ cm is $0.08$ cm. The length measured by a scale of least count $0.1$ cm is $150$ cm. When a weight of $100$ N is applied to the wire, the extension in length is $0.5$ cm, measured by a micrometer of least count $0.001$ cm. The error in the measured Young's modulus is $\alpha \times 10^9$ N/m$^2$. The value of $\alpha$ is _______.
(Ignore the contribution of the load to Young's modulus error calculation)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.3$
- **(B)** $1.65$
- **(C)** $0.13$
- **(D)** $0.25$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1.65$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The formula for Young's modulus is given by:

$Y = \dfrac{F L_0}{A \Delta L} = \dfrac{4 F L_0}{\pi d^2 \Delta L}$

Substituting the given values into the formula:

$Y = \dfrac{4 \times 100 \times 1.5}{\pi \times (0.08 \times 10^{-2})^2 \times (0.5 \times 10^{-2})}$

$Y = \dfrac{600}{\pi \times 64 \times 10^{-8} \times 5 \times 10^{-3}} = \dfrac{15}{8\pi} \times 10^{11} \text{ N/m}^2$

The relative error in Young's modulus is:

$\dfrac{\Delta Y}{Y} = \dfrac{\Delta L_0}{L_0} + 2\dfrac{\Delta d}{d} + \dfrac{\Delta (\Delta L)}{\Delta L}$

Substituting the least counts as absolute errors and the measured values:

$\dfrac{\Delta Y}{Y} = \dfrac{0.1}{150} + 2 \times \dfrac{0.001}{0.08} + \dfrac{0.001}{0.5}$

$\dfrac{\Delta Y}{Y} = \dfrac{1}{1500} + \dfrac{1}{40} + \dfrac{1}{500}$

$\dfrac{\Delta Y}{Y} = \dfrac{2 + 75 + 6}{3000} = \dfrac{83}{3000}$

The absolute error in Young's modulus is:

$\Delta Y = Y \times \dfrac{\Delta Y}{Y} = \left(\dfrac{15}{8\pi} \times 10^{11}\right) \times \dfrac{83}{3000}$

$\Delta Y = \dfrac{15 \times 83}{24000 \pi} \times 10^{11} = \dfrac{83}{1600 \pi} \times 10^{11} = \dfrac{83}{16\pi} \times 10^9 \text{ N/m}^2$

Using $\pi \approx 3.14$:

$\Delta Y = \dfrac{83}{16 \times 3.14} \times 10^9 = \dfrac{83}{50.24} \times 10^9 \approx 1.65 \times 10^9 \text{ N/m}^2$

Comparing this with $\alpha \times 10^9 \text{ N/m}^2$, we get $\alpha = 1.65$.

Answer: $1.65$

</details>

### Q10. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two wires $A$ and $B$ made of different materials of lengths 6.0 cm and 5.4 cm, respectively and area of cross sections $3.0 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~m}^{2}$ and $4.5 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~m}^{2}$, respectively are stretched by the same magnitude under a given load. The ratio of the Young's modulus of $A$ to that of $B$ is $x: 3$. The value of $x$ is $\_\_\_\_$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** 1
- **(C)** 4
- **(D)** 5

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 5**

**Detailed Solution:**

Young's modulus: $Y = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon} = \frac{F/A}{\Delta L/L}$.
When the same force is applied and both wires stretch by the same extension $\Delta L$, the ratio of Young's moduli is:
$\frac{Y_A}{Y_B} = \frac{F/A_A}{\Delta L/L_A} \times \frac{\Delta L/L_B}{F/A_B} = \frac{A_B}{A_A} \times \frac{L_A}{L_B}$ 
$=\frac{4.5 \times 10^{-5}}{3.0 \times 10^{-5}} \times \frac{6.0}{5.4} = 1.5 \times \frac{10}{9} = \frac{5}{3}$.
Therefore $x : 3 = 5 : 3$, giving $x = 5$.

</details>

### Q11. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The strain-stress plot for materials $A, B, C$ and $D$ is shown in the figure. Which material has the largest Young's modulus ? 
 <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/23S1_q_45_1.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $D$
- **(B)** $B$
- **(C)** $C$
- **(D)** $A$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $C$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Young's modulus ($Y$) is defined as the ratio of stress to strain: $Y = \frac{\text{stress}}{\text{strain}}$.
In the given graph, the y-axis represents strain and the x-axis represents stress.
The slope of the line in a strain-stress graph is given by $m = \frac{\text{strain}}{\text{stress}}$.
Therefore, $Y = \frac{1}{\text{slope}}$.
To have the largest Young's modulus, the material must have the smallest slope in the strain-stress plot.
From the figure, line $C$ has the smallest angle with the stress axis (x-axis), meaning it has the minimum slope.
Since $Y \propto \frac{1}{\text{slope}}$, material $C$ has the largest Young's modulus.

</details>
