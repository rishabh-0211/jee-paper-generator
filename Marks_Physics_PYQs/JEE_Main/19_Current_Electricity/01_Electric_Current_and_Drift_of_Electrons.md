### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In an experiment to determine the resistance of a given wire using Ohm's law, the voltmeter and ammeter readings are noted as $10\text{ V}$ and $5\text{ A}$, respectively. The least counts of voltmeter and ammeter are $500\text{ mV}$ and $200\text{ mA}$, respectively. The estimated error in the resistance measurement is _______ $\Omega$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.25$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $2.5$
- **(D)** $0.18$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $0.18$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $V = 10\text{ V}$ and $I = 5\text{ A}$

Least count of voltmeter, $\Delta V = 500\text{ mV} = 0.5\text{ V}$

Least count of ammeter, $\Delta I = 200\text{ mA} = 0.2\text{ A}$

Resistance is given by Ohm's law:

$R = \dfrac{V}{I} = \dfrac{10}{5} = 2\text{ }\Omega$

The maximum permissible relative error in resistance is:

$\dfrac{\Delta R}{R} = \dfrac{\Delta V}{V} + \dfrac{\Delta I}{I}$

$\dfrac{\Delta R}{2} = \dfrac{0.5}{10} + \dfrac{0.2}{5}$

$\dfrac{\Delta R}{2} = 0.05 + 0.04 = 0.09$

$\Delta R = 2 \times 0.09 = 0.18\text{ }\Omega$

Answer: $0.18$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which one of the following is not a measurable quantity?

**Options:**

- **(A)** Voltage
- **(B)** Resistance
- **(C)** Displacement current
- **(D)** Voltage difference

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Voltage**

**Detailed Solution:**

In electrostatics and circuit theory, potential (voltage) at a single point is defined relative to a reference point (usually infinity or ground where potential is assumed to be zero).
Absolute voltage at a point is not a physically measurable quantity because only the difference in potential between two points can be measured using instruments like a voltmeter.
Resistance can be measured using an ohmmeter or bridge circuits.
Displacement current is a physical quantity that can be calculated and its effects (magnetic field) can be measured.
Voltage difference (potential difference) is the standard quantity measured between two points in a circuit.
Therefore, absolute voltage is not a measurable quantity.

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

A cylindrical conductor of length 2 m and area of cross-section $0.2 \mathrm{~mm}^{2}$ carries an electric current of 1.6 A when its ends are connected to a 2 V battery. Mobility of electrons in the conductor is $\alpha \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m}^{2} / V. s$. The value of $\alpha$ is : 
 (electron concentration $=5 \times 10^{28} / \mathrm{m}^{3}$ and electron charge $\left.=1.6 \times 10^{-19} \mathrm{C}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **1**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a cylindrical conductor with length 2 m, cross-section 0.2 mm² = 0.2 × 10⁻⁶ m², carrying 1.6 A at 2 V.
Resistance: $R = V/I = 2/1.6 = 1.25$ Ω.
Resistivity: $\rho = RA/L = 1.25 \times 0.2 \times 10^{-6}/2$
$= 0.125 \times 10^{-6}$ Ω·m.
Using $\rho = 1/(ne\mu)$, mobility: $\mu = 1/(ne\rho)$
$= 1/(5 \times 10^{28} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 0.125 \times 10^{-6})$
$= 1/(1 \times 10^3) = 10^{-3}$ m²/V·s.
Thus $\alpha = 1$.

</details>
