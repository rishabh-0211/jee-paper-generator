### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

A moving coil of galvanometer when shunted with $2\,\Omega$ resistance gives a full scale deflection for a current of $500$ mA. When a resistance of $470\,\Omega$ is connected in series it gives a full scale deflection for $10$ V potential applied on it. The value of resistance of galvanometer coil is __________ $\Omega$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **50**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $G$ be the resistance of the galvanometer and $I_g$ be the current required for full scale deflection.

When the galvanometer is shunted with $S = 2\,\Omega$, it acts as an ammeter of range $I = 500\text{ mA} = 0.5\text{ A}$. The potential difference across the galvanometer and the shunt is equal:

$I_g G = (I - I_g) S$

$I_g G = (0.5 - I_g) \times 2$

$I_g G + 2 I_g = 1$

$I_g = \dfrac{1}{G + 2}$

When a resistance $R = 470\,\Omega$ is connected in series, it acts as a voltmeter of range $V = 10\text{ V}$. The total voltage is the sum of the voltage drops:

$V = I_g (G + R)$

$10 = I_g (G + 470)$

$I_g = \dfrac{10}{G + 470}$

Equating the two expressions for $I_g$:

$\dfrac{1}{G + 2} = \dfrac{10}{G + 470}$

$G + 470 = 10(G + 2)$

$G + 470 = 10G + 20$

$9G = 450$

$G = 50\,\Omega$

Answer: $50$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A voltmeter with internal resistance of $x\ \Omega$ can be used to measure upto $20$ V. In order to increase its measuring range to $30$ V, the required modification is to _____.

**Options:**

- **(A)** connect resistor of $\dfrac{x}{2}\ \Omega$, in series with voltmeter.
- **(B)** connect resistor of $\dfrac{x}{2}\ \Omega$, in parallel to voltmeter.
- **(C)** connect a resistor of $x\ \Omega$ in series with voltmeter.
- **(D)** connect resistor of $2x\ \Omega$ in parallel to voltmeter.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) connect resistor of $\dfrac{x}{2}\ \Omega$, in series with voltmeter.**

$V_2 = I_g(R_v + R)$

$30 = \dfrac{20}{x}(x + R)$

$30x = 20x + 20R$

$10x = 20R$

$R = \dfrac{x}{2}\ \Omega$

Thus, a resistor of $\dfrac{x}{2}\ \Omega$ should be connected in series with the voltmeter.

Answer: connect resistor of $\dfrac{x}{2}\ \Omega$, in series with voltmeter.

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the potentiometer, when the cell in the secondary circuit is shunted with $4 \Omega$ resistance, the balance is obtained at the length 120 cm of wire. Now when the same cell is shunted with $12 \Omega$ resistance, the balance is shifted to a length of 180 cm. The internal resistance of cell is $\_\_\_\_$ $\Omega$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 4
- **(B)** 12
- **(C)** 6
- **(D)** 3

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 4**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a potentiometer, the internal resistance $r$ of a cell is given by the formula $r = R \left( \frac{l_0}{l} - 1 \right)$, where $l_0$ is the balancing length in open circuit, $l$ is the balancing length when shunted with resistance $R$.
This can be rewritten as $\frac{l_0}{l} = 1 + \frac{r}{R}$ or $l = \frac{l_0}{1 + r/R}$.
Case 1: $R_1 = 4 \Omega$ and $l_1 = 120$ cm.
$120 = \frac{l_0}{1 + r/4} = \frac{4l_0}{4 + r}$ ... (i)
Case 2: $R_2 = 12 \Omega$ and $l_2 = 180$ cm.
$180 = \frac{l_0}{1 + r/12} = \frac{12l_0}{12 + r}$ ... (ii)
Dividing equation (ii) by (i):
$\frac{180}{120} = \frac{12l_0}{12 + r} \times \frac{4 + r}{4l_0}$
$\frac{3}{2} = \frac{3(4 + r)}{12 + r}$
$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{4 + r}{12 + r}$
$12 + r = 8 + 2r$
$r = 4 \Omega$
The internal resistance of the cell is $4 \Omega$.

</details>

A moving coil galvanometer of resistance $100 \Omega$ shows a full scale deflection for a current of 1 mA. The value of resistance required to convert this galvanometer into an ammeter, showing full scale deflection for a current of 5 mA, is $\_\_\_\_$ $\Omega$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0.5
- **(B)** 2.5
- **(C)** 10
- **(D)** 25

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 25**

**Detailed Solution:**

To convert a galvanometer to an ammeter, a shunt resistance $S$ is connected in parallel. For full-scale deflection:
Current through galvanometer: $I_g = 1$ mA
Total current: $I = 5$ mA
Current through shunt: $I_s = 5 - 1 = 4$ mA
Since voltage across both is equal: $I_g \cdot G = I_s \cdot S$
$0.001 \times 100 = 0.004 \times S$
$S = \frac{0.1}{0.004} = 25$ Ω

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

To compare EMF of two cells using potentiometer the balancing lengths obtained are 200 cm and 150 cm. The least count of scale is 1 cm. The percentage error in the ratio of EMFs is $\_\_\_\_$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1.65
- **(B)** 1.45
- **(C)** 1.75
- **(D)** 1.16

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 1.16**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\epsilon = \lambda \ell$ (Potential gradient)

$\epsilon_1 = \lambda \ell_1$
$\epsilon_2 = \lambda \ell_2$

$y = \frac{\epsilon_1}{\epsilon_2} = \frac{\ell_1}{\ell_2}$

$\frac{\Delta y}{y} = \frac{\Delta \ell_1}{\ell_1} + \frac{\Delta \ell_2}{\ell_2}$

$\frac{\Delta y}{y} = \frac{1}{200} + \frac{1}{150}$

$\frac{\Delta y}{y} \times 100 = \left(\frac{1}{200} + \frac{1}{150}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{3+4}{600}\right) \times 100 = \frac{7}{6} = 1.16\%$

</details>

### Q7. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A battery with EMF $E$ and internal resistance $r$ is connected across a resistance $R$. The power consumption in $R$ will be maximum when :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $R=2 r$
- **(B)** $R=r / 2$
- **(C)** $R=r$
- **(D)** $R=\sqrt{2} r$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $R=r$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Power consumed in external resistance R is $P = \frac{E^2 R}{(R + r)^2}$.
To find maximum, differentiate with respect to R:
$\frac{dP}{dR} = E^2 \frac{(R + r)^2 - 2R(R + r)}{(R + r)^4} = E^2 \frac{r - R}{(R + r)^3}$.
$\frac{dP}{dR} = 0$ gives $R = r$.
This is the maximum power transfer theorem: maximum power is delivered when the external load resistance equals the source internal resistance.

</details>
