### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

The de Broglie wavelength for an electron accelerated through the potential difference of $V_1$ volt is $\lambda_1$. When the potential difference is changed to $V_2$ volt, the associated de Broglie wavelength is increased by $50\%$. If $(V_1/V_2) = (9/\alpha)$, then the value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **4**

**Detailed Solution:**

The de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through a potential difference $V$ is given by $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2meV}}$.

This implies $\lambda \propto \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{V}}$.

Given that the new wavelength $\lambda_2$ is increased by $50\%$, we have $\lambda_2 = \lambda_1 + 0.5\lambda_1 = \dfrac{3}{2}\lambda_1$.

Taking the ratio of the wavelengths, we get $\dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}}$.

Substituting $\lambda_2 = \dfrac{3}{2}\lambda_1$, we get $\dfrac{2}{3} = \sqrt{\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}}$.

Squaring both sides yields $\dfrac{V_2}{V_1} = \dfrac{4}{9}$, which gives $\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{9}{4}$.

Comparing this with the given relation $\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{9}{\alpha}$, we find $\alpha = 4$.

Answer: $4$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

An electron is travelling with a velocity $v$ in free space and when it enters a medium, its velocity is reduced by $20\%$. The de Broglie wavelength of electron in the medium is $\alpha\lambda_0$, where $\lambda_0$ is its de Broglie wavelength in free space. The value of $\alpha$ is _______.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.20$
- **(B)** $1.0$
- **(C)** $1.25$
- **(D)** $0.75$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.25$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The de Broglie wavelength of an electron in free space is given by $\lambda_0 = \dfrac{h}{mv}$.

When the electron enters the medium, its velocity is reduced by $20\%$. The new velocity is $v' = v - 0.20v = 0.80v$.

The de Broglie wavelength in the medium is $\lambda' = \dfrac{h}{mv'}$.

Substituting $v' = 0.80v$, we get $\lambda' = \dfrac{h}{m(0.80v)} = \dfrac{1}{0.80} \left(\dfrac{h}{mv}\right)$.

Since \dfrac{1}{0.80} = \dfrac{100}{80} = 1.25$, we have $\lambda' = 1.25\lambda_0$.

Comparing this with $\lambda' = \alpha\lambda_0$, we get $\alpha = 1.25$.

Answer: $1.25$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An electron of mass $m$ is moving in an electric field $\vec{E} = -2E_0\hat{i}$ ($E_0 =$ constant $> 0$), with an initial velocity $\vec{V} = v_0\hat{i}$ ($v_0 =$ constant $> 0$). If $\lambda_0 = \dfrac{h}{4mv_0}$, its de Broglie wavelength at time $t$ is __________.
($e =$ charge of electron)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{4\lambda_0}{\left[1 - \dfrac{E_0 e}{2m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right]}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{4\lambda_0}{\left[1 + \dfrac{E_0 e}{2m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right]}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{4\lambda_0}{\left[1 + \dfrac{2E_0 e}{m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right]}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{4\lambda_0}{\left[1 - \dfrac{2E_0 e}{m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right]}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{4\lambda_0}{\left[1 + \dfrac{2E_0 e}{m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right]}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The force experienced by the electron in the electric field is given by $\vec{F} = q\vec{E}$.

Since the charge of an electron is $-e$ and the electric field is $\vec{E} = -2E_0\hat{i}$, the force is:

$\vec{F} = (-e)(-2E_0\hat{i}) = 2eE_0\hat{i}$

The acceleration of the electron is:

$\vec{a} = \dfrac{\vec{F}}{m} = \dfrac{2eE_0}{m}\hat{i}$

Using the first equation of motion, the velocity of the electron at time $t$ is:

$\vec{v}(t) = \vec{V} + \vec{a}t = \left(v_0 + \dfrac{2eE_0}{m}t\right)\hat{i}$

The de Broglie wavelength of the electron at time $t$ is:

$\lambda = \dfrac{h}{m|\vec{v}(t)|} = \dfrac{h}{m\left(v_0 + \dfrac{2eE_0}{m}t\right)}$

We are given that $\lambda_0 = \dfrac{h}{4mv_0}$, which implies $h = 4mv_0\lambda_0$. Substituting this into the expression for $\lambda$:

$\lambda = \dfrac{4mv_0\lambda_0}{m\left(v_0 + \dfrac{2eE_0}{m}t\right)}$

Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $mv_0$, we get:

$\lambda = \dfrac{4\lambda_0}{1 + \dfrac{2E_0 e}{m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}}$

Answer: $\dfrac{4\lambda_0}{\left[1 + \dfrac{2E_0 e}{m}\dfrac{t}{v_0}\right]}$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The de Broglie wavelength associated with an electron accelerated through a potential difference V is $\lambda_e$ and the de Broglie wavelength associated with a proton accelerated through the same potential difference is $\lambda_p$. If their corresponding masses are $m_e$ and $m_p$, respectively, then the ratio of their de Broglie wavelengths $\left(\dfrac{\lambda_e}{\lambda_p}\right)$ is ______.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_e}{m_p}}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}$
- **(D)** $\left(\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}\right)^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The de Broglie wavelength of a particle accelerated through a potential difference $V$ is given by $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}}$.

For an electron, the charge is $e$ and mass is $m_e$. Its de Broglie wavelength is $\lambda_e = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_e eV}}$.

For a proton, the charge is $e$ and mass is $m_p$. Its de Broglie wavelength is $\lambda_p = \dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_p eV}}$.

Taking the ratio of the two wavelengths, we get $\dfrac{\lambda_e}{\lambda_p} = \dfrac{\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_e eV}}}{\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_p eV}}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}}$.

Answer: $\sqrt{\dfrac{m_p}{m_e}}$

</details>

The ratio of de Broglie wavelength of a deutron with kinetic energy $E$ to that of an alpha particle with kinetic energy $2 E$, is $n: 1$. The value of $n$ is $\_\_\_\_$. 
 (Assume mass of proton $=$ mass of neutron) :

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

### Q74. JEE Main 2018 (15 Apr Shift 2 Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the de Broglie wavelengths associated with a proton and an $\alpha$-particle are equal, then the ratio of velocities of the proton and the $\alpha$-particle will be:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1: 4$
- **(B)** $1: 2$
- **(C)** $4: 1$
- **(D)** $2: 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4: 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to question, $\lambda_p=\lambda_\alpha$
Using, $\lambda=\frac{h}{p}=\frac{h}{m v}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text { So, } \frac{h}{m_p \times v_p}=\frac{h}{m_\alpha \times v_\alpha} \\
&\Rightarrow \frac{v_p}{v_\alpha}=\frac{m_\alpha}{m_p}=\frac{4 m_p}{m_p}
\end{aligned}
$$

$(\because$ mass of $\alpha$-particle is 4 times of mass of proton)
So, $\frac{v_p}{v_\alpha}=\frac{4}{1}$; i.e., $4: 1$

de-Broglie wavelength $\lambda=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{P}}=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\sqrt{2 \mathrm{mE}}}$

\begin{array}{r}

\end{array}
$$
$\begin{aligned} \therefore \frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{e}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{P}}} & =\frac{\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\sqrt{2 \mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{e}} \mathrm{eV}}}}{\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\sqrt{2 \mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{P}} \mathrm{e} 4 \mathrm{~V}}}}=\sqrt{\frac{2 \mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{P}} \mathrm{e} 4 \mathrm{~V}}{2 \mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{e}} \mathrm{eV}}} \\ & =2 \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{P}}}{\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{e}}}}\end{aligned}$

</details>
