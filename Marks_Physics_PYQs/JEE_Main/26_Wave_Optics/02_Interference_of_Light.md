### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In interference experiment the path difference between two interfering waves at a point $A$ on the screen is $\lambda/3$, where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of these waves, and at another point $B$ the path difference is $\lambda/6$. The ratio of intensities at points $A$ and $B$ is _______.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $4$
- **(C)** $1/3$
- **(D)** $1/4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1/3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The phase difference $\Delta \phi$ is related to the path difference $\Delta x$ by the relation $\Delta \phi = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \Delta x$.

For point $A$, the path difference is $\Delta x_A = \dfrac{\lambda}{3}$.

$\Delta \phi_A = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \times \dfrac{\lambda}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

The intensity at point $A$ is given by $I_A = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\Delta \phi_A}{2}\right)$.

$I_A = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = I_{max} \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{I_{max}}{4}$

For point $B$, the path difference is $\Delta x_B = \dfrac{\lambda}{6}$.

$\Delta \phi_B = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda} \times \dfrac{\lambda}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$

The intensity at point $B$ is given by $I_B = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\Delta \phi_B}{2}\right)$.

$I_B = I_{max} \cos^2\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = I_{max} \left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{3I_{max}}{4}$

The ratio of intensities at points $A$ and $B$ is:

$\dfrac{I_A}{I_B} = \dfrac{\dfrac{I_{max}}{4}}{\dfrac{3I_{max}}{4}} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

Answer: $1/3$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2025 (3 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

Two coherent monochromatic light beams of intensities 4I and 9I are superimposed. The difference between the maximum and minimum intensities in the resulting interference pattern is xI. The value of $x$ is ______.

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2025 (24 Jan Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

The ratio of the power of a light source $S_1$ to that the light source $S_2$ is $2 . S_1$ is emitting $2 \times 10^{15}$ photons per second at 600 nm . If the wavelength of the source $S_2$ is 300 nm , then the number of photons per second emitted by $S_2$ is $\ldots\ldots$ $\times 10^{14}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **5**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since power emitting by a source is given as
$\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{\text { Total energy emitted }}{\text { time }} \\
& =\frac{\left(E_1 \text { photon }\right) \times \text { Number of photons }(N)}{t} \\
& P_1=\left(E_1\right) n
\end{aligned}$

$\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\mathrm{P}_1}{\mathrm{P}_2}=\frac{\left(\mathrm{E}_1\right) \mathrm{n}_1}{\left(\mathrm{E}_2\right) \mathrm{n}_2}=\frac{\left(\frac{\mathrm{hC}}{\lambda_1}\right) \mathrm{n}_1}{\left(\frac{\mathrm{hC}}{\lambda_2}\right) \mathrm{n}_2} \\
& \frac{\mathrm{P}_1}{\mathrm{P}_2}=\left(\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}\right) \frac{\mathrm{n}_1}{\mathrm{n}_2}
\end{aligned}$

Substituting the given values
$\begin{aligned}
& 2=\left(\frac{300}{600}\right) \times \frac{2 \times 10^{15}}{\mathrm{n}_2} \\
& \mathrm{n}_2=\frac{1}{2} \times 10^{15}=5 \times 10^{14} \text { Photon } / \mathrm{sec}
\end{aligned}$

### Q16. JEE Main 2014 (11 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two monochromatic light beams of intensity 16 and 9 units are interfering. The ratio of intensities of bright and dark parts of the resultant pattern is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{16}{9}$
- **(B)** $\frac{4}{3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{7}{1}$
- **(D)** $\frac{49}{1}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{49}{1}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Intensity $\propto(\text { amplitude })^2$
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{I_1}{I_2}=\frac{16}{9}=\frac{a_1^2}{a_2^2} \\
&\Rightarrow \mathrm{a}_1=4 ; \mathrm{a}_2=3
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore the ratio of intensities of bright and dark parts
$$
\frac{I_{\text {Bright }}}{I_{\text {Dark }}}=\frac{\left(a_1+a_2\right)^2}{\left(a_1-a_2\right)^2}=\frac{(4+3)^2}{(4-3)^2}=\frac{49}{1}
$$

</details>
