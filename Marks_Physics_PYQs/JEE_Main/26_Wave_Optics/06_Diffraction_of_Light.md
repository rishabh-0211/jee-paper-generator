### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `1`

Some distant star is to be observed by some telescope of diameter of objective lens $a$, at an angular resolution of $3.0\times 10^{-7}$ radian. If the wavelength of light from the star reaching the telescope is $500$ nm, the minimum diameter of the objective lens of the telescope is ________ cm. (nearest integer)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **203**

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular resolution $\Delta \theta$ of a telescope is given by the formula:

$\Delta \theta = \dfrac{1.22 \lambda}{a}$

where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of light and $a$ is the diameter of the objective lens.

Given:
$\Delta \theta = 3.0 \times 10^{-7}$ rad
$\lambda = 500 \text{ nm} = 500 \times 10^{-9}$ m $= 5 \times 10^{-7}$ m

Substituting the values into the formula:

$3.0 \times 10^{-7} = \dfrac{1.22 \times 5 \times 10^{-7}}{a}$

$a = \dfrac{1.22 \times 5 \times 10^{-7}}{3.0 \times 10^{-7}}$

$a = \dfrac{6.1}{3.0}$ m

$a = 2.0333...$ m

Converting the diameter into centimeters:

$a = 2.0333... \times 100$ cm $= 203.33$ cm

Rounding to the nearest integer, we get $203$ cm.

Answer: $203$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A slit of width $a$ is illuminated by light of wavelength $\lambda$. The linear separation between $1^{st}$ and $3^{rd}$ minima in the diffraction pattern produced on a screen placed at a distance $D$ from the slit system is _____.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{D\lambda}{a}$
- **(B)** $1.5\dfrac{D\lambda}{a}$
- **(C)** $2\dfrac{D\lambda}{a}$
- **(D)** $3\dfrac{D\lambda}{a}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2\dfrac{D\lambda}{a}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The position of the $n^{th}$ minimum in a single slit diffraction pattern on a screen at distance $D$ is given by $y_n = \dfrac{n D \lambda}{a}$.

For the $1^{st}$ minimum, $n = 1$, so $y_1 = \dfrac{D \lambda}{a}$.

For the $3^{rd}$ minimum, $n = 3$, so $y_3 = \dfrac{3 D \lambda}{a}$.

The linear separation between the $1^{st}$ and $3^{rd}$ minima on the same side of the central maximum is $\Delta y = y_3 - y_1$.

$\Delta y = \dfrac{3 D \lambda}{a} - \dfrac{D \lambda}{a} = 2\dfrac{D \lambda}{a}$.

Answer: $2\dfrac{D\lambda}{a}$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `1`

In single slit diffraction pattern, the wavelength of light used is $628$ nm and slit width is $0.2$ mm, the angular width of central maximum is $\alpha \times 10^{-2}$ degrees. The value of $\alpha$ is _______.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **36**

**Detailed Solution:**

The angular width of the central maximum in a single slit diffraction pattern is given by $\Delta \theta = \dfrac{2\lambda}{a}$

Substituting the given values:
$\lambda = 628 \times 10^{-9}$ m
$a = 0.2 \times 10^{-3}$ m

$\Delta \theta = \dfrac{2 \times 628 \times 10^{-9}}{0.2 \times 10^{-3}}$ rad

$\Delta \theta = 6280 \times 10^{-6}$ rad $= 6.28 \times 10^{-3}$ rad

To convert radians to degrees, multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$:

$\Delta \theta = 6.28 \times 10^{-3} \times \dfrac{180}{3.14}$ degrees

$\Delta \theta = 2 \times 10^{-3} \times 180$ degrees

$\Delta \theta = 360 \times 10^{-3}$ degrees $= 36 \times 10^{-2}$ degrees

Comparing this with $\alpha \times 10^{-2}$ degrees, we get $\alpha = 36$.

Answer: $36$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Given below are two statements: 
 <b>Statement I</b>: A plane wave after passing through prism remains as plane wave but passing through small pin hole may become spherical wave. 
 <b>Statement II</b>: The curvature of a spherical wave emerging from a slit will increase for increasing slit width. 
 In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

**Options:**

- **(A)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true
- **(B)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true
- **(C)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false
- **(D)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Statement I is true but Statement II is false**

**Detailed Solution:**

Statement I: A plane wave passing through a prism remains a plane wave due to refraction at parallel surfaces. However, when passing through a small pin hole comparable to the wavelength, Huygens' principle applies where each point on the wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets, creating a spherical wavefront. Statement I is TRUE.
Statement II: When a spherical wave emerges from a slit, the diffraction effect depends on slit width. A narrower slit causes stronger diffraction and greater curvature of the emerging wavefront. As slit width increases, diffraction effects decrease and the wavefront becomes flatter with reduced curvature. Therefore, the curvature decreases with increasing slit width. Statement II is FALSE.
The correct answer identifies that Statement I is true but Statement II is false.

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Which of the following are true for a single slit diffraction? 
 A. Width of central maxima increases with increase in wavelength keeping slit width constant. 
 B. Width of central maxima increases with decrease in wavelength keeping slit width constant. 
 C. Width of central maxima increases with decrease in slit width at constant wavelength. 
 D. Width of central maxima increases with increase in slit width at constant wavelength. 
 E. Brightness of central maxima increases for decrease in wavelength at constant slit width.

**Options:**

- **(A)** A, C, E only
- **(B)** A, D only
- **(C)** B, D only
- **(D)** B, C only

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) A, C, E only**

**Detailed Solution:**

For single slit diffraction, linear width of central maximum is $W = \frac{2\lambda D}{a}$. 
 A: Width increases with wavelength (keeping $a$ constant) - TRUE since $W \propto \lambda$. 
 B: Width increases with decreasing wavelength - FALSE, opposite of A. 
 C: Width increases with decreasing slit width (keeping $\lambda$ constant) - TRUE since $W \propto \frac{1}{a}$. 
 D: Width increases with increasing slit width - FALSE, opposite of C. 
 E: Brightness increases with decreasing wavelength at constant slit width - TRUE since less wavelength means less diffraction spreading and more concentrated intensity.
Statements A, C, and E are true.

</details>

### Q31. JEE Main 2014 (12 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In an experiment of single slit diffraction pattern, first minimum for red light coincides with first maximum of some other wavelength. If wavelength of red light is $6600 Å$, then wavelength of first maximum will be:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3300 Å$
- **(B)** $4400 Å$
- **(C)** $5500 Å$
- **(D)** $6600 Å$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4400 Å$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a single slit experiment, For diffraction maxima, $a \sin \theta=\left(2 n+1 \frac{Å}{2}\right.$ and for diffraction minima, $\mathrm{a} \sin \theta=\mathrm{n} \lambda$ According to question,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left(2 \times 1+1 \frac{\lambda}{2}=1 \times 6600\right. \\
&\left(\because \lambda_{\mathrm{R}}=6600 Å\right) \\
&\lambda=\frac{6600 \times 2}{3} \\
&\lambda=4400 Å
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The first diffraction minimum due to the single slit diffraction is seen at $\theta=30^{\circ}$ for a light of wavelength $5000 Å$ falling perpendicularly on the slit. The width of the slit is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.5 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(B)** $1.25 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(C)** $10 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}$
- **(D)** $5 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $10 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For first minimum, $d \sin \theta=\lambda$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Rightarrow \quad d & =\frac{\lambda}{\sin \theta}=\frac{5000 \times 10^{-8} \mathrm{~cm}}{\sin 30^{\circ}} \\
& =\frac{5000 \times 10^{-8} \mathrm{~cm}}{1 / 2}=10 \times 10^{-5} \mathrm{~cm}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
