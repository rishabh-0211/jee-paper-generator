### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

A $5$ mg particle carrying a charge of $5\pi\times 10^{-6}$ C is moving with velocity of $(3\hat{i}+2\hat{k})\times 10^{-2}$ m/s in a region having magnetic field $\vec{B} = 0.1\hat{k}$ Wb/m$^2$. It moves a distance of $\alpha$ meter along $\hat{k}$ when it completes $5$ revolutions. The value of $\alpha$ is ________.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:
Mass of the particle, $m = 5 \text{ mg} = 5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ kg}$
Charge, $q = 5\pi \times 10^{-6} \text{ C}$
Velocity, $\vec{v} = (3\hat{i} + 2\hat{k}) \times 10^{-2} \text{ m/s}$
Magnetic field, $\vec{B} = 0.1\hat{k} \text{ Wb/m}^2$

The velocity component parallel to the magnetic field is $v_{\parallel} = 2 \times 10^{-2} \text{ m/s}$.

The time period of one revolution is given by:
$T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{qB}$

Substituting the given values:
$T = \dfrac{2\pi \times 5 \times 10^{-6}}{5\pi \times 10^{-6} \times 0.1} = \dfrac{2}{0.1} = 20 \text{ s}$

The time taken to complete $5$ revolutions is:
$t = 5T = 5 \times 20 = 100 \text{ s}$

The distance moved along the $\hat{k}$ direction (pitch for $5$ revolutions) is:
$\alpha = v_{\parallel} \times t$
$\alpha = 2 \times 10^{-2} \times 100 = 2 \text{ m}$

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

The charged particle moving in a uniform magnetic field of $(3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j})$ T has an acceleration $\left(4\hat{i} - \dfrac{x}{2}\hat{j}\right)$ m/s$^2$. The value of $x$ is __________.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **12**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic force on a moving charged particle is given by $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$.

Since the acceleration $\vec{a}$ is in the direction of the magnetic force, it must be perpendicular to the magnetic field $\vec{B}$.

Therefore, the dot product of acceleration and magnetic field is zero:

$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{B} = 0$

Substituting the given vectors:

$\left(4\hat{i} - \dfrac{x}{2}\hat{j}\right) \cdot (3\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}) = 0$

$(4)(3) + \left(-\dfrac{x}{2}\right)(2) = 0$

$12 - x = 0$

$x = 12$

Answer: $12$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle having charge $10^{-9}$ C moving in $x$-$y$ plane in fields of $0.4\hat{j}$ N/C and $4 \times 10^{-3} \hat{k}$ T experiences a force of $(4\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}) \times 10^{-10}$ N. The velocity of the particle at that instant is _______ m/s.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}$
- **(B)** $100\hat{i} + 50\hat{j}$
- **(C)** $-50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}$
- **(D)** $50\hat{i} - 100\hat{j}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The Lorentz force experienced by a charged particle is given by $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B})$.

Given that the particle moves in the $x$-$y$ plane, its velocity can be written as $\vec{v} = v_x \hat{i} + v_y \hat{j}$.

Substituting the given values into the force equation:

$\vec{F} = 10^{-9} [0.4\hat{j} + (v_x \hat{i} + v_y \hat{j}) \times (4 \times 10^{-3} \hat{k})]$

Using the cross product rules $\hat{i} \times \hat{k} = -\hat{j}$ and $\hat{j} \times \hat{k} = \hat{i}$:

$\vec{F} = 10^{-9} [0.4\hat{j} - 4 \times 10^{-3} v_x \hat{j} + 4 \times 10^{-3} v_y \hat{i}]$

$\vec{F} = 10^{-9} [4 \times 10^{-3} v_y \hat{i} + (0.4 - 4 \times 10^{-3} v_x) \hat{j}]$

The given force is $\vec{F} = (4\hat{i} + 2\hat{j}) \times 10^{-10}$ N, which can be rewritten as:

$\vec{F} = 10^{-9} (0.4\hat{i} + 0.2\hat{j})$ N

Comparing the coefficients of $\hat{i}$ and $\hat{j}$ from both expressions:

For the $\hat{i}$ component:
$4 \times 10^{-3} v_y = 0.4$
$v_y = \dfrac{0.4}{4 \times 10^{-3}} = 100$ m/s

For the $\hat{j}$ component:
$0.4 - 4 \times 10^{-3} v_x = 0.2$
$4 \times 10^{-3} v_x = 0.2$
$v_x = \dfrac{0.2}{4 \times 10^{-3}} = 50$ m/s

Therefore, the velocity of the particle is $\vec{v} = 50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}$ m/s.

Answer: $50\hat{i} + 100\hat{j}$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

$1\,\mu$C charge moving with velocity $\vec{v} = \left(\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}\right)$ m/s in the region of magnetic field $\vec{B} = \left(2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}\right)$ T. The magnitude of force acting on it is $\sqrt{\alpha} \times 10^{-6}$ N. The value of $\alpha$ is _______.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **171**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic force acting on a moving charge is given by $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$.

Given:
$q = 1 \, \mu\text{C} = 10^{-6} \text{ C}$
$\vec{v} = \hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$
$\vec{B} = 2\hat{i} + 3\hat{j} - 5\hat{k}$

Calculating the cross product $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$:
$\vec{v} \times \vec{B} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 1 & -2 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 & -5 \end{vmatrix}$

$\vec{v} \times \vec{B} = \hat{i}(10 - 9) - \hat{j}(-5 - 6) + \hat{k}(3 + 4)$
$\vec{v} \times \vec{B} = \hat{i} + 11\hat{j} + 7\hat{k}$

The magnitude of $\vec{v} \times \vec{B}$ is:
$|\vec{v} \times \vec{B}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 11^2 + 7^2} = \sqrt{1 + 121 + 49} = \sqrt{171}$

The magnitude of the force is:
$|\vec{F}| = q |\vec{v} \times \vec{B}| = 10^{-6} \times \sqrt{171} = \sqrt{171} \times 10^{-6} \text{ N}$

Comparing this with the given expression $\sqrt{\alpha} \times 10^{-6} \text{ N}$, we get:
$\alpha = 171$

Answer: $171$

</details>

=(2 \mathrm{q} \hat{\mathrm{i}}+3 \mathrm{q} \hat{\mathrm{j}})+\mathrm{q}(\overrightarrow{\mathrm{v}} \times \overrightarrow{\mathrm{B}})

$$

A charged particle of mass $\mathrm{m}$ and charge $q$ travels on a circular path of radius $r$ that is perpendicular to a magnetic field $\mathrm{B}$. The time taken by the particle to complete one revolution is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{2 \pi \mathrm{mq}}{\mathrm{B}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2 \pi q^2 B}{m}$
- **(C)** $\frac{2 \pi q B}{m}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 \pi m}{q B}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{2 \pi m}{q B}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\mathrm{m} \omega^2 \mathrm{r}=\mathrm{Bq} \omega \mathrm{r}$
$\omega=\mathrm{Bq} / \mathrm{m}$
$\mathrm{T}=\frac{2 \pi \mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{qB}}$

</details>

### Q76. JEE Main 2005

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A uniform electric field and a uniform magnetic field are acting along the same direction in a certain region. If an electron is projected along the direction of the fields with a certain velocity then

**Options:**

- **(A)** its velocity will decrease.
- **(B)** its velocity will increase.
- **(C)** it will turn towards right of direction of motion.
- **(D)** it will turn towards left of direction of motion

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) its velocity will decrease.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\vec{F}=-e[\vec{E}+\vec{v} \times \vec{B}]=-e \vec{E}$
$\vec{a}=-\frac{e \vec{E}}{m}$
$v(t)=v_{\circ}-\frac{e E}{m} t$

</details>

### Q77. JEE Main 2003

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A particle of charge $-16 \times 10^{-18}$ coulomb moving with velocity $10 \mathrm{~ms}^{-1}$ along the $\mathrm{x}$-axis enters a region where a magnetic field of induction $\mathrm{B}$ is along the $\mathrm{y}$-axis, and an electric field of magnitude $10^4 \mathrm{~V} / \mathrm{m}$ is along the negative z-axis. If the charged particle continues moving along the $\mathrm{x}$-axis, the magnitude of $\mathrm{B}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10^3 \mathrm{~Wb} / \mathrm{m}^2$
- **(B)** $10^5 \mathrm{~Wb} / \mathrm{m}^2$
- **(C)** $10^{16} \mathrm{~Wb} / \mathrm{m}^2$
- **(D)** $10^{-3} \mathrm{~Wb} / \mathrm{m}^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $10^3 \mathrm{~Wb} / \mathrm{m}^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since there is no deviation in the path of the charged particle, so net force due to presence of electric and magnetic field must be zero $\Rightarrow \mathrm{vB}=\mathrm{qE} \Rightarrow \mathrm{B}=\frac{\mathrm{E}}{\mathrm{V}}=\frac{10^4}{10}=10^3 \mathrm{~Wb} / \mathrm{m}^2$

</details>
