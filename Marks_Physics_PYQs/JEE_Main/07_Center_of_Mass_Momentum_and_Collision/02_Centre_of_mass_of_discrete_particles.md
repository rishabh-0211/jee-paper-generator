### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The position of center of mass of three masses $2$ kg, $3$ kg and $15$ kg placed with respect to mid point ($p$) of normal bisector, as shown in the figure is _______.
<img style="width: 350px" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/06APRS1__q33_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}, 1.25\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}, 1.0\right)$
- **(C)** $(0, 0)$
- **(D)** $(1.25, 0)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}, 1.25\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the midpoint $p$ of the normal bisector be the origin $(0,0)$.

The triangle is isosceles with two sides of $10\text{ m}$ and an included angle of $120^\circ$. The altitude from the $15\text{ kg}$ mass bisects the angle into two $60^\circ$ angles.

The length of the altitude is $10 \cos(60^\circ) = 5\text{ m}$.

Since $p$ is the midpoint of this altitude, the $15\text{ kg}$ mass is at a distance of $2.5\text{ m}$ above $p$, and the base is $2.5\text{ m}$ below $p$.

The distance of the $2\text{ kg}$ and $3\text{ kg}$ masses from the altitude is $10 \sin(60^\circ) = 5\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.

The coordinates of the masses with respect to $p$ are:
$m_1 = 15\text{ kg}$ at $(0, 2.5)$
$m_2 = 2\text{ kg}$ at $(-5\sqrt{3}, -2.5)$
$m_3 = 3\text{ kg}$ at $(5\sqrt{3}, -2.5)$

The x-coordinate of the center of mass is:
$x_{cm} = \dfrac{m_1 x_1 + m_2 x_2 + m_3 x_3}{m_1 + m_2 + m_3} = \dfrac{15(0) + 2(-5\sqrt{3}) + 3(5\sqrt{3})}{15 + 2 + 3} = \dfrac{5\sqrt{3}}{20} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}$

The y-coordinate of the center of mass is:
$y_{cm} = \dfrac{m_1 y_1 + m_2 y_2 + m_3 y_3}{m_1 + m_2 + m_3} = \dfrac{15(2.5) + 2(-2.5) + 3(-2.5)}{20} = \dfrac{37.5 - 12.5}{20} = \dfrac{25}{20} = 1.25$

The position of the center of mass is $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}, 1.25\right)$.

Answer: $\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}, 1.25\right)$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2025 (28 Jan Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The center of mass of a thin rectangular plate (fig - x ) with sides of length $a$ and $b$, whose mass per unit area $(\sigma)$ varies as $\sigma=\frac{\sigma_0 x}{a b}$ (where $\sigma_0$ is a constant), would be $\qquad$
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/bGydijWffNDkRTTeUKSXfU73xruwP03ZKOpkAOkKj6c.original.fullsize.png"/>

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\frac{2}{3} a, \frac{\mathrm{~b}}{2}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(\frac{a}{2}, \frac{\mathrm{~b}}{2}\right)$
- **(C)** $\left(\frac{1}{3} a, \frac{\mathrm{~b}}{2}\right)$
- **(D)** $\left(\frac{2}{3} a, \frac{2}{3} b\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\left(\frac{2}{3} a, \frac{\mathrm{~b}}{2}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & d m=\sigma d A \\ & \begin{aligned} d & =\sigma(d x)(d y)=\frac{\sigma_0 x}{a b}(d x)(d y) \\ x_{c o m} & =\frac{\int x d m}{\int d m}=\frac{\int x \frac{\left(\sigma_0 x\right)}{a b}(d x)(d y)}{\int_0 \frac{\sigma_0}{a b}(d x)(d y)} \\ & =\frac{\int_0^a x^2 d x \int_0^b d y}{\int_0^b x d x \int_0^b d y}=\frac{2 a}{3} \\ y_{c o m} & =\frac{\int y d m}{\int d m}=\frac{\int y\left(\frac{\sigma_0 x}{a b}\right)(d x)(d y)}{\int \frac{\sigma_0 x}{a b}(d x)(d y)}\end{aligned}\end{aligned}$
$=\frac{\int_0^a x d x \int_0^b y d y}{\int_0^a x d x \int_0^b d y}=\frac{b}{2}$
i.e., $\vec{r}_{\mathrm{com}} \equiv\left(\frac{2 a}{3}, \frac{b}{2}\right)$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2024 (04 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

In a system two particles of masses $m_1=3 \mathrm{~kg}$ and $m_2=2 \mathrm{~kg}$ are placed at certain distance from each other. The particle of mass $m_1$ is moved towards the center of mass of the system through a distance $2 \mathrm{~cm}$. In order to keep the center of mass of the system at the original position, the particle of mass $m_2$ should move towards the center of mass by the distance _____ $\mathrm{cm}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **3**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & \Delta \mathrm{X}_{\text {C.O.M. }}=\frac{\mathrm{m}_1 \Delta \mathrm{x}_1+\mathrm{m}_2 \Delta \mathrm{x}_2}{\mathrm{~m}_1+\mathrm{m}_2} \\ & \Rightarrow 0=\frac{3 \times 2+2(-\mathrm{x})}{3+2} \\ & \Rightarrow \mathrm{x}=3 \mathrm{~cm}\end{aligned}$

</details>
