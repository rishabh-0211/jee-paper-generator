### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The energy released if hydrogen atoms are combined to form $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ is __________ MeV. (Take binding energies per nucleon of $^{2}_{1}\text{H}$ and $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ as $1.1$ MeV and $7.2$ MeV, respectively)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.1$
- **(B)** $24.4$
- **(C)** $26.6$
- **(D)** $5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $24.4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The fusion reaction is given by:

$2 ^{2}_{1}\text{H} \rightarrow ^{4}_{2}\text{He}$

Binding energy of one $^{2}_{1}\text{H}$ nucleus = $2 \times 1.1 = 2.2$ MeV

Total initial binding energy = $2 \times 2.2 = 4.4$ MeV

Binding energy of $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ nucleus = $4 \times 7.2 = 28.8$ MeV

Energy released $\Delta E$ = Final binding energy - Initial binding energy

$\Delta E = 28.8 - 4.4 = 24.4$ MeV

Answer: $24.4$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

The energy released when $\dfrac{7}{17.13}$ kg of $^{7}_{3}\text{Li}$ is converted into $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ by proton bombardment is $\alpha \times 10^{32}$ eV. The value of $\alpha$ is _______. (Nearest integer) (Mass of $^{7}_{3}\text{Li} = 7.0183$ u, mass of $^{4}_{2}\text{He} = 4.004$ u, mass of proton $= 1.008$ u and $1$ u $= 931$ MeV/c$^2$ and Avogadro number $= 6.0 \times 10^{23}$)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **6**

**Detailed Solution:**

The nuclear reaction for the proton bombardment of Lithium is:

$^{7}_{3}\text{Li} + ^{1}_{1}\text{H} \rightarrow 2 ^{4}_{2}\text{He}$

The mass defect ($\Delta m$) for the reaction is:

$\Delta m = m(^{7}_{3}\text{Li}) + m(^{1}_{1}\text{H}) - 2m(^{4}_{2}\text{He})$

$\Delta m = 7.0183 + 1.008 - 2(4.004)$

$\Delta m = 8.0263 - 8.008 = 0.0183 \text{ u}$

Energy released per reaction ($Q$) is:

$Q = \Delta m \times 931 \text{ MeV} = 0.0183 \times 931 \text{ MeV} = 17.0373 \text{ MeV}$

Number of moles of $^{7}_{3}\text{Li}$ in $\dfrac{7}{17.13} \text{ kg}$:

$n = \dfrac{\dfrac{7}{17.13} \times 10^3 \text{ g}}{7 \text{ g/mol}} = \dfrac{1000}{17.13} \text{ mol}$

Number of atoms of $^{7}_{3}\text{Li}$:

$N = n \times N_A = \dfrac{1000}{17.13} \times 6.0 \times 10^{23} = \dfrac{6}{17.13} \times 10^{26}$

Total energy released ($E$) is:

$E = N \times Q = \left(\dfrac{6}{17.13} \times 10^{26}\right) \times (17.0373 \times 10^6 \text{ eV})$

$E = \dfrac{102.2238}{17.13} \times 10^{32} \text{ eV} \approx 5.967 \times 10^{32} \text{ eV}$

Rounding to the nearest integer, we get $\alpha = 6$.

Answer: $6$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two nuclei of mass number $3$ combine with another nucleus of mass number $4$ to yield a nucleus of mass number $10$. If the binding energy per nucleon for the mass numbers $3$, $4$ and $10$ are $5.6$ MeV, $7.4$ MeV and $6.1$ MeV, respectively, then in the process, $\Delta Mc^2 = $ _____ MeV.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6.9$
- **(B)** $7.9$
- **(C)** $2.2$
- **(D)** $4.3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2.2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The nuclear reaction is given by:

$2 X^{3} + Y^{4} \rightarrow Z^{10}$

The total binding energy of the reactants is the sum of the binding energies of the two nuclei of mass number $3$ and the nucleus of mass number $4$.

Binding energy of reactants $= 2 \times (3 \times 5.6) + (4 \times 7.4)$

$= 33.6 + 29.6 = 63.2$ MeV

The total binding energy of the product nucleus of mass number $10$ is:

Binding energy of product $= 10 \times 6.1 = 61.0$ MeV

The change in mass energy $\Delta M c^2$ is equal to the difference in the total binding energies of the reactants and the product.

$\Delta M c^2 = |63.2 - 61.0| = 2.2$ MeV

Answer: $2.2$

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

$[\text{Take } m(^{209}_{83}Bi) = 208.980388 \, u, \, m_p = 1.007825 \, u, \, m_n = 1.008665 \, u, \, 1 \, u = 931 \, \text{MeV}/c^2]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $7.48$
- **(B)** $7.84$
- **(C)** $8.79$
- **(D)** $6.94$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $7.84$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the nucleus $^{209}_{83}Bi$, the number of protons is $Z = 83$ and the number of neutrons is $N = 209 - 83 = 126$.

Mass of $83$ protons $= 83 \times 1.007825 = 83.649475 \, u$

Mass of $126$ neutrons $= 126 \times 1.008665 = 127.091790 \, u$

The mass defect $\Delta m$ is given by:

$\Delta m = \text{Total mass of nucleons} - \text{Mass of nucleus}$

$BE = \Delta m \times 931 \, \text{MeV}$

$BE = 1.760877 \times 931 = 1639.376 \, \text{MeV}$

The binding energy per nucleon is:

$\dfrac{BE}{A} = \dfrac{1639.376}{209} \approx 7.84 \, \text{MeV}$

### Q7. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Given below are two statements: 
 <b>Statement I</b>: For all elements, greater the mass of the nucleus, greater is the binding energy per nucleon. 
 <b>Statement II</b>: For all elements, nuclei with less binding energy per nucleon transforms to nuclei with greater binding energy per nucleon. 
 In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

**Options:**

- **(A)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false
- **(B)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true
- **(C)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false
- **(D)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Statement I is false but Statement II is true**

**Detailed Solution:**

Statement I is false because binding energy per nucleon does not increase monotonically with mass number. It peaks at Iron-56 and decreases for heavier elements.
Statement II is true. It describes the fundamental principle of nuclear reactions: fission of heavy nuclei (low BE/A) and fusion of light nuclei (low BE/A) both produce nuclei with higher binding energy per nucleon, releasing energy in the process. Both processes are energetically favorable for the same reason: moving toward higher stability (higher BE/A).
