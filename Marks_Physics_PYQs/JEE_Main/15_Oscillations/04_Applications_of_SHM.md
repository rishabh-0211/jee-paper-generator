### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The frequency of oscillation of a mass $m$ suspended by a spring is $v_1$. If the length of the spring is cut to half, the same mass oscillates with frequency $v_2$. The value of $v_2/v_1$ is ________.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The frequency of oscillation of a spring-mass system is given by $v = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}$, where $k$ is the spring constant and $m$ is the mass.

The spring constant $k$ is inversely proportional to the length of the spring $L$, so $k \propto \dfrac{1}{L}$.

When the length of the spring is cut to half, the new length is $L' = \dfrac{L}{2}$. The new spring constant becomes $k' = 2k$.

The new frequency of oscillation is $v_2 = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{k'}{m}} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\dfrac{2k}{m}} = \sqrt{2} v_1$.

Therefore, the ratio $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = \sqrt{2}$.

Answer: $\sqrt{2}$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A uniform disc of radius $R$ and mass $M$ is free to oscillate about the axis $A$ as shown in the figure. For small oscillations the time period is ______.
($g$ is acceleration due to gravity)
<img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/04APRS2__q35_d1.jpg" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{5R}{4g}}$
- **(B)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{2R}{3g}}$
- **(C)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{3R}{2g}}$
- **(D)** $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{3R}{g}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{5R}{4g}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

From the given figure, the axis of rotation is a horizontal line passing through the point $A$ on the edge of the disc. The circular arrow around the axis indicates that the disc oscillates out of its plane, meaning the axis of rotation is a tangent to the disc in its own plane.

The moment of inertia of a uniform disc about its diameter is $I_{cm} = \dfrac{1}{4}MR^2$.

Using the parallel axis theorem, the moment of inertia of the disc about the given axis of rotation is:
$I = I_{cm} + MR^2 = \dfrac{1}{4}MR^2 + MR^2 = \dfrac{5}{4}MR^2$

The distance from the center of mass of the disc to the axis of rotation is $d = R$.

The time period of a physical pendulum is given by:
$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{I}{Mgd}}$

Substituting the values of $I$ and $d$:
$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{\dfrac{5}{4}MR^2}{MgR}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{5R}{4g}}$

Answer: $2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{5R}{4g}}$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A simple pendulum of string length 30 cm performs 20 oscillations in 10 s. The length of the string required for the pendulum to perform 40 oscillations in the same time duration is $\_\_\_\_$ cm. [Assume that the mass of the pendulum remains same.]

**Options:**

- **(A)** 7.5
- **(B)** 0.75
- **(C)** 120
- **(D)** 15

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 7.5**

**Detailed Solution:**

Period of simple pendulum: $T = \frac{\text{time}}{\text{number of oscillations}}$
Initial period: $T_1 = \frac{10}{20} = 0.5$ s
Required period: $T_2 = \frac{10}{40} = 0.25$ s
Using $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$, the ratio is: $\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \sqrt{\frac{L_2}{L_1}}$
$\frac{0.25}{0.5} = \sqrt{\frac{L_2}{30}} \Rightarrow 0.5 = \sqrt{\frac{L_2}{30}}$
$0.25 = \frac{L_2}{30} \Rightarrow L_2 = 7.5$ cm

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Using a simple pendulum experiment $g$ is determind by measuring its time period $T$. Which of the following plots represent the correct relation between the pendulum length $L$ and time period $T$ ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/22S2_o_32_1_1.png">
- **(B)** <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/22S2_o_32_2_1.png">
- **(C)** <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/22S2_o_32_3_1.png">
- **(D)** <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/22S2_o_32_4_1.png">

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/22S2_o_32_2_1.png">**

**Detailed Solution:**

The time period $T$ of a simple pendulum of length $L$ is given by the formula:
$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$
Squaring both sides, we get:
$T^2 = 4\pi^2 \frac{L}{g}$
Rearranging the equation to find the relationship between $\frac{1}{T^2}$ and $L$:
$\frac{1}{T^2} = \frac{g}{4\pi^2 L}$
Let $y = \frac{1}{T^2}$ and $x = L$. The equation becomes:
$y = \frac{k}{x}$ where $k = \frac{g}{4\pi^2}$ is a constant.
This is the equation of a rectangular hyperbola in the first quadrant.
As $L$ increases, $\frac{1}{T^2}$ decreases non-linearly, approaching the axes asymptotically.
Comparing this with the given plots, option (2) correctly represents a hyperbolic relationship between $\frac{1}{T^2}$ and $L$.

</details>

### Q36. JEE Main 2012 (Offline)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If a simple pendulum has significant amplitude (up to a factor of $1 / e$ of original) only in the period between $t=0s$ to $t=\tau s$, then $\tau$ may be called the average life of the pendulum. When the spherical bob of the pendulum suffers a retardation (due to viscous drag) proportional to its velocity, with '$b$' as the constant of proportionality, the average life time of the pendulum is (assuming damping is small) in seconds:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{0.693}{b}$
- **(B)** $b$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{b}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2}{b}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{2}{b}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/u6onzww-6Tb1MlCgGBbGm2uVMEq2eEKi4MaR3424yvc.original.fullsize.png">

As retardation $=\mathrm{bv}$
$\therefore$ retarding force $=\mathrm{mbv}$
$\therefore$ net restoring torque when angular displacement is $\theta$ is given by $=-\operatorname{mg} \ell \sin \theta+\operatorname{mbv} \ell$
$\therefore \quad \mathrm{I} \alpha=-m g \ell \sin \theta+\operatorname{mbv} \ell$
where, $\mathrm{I}=\mathrm{m} \ell^2$
$\therefore \quad \frac{\mathrm{d}^2 \theta}{\mathrm{dt}^2}=\alpha=-\frac{\mathrm{g}}{\ell} \sin \theta+\frac{\mathrm{bv}}{\ell}$
for small damping, the solution of the above differential equation will be
$\therefore \quad \theta=\theta_0 \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\mathrm{bt}}{2}} \sin (w t+\phi)$
$\therefore \quad$ angular amplitude will be $=\theta . e^{\frac{-b t}{2}}$
According to question, in $\tau$ time (average life-time),
angular amplitude drops to $\frac{1}{e}$ value of its original value
$(\theta)$
$\therefore \quad \frac{\theta_0}{\mathrm{e}}=\theta_0 \mathrm{e}^{-\frac{6 \tau}{2}}$
$\frac{6 \tau}{2}=1$
$\therefore \quad \tau=\frac{2}{b}$

</details>

- **(A)** $\mathrm{f / 2}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{f / 4}$
- **(C)** $4 \mathrm{f}$
- **(D)** $2 \mathrm{f}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2 \mathrm{f}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

If a spring has time period T, and is cut into n equal parts, then the time period of each part will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{T} \sqrt{\mathrm{n}}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{T} / \sqrt{\mathrm{n}}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{nT}$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{T}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{T} / \sqrt{\mathrm{n}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Spring constant becomes $n$ times for each piece. $T=2 \pi \sqrt{\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{k}}$
$$
\frac{T_1}{T_2}=\frac{\sqrt{n K}}{K} \text { or } T_2=T / \sqrt{n}
$$

</details>
