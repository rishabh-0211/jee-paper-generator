### Q4. JEE Main 2013 (23 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Bob of a simple pendulum of length $l$ is made of iron. The pendulum is oscillating over a horizontal coil carrying direct current. If the time period of the pendulum is $\mathrm{T}$ then :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $T < 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ and damping is smaller than in air alone.
- **(B)** $T=2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ and damping is larger than in air alone.
- **(C)** $T>2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ and damping is smaller than in air alone.
- **(D)** $T < 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ and damping is larger than in air alone.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $T < 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$ and damping is larger than in air alone.**

**Detailed Solution:**

When the pendulum is oscillating over a current carrying coil, and when the direction of oscillating pendulum bob is opposite to the direction of current. Its instantaneous acceleration increases.
Hence time period $\mathrm{T} < 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{\ell}{\mathrm{g}}}$ and damping is larger than in air alone due energy dissipation.

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2004

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A particle of mass $m$ is attached to a spring (of spring constant $k$ ) and has a natural angular frequency $\omega_0$. An external force $F(t)$ proportional to $\cos \omega t\left(\omega \neq \omega_0\right)$ is applied to the oscillator. The time displacement of the oscillator will be proportional to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{m\left(\omega_0^2-\omega^2\right)}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{m\left(\omega_0^2+\omega^2\right)}$
- **(D)** $\frac{m}{\omega_0^2+\omega^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{m\left(\omega_0^2-\omega^2\right)}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For forced oscillations, the displacement is given by
$$
\mathrm{x}=\mathrm{A} \sin (\omega \mathrm{t}+\phi) \text { with } \mathrm{A}=\frac{\mathrm{F}_0 / \mathrm{m}}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2}
$$

</details>
