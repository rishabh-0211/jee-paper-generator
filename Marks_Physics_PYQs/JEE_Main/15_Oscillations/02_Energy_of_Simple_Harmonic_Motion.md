### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `1`

The displacement of a particle, executing simple harmonic motion with time period $T$, is expressed as $x(t)=A \sin \omega t$, where $A$ is the amplitude. The maximum value of potential energy of this oscillator is found at $t=T / 2 \beta$. The value of $\beta$ is $\_\_\_\_$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **2**

**Detailed Solution:**

For SHM with $x(t) = A\sin(\omega t)$, the potential energy is 
 $U = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2 = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 A^2 \sin^2(\omega t)$.
Maximum potential energy occurs when $\sin^2(\omega t) = 1$, i.e., when $\omega t = \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}, ...$
The first maximum occurs at $\omega t = \frac{\pi}{2}$, giving $t = \frac{\pi}{2\omega} = \frac{\pi}{2} \cdot \frac{T}{2\pi} = \frac{T}{4}$.
Since this is given as $t = \frac{T}{2\beta}$.
 We have $\frac{T}{4} = \frac{T}{2\beta}$, so $\beta = 2$.

</details>

Two blocks with masses 100 g and 200 g are attached to the ends of springs $A$ and $B$ as shown in figure. The energy stored in $A$ is $E$. The energy stored in $B$, when spring constants $k_{A}, k_{B}$ of $A$ and $B$, respectively satisfy the relation $4 k_{A}=3 k_{B^{\prime}}$, is : 
 <img style="width:100px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/23S1_q_41_1.png"> <img style="width:100px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/23S1_q_41_2.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{4}{3} E$
- **(B)** 4E
- **(C)** 3E
- **(D)** $2 E$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 3E**

**Detailed Solution:**

For blocks hanging in equilibrium, spring extensions are:
$x_A = \frac{m_A g}{k_A} = \frac{100g}{k_A}$ and $x_B = \frac{m_B g}{k_B} = \frac{200g}{k_B}$.
Energy stored:
$E = \frac{1}{2}k_A x_A^2 = \frac{5000g^2}{k_A}$ and $U_B = \frac{1}{2}k_B x_B^2 = \frac{20000g^2}{k_B}$.
Taking ratio: $\frac{U_B}{E} = 4 \cdot \frac{k_A}{k_B}$.
Given $4k_A = 3k_B$, we have $\frac{k_A}{k_B} = \frac{3}{4}$.
Therefore: $U_B = 4E \cdot \frac{3}{4} = 3E$.

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The kinetic energy of a simple harmonic oscillator is oscillating with angular frequency of $176 \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$. The frequency of this simple harmonic oscillator is $\_\_\_\_$ Hz. $\left[\right.$ take $\left.\pi=\frac{22}{7}\right]$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 14
- **(B)** 28
- **(C)** 176
- **(D)** 88

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 14**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a simple harmonic oscillator, if the displacement is $x = A \sin(\omega t + \phi)$, the velocity is $v = A\omega \cos(\omega t + \phi)$.
The kinetic energy is given by $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m A^2 \omega^2 \cos^2(\omega t + \phi)$.
Using the identity $\cos^2 \theta = \frac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2}$, we get $K = \frac{1}{4}m A^2 \omega^2 [1 + \cos(2\omega t + 2\phi)]$.
The angular frequency of the kinetic energy oscillation is $\omega_{KE} = 2\omega$, where $\omega$ is the angular frequency of the SHM.
Given $\omega_{KE} = 176$ rad/s, we have $2\omega = 176$, which gives $\omega = 88$ rad/s.
The frequency of the simple harmonic oscillator is $f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi}$.
Substituting the values: $f = \frac{88}{2 \times \frac{22}{7}} = \frac{88 \times 7}{44} = 2 \times 7 = 14$ Hz.

</details>
