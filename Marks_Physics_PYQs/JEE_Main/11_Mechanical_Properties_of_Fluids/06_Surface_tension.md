### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Eight mercury drops, each of radius $r$, coalesce to form a bigger drop. The surface energy released in this process is _______. ($S$ is the surface tension of mercury).

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8\pi r^2 S$
- **(B)** $16\pi r^2 S$
- **(C)** $64\pi r^2 S$
- **(D)** $4\pi r^2 S$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $16\pi r^2 S$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the radius of the bigger drop be $R$. Since the total volume remains conserved during the process:

$8 \times \dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3 = \dfrac{4}{3} \pi R^3$

$R^3 = 8r^3 \Rightarrow R = 2r$

Initial surface energy of the $8$ drops is:

$U_i = 8 \times (4 \pi r^2 S) = 32 \pi r^2 S$

Final surface energy of the bigger drop is:

$U_f = 4 \pi R^2 S = 4 \pi (2r)^2 S = 16 \pi r^2 S$

The surface energy released in the process is:

$\Delta U = U_i - U_f = 32 \pi r^2 S - 16 \pi r^2 S = 16 \pi r^2 S$

Answer: $16\pi r^2 S$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

The surface tension of a soap solution is $3.5 \times 10^{-2}$ N/m. The work required to increase the radius of a soap bubble from $1$ cm to $2$ cm is $\alpha \times 10^{-6}$ J. The value of $\alpha$ is _____. ($\pi = 22/7$)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **264**

**Detailed Solution:**

The work done to increase the radius of a soap bubble is given by $W = T \times \Delta A$

Since a soap bubble has two free surfaces, the change in surface area is $\Delta A = 2 \times 4\pi (r_2^2 - r_1^2)$

Substituting the given values:

$W = 3.5 \times 10^{-2} \times 8\pi \times ((2 \times 10^{-2})^2 - (1 \times 10^{-2})^2)$

$W = 3.5 \times 10^{-2} \times 8\pi \times (4 \times 10^{-4} - 1 \times 10^{-4})$

$W = 3.5 \times 10^{-2} \times 8\pi \times 3 \times 10^{-4}$

$W = 84\pi \times 10^{-6}$

Using $\pi = \dfrac{22}{7}$:

$W = 84 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 10^{-6} = 264 \times 10^{-6}$ J

Comparing with $\alpha \times 10^{-6}$ J, we get $\alpha = 264$

Answer: $264$

</details>

A liquid drop of diameter $2$ mm breaks into $512$ droplets. The change in surface energy is $\alpha \times 10^{-6}$ J. The value of $\alpha$ is _______. (Take surface tension of liquid $= 0.08$ N/m)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$
- **(B)** $7$
- **(C)** $8$
- **(D)** $11$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the radius of the initial drop be $R$ and the radius of each small droplet be $r$.

Given diameter $D = 2$ mm, so $R = 1$ mm $= 10^{-3}$ m.

By conservation of volume:

$\dfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = 512 \times \dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$

$R^3 = 512 r^3 \Rightarrow R = 8r \Rightarrow r = \dfrac{R}{8}$

Initial surface area, $A_i = 4\pi R^2$

Final surface area, $A_f = 512 \times 4\pi r^2 = 512 \times 4\pi \left(\dfrac{R}{8}\right)^2 = 32\pi R^2$

Change in surface area, $\Delta A = A_f - A_i = 32\pi R^2 - 4\pi R^2 = 28\pi R^2$

Substituting $R = 10^{-3}$ m and $\pi \approx \dfrac{22}{7}$:

$\Delta U = 0.08 \times 28 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times (10^{-3})^2$

$\Delta U = 0.08 \times 4 \times 22 \times 10^{-6}$

$\Delta U = 7.04 \times 10^{-6}$ J

Comparing with $\alpha \times 10^{-6}$ J, we get $\alpha \approx 7$.

Answer: $7$

</details>

### Q7. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Given below are two statements: 
 <b>Statement I</b>: Pressure of a fluid is exerted only on a solid surface in contact as the fluid-pressure does not exist everywhere in a still fluid. 
 <b>Statement II</b>: Excess potential energy of the molecules on the surface of a liquid, when compared to interior, results in surface tension. 
 In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

**Options:**

- **(A)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true
- **(B)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false
- **(C)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false
- **(D)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Statement I is false but Statement II is true**

**Detailed Solution:**

Statement I is false: Fluid pressure exists everywhere in a still fluid, not only on solid surfaces. According to Pascal's principle, pressure at any point in a static fluid acts equally in all directions and throughout the fluid volume.

Statement II is true: Surface tension results from excess potential energy of molecules at the liquid surface. Surface molecules experience net inward attractive forces from interior molecules, creating an imbalance that increases their potential energy and manifests as surface tension acting along the surface.

</details>

### Q8. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Surface tension of two liquids (having same densities), $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$, are measured using capillary rise method utilizing two tubes with inner radii of $r_{1}$ and $r_{2}$ where $r_{1}>r_{2}$. The measured liquid heights in these tubes are $h_{1}$ and $h_{2}$ respectively. [Ignore the weight of the liquid about the lowest point of miniscus]. The heights $h_{1}$ and $h_{2}$ and surfaces tensions $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$ satisfy the relation:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $h_{1}>h_{2} \text { and } T_{1}=T_{2}$
- **(B)** $h_{1}=h_{2} \text { and } T_{1}=T_{2}$
- **(C)** $h_{1} > h_{2} \text { and } T_{1} < T_{2}$
- **(D)** $h_{1} < h_{2} \text { and } T_{1}=T_{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $h_{1} < h_{2} \text { and } T_{1}=T_{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For capillary rise, the height of liquid in a tube is given by: $h = \frac{2T\cos\theta}{\rho g r}$, where $T$ is surface tension and $r$ is the tube radius.

For two liquids with equal density and contact angles:
$h_1 = \frac{2T_1\cos\theta}{\rho g r_1}$ and $h_2 = \frac{2T_2\cos\theta}{\rho g r_2}$

Taking the ratio: $\frac{h_1}{h_2} = \frac{T_1}{T_2} \times \frac{r_2}{r_1}$

Since $r_1 > r_2$, we have $\frac{r_2}{r_1} < 1$. The capillary rise is inversely proportional to tube radius, so the smaller radius tube will have greater height. Since the liquids are measured to have heights $h_1$ and $h_2$ with $r_1 > r_2$, and they have the same density, the empirical relation shows that both liquids rise to different heights inversely proportional to their radii. For liquids with the same density measured in different tubes, if the heights and radii satisfy the capillary rise equation, the surface tensions must be equal: $T_1 = T_2$, and $h_1 < h_2$ since $r_1 > r_2$.

</details>

- **(B)** $0.15 \mathrm{~m}$
- **(C)** $0.20 \mathrm{~m}$

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
Surface tension of liquid,
$$

$$
Density of liquid, $\rho=10^3 \mathrm{~kg} / \mathrm{m}^3$
Excess pressure inside the bubble,

### Q67. JEE Main 2012 (19 May Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A large number of droplets, each of radius, $r$ coalesce to form a bigger drop of radius, $R$. An engineer designs a machine so that the energy released in this process is converted into the kinetic energy of the drop. Velocity of the drop is ( $T=$ surface tension, $\rho=$ density)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left[\frac{T}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right)\right]^{1 / 2}$
- **(B)** $\left[\frac{6 T}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right)\right]^{1 / 2}$
- **(C)** $\left[\frac{3 T}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right)\right]^{1 / 2}$
- **(D)** $\left[\frac{2 T}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right)\right]^{1 / 2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\left[\frac{6 T}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right)\right]^{1 / 2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When small droplets coelesce to form a bigger drop, energy released in this process is given by,
$$
4 \pi R^3 T\left[\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right]
$$
Where, $R=$ radius of big drop
$r=$ radius of small drop
$T=$ surface tension
According to question
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{1}{2} m v^2=4 \pi R^3 T\left[\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right] \\
\Rightarrow & \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 \rho\right] v^2=4 \pi R^3 T\left[\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right] \\
\Rightarrow V^2 & =\frac{6 T}{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right] \Rightarrow V=\left[\frac{6 T}{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{R}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$
