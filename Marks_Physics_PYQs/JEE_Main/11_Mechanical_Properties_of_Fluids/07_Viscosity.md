### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A spherical liquid drop of radius $R$ acquires the terminal velocity $v_1$ when falls through a gas of viscosity $\eta$. Now the drop is broken into $64$ identical droplets and each droplet acquires terminal velocity $v_2$ falling through the same gas. The ratio of terminal velocities $v_1/v_2$ is ________.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $0.25$
- **(C)** $32$
- **(D)** $16$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $16$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The terminal velocity of a spherical drop of radius $r$ falling through a viscous medium is given by Stokes' law:

$v = \dfrac{2}{9} \dfrac{r^2 (\rho - \sigma) g}{\eta}$

From this relation, the terminal velocity is directly proportional to the square of the radius of the drop:

$v \propto r^2$

When the large drop of radius $R$ is broken into $64$ identical droplets of radius $r$, the total volume remains conserved:

$\dfrac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = 64 \times \dfrac{4}{3} \pi r^3$

$R^3 = 64 r^3$

$R = 4r$

The ratio of the terminal velocities of the original drop and the smaller droplet is:

$\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{R^2}{r^2}$

Substituting $R = 4r$:

$\dfrac{v_1}{v_2} = \dfrac{(4r)^2}{r^2} = 16$

Answer: $16$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If an air bubble of diameter $2$ mm rises steadily through a liquid of density $2000$ kg/m$^3$ at a rate of $0.5$ cm/s, then the coefficient of viscosity of liquid is _______ Poise. (Take $g = 10$ m/s$^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.88$
- **(B)** $8.8$
- **(C)** $88.8$
- **(D)** $0.088$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $8.8$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The terminal velocity of an air bubble rising in a liquid is given by Stokes' law:

$v = \dfrac{2}{9} \dfrac{r^2 \rho g}{\eta}$

Where:
$r = \dfrac{D}{2} = 1 \text{ mm} = 10^{-3} \text{ m}$
$\rho = 2000 \text{ kg/m}^3$
$g = 10 \text{ m/s}^2$
$v = 0.5 \text{ cm/s} = 5 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m/s}$

Rearranging the formula for the coefficient of viscosity $\eta$:

$\eta = \dfrac{2}{9} \dfrac{r^2 \rho g}{v}$

Substituting the given values:

$\eta = \dfrac{2}{9} \times \dfrac{(10^{-3})^2 \times 2000 \times 10}{5 \times 10^{-3}}$

$\eta = \dfrac{2}{9} \times \dfrac{10^{-6} \times 20000}{5 \times 10^{-3}}$

$\eta = \dfrac{2}{9} \times \dfrac{2 \times 10^{-2}}{5 \times 10^{-3}}$

$\eta = \dfrac{2}{9} \times 4 = \dfrac{8}{9} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$

To convert the viscosity from SI units (Pa$\cdot$s) to CGS units (Poise), we multiply by $10$:

$\eta = \dfrac{8}{9} \times 10 = \dfrac{80}{9} \text{ Poise}$

$\eta \approx 8.88 \text{ Poise}$

The closest given option is $8.8$.

Answer: $8.8$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

Sixty four rain drops of radius 1 mm each falling down with a terminal velocity of $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$ coalesce to form a bigger drop. The terminal velocity of bigger drop is $\_\_\_\_$ $\mathrm{cm} / \mathrm{s}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **160**

**Detailed Solution:**

At terminal velocity, gravitational force equals Stokes drag: $mg = 6\pi\eta rv$
For a sphere with mass $m = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho$, this gives $v = \frac{2r^2\rho g}{9\eta}$
Therefore, $v \propto r^2$
When 64 drops coalesce: $64 \times \frac{4}{3}\pi r_1^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi r_2^3$, so $r_2 = 4r_1$
New terminal velocity: $\frac{v_2}{v_1} = \left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^2 = 16$
$v_2 = 16 \times 10 = 160$ cm/s

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A small metallic sphere of diameter 2 mm and density $10.5 \mathrm{~g} / \mathrm{cm}^{3}$ is dropped in glycerine having viscosity 10 Poise and density $1.5 \mathrm{~g} / \mathrm{cm}^{3}$ respectively. The terminal velocity attained by the sphere is $\_\_\_\_$ $\mathrm{cm} / \mathrm{s}$. 
($\pi=\frac{22}{7}$ and $g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** 3.0
- **(B)** 1.0
- **(C)** 2.0
- **(D)** 1.5

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 2.0**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using Stokes' Law for terminal velocity:
$v_t = \frac{2r^2g(\rho_s - \rho_f)}{9\eta}$. 
Given: r = 0.1 cm, g = 1000 cm/s², $\rho_s = 10.5$ g/cm³, $\rho_f = 1.5$ g/cm³, η = 10 Poise = 10 g/(cm⋅s). 
Substituting:
$v_t = \frac{2 \times (0.1)^2 \times 1000 \times (10.5 - 1.5)}{9 \times 10}$ 
$= \frac{2 \times 0.01 \times 1000 \times 9}{90}$ 
$= \frac{180}{90} = 2.0$ cm/s.

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `1`

The terminal velocity of a metallic ball of radius 6 mm in a viscous fluid is $20 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$. The terminal velocity of another ball of same material and having radius 3 mm in the same fluid will be $\_\_\_\_$ $\mathrm{cm} / \mathrm{s}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **5**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a sphere in a viscous fluid, terminal velocity is proportional to $r^2$: $v_t \propto r^2$.
For two balls of the same material in the same fluid: $\frac{v_{t1}}{v_{t2}} = \frac{r_1^2}{r_2^2}$.
Given $r_1 = 6$ mm, $v_{t1} = 20$ cm/s, and $r_2 = 3$ mm: $\frac{20}{v_{t2}} = \frac{36}{9} = 4$.
Therefore, $v_{t2} = 5$ cm/s.

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A spherical body of radius $r$ and density $\sigma$ falls freely through a viscous liquid having density $\rho$ and viscosity $\eta$ and attains a terminal velocity $v_{0}$. Estimated maximum error in the quantity $\eta$ is : (Ignore errors associated with $\sigma, \rho$ and $g$, gravitational acceleration)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{2 \Delta r}{r}+\frac{\Delta v_{\mathrm{o}}}{v_{\mathrm{o}}}$
- **(B)** $2 \frac{\Delta r}{r}-\frac{\Delta v_{\mathrm{o}}}{v_{\mathrm{o}}}$
- **(C)** $2\left[\frac{\Delta r}{r}-\frac{\Delta v_{\mathrm{o}}}{v_{\mathrm{o}}}\right]$
- **(D)** $2\left[\frac{\Delta r}{r}+\frac{\Delta v_{\mathrm{o}}}{v_{\mathrm{o}}}\right]$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{2 \Delta r}{r}+\frac{\Delta v_{\mathrm{o}}}{v_{\mathrm{o}}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The terminal velocity $v_0$ of a spherical body falling through a viscous liquid is given by Stokes' Law:
$v_0 = \frac{2r^2(\sigma - \rho)g}{9\eta}$
Rearranging the formula to express viscosity $\eta$ in terms of other variables:
$\eta = \frac{2r^2(\sigma - \rho)g}{9v_0}$
Since we are told to ignore errors associated with $\sigma$, $\rho$, and $g$, these quantities along with the numerical factor $\frac{2}{9}$ are treated as constants.
The expression for $\eta$ can be written as $\eta = k \cdot \frac{r^2}{v_0}$, where $k = \frac{2(\sigma - \rho)g}{9}$ is a constant.
Taking the natural logarithm on both sides:
$\ln \eta = \ln k + 2\ln r - \ln v_0$
Differentiating both sides to find the relative error:
$\frac{d\eta}{\eta} = 0 + 2\frac{dr}{r} - \frac{dv_0}{v_0}$
For the estimated maximum fractional error, we take the absolute sum of the individual relative errors:
$\frac{\Delta \eta}{\eta} = 2\frac{\Delta r}{r} + \frac{\Delta v_0}{v_0}$
This matches option (1).

</details>

### Q35. JEE Main 2013 (25 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

In an experiment, a small steel ball falls through a liquid at a constant speed of $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$. If the steel ball is pulled upward with a force equal to twice its effective weight, how fast will it move upward ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(B)** Zero
- **(C)** $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$
- **(D)** $20 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/KsRauQDYYm-xLRFJrajBRD9-uZmdCAdaTJqNGG4B4q8.original.fullsize.png">

Weight of the body
$\mathrm{W}=\mathrm{mg}=\frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{r}^3 \rho \mathrm{g}$
$\mathrm{T}=\frac{4}{3} \pi \mathrm{r}^3 \sigma \mathrm{g}$
and $\mathrm{F}=6 \pi \eta \mathrm{vr}$
When the body attains terminal velocity net force acting on the body is zero. i.e., 
$\mathrm{W}-\mathrm{T}-\mathrm{F}=0$
And terminal velocity $\mathrm{v}=\frac{2}{9} \frac{\mathrm{r}^2(\rho-\sigma) \mathrm{g}}{\eta}$
As in case of upward motion upward force is twice its effective weight, therefore, it will move with same speed $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$

</details>

### Q36. JEE Main 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A spherical solid ball of volume $V$ is made of a material of density $\rho_1$. It is falling through a liquid of density $\rho_2\left(\rho_2 < \rho_1\right)$. Assume that the liquid applies a viscous force on the ball that is proportional to the square of its speed v, i.e., $F_{\text {viscous }}=-k v^2(k>0)$. The terminal speed of the ball is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Vg}\left(\rho_1-\rho_2\right)}{\mathrm{k}}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\mathrm{Vg} \rho_1}{\mathrm{k}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\frac{V g \rho_1}{\mathrm{k}}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\operatorname{Vg}\left(\rho_1-\rho_2\right)}{\mathrm{k}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\sqrt{\frac{\operatorname{Vg}\left(\rho_1-\rho_2\right)}{\mathrm{k}}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \rho_1 V g-\rho_2 V g=k v_T^2 \\
& \Rightarrow \quad V_T=\sqrt{\frac{V g\left(\rho_1-\rho_2\right)}{k}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Spherical balls of radius $R$ are falling in a viscous fluid of viscosity $\eta$ with a velocity $v$. The retarding viscous force acting on the spherical ball is

**Options:**
