### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

Two identical small bar magnets each of dipole moment $3\sqrt{5}$ J/T are placed at a center to center separation of $10$ cm, with their axes perpendicular to each other as shown in figure. The value of magnetic field at the point P midway between the magnets is $\alpha \times 10^{-3}$ T. The value of $\alpha$ is ______.
($\mu_0=4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ Tm/A)
<img style="width:400px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/04APRS2__q48_d1.jpg" />

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **12**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the magnetic moment of each magnet be $M = 3\sqrt{5}$ J/T.

The distance of point P from the center of each magnet is $r = 5$ cm $= 0.05$ m.

For the first magnet (horizontal), point P lies on its axial line. The magnetic field at P due to the first magnet is:

$B_1 = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{2M}{r^3}$

The direction of $\vec{B}_1$ is along the axis of the first magnet.

For the second magnet (vertical), point P lies on its equatorial line. The magnetic field at P due to the second magnet is:

$B_2 = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{M}{r^3}$

The direction of $\vec{B}_2$ is parallel to the axis of the second magnet, which is perpendicular to the axis of the first magnet.

Since $\vec{B}_1$ and $\vec{B}_2$ are perpendicular to each other, the net magnetic field at P is:

$B = \sqrt{B_1^2 + B_2^2}$

Substituting the expressions for $B_1$ and $B_2$:

$B = \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{2M}{r^3}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{M}{r^3}\right)^2}$

$B = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{M}{r^3} \sqrt{2^2 + 1^2} = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \dfrac{\sqrt{5}M}{r^3}$

Substituting the given values $\dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7}$ T m/A, $M = 3\sqrt{5}$ J/T, and $r = 0.05$ m:

$B = 10^{-7} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{5} \times 3\sqrt{5}}{(0.05)^3}$

$B = 10^{-7} \times \dfrac{15}{125 \times 10^{-6}}$

$B = \dfrac{15 \times 10^{-1}}{125}$

$B = 0.1 \times 0.12$ T

$B = 0.012$ T

$B = 12 \times 10^{-3}$ T

Comparing this with the given value $\alpha \times 10^{-3}$ T, we get $\alpha = 12$.

Answer: $12$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A solenoid has a core made of material with relative permeability $400$. The magnetic field produced in the interior of solenoid is $1.0$ T. The magnetic intensity in SI units is $\alpha \times 10^5$. The value of $\alpha$ is ______.
(Free space permeability $\mu_0=4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ SI units.)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{25}{\pi}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{16\pi}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{\pi}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{4\pi}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{1}{16\pi}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field $B$ inside the solenoid is related to the magnetic intensity $H$ by the formula:

$B = \mu H = \mu_0 \mu_r H$

Rearranging for $H$, we get:

$H = \dfrac{B}{\mu_0 \mu_r}$

Substituting the given values ($B = 1.0$ T, $\mu_r = 400$, and $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ T m/A):

$H = \dfrac{1.0}{4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 400}$

$H = \dfrac{1.0}{1600\pi \times 10^{-7}}$

$H = \dfrac{1}{16\pi \times 10^{-5}}$

$H = \dfrac{1}{16\pi} \times 10^5$ A/m

Comparing this with the given expression $H = \alpha \times 10^5$, we find:

$\alpha = \dfrac{1}{16\pi}$

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{16\pi}$

</details>

\end{aligned}$

### Q33. JEE Main 2012 (07 May Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A bar magnet of length $6 \mathrm{~cm}$ has a magnetic moment of $4 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~T}^{-1}$. Find the strength of magnetic field at a distance of $200 \mathrm{~cm}$ from the centre of the magnet along its equatorial line.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4 \times 10^{-8}$ tesla
- **(B)** $3.5 \times 10^{-8}$ tesla
- **(C)** $5 \times 10^{-8}$ tesla
- **(D)** $3 \times 10^{-8}$ tesla

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5 \times 10^{-8}$ tesla**

**Detailed Solution:**

Along the equatorial line, magnetic field strength
$$
\left.B=\frac{\mu_0}{4 \pi} \frac{M}{\left(r^2+\ell^2{ }^{3 / 2}\right.}\right)
$$
Given: $M=4 \mathrm{~J} T^{-1}$
$$
r=200 \mathrm{~cm}=2 \mathrm{~m}
$$
$\ell=\frac{6 \mathrm{~cm}}{2}=3 \mathrm{~cm}=3 \times 10^{-2} \mathrm{~m}$
$$
\therefore B=\frac{4 \pi \times 10^{-7}}{4 \pi} \times \frac{4}{\left[2^2+\left(3 \times 10^{-2}\right)^2\right]^{3 / 2}}
$$
Solving we get, $B=5 \times 10^{-8}$ tesla

</details>
