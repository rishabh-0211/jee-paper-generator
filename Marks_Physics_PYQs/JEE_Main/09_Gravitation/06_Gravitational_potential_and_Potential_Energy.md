### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body of mass $m$ is taken from the surface of earth to a height equal to twice the radius of earth ($R_e$). The increase in potential energy will be _______.
($g$ is acceleration due to gravity at the surface of earth)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2}mgR_e$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3}{4}mgR_e$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{4}mgR_e$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{2}{3}mgR_e$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{2}{3}mgR_e$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial potential energy of the body at the surface of the earth is given by:

$U_i = -\dfrac{GMm}{R_e}$

Final potential energy of the body at a height $h = 2R_e$ from the surface of the earth is:

$U_f = -\dfrac{GMm}{R_e + h} = -\dfrac{GMm}{R_e + 2R_e} = -\dfrac{GMm}{3R_e}$

The increase in potential energy is:

$\Delta U = U_f - U_i = -\dfrac{GMm}{3R_e} - \left(-\dfrac{GMm}{R_e}\right)$

$\Delta U = \dfrac{GMm}{R_e} \left(1 - \dfrac{1}{3}\right) = \dfrac{2GMm}{3R_e}$

Acceleration due to gravity at the surface of the earth is $g = \dfrac{GM}{R_e^2}$, which gives $GM = gR_e^2$.

Substituting this value, we get:

$\Delta U = \dfrac{2(gR_e^2)m}{3R_e} = \dfrac{2}{3}mgR_e$

Answer: $\dfrac{2}{3}mgR_e$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If a body of mass $1$ kg falls on the earth from infinity, it attains velocity $(v)$ and kinetic energy $(k)$ on reaching the surface of earth. The values of $v$ and $k$ respectively are _______. (Take radius of earth to be $6400$ km and $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $11.2$ km/s; $6.27 \times 10^7$ J
- **(B)** $11.2$ km/s; $12.54 \times 10^7$ J
- **(C)** $8.8$ km/s; $6.27 \times 10^7$ J
- **(D)** $8.8$ km/s; $12.54 \times 10^7$ J

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $11.2$ km/s; $6.27 \times 10^7$ J**

**Detailed Solution:**

By conservation of mechanical energy, the total energy of the body at infinity is zero.

When the body reaches the surface of the earth, its potential energy is $-\dfrac{GMm}{R}$ and kinetic energy is $k = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.

$0 = k - \dfrac{GMm}{R}$

$k = \dfrac{GMm}{R} = mgR$

Substituting $m = 1$ kg, $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$, and $R = 6400 \times 10^3$ m:

$k = 1 \times 9.8 \times 6400 \times 10^3 = 6.272 \times 10^7$ J

The velocity $v$ is given by $\dfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = k$.

$v = \sqrt{2gR} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 6400 \times 10^3}$

$v = \sqrt{125.44 \times 10^6} = 11.2 \times 10^3$ m/s $= 11.2$ km/s

Answer: $11.2$ km/s; $6.27 \times 10^7$ J

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Three masses $200 \mathrm{~kg}, 300 \mathrm{~kg}$ and 400 kg are placed at the vertices of an equilateral triangle with sides 20 m. They are rearranged on the vertices of a bigger triangle of side 25 m and with the same centre. The work done in this process $\_\_\_\_$ J. (Gravitational constant $\mathrm{G}=6.7 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{~N} \mathrm{~m}^{2} / \mathrm{kg}^{2}$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.85 \times 10^{-7}$
- **(B)** $4.77 \times 10^{-7}$
- **(C)** $1.74 \times 10^{-7}$
- **(D)** $9.86 \times 10^{-6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1.74 \times 10^{-7}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Work done equals negative change in gravitational potential energy.
For three masses in an equilateral triangle, gravitational PE is $U = -G\left(\frac{m_1m_2}{r_{12}} + \frac{m_2m_3}{r_{23}} + \frac{m_1m_3}{r_{13}}\right)$.
Initial configuration (side 20 m):
$U_i = -\frac{G}{20}(200 \times 300 + 300 \times 400 + 200 \times 400)$ 
 $= -\frac{260000G}{20} = -13000G$
Final configuration (side 25 m):
$U_f = -\frac{G}{25}(260000) = -10400G$
Work done:
$W = -(U_f - U_i) = -10400G + 13000G$ 
$= 2600G = 2600 \times 6.7 \times 10^{-11}$ 
$= 1.742 \times 10^{-7}$ J $\approx 1.74 \times 10^{-7}$ J

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1000
- **(B)** $1000 \sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $200 \sqrt{5}$
- **(D)** $100 \sqrt{10}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $100 \sqrt{10}$**

$v_B = 10 \times \frac{1}{10\sqrt{10}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$ km/s $= \frac{1000}{\sqrt{10}} = 100\sqrt{10}$ m/s.

### Q46. JEE Main 2018 (15 Apr Shift 1 Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A body of mass $m$ is moving in a circular orbit of radius $R$ about a planet of mass $M$. At some instant, it splits into two equal masses. The first mass moves in a circular orbit of radius $\frac{R}{2}$, and the other mass, in a circular orbit of radius $\frac{3 R}{2}$. The difference between the final and initial total energies is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-\frac{G M m}{2 R}$
- **(B)** $+\frac{G M m}{6 R}$
- **(C)** $-\frac{G M m}{6 R}$
- **(D)** $\frac{G M m}{2 R}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $-\frac{G M m}{6 R}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial gravitational potential energy,
$$
E_i=-\frac{G M m}{2 R}
$$
Final gravitational potential energy,
$$
\begin{aligned}
E_f &=-\frac{G M m / 2}{2\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)}-\frac{G M m / 2}{2\left(\frac{3 R}{2}\right)} \\
&=-\frac{G M m}{2 R}-\frac{G M m}{6 R} \\
&=-\frac{4 G M m}{6 R}=-\frac{2 G M m}{3 R}
\end{aligned}
$$
$\therefore$ Difference between initial and final energy,
$$
E_f-E_i=\frac{G M m}{R}\left(-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right)=-\frac{G M m}{6 R}
$$

**Correct Option:** **(B) $$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathrm{v}_1=\mathrm{m}_2 \sqrt{\left.\frac{2 \mathrm{G}}{\mathrm{d}\left(\mathrm{m}_1+\mathrm{m}_2\right.}\right)} \\
&\mathrm{v}_2=\mathrm{m}_1 \sqrt{\left.\frac{2 \mathrm{G}}{\mathrm{d}\left(\mathrm{m}_1+\mathrm{m}_2\right.}\right)}
\end{aligned}
$$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We choose reference point, infinity, where total energy of the system is zero.
So, initial energy of the system $=0$
Final energy
$$
=\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m}_1 \mathrm{v}_1^2+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m}_2 \mathrm{v}_2^2-\frac{\mathrm{Gm}_1 \mathrm{~m}_2}{\mathrm{~d}}
$$
From conservation of energy, Initial energy $=$ Final energy
$$
\therefore 0=\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m}_1 \mathrm{v}_1{ }^2+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m}_2 \mathrm{v}_2{ }^2-\frac{\mathrm{Gm}_1 \mathrm{~m}_2}{\mathrm{~d}}
$$
or $\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m}_1 \mathrm{v}_1^2+\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m}_1 \mathrm{v}_2^2=\frac{\mathrm{Gm}_1 \mathrm{~m}_2}{\mathrm{~d}} \ldots(1)$
By conservation of linear momentum
$$
\mathrm{m}_1 \mathrm{v}_1+\mathrm{m}_2 \mathrm{v}_2=0
$$
or $\frac{\mathrm{v}_1}{\mathrm{v}_2}=-\frac{\mathrm{m}_2}{\mathrm{~m}_1} \Rightarrow \mathrm{v}_2=-\frac{\mathrm{m}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2} \mathrm{v}_1$
Putting value of $\mathrm{v}_2$ in equation (1), we get
$$
\mathrm{m}_1 \mathrm{v}_1^2+\mathrm{m}_2\left(-\frac{\mathrm{m}_1 \mathrm{v}_1}{\mathrm{~m}_2}\right)^2=\frac{2 \mathrm{Gm}_1 \mathrm{~m}_2}{\mathrm{~d}}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
