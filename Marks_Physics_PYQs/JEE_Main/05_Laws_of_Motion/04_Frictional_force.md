### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

A block takes $t$ time to slide down a plane inclined at $45°$ to the horizontal. If the surface is made smooth (frictionless), the block takes time $\dfrac{t}{2}$ to slide down the plane. The coefficient of friction between the block and the inclined plane is $\left(\dfrac{\alpha}{100}\right)$. The value of $\alpha$ is __________.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **75**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the length of the inclined plane be $s$.

For the smooth inclined plane, the acceleration of the block is $a_1 = g \sin \theta$.

The time taken is $t_1 = \dfrac{t}{2}$. Using the second equation of motion $s = ut + \dfrac{1}{2}at^2$ with $u = 0$:

$s = \dfrac{1}{2} (g \sin \theta) \left(\dfrac{t}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{8} g \sin \theta t^2$

For the rough inclined plane, the acceleration of the block is $a_2 = g(\sin \theta - \mu \cos \theta)$.

The time taken is $t_2 = t$. Using the equation of motion again:

$s = \dfrac{1}{2} g(\sin \theta - \mu \cos \theta) t^2$

Equating the two expressions for $s$:

$\dfrac{1}{8} g \sin \theta t^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} g(\sin \theta - \mu \cos \theta) t^2$

$\dfrac{1}{4} \sin \theta = \sin \theta - \mu \cos \theta$

Given $\theta = 45^{\circ}$, we have $\sin 45^{\circ} = \cos 45^{\circ} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. Substituting these values:

$\dfrac{1}{4} = 1 - \mu$

$\mu = 1 - \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{4} = 0.75$

We are given $\mu = \dfrac{\alpha}{100}$.

$\dfrac{\alpha}{100} = 0.75 \Rightarrow \alpha = 75$

Answer: $75$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A mass of $1\text{ kg}$ is kept on an inclined plane with $30°$ inclination with respect to horizontal plane and it is at rest initially. Then the whole assembly is moved up with constant velocity of $4\text{ m/s}$. The work done by the frictional force in time $2\text{ s}$ is _______ J. (Take $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $20$
- **(B)** $25$
- **(C)** $30$
- **(D)** $10$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $20$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since the assembly moves with a constant velocity, the acceleration of the mass is zero. The mass is in equilibrium relative to the incline.

The frictional force $f$ acting on the mass balances the component of gravity along the inclined plane. Therefore, the magnitude of the frictional force is:

$f = mg \sin 30^\circ$

Substituting the given values ($m = 1\text{ kg}$, $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$):

$f = 1 \times 10 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 5\text{ N}$

The direction of the frictional force is upwards along the incline. Since the incline makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the horizontal, the frictional force makes an angle of $90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$ with the vertical upward direction.

The displacement $s$ of the assembly in time $t = 2\text{ s}$ is vertically upwards:

$s = vt = 4 \times 2 = 8\text{ m}$

The work done by the frictional force is:

$W = f s \cos 60^\circ$

$W = 5 \times 8 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 20\text{ J}$

Answer: $20$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The time taken by a block of mass $m$ to slide down from the highest point to the lowest point on a rough inclined plane is $50\%$ more compared to the time taken by the same block on identical inclined smooth plane. Both inclined planes are at $45^\circ$ with the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the rough inclined surface and block is _____.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3/4$
- **(B)** $2/3$
- **(C)** $5/9$
- **(D)** $4/9$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5/9$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the length of the inclined plane be $s$.

For the smooth inclined plane, the acceleration of the block is $a_1 = g \sin \theta$.

The time taken to slide down is $t_1 = \sqrt{\dfrac{2s}{a_1}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2s}{g \sin \theta}}$.

For the rough inclined plane, the acceleration of the block is $a_2 = g \sin \theta - \mu_k g \cos \theta$.

The time taken to slide down is $t_2 = \sqrt{\dfrac{2s}{a_2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2s}{g (\sin \theta - \mu_k \cos \theta)}}$.

Given that $t_2$ is $50\%$ more than $t_1$, we have $t_2 = t_1 + 0.5 t_1 = \dfrac{3}{2} t_1$.

Squaring both sides gives $t_2^2 = \dfrac{9}{4} t_1^2$.

Substituting the expressions for $t_1^2$ and $t_2^2$:

$\dfrac{2s}{g (\sin \theta - \mu_k \cos \theta)} = \dfrac{9}{4} \left( \dfrac{2s}{g \sin \theta} \right)$

$\dfrac{1}{\sin \theta - \mu_k \cos \theta} = \dfrac{9}{4 \sin \theta}$

$4 \sin \theta = 9 \sin \theta - 9 \mu_k \cos \theta$

$9 \mu_k \cos \theta = 5 \sin \theta$

$\mu_k = \dfrac{5}{9} \tan \theta$

Given $\theta = 45^\circ$, we have $\tan 45^\circ = 1$.

$\mu_k = \dfrac{5}{9} \times 1 = \dfrac{5}{9}$

Answer: $5/9$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A $0.5$ kg mass is in contact against the inner wall of a cylindrical drum of radius $4$ m rotating about its vertical axis. The minimum rotational speed of the drum to enable the mass to remain stuck to the wall (without falling) is $5$ rad/s. The coefficient of friction between the drum's inner wall surface and mass is _______. (Take $g = 10$ m/s$^2$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.1$
- **(B)** $0.5$
- **(C)** $0.7$
- **(D)** $0.3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $0.1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The normal force $N$ provides the necessary centripetal force for the mass to move in a circle:

$N = m \omega^2 R$

For the mass to remain stuck to the wall without falling, the upward frictional force must balance the downward gravitational force:

$f \ge mg$

Since the maximum static friction is $f_{max} = \mu N$, we have:

$\mu N \ge mg$

$\mu (m \omega^2 R) \ge mg$

$\mu \ge \dfrac{g}{\omega^2 R}$

Substituting the given values $g = 10$ m/s$^2$, $\omega = 5$ rad/s, and $R = 4$ m:

$\mu \ge \dfrac{10}{5^2 \times 4}$

$\mu \ge \dfrac{10}{100}$

$\mu \ge 0.1$

The minimum coefficient of friction is $0.1$.

Answer: $0.1$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A block of mass 5 kg is moving on an inclined plane which makes an angle of $30^{\circ}$ with the horizontal. Friction coefficient between the block and inclined plane surface is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The force to be applied on the block so that the block will move down without acceleration is $\_\_\_\_$ N. 
 $\left(g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}\right)$.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 25
- **(B)** 7.5
- **(C)** 12.5
- **(D)** 15

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 12.5**

**Detailed Solution:**

For motion without acceleration, net force = 0.
Component of weight along incline: $mg\sin(30°) = 5 \times 10 \times 0.5 = 25$ N (down).
Normal force: $N = mg\cos(30°) = 50 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 25\sqrt{3}$ N.
Friction force opposing downward motion:
$f = \mu N = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \times 25\sqrt{3} = 37.5$ N (up).
For equilibrium along the incline, applying force F down the incline:
$mg\sin\theta + F = f$, so $25 + F = 37.5$, giving $F = 12.5$ N.

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

In the given figure the blocks $A, B$ and $C$ weigh $4 \mathrm{~kg}, 6 \mathrm{~kg}$ and 8 kg respectively. The co-efficient of sliding friction between any two surfaces is 0.5. The force $\vec{F}$ required to slide the block $C$ with constant speed is $\_\_\_\_$ N. 
 (Use $g=10 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}^{2}$) 
 <img style="width:200px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/24S1_q_47_1.png">

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **210**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the 8 kg block (C) to move with constant velocity, $F_{net} = 0$.

Friction forces acting:
- Between C and ground: $f = \mu(m_A + m_B + m_C)g = 0.5 \times (4+6+8) \times 10 = 90$ N
- Between C and B: $f = \mu(m_A + m_B)g = 0.5 \times (4+6) \times 10 = 50$ N

For the 6 kg block (B):
$T = f_{\text{B on ground}} + f_{\text{A on B}}$
$T = \mu(m_A + m_B)g \cdot \frac{m_B}{m_A+m_B} + \mu \cdot m_A \cdot g$
$T = 20 + 50 = 70$ N

For the 8 kg block (C):
$F = 90 + T + 50 = 90 + 70 + 50 = 210$ N

### Q8. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A large drum having radius $R$ is spinning around its axis with angular velocity $\omega$, as shown in figure. The minimum value of $\omega$ so that a body of mass $M$ remains stuck to the inner wall of the drum, taking the coefficient of friction between the drum surface and mass $M$ as $\mu$, is : 
 <img style="width:100px;" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_watermark_improved/21S2_q_43_1.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\frac{\mu g}{R}}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{\frac{g}{2 \mu R}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{\frac{2 g}{\mu R}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\frac{g}{\mu R}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{\frac{g}{\mu R}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Consider the mass $M$ in the frame of the rotating drum. The forces acting on the mass are:
1. Weight $Mg$ acting vertically downwards.
2. Normal reaction $N$ from the wall acting towards the center, which provides the centripetal force: $N = M\omega^2 R$.
3. Frictional force $f$ acting vertically upwards to balance the weight.
For the mass to remain stuck to the wall, the frictional force must balance the weight: $f = Mg$.
The maximum available static friction is $f_{max} = \mu N = \mu M\omega^2 R$.
To prevent slipping, we must have $f_{max} \ge Mg$.
$\mu M\omega^2 R \ge Mg$
$\omega^2 \ge \frac{g}{\mu R}$
$\omega \ge \sqrt{\frac{g}{\mu R}}$
The minimum value of $\omega$ is $\sqrt{\frac{g}{\mu R}}$.

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\theta=\tan ^{-1}(\mu), F=\frac{\mu W}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}$
- **(B)** $\theta=\tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\right), F=\frac{\mu W}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}$

- **(D)** $\theta=\tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu}{1+\mu}\right), F=\frac{\mu W}{1+\mu}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\theta=\tan ^{-1}(\mu), F=\frac{\mu W}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the force $F$ is applied at an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal.
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/pK9-C8aYZ0g71NL8VJCDOmLPkfkSipV_Td2hJ-BuVAA.original.fullsize.png">

For horizontal equilibrium, $F \cos \theta=\mu R$
For vertical equilibrium, $R+F \sin \theta=\mathrm{mg}$ or, $R=m g-F \sin \theta$ Substituting this value of $R$ in eq. (i), we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
& F \cos \theta=\mu(\mathrm{mg}-F \sin \theta) \\
& =\mu \mathrm{mg}-\mu F \sin \theta \\
& \text { or, } F(\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta)=\mu \mathrm{mg} \\
& \text { or, } \mathrm{F}=\frac{\mu \mathrm{mg}}{\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta}
\end{aligned}
$$
For $F$ to be minimum, the denominator $(\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta)$ should be maximum.
$$
\therefore \frac{d}{d \theta}(\cos \theta+\mu \sin \theta)=0
$$
or, $-\sin \theta+\mu \cos \theta=0$
or, $\tan \theta=\mu$
or, $\theta=\tan ^{-1}(\mu)$
Then, $\sin \theta=\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}$ and
$$
\cos \theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}
$$
Hence, $F_{\text {min }}$
$$
=\frac{\mu w}{\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}+\frac{\mu^2}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}}=\frac{\mu w}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}}
$$
