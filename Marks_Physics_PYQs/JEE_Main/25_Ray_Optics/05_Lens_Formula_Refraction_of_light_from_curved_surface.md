### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A thin biconvex lens is prepared from the glass ($\mu=1.5$) both curved surfaces of which have equal radii of $20$ cm each. Left side surface of the lens is silvered from outside to make it reflecting. To have the position of image and object at the same place, the object should be placed, from the lens at a distance of ________ cm.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$
- **(B)** $12.5$
- **(C)** $13$
- **(D)** $13.5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $10$**

**Detailed Solution:**

When one surface of a lens is silvered, the system behaves like an equivalent mirror. The light refracts through the lens, reflects off the silvered surface, and refracts through the lens again, so the equivalent power is:
$P_{eq} = P_L + P_M + P_L = 2P_L + P_M$

Power of the biconvex lens:
For a thin biconvex lens with $R_1 = +20\text{ cm}$, $R_2 = -20\text{ cm}$ and $\mu = 1.5$, the lens maker's formula gives:
$\dfrac{1}{f_L} = (\mu - 1)\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2}\right)$

$\dfrac{1}{f_L} = (1.5 - 1)\left(\dfrac{1}{20} - \dfrac{1}{-20}\right) = 0.5 \cdot \dfrac{2}{20} = \dfrac{1}{20}\text{ cm}^{-1}$

So, $P_L = \dfrac{1}{20}\text{ cm}^{-1}$.

Power of the silvered surface (acting as a concave mirror):
The back surface has radius of curvature $R = 20\text{ cm}$, so its focal length is:
$f_M = \dfrac{R}{2} = 10\text{ cm}$

$P_M = \dfrac{1}{f_M} = \dfrac{1}{10}\text{ cm}^{-1}$

Equivalent focal length:
$P_{eq} = 2P_L + P_M = 2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{20} + \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{1}{5}\text{ cm}^{-1}$

$F_{eq} = \dfrac{1}{P_{eq}} = 5\text{ cm}$

Since $P_{eq} > 0$, the system behaves as a converging (concave) equivalent mirror of focal length $5\text{ cm}$.

Object position for object and image to coincide:
For a concave mirror, the image coincides with the object when the object is placed at the centre of curvature, i.e., at a distance $2F_{eq}$ from the mirror:
$u = 2F_{eq} = 2 \times 5 = 10\text{ cm}$

Hence, the correct option is $(1)\ 10$.

</details>

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A spherical interface lens of radius $R$ separates two media of refractive indices $1$ and $1.4$ respectively as shown in the figure below. A point source is placed at a distance of $4R$ in front of spherical interface. The magnitude of the magnification of point source image is _______.
<img style="width: 400px" src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main_2026_april_watermarked/06APRS1__q41_d1.png" />

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1.66$
- **(B)** $2.33$
- **(C)** $2.66$
- **(D)** $1.33$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1.66$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given:
Refractive index of the first medium, $n_1 = 1$
Refractive index of the second medium, $n_2 = 1.4 = \dfrac{7}{5}$
Object distance, $u = -4R$
Radius of curvature of the spherical interface, $R = +R$ (since the surface is convex towards the rarer medium)

Using the formula for refraction at a single spherical surface:
$\dfrac{n_2}{v} - \dfrac{n_1}{u} = \dfrac{n_2 - n_1}{R}$

Substituting the given values:
$\dfrac{1.4}{v} - \dfrac{1}{-4R} = \dfrac{1.4 - 1}{R}$

$\dfrac{7}{5v} + \dfrac{1}{4R} = \dfrac{0.4}{R}$

$\dfrac{7}{5v} + \dfrac{1}{4R} = \dfrac{2}{5R}$

$\dfrac{7}{5v} = \dfrac{2}{5R} - \dfrac{1}{4R}$

$\dfrac{7}{5v} = \dfrac{8 - 5}{20R} = \dfrac{3}{20R}$

$v = \dfrac{7 \times 20R}{5 \times 3} = \dfrac{28R}{3}$

The transverse magnification $m$ for a spherical refracting surface is given by:
$m = \dfrac{n_1 v}{n_2 u}$

Substituting the values of $n_1$, $n_2$, $u$, and $v$:
$m = \dfrac{1 \times \left(\dfrac{28R}{3}\right)}{1.4 \times (-4R)}$

$m = \dfrac{\dfrac{28R}{3}}{-\dfrac{28R}{5}}$

$m = -\dfrac{5}{3}$

The magnitude of the magnification is:
$|m| = \dfrac{5}{3} \approx 1.66$

Answer: $1.66$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An object $AB$ is placed $15\text{ cm}$ on the left of a convex lens $P$ of focal length $10\text{ cm}$. Another convex lens $Q$ is now placed $15\text{ cm}$ right of lens $P$. If the focal length of lens $Q$ is $15\text{ cm}$, the final image is _______.

**Options:**

- **(A)** virtual, formed at $7.5\text{ cm}$ right of lens $Q$, with a size bigger than that of $AB$
- **(B)** real, formed at $7.5\text{ cm}$ right of lens $Q$, with a size same as that of $AB$
- **(C)** formed at infinity.
- **(D)** real, formed at $7\text{ cm}$ right of lens $Q$, with a size smaller than that of $AB$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) real, formed at $7.5\text{ cm}$ right of lens $Q$, with a size same as that of $AB$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For lens $P$:

$\dfrac{1}{v_1} - \dfrac{1}{u_1} = \dfrac{1}{f_1}$

$\dfrac{1}{v_1} - \dfrac{1}{-15} = \dfrac{1}{10}$

$\dfrac{1}{v_1} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{1}{30} \Rightarrow v_1 = 30\text{ cm}$

Magnification $m_1 = \dfrac{v_1}{u_1} = \dfrac{30}{-15} = -2$

The image formed by lens $P$ acts as a virtual object for lens $Q$. Since the distance between the lenses is $15\text{ cm}$, the object distance for lens $Q$ is:

$u_2 = 30 - 15 = +15\text{ cm}$

For lens $Q$:

$\dfrac{1}{v_2} - \dfrac{1}{u_2} = \dfrac{1}{f_2}$

$\dfrac{1}{v_2} - \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{1}{15}$

$\dfrac{1}{v_2} = \dfrac{2}{15} \Rightarrow v_2 = 7.5\text{ cm}$

The positive sign indicates that the final image is real and formed $7.5\text{ cm}$ to the right of lens $Q$.

Magnification $m_2 = \dfrac{v_2}{u_2} = \dfrac{7.5}{15} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Total magnification $m = m_1 \times m_2 = -2 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = -1$

Since $|m| = 1$, the size of the final image is the same as that of the object $AB$.

Answer: real, formed at $7.5\text{ cm}$ right of lens $Q$, with a size same as that of $AB$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A thin convex lens and a thin concave lens are kept in contact and are co-axial. Which of the following statements is correct for this combination of two lenses ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** behaves as concave lens if $|f_{\text{convex}}| > |f_{\text{concave}}|$
- **(B)** behaves as concave lens if $|f_{\text{convex}}| < |f_{\text{concave}}|$
- **(C)** behaves as convex lens if $|f_{\text{convex}}| > |f_{\text{concave}}|$
- **(D)** Focal length of the lens system will change if the positions of two lenses are interchanged

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) behaves as concave lens if $|f_{\text{convex}}| > |f_{\text{concave}}|$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equivalent focal length $F$ of two thin lenses in contact is given by $\dfrac{1}{F} = \dfrac{1}{f_1} + \dfrac{1}{f_2}$

For a convex lens, the focal length is positive, so $f_1 = |f_{\text{convex}}|$

For a concave lens, the focal length is negative, so $f_2 = -|f_{\text{concave}}|$

Substituting these values, we get $\dfrac{1}{F} = \dfrac{1}{|f_{\text{convex}}|} - \dfrac{1}{|f_{\text{concave}}|}$

If $|f_{\text{convex}}| > |f_{\text{concave}}|$, then $\dfrac{1}{|f_{\text{convex}}|} < \dfrac{1}{|f_{\text{concave}}|}$

This implies $\dfrac{1}{F} < 0$, so $F$ is negative.

A negative equivalent focal length indicates that the combination behaves as a diverging or concave lens.

Interchanging the positions of the lenses does not change the equivalent focal length since $\dfrac{1}{f_1} + \dfrac{1}{f_2} = \dfrac{1}{f_2} + \dfrac{1}{f_1}$.

Answer: behaves as concave lens if $|f_{\text{convex}}| > |f_{\text{concave}}|$

</details>

$\dfrac{f}{R} = \dfrac{1}{0.8} = 1.25$

Answer: $1.25$

Sunlight comes from infinity and is focused at the principal focus of the convex lens. The paper burns in the shortest time when the intensity of light is maximum, which occurs at the focal point.

Assuming the lens is equiconvex, the radii of curvature are $R_1 = 60$ cm and $R_2 = -60$ cm.

$\dfrac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1) \left( \dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2} \right)$

$\dfrac{1}{30} = (\mu - 1) \left( \dfrac{1}{30} \right)$

 (refractive index of water $=4 / 3$)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

Using the lensmaker's equation, in air:
$\frac{1}{f_{air}} = (n - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$ where $(n-1) = 0.5$ for glass with $n = 1.5$.
In water:
$\frac{1}{f_{water}} = \left(\frac{n}{n_{water}} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$

### Q11. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

The size of the images of an object, formed by a thin lens are equal when the object is placed at two different positions 8 cm and 24 cm from the lens. The focal length of the lens is $\_\_\_\_$ cm.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **16**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnification of a thin lens is given by $m = \frac{f}{f + u}$.
Given that the size of the images is equal for two different object positions, the magnitudes of the magnifications must be equal: $|m_1| = |m_2|$.
For a single lens to produce images of the same size at two different object distances, one image must be real and the other must be virtual.
Let $u_1 = -8$ cm and $u_2 = -24$ cm.
Then, $m_1 = -m_2$ (since one is real and one is virtual).
$\frac{f}{f + u_1} = -\frac{f}{f + u_2}$
$\frac{1}{f - 8} = -\frac{1}{f - 24}$
$f - 24 = -(f - 8)$
$f - 24 = -f + 8$
$2f = 32$
$f = 16$ cm.
The focal length of the lens is 16 cm.

</details>

### Q77. JEE Main 2019 (11 Jan Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An object is at a distance of $20 \mathrm{~m}$ from a convex lens of focal length $0.3 \mathrm{~m}$. The lens forms an image of the object. If the object moves away from the lens at a speed of $5 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ the speed and direction of the image will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2.26 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ away from the lens
- **(B)** $0.92 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ away from the lens
- **(C)** $3.22 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ towards the lens
- **(D)** $1.16 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ towards the lens

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1.16 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ towards the lens**

**Detailed Solution:**

Bylens formula

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{u}=\frac{1}{f} \\

\frac{1}{v}-\frac{1}{(-20)}=\frac{10}{3} \\

\frac{1}{v}=\frac{10}{3}-\frac{1}{20} \\

\frac{1}{v}=\frac{197}{60} ; \quad v=\frac{60}{197}

\end{array}

$$

Magnification of lens (m) is given by

$$

\mathrm{m}=\left(\frac{\mathrm{v}}{\mathrm{u}}\right)=\frac{\left(\frac{60}{197}\right)}{20}

$$

velocity of image wrt. to lens is given by

$$

\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{J} / \mathrm{L}}=\mathrm{m}^{2} \mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{O} / \mathrm{L}}

$$

direction of velocity of image is same as that of object

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{O} / \mathrm{L}}=5 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s} \\

\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{I} / \mathrm{L}}=\left(\frac{60 \times 1}{197 \times 20}\right)^{2}

\end{array}

$$

$=1.16 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ towards the lens

</details>

- **(C)** <img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/JHN8Pgw1aUyKAN78XcF7r4UmFAZm3zRXiRC-E77oV-c.original.fullsize.png">
- **(D)** <img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/O01C_lugfOtLqBtZW6SW9E6Q5QKVZe2ApdjKuW76RDQ.original.fullsize.png">

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) <img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/aB-5WgKn2vub7NnarThqFTb-tkdOU6bL5L5wcwpeax8.original.fullsize.png">**

**Detailed Solution:**

If a lens of refractive index $\mu$ is immersed in a medium of refractive index $\mu_1$, then its focal length in medium is given by
$$
\frac{1}{\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{m}}}=\left({ }_{\mathrm{m}} \mu_l-1\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_1}\right)-\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_2}\right)
$$
If $\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{a}}$ is the focal length of lens in air, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{1}{\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{a}}}=\left({ }_{\mathrm{a}} \mu_l-1\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_1}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_2}\right)\right. \\
&\Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{m}}}{\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{a}}}=\frac{\left({ }_{\mathrm{a}} \mu_l-1\right)}{\left({ }_{\mathrm{m}} \mu_l-1\right)}
\end{aligned}
$$
If $\mu_1>\mu$, then $\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{a}}$ have opposite signs and the nature of lens changes i.e. a convex lens diverges the light rays and concave lens converges the light rays. Thus given option (a) is correct.

$\mathrm{u}=-40 \mathrm{~cm}$
$\mathrm{v}=3 \times 40=120 \mathrm{~cm}$
$$
\mathrm{f}=?
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{1}{120}-\frac{1}{-40}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{f}} \\

Object should be placed at $20 \mathrm{~cm}$ from the lens.
