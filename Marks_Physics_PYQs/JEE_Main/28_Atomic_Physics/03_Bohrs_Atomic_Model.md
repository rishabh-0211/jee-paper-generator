### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The ratio of momentum of the photons of the $1^{st}$ and $2^{nd}$ line of Balmer series of Hydrogen atoms is $\alpha/\beta$. The possible values of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are:-

**Options:**

- **(A)** $27$ and $20$
- **(B)** $3$ and $16$
- **(C)** $5$ and $36$
- **(D)** $20$ and $27$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $20$ and $27$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The momentum of a photon is given by $p = \dfrac{h}{\lambda}$.

From Rydberg's formula, the wave number is $\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R \left( \dfrac{1}{n_1^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$.

For the first line of the Balmer series, the transition is from $n_2 = 3$ to $n_1 = 2$.

$p_1 = \dfrac{h}{\lambda_1} = hR \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{3^2} \right) = hR \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9} \right) = \dfrac{5hR}{36}$

For the second line of the Balmer series, the transition is from $n_2 = 4$ to $n_1 = 2$.

$p_2 = \dfrac{h}{\lambda_2} = hR \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2} \right) = hR \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{16} \right) = \dfrac{3hR}{16}$

The ratio of the momenta is:

$\dfrac{p_1}{p_2} = \dfrac{\dfrac{5hR}{36}}{\dfrac{3hR}{16}} = \dfrac{5}{36} \times \dfrac{16}{3} = \dfrac{20}{27}$

Comparing with $\dfrac{\alpha}{\beta}$, the possible values are $\alpha = 20$ and $\beta = 27$.

Answer: $20$ and $27$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In the hydrogen atom, the electron makes a transition from the higher orbit ($i$) to a lower orbit ($f$). The ratio of the radius of the orbits in given by $r_i : r_f = 16 : 4$. The wavelength of photon emitted due to this transition is _____ nm.
(Given Rydberg constant $= 1.0973 \times 10^7$ /m)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $121$
- **(B)** $242$
- **(C)** $486$
- **(D)** $974$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $486$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The radius of the $n$-th orbit in a hydrogen atom is proportional to the square of the principal quantum number $n$, i.e., $r_n \propto n^2$.

Given the ratio of the radii of the initial and final orbits:
$\dfrac{r_i}{r_f} = \dfrac{16}{4}$

This implies:
$\dfrac{n_i^2}{n_f^2} = \dfrac{16}{4} \Rightarrow n_i = 4 \text{ and } n_f = 2$

The wavelength $\lambda$ of the emitted photon is given by the Rydberg formula:
$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R \left( \dfrac{1}{n_f^2} - \dfrac{1}{n_i^2} \right)$

Substituting the values:
$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 1.0973 \times 10^7 \left( \dfrac{1}{2^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2} \right)$

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 1.0973 \times 10^7 \left( \dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{16} \right)$

$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = 1.0973 \times 10^7 \times \dfrac{3}{16}$

$\lambda = \dfrac{16}{3 \times 1.0973 \times 10^7} \text{ m}$

$\lambda \approx 4.86 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m} = 486 \text{ nm}$

Answer: $486$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

Using Bohr's model, calculate the ratio of the magnetic fields generated due to the motion of the electrons in the $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $4^{\text{th}}$ orbits of hydrogen atom _______.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **32**

**Detailed Solution:**

The magnetic field at the center of a circular orbit is given by $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 I}{2r}$.

The equivalent current due to the revolving electron is $I = \dfrac{e}{T} = \dfrac{ev}{2\pi r}$.

Substituting $I$ into the expression for $B$, we get $B = \dfrac{\mu_0 ev}{4\pi r^2}$.

According to Bohr's model for a hydrogen atom, the velocity of the electron in the $n^{\text{th}}$ orbit is $v \propto \dfrac{1}{n}$ and the radius of the orbit is $r \propto n^2$.

Therefore, the magnetic field $B$ depends on the principal quantum number $n$ as $B \propto \dfrac{1/n}{(n^2)^2} \Rightarrow B \propto \dfrac{1}{n^5}$.

The ratio of the magnetic fields in the $2^{\text{nd}}$ and $4^{\text{th}}$ orbits is $\dfrac{B_2}{B_4} = \left(\dfrac{n_4}{n_2}\right)^5 = \left(\dfrac{4}{2}\right)^5 = 2^5 = 32$.

Answer: $32$

</details>

- **(D)** 4

& \text { or } \mathrm{mvr}=\frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi} \text { or } \mathrm{mv}=\frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi \mathrm{r}} \\
& \mathrm{F}=\mathrm{qv}_{\mathrm{B}}=\frac{\mathrm{mv}^2}{\mathrm{r}} \\
& \text { or, } \mathrm{q}_{\mathrm{B}}=\frac{\mathrm{mv}}{\mathrm{r}}=\frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi \mathrm{r} \cdot \mathrm{r}} \\
& \text { or, } \mathrm{r}^2=\frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi \mathrm{qB}} \\
& \text { or, } \mathrm{r}=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{nh}}{2 \pi \mathrm{qB}}} \\
& \text { i.e., } \mathrm{r} \propto \mathrm{n}^{1 / 2}
\end{aligned}
$$
