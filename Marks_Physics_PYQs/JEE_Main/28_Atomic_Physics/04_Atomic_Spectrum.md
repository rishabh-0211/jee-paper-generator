### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In hydrogen atom spectrum, $(R \rightarrow$ Rydberg's constant $)$ A. the maximum wavelength of the radiation of Lyman series is $\frac{4}{3 R}$ B. the Balmer series lies in the visible region of the spectrum C. the minimum wavelength of the radiation of Paschen series is $\frac{9}{R}$ D. the minimum wavelength of Lyman series is $\frac{5}{4 R}$ Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

**Options:**

- **(A)** B, D Only
- **(B)** A, B and C Only
- **(C)** A, B and D Only
- **(D)** A, B Only

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) A, B and C Only**

**Detailed Solution:**

A. Maximum wavelength of Lyman series ($n=1$ to $n=2$):
$\frac{1}{\lambda} = R\left(1 - \frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{3R}{4}$
$\lambda = \frac{4}{3R}$ TRUE

B. The Balmer series lies in the visible region of the spectrum. TRUE

C. Minimum wavelength of Paschen series ($n=3$ to $n=\infty$):
$\frac{1}{\lambda'} = R\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)$
$\lambda' = \frac{9}{R}$ TRUE

D. Minimum wavelength of Lyman series ($n=1$ to $n=\infty$):
$\frac{1}{\lambda} = R\left(1\right)$
$\lambda = \frac{1}{R}$, NOT $\frac{5}{4R}$ FALSE

So A, B and C are correct.

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The smallest wavelength of Lyman series is 91 nm. The difference between the largest wavelengths of Paschen and Balmer series is nearly $\_\_\_\_$ nm .

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1550
- **(B)** 1217
- **(C)** 1875
- **(D)** 1784

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 1217**

**Detailed Solution:**

Smallest wavelength of lyman
$\dfrac{1}{\lambda} = R\left(\dfrac{1}{1^2} - \dfrac{1}{\infty^2}\right)$
$R = \dfrac{1}{\lambda} = \dfrac{1}{91} \text{ nm}^{-1}$
$\lambda_{max}$ for balmer series
$n_1 = 2 \rightarrow n_2 = 3$
$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_B} = R\left(\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9}\right)$
$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_B} = \dfrac{1}{91}\left(\dfrac{5}{36}\right)$
$\lambda_B = \left(\dfrac{91 \times 36}{5}\right) = 655.2 \text{ nm}$
$\lambda_{max}$ paschen
$n_1 = 3 \rightarrow n_2 = 4$
$\dfrac{1}{\lambda_p} = \dfrac{1}{91}\left(\dfrac{1}{3^2} - \dfrac{1}{4^2}\right) = \dfrac{1}{91} \times \dfrac{7}{144}$
$\lambda_p = \left(\dfrac{91 \times 144}{7}\right) = 1872 \text{ nm}$
$\Delta\lambda = \lambda_P - \lambda_B = 1872 - 655.2$
$\Delta\lambda = 1216.8$
$\Delta\lambda \approx 1217$

**Options:**

- **(B)** $2000 Å$
- **(C)** $3000 Å$
- **(D)** $6000 Å$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3000 Å$**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/ZurKCZTYIgt12LEi1nzo3n-INSfpR1zb_zDupRYJBrg.original.fullsize.png">

$\frac{h c}{\lambda_1}=E_0 z^2\left(\frac{1}{n_c^2}-\frac{1}{n_A^2}\right)$ ....(i)
And $\frac{h c}{\lambda_2}=E_0 z^2\left(\frac{1}{n_c^2}-\frac{1}{n_B^2}\right)$
So for $A$ and $B$ ....(ii)
$\frac{h c}{\lambda_3}=E_0 z^2\left(\frac{1}{n_B^2}-\frac{1}{n_A^2}\right)$
Clearly subtracting equation (ii) from equation (i)
$\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}$

### Q12. JEE Main 2024 (04 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `1`

A hydrogen atom changes its state from $n=3$ to $n=2$. Due to recoil, the percentage change in the wave length of emitted light is approximately $1 \times 10^{-n}$. The value of $n$ is_____. 
[Given $\mathrm{Rhc}=13.6 \mathrm{eV}, \mathrm{hc}=1242 \mathrm{eV} \mathrm{nm}, \mathrm{h}=6.6 \times 10^{-34} \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~s}$ mass of the hydrogenatom $=1.6 \times 10^{-27} \mathrm{~kg}$ ]

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **7**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& \Delta \mathrm{E}=13.6\left(\frac{1}{2^2}-\frac{1}{3^2}\right)=1.9 \mathrm{eV} \\
& \Delta \mathrm{E}=\frac{\mathrm{hc}}{\lambda} \\
& \lambda=\frac{\mathrm{hc}}{\Delta \mathrm{E}} \\
& \mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{t}} \\
& 0=-\mathrm{mv}+\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\lambda^{\prime \prime}} \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{v}=\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{m} \lambda^{\prime}} \\
& \Delta \mathrm{E}=\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{mv^{2 }}+\frac{\mathrm{hc}}{\lambda^{\prime \prime}} \\
& =\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m}\left(\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{m} \lambda^{\prime}}\right)^2+\frac{\mathrm{hc}}{\lambda^{\prime}}
\end{aligned}$
Now
$\begin{aligned}
& \Delta \mathrm{E}=\frac{h^2}{2 \mathrm{~m} \lambda^{\prime^2}}+\frac{\mathrm{hc}}{\lambda^{\prime}} \\
& \lambda^{\prime 2} \Delta \mathrm{E}-\mathrm{hc} \lambda^{\prime}-\frac{\mathrm{h}^2}{2 \mathrm{~m}}=0 \\
& \lambda^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{hc} \pm \sqrt{\mathrm{h}^2 \mathrm{c}^2+\frac{4 \Delta \mathrm{Eh}^2}{2 \mathrm{~m}}}}{2 \Delta \mathrm{E}} \\
& \lambda^{\prime}=\frac{\mathrm{hc} \pm \mathrm{hc} \sqrt{1+\frac{2 \Delta \mathrm{E}}{\mathrm{mc}}}}{2 \Delta \mathrm{E}}
\end{aligned}$
$\begin{aligned}
& \frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}=\frac{1+\left(1+\frac{2 \Delta \mathrm{E}}{\mathrm{mc}^2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2}=\frac{1+1+\frac{\Delta \mathrm{E}}{\mathrm{mc}^2}}{2} \\
& \frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}=1+\frac{\Delta \mathrm{E}}{2 \mathrm{mc}^2} \\
& \frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{\lambda}=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{E}}{2 \mathrm{mc}^2}=\frac{1.9 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}}{2 \times 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 9 \times 10^{15}}=10^{-9} \\
& \therefore \% \text { change } \approx 10^{-7}
\end{aligned}$
Correct answer 7

</details>
