### Q1. BITSAT 2022

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $a_1, a_2, a_3 \ldots$ be a harmonic progression with $a_1=5$ and $a_{20}=25$. The least positive integer $n$ for which $a_n < 0$, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 22
- **(B)** 23
- **(C)** 24
- **(D)** 25

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 25**

**Detailed Solution:**

It is given that $a_1, a_2, a_3 \ldots$. are in HP. Therefore, $\frac{1}{a_1}, \frac{1}{a_2}, \frac{1}{a_3}, \ldots \ldots .$. are in AP. Let $d$ be the common difference of the AP.
$\therefore \quad \frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{1}{a_1}+(n-1) d$ and $\frac{1}{a_{20}}=\frac{1}{a_1}+19 d$
$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{1}{5}+(n-1) d$ and $\frac{1}{25}=\frac{1}{5}+19 d$
$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{1}{5}+(n-1) d$ and $d=\frac{-4}{19 \times 25}$
$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{1}{5}-\frac{4(n-1)}{19 \times 25}$
$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{1}{a_n}=\frac{95-4 n+4}{19 \times 25}$
$\Rightarrow \quad a_n=\frac{99-4 n}{19 \times 25}$
Now, $a_n < 0$
$\Rightarrow \quad \frac{99-4 n}{19 \times 25} < 0$
$\Rightarrow \quad 99-4 n < 0$
$\Rightarrow \quad 4 n>99$
$\Rightarrow \quad n>24 \frac{3}{4} \Rightarrow n \geq 25$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2022

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $a_1, a_2, \ldots \ldots a_{40}$ be in AP and $h_1, h_2, \ldots . h_{10}$ be in HP. If $a_1=h_1=2$ and $a_{10}=h_{10}=3$, then $a_4 h_7$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** 3
- **(C)** 5
- **(D)** 6

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 6**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $d$ be the common difference of the AP. Then,
$\begin{aligned}
& a_{10}=3 \Rightarrow a_1+9 d=3 \\
\Rightarrow & 2+9 d=3 \Rightarrow d=\frac{1}{9} \\
\therefore & a_4=a_1+3 d=2+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{7}{3}
\end{aligned}$
Let $D$ be the common difference of $\frac{1}{h_1}, \frac{1}{h_2}, \ldots .-\frac{1}{h_{10}}$.
Then, $\quad h_{10}=3$
$\begin{array}{ll}
\Rightarrow & \frac{1}{h_{10}}=\frac{1}{3} \Rightarrow \frac{1}{2}+9 D=\frac{1}{3} \\
\Rightarrow & 9 D=-\frac{1}{6} \Rightarrow D=-\frac{1}{54} \\
\therefore & \frac{1}{h_7}=\frac{1}{h_1}+6 D=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{9}=\frac{7}{18} \\
\Rightarrow & h_7=\frac{18}{7} \\
\therefore & a_4 h_7=\frac{7}{3} \times \frac{18}{7}=6
\end{array}$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If binomial coefficients of three consecutive terms of $(1+x)^{2}$ are in $H P$, then the maximum value of $\mathrm{n}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1
- **(B)** 2
- **(C)** 0
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) None of these**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the coefficients of rth, $(r+1)$ th, and (r $+$

2) th terms be in HP.

Then, $\frac{2}{{ }^{n} C_{r}}=\frac{1}{{ }^{n} C_{r-1}}+\frac{1}{{ }^{n} C_{r}+1}$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\Rightarrow \quad 2=\frac{{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}}{{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}-1}}=\frac{{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}}{{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}+1}} \\

\Rightarrow \quad 2=\frac{\mathrm{n}-\mathrm{r}+1}{\mathrm{r}}+\frac{\mathrm{r}+1}{\mathrm{n}-\mathrm{r}} \\

\Rightarrow \mathrm{n}^{2}-4 \mathrm{nr}+4 \mathrm{r}^{2}+\mathrm{n}=0 \\

\Rightarrow(\mathrm{n}-2 \mathrm{r})^{2}+\mathrm{n}=0

\end{array}

$$

which is not possible for any value for $n$.

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the $(2 \mathrm{p})^{\text {th }}$ term of a $\mathrm{H}$. $\mathrm{P}$. is $\mathrm{q}$ and the $(2 \mathrm{q})^{\text {th }}$ term is $\mathrm{p},$ then the $2(\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q})^{\mathrm{th}}$ term is-

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{p q}{2(p+q)}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2 \mathrm{pq}}{\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{pq}}{\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q}}{\mathrm{pq}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q}}{\mathrm{pq}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

If a is the first term and d is the common difference of the associated A.P. $\frac{1}{\mathrm{q}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}}+(2 \mathrm{p}-1) \mathrm{d}, \frac{1}{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}}+(2 \mathrm{q}-1) \mathrm{d}$

$\Rightarrow \mathrm{d}=\frac{1}{2 \mathrm{pq}}$

If h is the $2(\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q})^{\mathrm{th}}$ term $\frac{1}{\mathrm{~h}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}}+(2 \mathrm{p}+2 \mathrm{q}-1) \mathrm{d}$

$=\frac{1}{\mathrm{q}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{\mathrm{p}+\mathrm{q}}{\mathrm{pq}}$

</details>

### Q6. BITSAT 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If \(\mathrm{p}^{\text {th }}, \mathrm{q}^{\text {th }}\) and \(\mathrm{r}^{\text {th }}\) terms of H.P. are \(\mathrm{u}, \mathrm{v}, \mathrm{w}\) respectively, then find the value of the expression \((q-r) v w+(r-p) w u+(p-q) u v\).

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** 0
- **(C)** 4
- **(D)** 8

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 0**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let H.P. be \(\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{d}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}+2 \mathrm{~d}}+\ldots \ldots\)
\(\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \mathrm{u}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{p}-1) \mathrm{d}}, \mathrm{v}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{q}-1) \mathrm{d}}, \\
& \mathrm{w}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{r}-1) \mathrm{d}} \Rightarrow \mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{p}-1) \mathrm{d}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{u}} \\
& \mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{q}-1) \mathrm{d}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{v}}, \mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{r}-1) \mathrm{d}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{w}} \\
& \Rightarrow(\mathrm{q}-\mathrm{r})\{\mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{p}-1) \mathrm{d}\}+(\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{p})\{\mathrm{a}+(\mathrm{q}-1) \mathrm{d}\}+\ldots \ldots \\
& =\frac{1}{\mathrm{u}}(\mathrm{q}-\mathrm{r})+\frac{1}{\mathrm{v}}(\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{p})+\ldots . \Rightarrow(q-r) \mathrm{vw}+\ldots . . = 0
\end{aligned}\)

</details>
