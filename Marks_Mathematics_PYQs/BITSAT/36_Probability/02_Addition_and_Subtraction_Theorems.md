### Q1. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\mathrm{E}$ and $\mathrm{F}$ are events such that $0 < \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{F}) < 1$, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $P(E \mid F)+P(\bar{E} \mid F)=1$
- **(B)** $P(E \mid F)+P(E \mid \bar{F})=1$
- **(C)** $P(\bar{E} \mid F)+P(E \mid \bar{F})=1$
- **(D)** $P(E \mid \bar{F})+P(\bar{E} \mid \bar{F})=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $P(E \mid F)+P(\bar{E} \mid F)=1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$P(E \mid F)+P(\bar{E} \mid F)$
$=\frac{P(E \cap F)+P(\bar{E} \cap F)}{P(F)}=\frac{P((E \cup \bar{E}) \cap F)}{P(F)}=\frac{P(F)}{P(F)}=1$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive events and if $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B})=\frac{1}{3}, \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B})=\frac{13}{21}$, then $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1/7
- **(B)** 4/7
- **(C)** 2/7
- **(D)** 5/7

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 2/7**

**Detailed Solution:**

For mutually exclusive events
$\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B})=\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})+\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B}) \Rightarrow \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})=\frac{2}{7}$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The probability of simultaneous occurrence of atleast one of two events $A$ and $B$ is $p .$ If the probability that exactly one of $A, B$ occurs is $q$ then $P\left(A^{\prime}\right)+P\left(B^{\prime}\right)$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2-2 p+q$
- **(B)** $2+2 p-q$
- **(C)** $3-3 p+q$
- **(D)** $2-p+q$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2-2 p+q$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since, $P($ exactly one of $A, B$ occurs $)=q$.
$\therefore \quad P(A \cup B)-P(A \cap B)=q$
$\Rightarrow \quad p-P(A \cap B)=q \Rightarrow P(A \cap B)=p-q$
$\Rightarrow \quad 1-P\left(A^{\prime} \cup B^{\prime}\right)=p-q \Rightarrow P\left(A^{\prime} \cup B^{\prime}\right)=1-p+q$
$\Rightarrow \quad P\left(A^{\prime}\right)+P\left(B^{\prime}\right)-P\left(A^{\prime} \cap B^{\prime}\right)=1-p+q$
$\Rightarrow \quad P\left(A^{\prime}\right)+P\left(B^{\prime}\right)=(1-p+q)+[1-P(A \cup B)]$
$=(1-p+q)+(1-p)=2-2 p+q$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A die is loaded such that the probability of throwing the number \(i\) is proportional to its reciprocal. The probability that 3 appears in a single throw is-

**Options:**

- **(A)** \(3 / 22\)
- **(B)** \(3 / 11\)
- **(C)** \(9 / 22\)
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) None of these**

**Detailed Solution:**

\(\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{i})=\frac{\mathrm{k}}{\mathrm{i}} \Rightarrow \mathrm{1}=\sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^6 \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{i})=\mathrm{k} \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^6 \frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}=\mathrm{k} \frac{49}{20} \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{k}=\frac{20}{49} . \mathrm{P}(3)=\frac{20}{147}
\end{aligned}\)

</details>
