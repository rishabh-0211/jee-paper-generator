### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The probability of getting 10 in a single throw of three fair dice is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{6}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{8}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{9}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{5}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{8}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Total outcomes $=216$
Now, number of cases of getting 10 from 3 dices in single throw are
Case $1:~1+3+6 \rightarrow$ outcomes $=3!=6$
Case $2:~1+4+5 \rightarrow$ outcomes $=3$ ! $=6$
Case $3:~2+2+6 \rightarrow$ outcomes $=\frac{3!}{2!}=3$
Case $4:~2+3+5 \rightarrow$ outcomes $=3!=6$
Case $5:~2+4+4 \rightarrow$ outcomes $=\frac{3!}{2!}=3$
Case $6:~3+3+4 \rightarrow$ outcomes $=\frac{3!}{2!}=3$
Favourable outcomes $=27$
$\therefore$ Probability $=\frac{27}{216}=\frac{1}{8}$
$\begin{aligned}

\Rightarrow & & x & =20
\end{aligned}$

### Q2. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A coin is tossed twice. Then, the probability that atleast one tail occurs is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{4}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{3}{4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{3}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The sample space is $\mathrm{S}=\{\mathrm{HH}, \mathrm{HT}, \mathrm{TH}, \mathrm{TT}\}$
Let $\mathrm{E}$ be the event of getting atleast one tail
$\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \mathrm{E}=\{\mathrm{HT}, \mathrm{TH}, \mathrm{TT}\} \\
& \therefore \text { Required probability } \mathrm{p} \\
& =\frac{\text { Number of favourable outcomes }}{\text { Total number of outcomes }}=\frac{\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{E})}{\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{S})}=\frac{3}{4}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The probability that a card drawn from a pack of 52 cards will be a diamond or king is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{52}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2}{13}$
- **(C)** $\frac{4}{13}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{13}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{4}{13}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Total no. of cards $=52$
13 cards are diamonds and 4 cards are king.
There is only one card which is a king of diamond.
$\therefore \mathrm{P}($ card is diamond $)=\frac{13}{52}, \mathrm{P}$ (card is king)
$=\frac{4}{52}$
$\mathrm{P}($ card is king of diamond $)=\frac{1}{52}$
$\therefore \quad P$ (card is diamond or king)
$=\frac{13}{52}+\frac{4}{52}-\frac{1}{52}=\frac{16}{52}=\frac{4}{13}$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The probability of getting sum more than 7 when a pair of dice are thrown is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{7}{36}$
- **(B)** $\frac{5}{12}$
- **(C)** $\frac{7}{12}$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{5}{12}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Here $n(S)=6^2=36$
Let $\mathrm{E}$ be the event "getting sum more than 7" i.e. sum of pair of dice $=8,9,10,11,12$
i.e. $E=\left\{\begin{array}{lllll}(2,6) & (3,5) & (4,4) & (5,3) & (6,2) \\ (3,6) & (4,5) & (5,4) & (6,3) \\ (4,6) & (5,5) & (6,4) & \\ (5,6) & (6,5) & (6,6) & \end{array}\right\}$
$\therefore \quad n(E)=15 . \therefore$ Req. prob $=\frac{n(E)}{n(S)}=\frac{15}{36}=\frac{5}{12}$

### Q8. BITSAT 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A boy is throwing stones at a target. The probability of hitting the target at any trial is $\frac{1}{2}$ The probability of hitting the target 5 th time at the 10 th throw is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{5}{2^{10}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{63}{2^{9}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{10}{2^{10}}$
- **(D)** None

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{63}{2^{9}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The probability of hitting the target 5 th time at the 10 th throw $=\mathrm{P}($ the probability of hitting the target 4 times in the first 9 throws) $\mathrm{P}($ the probability of hitting the target at the 10 th throw $)=$
$\left[{ }^{9} \mathrm{C}_{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{5}\right]\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{9 !}{4 ! 5 !} \times\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{10}=\frac{63}{2^{9}}$

</details>
