### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The function $f: \boldsymbol{R} \rightarrow \boldsymbol{R}$ defined by $f(x)=$ $\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** surjective but not injective
- **(B)** bijective
- **(C)** injective but not surjective
- **(D)** neither injective nor surjective

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) injective but not surjective**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given that, $f(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$
For injective: Let $x_1, x_2 \in \boldsymbol{R}$ such that $f\left(x_1\right)=f\left(x_2\right)$
$\Rightarrow \frac{x_1}{\sqrt{1+x_1^2}}=\frac{x_2}{\sqrt{1+x_2^2}} \Rightarrow \frac{x_1^2}{1+x_1^2}=\frac{x_2^2}{1+x_2^2}$
$\Rightarrow x_1^2+x_1^2 x_2^2=x_2^2+x_1^2 x_2^2 \Rightarrow x_1^2=x_2^2$
$\Rightarrow x_1=x_2$
So, $f(x)$ is injective.
For surjective: Let $y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}$
$\Rightarrow y^2\left(1+x^2\right)=x^2 \Rightarrow y^2+y^2 x^2=x^2$
$\Rightarrow x^2\left(1-y^2\right)=y^2 \Rightarrow x=\sqrt{\frac{y^2}{1-y^2}}$
$\Rightarrow \frac{y^2}{1-y^2} \geq 0$

$\therefore y \in(-1,1)$
So, $f(x)$ is not surjective.

### Q2. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$
- **(B)** $12$
- **(C)** $8$
- **(D)** $14$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $8$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$f(x)=\frac{x+3}{x-2}$
$\because \quad f(x)$ is not defined for $x=2$
i.e. domain of $f(x)$ is $\mathrm{R}-\{2\}$
$\therefore l=2$
Now, $y=\frac{x+3}{x-2}$
$x y-2 y=x+3$
$x(y-1)=2 y+3$
$x=\frac{2 y+3}{y-1}$
$y$ can take any value except $y=1$
Co-domain $=\mathrm{R}-\{1\}$
$m=1$
$3 l+2 m=3(2)+2(1)=8$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Let $\mathrm{f}: \mathrm{R} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}$ be a function defined by

$f(x)=\frac{x-m}{x-n},$ where $m \neq n,$ then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{f}$ is one-one onto
- **(B)** $\mathrm{f}$ is one-one into
- **(C)** $\mathrm{f}$ is many-one onto
- **(D)** fis many-one into

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{f}$ is one-one into**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $f: R \rightarrow R$ be a function defined by

$f(x)=\frac{x-m}{x-n}$

For any $(\mathrm{x}, \mathrm{y}) \in \mathrm{R}$

Let $\mathrm{f}(\mathrm{x})=\mathrm{f}(\mathrm{y})$

$\Rightarrow \frac{x-m}{x-n}=\frac{y-m}{y-n} \Rightarrow x=y$

$\therefore \mathrm{f}$ is one $-$ one

Let $\alpha \in \mathrm{R}$ such that $\mathrm{f}(\mathrm{x})=\alpha$

$$

\Rightarrow \alpha=\frac{x-m}{x-n} \Rightarrow(x-n) \alpha=x-m

$$

$\Rightarrow x \alpha-n \alpha=x-m$

$$

\Rightarrow x \alpha-x=n \alpha-m

$$

$\Rightarrow x(\alpha-1)=n \alpha-m$

$\Rightarrow \mathrm{x}=\frac{\mathrm{n} \alpha-\mathrm{m}}{\alpha-1} \cdot$ for $\alpha=1, \mathrm{x} \notin \mathrm{R}$

So, $\mathrm{f}$ is not onto.

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A black die and a white die are rolled. Find the probability that the number shown by the black die will be more than twice that shown by the white die.

**Options:**

- **(A)** \(1 / 8\)
- **(B)** \(1 / 6\)
- **(C)** \(1 / 3\)
- **(D)** \(1 / 4\)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) \(1 / 6\)**

**Detailed Solution:**

The number of favourable cases are shown below:
\(\begin{array}{cl}\text { Number on white die } & \text { Number on black die } \\ 1 & 3 \\ 1 & 4 \\ 1 & 5 \\ 1 & 6 \\ 2 & 5 \\ 2 & 6\end{array}\)
There are 6 favourable cases in which the number on black die is more than twice the number on the white die.
\(\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \mathrm{m}=6 \\
& \mathrm{n}=\text { Total number of cases }=6 \times 6
\end{aligned}\)
( \(\therefore\) with each die there are six possibilities)
\(\therefore\) Probability \(\mathrm{p}=\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{n}}=\frac{6}{6 \times 6}=\frac{1}{6}\)

</details>
