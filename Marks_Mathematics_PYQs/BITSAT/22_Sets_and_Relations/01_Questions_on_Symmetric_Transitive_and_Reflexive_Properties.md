### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Let $R$ be the relation "is congruent to" on the set of all triangles in a plane is

**Options:**

- **(A)** Reflexive only
- **(B)** Symmetric only
- **(C)** Symmetric and reflexive only
- **(D)** Equivalence relation

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Equivalence relation**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $S$ denote the set of all triangles in a plane.
Let $R$ be the relation on $S$ defined by $\left(\Delta_1, \Delta_2\right) \in$ $R$
$\Rightarrow$ triangle $\Delta_1 \cong \Delta_2$ :
(i) Let any triangle $\Delta \in S$, we have
$\Delta_1 \cong \Delta_2 \Rightarrow(\Delta, \Delta) \in R \forall \Delta \in S \Rightarrow R$ is reflexive on
(ii) Let $\Delta_1, \Delta_2 \in \mathrm{~S}$, such that $\left(\Delta_1, \Delta_2\right) \in R$, then $\Delta_1 \cong \Delta_2 \Rightarrow \Delta_2 \cong \Delta_1 \Rightarrow\left(\Delta_2, \Delta_1\right) \in R \Rightarrow R$ is symmÄ™tric
(iii) Again, let $\Delta_1, \Delta_2, \Delta_3 \in \mathrm{~S}$ such that $\left(\Delta_1, \Delta_2\right)$ $\in R$ and
$\left(\Delta_2, \Delta_3\right) \in R \therefore \Delta_1 \cong \Delta_2 \cong \Delta_3$
$\therefore\left(\Delta_1, \Delta_2\right) \in R$
$\Rightarrow R$ is transitive.
$\therefore \mathrm{R}$ is an equivalence relation.

### Q3. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $\rho=\left\{(x, y) \mid x^{2}+y^{2}=1 ; x, y \in \mathbb{R}\right\}$. Then, $\rho$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** reflexive
- **(B)** symmetric
- **(C)** transitive
- **(D)** anti-symmetric

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) symmetric**

**Detailed Solution:**

Obviously, the relation is not reflexive and transitive, but it is symmetric, because $x^{2}+x^{2}=2 x^{2} \neq 1$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\text { and } x^{2}+y^{2}=1, y^{2}+z^{2}=1 \\

\Rightarrow x^{2}+z^{2}=1 \\

\text { But } x^{2}+y^{2}=1 \Rightarrow y^{2}+x^{2}=1

\end{array}

$$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Let $R$ be the relation on the set $R$ of all real numbers, defined by $a R b$ If $|a-b| \leq 1$. Then, $R$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** reflexive and symmetric only
- **(B)** reflexive and transitive only
- **(C)** equivalence
- **(D)** None of the above

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) reflexive and symmetric only**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since, $|a-a|=0 \leq 1,$ so $a R a, \forall a \in R$ $\therefore R$ is reflexive. Now, $a R b \Rightarrow|a-b| \leq 1 \Rightarrow|b-a| \leq 1 \Rightarrow b R a$

$\therefore R$ is symmetric. But $R$ is not transitive as

$1 R 2,2 R 3$ but $1 \not R^{\prime} 3$

$$

[\because|1-3|=2>1]

$$

</details>
