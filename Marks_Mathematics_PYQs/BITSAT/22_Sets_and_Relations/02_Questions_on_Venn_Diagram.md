### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In a statistical investigation of 1003 families of Calcutta, it was found that 63 families has neither a radio nor a TV, 794 families has a radio and 187 has TV. The number of families in that group having both a radio and a TV is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 36
- **(B)** 41
- **(C)** 32
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 41**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
\text {  Given, } n(R) & =794 \\
n(T) & =187 \\
n(R \cup T)^{\prime} & =63 \\
n(\mathrm{Total}) & =n(R \cup T)+n(R \cup T)^{\prime} \\
\Rightarrow \quad 1003 & =n(R \cup T)+63 \\
\Rightarrow \quad n(R \cup T) & =940
\end{aligned}$
By set theory
$\begin{array}{lc}
\Rightarrow & n(R \cup T)=n(R)+n(T)-n(R \cap T) \\
\Rightarrow & 940=794+187-n(R \cap T) \\
\Rightarrow & n(R \cap T)=981-940=41
\end{array}$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A set $A$ has 3 elements and another set B has 6 elements. Then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 \leq \mathrm{n}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B}) \leq 6$
- **(B)** $3 \leq \mathrm{n}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B}) \leq 9$
- **(C)** $6 \leq \mathrm{n}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B}) \leq 9$
- **(D)** $0 \leq n(A \cup B) \leq 9$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $6 \leq \mathrm{n}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B}) \leq 9$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We have
$\min n(A \cup B)=\max \{n(A), n(B)\}=\max \{3,6\}=6$
$\operatorname{Max} n(A \cup B)=n(A)+n(B)=9$
$\therefore 6 \leq n(A \cup B) \leq 9$

</details>

**Options:**

- **(A)** 36
- **(B)** 41
- **(C)** 32
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 41**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/9Jni0yQvN0bRVYO4WI8L2yws0JP2EPtvxPPdAPz7vIs.original.fullsize.png">


Let $\mathrm{R}$ be the set of families having a radio and $T$ the set families having a T.V. then $\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{R} \cup \mathrm{T})=$ The number of families having at least on of the radio and $\mathrm{T} \mathrm{V}=1003-63=940$ $n(\mathrm{R})=794$ and $\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{T})=187$

Let $x$ families have both a radio and a T.V. Then number of families who have only radio $=$ $794-x$

And the number of families who have only T.V. $=187-x$

Erom Venn diagram, $794-x+x-187-x=940$ $\Rightarrow 981-x=940$ or $x=981-940=41$

Hence, the required number of families having both a radio and a T.V.=41

### Q7. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Universal set,
$U=\left\{x \mid x^{5}-6 x^{4}+11 x^{3}-6 x^{2}=0\right\}$
 $A=\left\{x \mid x^{2}-5 x+6=0\right\}$  $\mathrm{B}=\left\{\mathrm{x} \mid \mathrm{x}^{2}-3\mathrm{x}+2=0\right\}$

What is $(A \cap B)^{\prime}$ equal to ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** {1,3}
- **(B)** {1,2,3}
- **(C)** {0,1,3}
- **(D)** {0,1,2,3}

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) {0,1,3}**

**Detailed Solution:**

$U=\left\{x: x^{5}-6 x^{4}+11 x^{3}-6 x^{2}=0\right\}$

Solving for values of $x$, we get $\mathrm{U}=\{0,1,2,3\}$

$\mathrm{A}=\left\{\mathrm{x}: \mathrm{x}^{2}-5 \mathrm{x}+6=0\right\}$

Solving for values of $x$, we get $\mathrm{A}=\{2,3\}$

and $\mathrm{B}=\left\{\mathrm{x}: \mathrm{x}^{2}-3 \mathrm{x}+2=0\right\}$

Solving for values of $x$, we get $\mathrm{B}=\{2,1\}$

$A \cap B=\{2\}$

$\therefore(A \cap B)^{\prime}=U-(A \cap B)$

$=\{0,1,2,3\}-\{2\}=\{0,1,3\}$

</details>

### Q8. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The set $(A \backslash B) \cup(B \backslash A)$ is equal to]

**Options:**

- **(A)** $[A \backslash(A \cap B)] \cap[B \backslash(A \cap B)]$
- **(B)** $(A \cup B) \backslash(A \cap B)$
- **(C)** $A \backslash(A \cap B)$
- **(D)** $\overrightarrow{A \cap B} \backslash A \cup B$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $(A \cup B) \backslash(A \cap B)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given set can be written as $(A-B) \cup(B-A)=(A \cup B)-(A \cap B)$

(By definition of symmetric difference) Hence, $(\mathrm{A} \backslash \mathrm{B}) \cup(\mathrm{B} \backslash \mathrm{A})=(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B}) \backslash(\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B})$

</details>
