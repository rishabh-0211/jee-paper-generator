### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The foci of hyperbola $4 x^2-9 y^2-1=0$ are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $( \pm \sqrt{13}, 0)$
- **(B)** $\left( \pm \frac{\sqrt{13}}{6}, 0\right)$
- **(C)** $\left(0, \pm \frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}\right)$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\left( \pm \frac{\sqrt{13}}{6}, 0\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given, Hyperbola $=4 x^2-9 y^2-1=0$
$\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow \frac{x^2}{\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)}-\frac{y^2}{\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)}=1 \\
& \Rightarrow \frac{x^2}{\frac{1}{2}^2}-\frac{y^2}{\frac{1}{3}^2}=1
\end{aligned}$
We know that. if hyperbola is $\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$. then Eccentricity: 
$e=\sqrt{1+\frac{b^2}{a^2}}$
$\begin{aligned}
& \text {and focus } \equiv( \pm \text{be}, 0)=\left( \pm \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{\sqrt{1 / 3}}{3}, 0\right)=\left( \pm \frac{\sqrt{13}}{6}, 0\right)
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the eccentricity and length of latus rectum of a hyperbola are $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$ and $\frac{10}{3}$ units respectively, then what is the length of the transverse axis?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{7}{2}$ unit
- **(B)** 12 unit
- **(C)** $\frac{15}{2}$ unit
- **(D)** $\frac{15}{4}$ unit

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{15}{4}$ unit**

**Detailed Solution:**

Length of latus rectum of a hyperbola is $\frac{2 b^2}{a}$ where $a$ is the half of the distance between two vertex of the hyperbola.
Latus rectum $=\frac{2 b^2}{a}=\frac{10}{3}$ or, $b^2=\frac{5 a}{3}$ ...(i)
In case of hyperbola, $\mathrm{b}^2=\mathrm{a}^2\left(\mathrm{e}^2-1\right)$ ...(ii)
Putting value of $b^2$ from equation (i) and $e=$
$\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$ in equation (ii), $\frac{5 \mathrm{a}}{3}=\mathrm{a}^2\left(\frac{13}{9}-1\right)$ or,
$\frac{5 a}{3}=\frac{4 a^2}{9}$
$\Rightarrow 4 a^2-15 a=0$ or, $a(4-15 a)=0$
$a \neq 0$, hence, $a=\frac{15}{4}$

</details>
