### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\tan 15^{\circ}$ and $\tan 30^{\circ}$ are the roots of the equation
$x^2+p x+q=0$, then $p q=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{6 \sqrt{3}+10}{\sqrt{3}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{10-6 \sqrt{3}}{3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{10+6 \sqrt{3}}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{10-6 \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{10-6 \sqrt{3}}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\because \quad \tan 15^{\circ}=\tan (45-30)^{\circ}=\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{3}+1}$
$\therefore \quad \tan 15^{\circ}+\tan 30^{\circ}=-p$
$\Rightarrow \quad p=\frac{-4}{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3}+1)}$
$q=\tan 15^{\circ} \times \tan 30^{\circ}$
$=\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3}+1)} \Rightarrow q=\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3}+1)}$
$\therefore p \cdot q=\frac{-4}{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3}+1)} \times \frac{(\sqrt{3}-1)}{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3}+1)}$
$\Rightarrow \quad p \cdot q=\frac{-4(\sqrt{3}-1)}{3(\sqrt{3}+1)^2}=\frac{10-6 \sqrt{3}}{3}$
$\Rightarrow p \cdot q=\frac{10-6 \sqrt{3}}{3}$
$\Rightarrow$ Option (2) is correct.

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

The roots of the given equation $(p-q) x^2+(q-r) x+(r-p)=0$ are :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{p-q}{r-p}, 1$
- **(B)** $\frac{q-r}{p-q}, 1$
- **(C)** $\frac{\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{p}-\mathrm{q}}, 1$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{\mathrm{r}-\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{p}-\mathrm{q}}, 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation is
$(p-q) x^2+(q-r) x+(r-p)=0$
By using formula for finding the roots viz: $\frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4 a c}}{2 a}$, we get
$\begin{aligned} & x=\frac{(r-q) \pm \sqrt{(q-r)^2-4(r-p)(p-q)}}{2(p-q)} \\ & \Rightarrow x=\frac{(r-q) \pm(q+r-2 p)}{2(p-q)}=\frac{r-p}{p-q}, 1\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

Solution of $2^x+2^{|x|} \geq 2 \sqrt{2}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(-\infty, \log _2(\sqrt{2}+1)\right)$
- **(B)** $(0, \infty)$
- **(C)** $\left(\frac{1}{2}, \log _2(\sqrt{2}-1)\right)$
- **(D)** $\left(-\infty, \log _2(\sqrt{2}-1)\right] \cup\left[\frac{1}{2}, \infty\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\left(-\infty, \log _2(\sqrt{2}-1)\right] \cup\left[\frac{1}{2}, \infty\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$2^x+2^{|x|} \geq 2 \sqrt{2}$ ...(i)
Case I: $x \geq 0$, then Eq. (i) becomes
$2^x+2^x \geq 2 \sqrt{2}$
$\Rightarrow 2^x \geq \sqrt{2} \Rightarrow x \geq \frac{1}{2}$
Case II: $x < 0$, then Eq. (i) becomes
$2^x+2^{-x} \geq 2 \sqrt{2}$
$\Rightarrow t+\frac{1}{t} \geq 2 \sqrt{2}$, where $2^x=t$
$\Rightarrow t^2-2 \sqrt{2} t+1 \geq 0$
$\Rightarrow x \leq \log _2(\sqrt{2}-1)$
Also, $0 < \sqrt{2}-1 < 1, \log _2(\sqrt{2}-1) < 0$.
$\therefore$ The solution is
$\left.\left(-\infty, \log _2(\sqrt{2}-1)\right)\right] \cup\left[\frac{1}{2}, \infty\right)$

### Q8. BITSAT 2022

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $\alpha$ be a root of the equation $4 x^2+2 x-1=0$, then the other root of the equation is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4 \alpha^3+2 \alpha$
- **(B)** $4 \alpha^2-2 \alpha$
- **(C)** $4 \alpha^3-3 \alpha$
- **(D)** $4 \alpha^3+3 \alpha$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4 \alpha^3-3 \alpha$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$4 x^2+2 x-1=0$
$\therefore \quad \alpha+\beta=-\frac{1}{2}, \alpha \beta=-\frac{1}{4}$
Also, $4 \alpha^2+2 \alpha-1=0$ as $\alpha$ is a root and we have to prove that $\beta=4 \alpha^3-3 \alpha$.
Now, $4 \alpha^3-3 \alpha=4 \alpha^2 \cdot \alpha-3 \alpha$
$=\alpha(1-2 \alpha)-3 \alpha=-2 \alpha^2-2 \alpha$
$=-\frac{1}{2}\left[4 \alpha^2+4 \alpha\right]=-\frac{1}{2}[1-2 \alpha+4 \alpha]$
$=-\frac{1}{2}(1+2 \alpha)=-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha=\beta$
Now, $\alpha+\beta=-\frac{1}{2}$
[from Eq. (i)]
Hence, $\beta=4 \alpha^3-3 \alpha$.

</details>

### Q9. BITSAT 2021

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Find the value of $a$ such that the sum of the squares of the roots of the equation $x^{2}-(a-2) x-(a+1)=0$ is least.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 4
- **(B)** 2
- **(C)** 1
- **(D)** 3

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\alpha, \beta$ be the roots of the equation.

$\therefore \alpha+\beta=a-2$ and $\alpha \beta=-(a+1)$

Now, $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=(\alpha+\beta)^{2}-2 \alpha \beta$

$=(a-2)^{2}+2(a+1)=(a-1)^{2}+5$

$\therefore \alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}$ will be minimum if $(a-1)^{2}=0$,

i.e. $a=1$.

</details>
