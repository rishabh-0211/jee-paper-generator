### Q1. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The mean square deviation of a set of n observation $\mathrm{x}_{1}, \mathrm{x}_{2^{2}}, \ldots \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{n}}$ about a point $\mathrm{c}$ is defined as $\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}-c\right)^{2}$

The mean square deviations about -2 and 2 are 18 and 10 respectively, the standard deviation of this set of observations is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 3
- **(B)** 2
- **(C)** 1
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 3**

**Detailed Solution:**

We have $\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}+2\right)^{2}=18$ and

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}-2\right)^{2}=10 \\

\Rightarrow \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}+2\right)^{2}=18 n \text { and }

\end{array}

$$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}-2\right)^{2}=10 n \\

\Rightarrow \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}+2\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}-2\right)^{2}=28 n

\end{array}

$$

and $\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}+2\right)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}-2\right)^{2}=8 n$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\Rightarrow 2 \sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}+4\right)^{2}=28 n \text { and } 2 \sum_{i=1}^{n} 4 x_{i}=8 n \\

\Rightarrow \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{1}^{2}+4 n=14 n \text { and } \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{i}=n \\

\Rightarrow \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{i}^{2}=10 n \text { and } \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{i}=n \\

\therefore \sigma=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{i}^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1^{\prime}}^{n} x_{i}\right)^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{10 n}{n}-\left(\frac{n}{n}\right)^{2}}=3

\end{array}

$$

</details>

### Q2. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The arithmetic mean of the data $0,1,2, \ldots \ldots, n$ with frequencies $1,{ }^{4} \mathrm{C}_{1}{ }^{4} \mathrm{C}_{2}, \ldots . .,{ }^{\infty} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{a}}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{n}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2^{n}}{n}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{n}+1$
- **(D)** $\frac{\mathrm{n}}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\mathrm{n}}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since, Mean $=\frac{\sum \mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{i}} \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}}{\sum \mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{i}}}$ where $\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}$ are

observations with frequencies $\mathrm{f}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{i}=1,2,$ $\ldots \ldots \ldots$

The required mean is given by

$$

\begin{aligned}

\overline{\mathrm{X}} &=\frac{0.1+1 .{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{1}+2{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{2}+\ldots \ldots+\mathrm{n} .{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{n}}}{1+{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{1}+{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{2}+\ldots+{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{n}}} \\

=& \frac{\sum_{\mathrm{r}=0}^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}}{\sum_{\mathrm{r}=0}^{\mathrm{n}}{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}}=\frac{\sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{r} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{n}}{\mathrm{r}}{ }^{\mathrm{n}-1} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}-1}}{\sum_{\mathrm{r}=0}^{\mathrm{n}}{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}} \\

=& \frac{\mathrm{n} \sum{\sum}^{\mathrm{n}-1} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}-1}}{\sum_{\mathrm{r}=0}^{\mathrm{n}}{ }^{\mathrm{n}} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}}=\frac{\mathrm{n} .2^{\mathrm{n}-1}}{2^{\mathrm{n}}}=\frac{\mathrm{n}}{2}

\end{aligned}

$$

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

IfM. D. is 12, the value of S.D. will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** 15
- **(B)** 12
- **(C)** 24
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 15**

**Detailed Solution:**

We know that Q.D $=\frac{5}{6} \times M \cdot D .=\frac{5}{6} \times 12=10$

$\therefore \mathrm{S} . \mathrm{D}=\frac{3}{2} \times Q \cdot D .=\frac{3}{2} \times 10 \Rightarrow S . D .=15$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the value of mode and mean is 60 and 66 respectively, then the value of median is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 70
- **(B)** 64
- **(C)** 90
- **(D)** 50

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 64**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\quad$ Mode $=3$ Median -2 Mean

$\therefore \quad$ Median $=\frac{1}{3}($ mode +2 mean $)=\frac{1}{3}(60+2 \times 66)=64$

</details>

### Q6. BITSAT 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If sample A contains 100 observations $101,102, \ldots$ 200 and sample B contains 100 obsections $151,152,$ \ldots 250, then ratio of variance $\mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{A}} / \mathrm{v}_{\mathrm{B}}=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1
- **(B)** $\frac{9}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{4}{9}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\because \quad \delta_{\mathrm{x}}^{2}=\frac{\Sigma \mathrm{d}^{2} \mathrm{i}}{\mathrm{n}}$
But both $A$ and $B$ have 100 observations, then both the sample $A$ and $B$ have same standard deviation and the same variance.
Hence, $\frac{\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{A}}}{\mathrm{V}_{\mathrm{B}}}=1$

</details>
