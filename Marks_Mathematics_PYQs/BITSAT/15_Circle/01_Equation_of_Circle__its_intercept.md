### Q2. BITSAT 2021

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of the circle, which touches the line $y=5$ and passes through $(-1,2)$ and $(1,2)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9 x^{2}+9 y^{2}-60 y+75=0$
- **(B)** $9 x^{2}+9 y^{2}-60 x-75=0$
- **(C)** $9 x^{2}+9 y^{2}+60 y-75=0$
- **(D)** $9 x^{2}+9 y^{2}+60 x+75=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $9 x^{2}+9 y^{2}-60 y+75=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/qSv62DZUInezs9OtYo4L1dI8LmR24DBWnsy_SoSgY6A.original.fullsize.png">


The centre of the circle is on the perpendicular bisector of the line joining $(-1,2)$ and $(1,2)$, which is the $y$-axis. The ordinate of the centre is given by

$\begin{array}{l}

(5-y)^{2}=1+(y-2)^{2} \\

\Rightarrow y=\frac{10}{3}

\end{array}$

Hence, eq. of the circle is:

$\begin{array}{l}

x^{2}+\left(y-\frac{10}{3}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^{2} \\

\Rightarrow 9 x^{2}+9 y^{2}-60 y+75=0

\end{array}$

</details>

Let $A \equiv(-3,2)$

### Q4. BITSAT 2020

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In the given figure, the equation of the larger circle is $x^{2}+y^{2}+4 y-5=0$ and the distance between centres is 4 . Then the equation of smaller circle is

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/XO5x82sLhpvVZjtM7P65tDrKkKEzFvjh-JRXhpSCYKw.original.fullsize.png">

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(x-\sqrt{7})^{2}+(y-1)^{2}=1$
- **(B)** $\quad(x+\sqrt{7})^{2}+(y-1)^{2}=1$
- **(C)** $x^{2}+y^{2}=2 \sqrt{7} x+2 y$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $(x-\sqrt{7})^{2}+(y-1)^{2}=1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We have $x^{2}+y^{2}+4 y-5=0$. Its centre is $\mathrm{C}_{1}(0,-2)$

$\mathrm{r}_{1}=\sqrt{4+5}=3$. Let $\mathrm{C}_{2}(\mathrm{~h}, \mathrm{k})$ be the centre of the

smaller circle and its radius $\mathrm{r}_{2}$. Then $\mathrm{C}_{1} \mathrm{C}_{2}=4$ $\Rightarrow \sqrt{\mathrm{h}^{2}+(\mathrm{k}+2)^{2}}=3+\mathrm{r}_{2}=4$

$\Rightarrow \mathrm{r}_{2}=1$

But $\mathrm{k}=\mathrm{r}_{2}=1 \quad$ [it touches $\mathrm{x}$ -axis

$\therefore$ From eq $(1), 4=\sqrt{\mathrm{h}^{2}+(1+2)^{2}}$

$\Rightarrow 16=\mathrm{h}^{2}+9 \Rightarrow \mathrm{h}^{2}=7 \Rightarrow \mathrm{h}=\pm \sqrt{7}$

Since $\mathrm{h}>0 \quad \therefore \mathrm{h}=\sqrt{7}$

Hence, required circle is $(x-\sqrt{7})^{2}+(y-1)^{2}=1$

</details>

### Q6. BITSAT 2016

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The number of integral values of $\lambda$ for which $\mathrm{x}^{2}+\mathrm{y}^{2}+\lambda \mathrm{x}+(1-\lambda) \mathrm{y}+5=0$ is the equation of a ircle whose radius cannot exceed $5,$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 14
- **(B)** 18
- **(C)** 16
- **(D)** None

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 16**

**Detailed Solution:**

Radius $\leq 5$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\sqrt{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}+\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{4}-5} \leq 5 \Rightarrow \lambda^{2}+(1-\lambda)^{2}-20 \leq 100 \\

\Rightarrow 2 \lambda^{2}-2 \lambda-119 \leq 0 \\

\therefore \frac{1-\sqrt{239}}{2} \leq \lambda \leq \frac{1+\sqrt{239}}{2} \Rightarrow-7.2 \leq \lambda \leq 8.2 \\

\quad(\text { approx. }) \\

\therefore \lambda=-7,-6,-5, \ldots \ldots .7,8, \text { in all } 16

\end{array}

$$ values

</details>

### Q7. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Area of the circle in which a chord of length $\sqrt{2}$ makes an angle $\pi / 2$ at the centre, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\pi / 2$ sq units
- **(B)** $2 \pi$ sq units
- **(C)** $\pi$ sq units
- **(D)** $\pi / 4 \mathrm{sq}$ units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\pi$ sq units**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\mathrm{AB}$ be the chord of length $\sqrt{2}$. Let $\mathrm{O}$ be the centre of the circle and let $O C$ be the perpendicular from $\mathrm{O}$ on $\mathrm{AB}$.

Then $, \mathrm{AC}=\mathrm{BC}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

In $\Delta \mathrm{OBC},$ we have

$$

\begin{aligned}

\mathrm{OB} &=\mathrm{BC} \operatorname{cosec} 45^{\circ} \\

&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \times \sqrt{2}=1

\end{aligned}

$$

$\therefore$ Area of the circle $=\pi(\mathrm{OB})^{2}=\pi$ sq units
