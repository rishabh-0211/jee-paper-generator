### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The angle between the lines whose direction cosines are given by the equations $3 l+m+5 n=$ $0,6 \mathrm{~nm}-2 \mathrm{n} l+5 l \mathrm{m}=0$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)$
- **(B)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{6}\right)$
- **(C)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)$
- **(D)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(-\frac{5}{6}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\cos ^{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{6}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equations are
$3 l+m+5 n=0$  ...(i)
and $6 \mathrm{mn}-2 \mathrm{n} l+5 l \mathrm{m}=0$   ...(ii)
From (i), we have $m=-3 l-5 n$.
Putting $\mathrm{m}=-3 l-5 \mathrm{n}$ in (ii),
we get $6(-3 l-5 n) n-2 n l+5 l(-3 l-5 n)=0$
$\Rightarrow(n+l)(2 n+l)=0$
$\Rightarrow$ either $l=-\mathrm{n}$ or $l=-2 \mathrm{n}$.
If $l=-n$, then putting $l=-n$ in (i), we obtain $m=-2 n$.
If $l=-2 \mathrm{n}$, then putting $l=-2 \mathrm{n}$ in (i), we obtain $\mathrm{m}=\mathrm{n}$.
Thus, the direction ratios of two lines are $-\mathrm{n},-$ $2 \mathrm{n}, \mathrm{n}$ and $-2 \mathrm{n}, \mathrm{n}, \mathrm{n}$ i.e., $1,2,-1$ and $-2,1,1$.
Hence, the direction cosines are
$\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}, \frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}, \frac{-1}{\sqrt{6}}$ or $\frac{-2}{\sqrt{6}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}$. The angle $\theta$
between the lines is given by
$\begin{aligned}
& \cos \theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \times \frac{-2}{\sqrt{6}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}+\frac{-1}{\sqrt{6}} \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}=\frac{-1}{6} \\
& \Rightarrow \theta=\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{-1}{6}\right)
\end{aligned}$

</details>

A line makes angles of $45^{\circ}$ and $60^{\circ}$ with the positive axes of $X$ and $Y$ respectively. The angle made by the same line with the positive axis of $Z$, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $30^{\circ}$ or $60^{\circ}$
- **(B)** $60^{\circ}$ or $90^{\circ}$
- **(C)** $90^{\circ}$ or $120^{\circ}$
- **(D)** $60^{\circ}$ or $120^{\circ}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $60^{\circ}$ or $120^{\circ}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $\alpha=45^{\circ}, \beta=60^{\circ}, \gamma=?$

$\begin{array}{l}

\because \cos ^{2} \alpha+\cos ^{2} \beta+\cos ^{2} \gamma=1 \\

\therefore \cos ^{2} \gamma=1-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{4} \\

\Rightarrow \mathrm{y}=60^{\circ} \text { or } 120^{\circ}

\end{array}$

### Q6. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A line makes the same angle $\theta$ with each of the $\mathrm{X}$ and $\mathrm{Z}$ -axes. If the angle $\beta$, which it makes with Y-axis, is such that $\sin ^{2} \beta=3 \sin ^{2} \theta,$ then $\cos ^{2} \theta$ equals

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 / 5$
- **(B)** $1 / 5$
- **(C)** $3 / 5$
- **(D)** $2 / 3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3 / 5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $l, \mathrm{~m}$ and $\mathrm{n}$ be the direction cosines. Then, $l=\cos \theta, \mathrm{m}=\cos \beta, \mathrm{n}=\cos \theta$

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\text { we have } l^{2}+m^{2}+n^{2}=1 \\

\Rightarrow \cos ^{2} \theta+\cos ^{2} \beta+\cos ^{2} \theta=1 \\

\Rightarrow 2 \cos ^{2} \theta+1-\sin ^{2} \beta=1 \\

\Rightarrow 2 \cos ^{2} \theta-\sin ^{2} \beta=0 \\

\Rightarrow 2 \cos ^{2} \theta-3 \sin ^{2} \beta=0 \\

\quad\left[\because \sin ^{2} \beta=3 \sin ^{2} 0(\text { given })\right] \\

\Rightarrow \tan ^{2} \theta=2 / 3 \\

\therefore \cos ^{2} \theta=\frac{1}{1+\tan ^{2} \theta}=\frac{1}{1+2 / 3}=\frac{3}{5}

\end{array}

$$

</details>
