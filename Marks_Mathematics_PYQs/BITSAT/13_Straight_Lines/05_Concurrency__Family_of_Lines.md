$-1-\lambda=0$

### Q2. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the lines $\mathrm{p}_{1} \mathrm{x}+\mathrm{q}_{1} \mathrm{y}=1, \mathrm{p}_{2} \mathrm{x}+\mathrm{q}_{2} \mathrm{y}=1$ and $\mathrm{p}_{3} \mathrm{x}+ \mathrm{q}_{3} \mathrm{y}=1$ be concurrent, then the points $\left(\mathrm{p}_{1}, \mathrm{q}_{1}\right),$

$\left(p_{2}, q_{2}\right)$ and $\left(p_{3}, q_{3}\right)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** are collinear
- **(B)** form an equilateral triangle
- **(C)** form a sealene triangle
- **(D)** form a right angled triangle

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) are collinear**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equations of the lines are

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\mathrm{p}_{1} \mathrm{x}+\mathrm{q}_{1} \mathrm{y}-1=0 \\

\mathrm{p}_{2} \mathrm{x}+\mathrm{q}_{2} \mathrm{y}-1=0

\end{array}

$$

and $\mathrm{p}_{3} \mathrm{x}+\mathrm{q}_{3} \mathrm{y}-1=0$

As they are concurrent,

$$

\left|\begin{array}{lll}

\mathrm{p}_{1} & \mathrm{q}_{1} & -1 \\

\mathrm{p}_{2} & \mathrm{q}_{2} & -1 \\

\mathrm{p}_{3} & \mathrm{q}_{3} & -1

\end{array}\right|=0 \Rightarrow\left|\begin{array}{lll}

\mathrm{p}_{1} & \mathrm{q}_{1} & 1 \\

\mathrm{p}_{2} & \mathrm{q}_{2} & 1 \\

\mathrm{p}_{3} & \mathrm{q}_{3} & 1

\end{array}\right|=0

$$

This is also the condition for the points $\left(\mathrm{p}_{1}, \mathrm{q}_{1}\right),\left(\mathrm{p}_{2}, \mathrm{q}_{2}\right)$ and $\left(\mathrm{p}_{3}, \mathrm{q}_{3}\right)$ to be collinear.

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The value of $\lambda$, for which the lines $3 x-4 y=13,8 x-11 y=33$ and $2 x-3 y+\lambda=0$

are concurrent is

**Options:**

- **(A)** -1
- **(B)** -7
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{7}$
- **(D)** 9

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) -7**

**Detailed Solution:**

For concurrency of 3 lines the determinant of coefficients of equations should be 0 .

i.e. $\left|\begin{array}{ccc}3 & -4 & -13 \\ 8 & -11 & -33 \\ 2 & -3 & \lambda\end{array}\right|=0$

$\Rightarrow 3(-11 \lambda-99)+4(8 \lambda+66)-13(-24+22)=0$

$\Rightarrow-33 \lambda-297+32 \lambda+264+312-286=0$

$\Rightarrow-\lambda-583+576=0 \Rightarrow \lambda=-7$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the lines $\ell \mathrm{x}+\mathrm{my}+\mathrm{n}=0, \mathrm{mx}+\mathrm{ny}+\ell=0$ and

$\mathrm{nx}+\ell \mathrm{y}+\mathrm{m}=0$ are concurrent then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\ell+m+n=0$
- **(B)** $\ell-m-n=0$
- **(C)** $\ell+m-n=0$
- **(D)** $m+n-\ell=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\ell+m+n=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since the lines are concurrent, so

$$

\Rightarrow \begin{array}{l}

\left|\begin{array}{lll}

\ell & \mathrm{m} & \mathrm{n} \\

\mathrm{m} & \mathrm{n} & \ell \\

\mathrm{n} & \ell & \mathrm{m}

\end{array}\right|=0 \Rightarrow 3 \ell \mathrm{mn}-\ell^{3}-\mathrm{m}^{3}-\mathrm{n}^{3}=0 \\

(\ell+\mathrm{m}+\mathrm{n})\left(\ell^{2}+\mathrm{m}^{2}+\mathrm{n}^{2}-(\mathrm{m}-\mathrm{mn}-\mathrm{n} \ell)=0\right.

\end{array}

$$

$\Rightarrow \quad \ell+\mathrm{m}+\mathrm{n}=0\left[\because \ell^{2}+\mathrm{m}^{2}+\mathrm{n}^{2}>6 \mathrm{~m}+\mathrm{mn}+\mathrm{n}\right]$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

$\quad$ If $4 a^{2}+b^{2}+2 c^{2}+4 a b-6 a c-3 b c=0,$ the family of lines $\mathrm{ax}+\mathrm{by}+\mathrm{c}=0$ is concurrent at one or the other of the two points-

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(-1,-\frac{1}{2}\right),(-2,-1)$
- **(B)** $(-1,-1),\left(-2,-\frac{1}{2}\right)$
- **(C)** $(-1,2),\left(\frac{1}{2},-1\right)$
- **(D)** $(1,2),\left(\frac{1}{2},-1\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\left(-1,-\frac{1}{2}\right),(-2,-1)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$

\text {  } \begin{array}{l}

4 a^{2}+b^{2}+2 c^{2}+4 a b-6 a c-3 b c \\

\quad \equiv(2 a+b)^{2}-3(2 a+b) c+2 c^{2}=0 \\

\quad \Rightarrow(2 a+b-2 c)(2 a+b-c)=0 \Rightarrow c=2 a+b \\

\quad \text { or } c=a+\frac{1}{2} b

\end{array}

$$

The equation of the family of lines is

$$

a(x+2)+b(y+1)=0 \text { or } a(x+1)+b\left(y+\frac{1}{2}\right)=0

$$

giving the point of concurrence (-2,-1) or

$$

\left(-1,-\frac{1}{2}\right)

$$

</details>

- **(A)** \(\left(\frac{2}{5}, \frac{-14}{5}\right)\)

- **(C)** \(\left(\frac{-2}{5}, \frac{14}{5}\right)\)
- **(D)** \(\left(\frac{2}{5}, \frac{14}{5}\right)\)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) \(\left(\frac{2}{5}, \frac{-14}{5}\right)\)**

**Detailed Solution:**

Rewriting the equation \((2 x+y+2) a+(3 x-y-4) b=0\) and for all \(a\), \(\mathrm{b}\) the straight lines pass through the intersection of \(2 x+y+2=0\) and \(3 x-y-4=0\) i.e., the point \(\left(\frac{2}{5},-\frac{14}{5}\right)\).

</details>

### Q7. BITSAT 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

The reflection of the point \((4,-13)\) in the line \(5 x+y+6=0\), is

**Options:**

- **(A)** \((-1,-14)\)
- **(B)** \((3,4)\)
- **(C)** \((1,2)\)
- **(D)** \((-4,13)\)

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) \((-1,-14)\)**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let \(\mathrm{Q}(a, b)\) be the reflection of \(\mathrm{P}(4,-13)\) in the line \(5 x+y+6=0\)
Then the mid-point \(R\left(\frac{a+4}{2}, \frac{b-13}{2}\right)\) lies on
\(\begin{aligned}
& 5 x+y+6=0 \\
& \therefore 5\left(\frac{a+4}{2}\right)+\frac{b-13}{2}+6=0 \\
& \Rightarrow 5 a+b+19=0 \quad ...(i)
\end{aligned}\)
Also \(\mathrm{PQ}\) is perpendicular to \(5 x+y+6=0\)
Therefore \(\frac{b+13}{a-4} \times\left(-\frac{5}{1}\right)=-1\)
\(\Rightarrow a-5 b-69=0 \quad ...(ii)\)
Solving (i) and (ii), we get \(a=-1, b=-14\)

</details>
