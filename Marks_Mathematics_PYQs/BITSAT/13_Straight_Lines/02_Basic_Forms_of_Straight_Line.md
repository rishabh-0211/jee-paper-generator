### Q1. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $(-4,5)$ is one vertex and $7 x-y+8=0$ is one diagonal of a square, then the equation of second diagonal is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x+3 y=21$
- **(B)** $2 x-3 y=7$
- **(C)** $x+7 y=31$
- **(D)** $2 x+3 y=21$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $x+7 y=31$**

**Detailed Solution:**

One vertex of square is $(-4,5)$ and equation of one diagonal is $7 x-y+8=0$ Diagonal of a square are perpendicular and bisect each other
Let the equation of the other diagonal be $y=m x+$ $c$ where $m$ is the slope of the line and $c$ is the $\mathrm{y}$-intercept.
Since this line passes through $(-4,5)$

Since this line is at right angle to the line
$7 x-y+8=0$ or $y=7 x+8$, having slope $=7$,
$\therefore 7 \times \mathrm{m}=-1$ or $\mathrm{m}=\frac{-1}{7}$
Putting this value of $m$ in equation (i) we get
$5=-4 \times\left(\frac{-1}{7}\right)+c$
or $5=\frac{4}{7}+c \quad$ or $\quad c=5-\frac{4}{7}=\frac{31}{7}$
Hence equation of the other diagonal is
$y=-\frac{1}{7} x+\frac{31}{7}$ or $7 y=-x+31$ or $x+7 y-31=0$ or $x+7 y=31$

### Q2. BITSAT 2023 (Memory Based Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the coordinates of the points $A$ and $B$ be $(3,3)$ and $(7,6)$, then the length of the portion of the line $\mathrm{AB}$ intercepted between the axes is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{5}{4}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\sqrt{10}}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{5}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Equation of line $A B$ is $y-3=\frac{6-3}{7-3}(x-3)$
$\Rightarrow 3 x-4 y+3=0 \Rightarrow \frac{x}{-1}+\frac{y}{3 / 4}=1$
Hence required length is $\sqrt{(-1)^2+\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^2}=\frac{5}{4}$.

</details>

### Q3. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The quadratic equation whose roots are the $\mathrm{x}$ and yintercepts of the line passing through (1,1) and making a triangle of area $\mathrm{A}$ with the co-ordinate axes is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x^{2}+A x+2 A=0$
- **(B)** $x^{2}-2 A x+2 A=0$
- **(C)** $x^{2}-A x+2 A=0$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $x^{2}-2 A x+2 A=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Equation of the line making intercepts a and

$b$ on the axes is $\frac{x}{a}+\frac{y}{b}=1$ Since, it passes through (1,1) $\Rightarrow \frac{1}{\mathrm{a}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{~b}}=1$

Also the area of the triangle formed by the line and the axes is $\mathrm{A}$.

$\therefore \frac{1}{2} \mathrm{ab}=\mathrm{A} \Rightarrow \mathrm{ab}=2 \mathrm{~A}$

From eqs. (i) and (ii), we get, $a+b=2 A$ Hence, a and b are the roots of the eq. $x^{2}-(a+b) x+a b=0 \Rightarrow x^{2}-2 A x+2 A=0$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The \(100^{\text {th }}\) term of the sequence \(1,2,2,3,3,3,4,4\), \(4,4, \ldots\) is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 12
- **(B)** 13
- **(C)** 14
- **(D)** 15

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 14**

**Detailed Solution:**

\(\begin{aligned}
& 1^{\text {st }} \text { term } \rightarrow 1,2^{\text {nd }} \text { term }=2,4^{\text {th }} \text { term } \rightarrow 3, \\
& 7^{\text {th }} \text { term } \rightarrow 4,11^{\text {th }} \text { term } \rightarrow 5, \ldots
\end{aligned}\)
Series is \(1,2,4,7,11, \ldots\)
\(a_n=1+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}=\frac{n^2-n+2}{2}\)
If \(n=14\), then \(a_n=92, \mathrm{If} n=15\), then \(a_n=106\).

</details>
