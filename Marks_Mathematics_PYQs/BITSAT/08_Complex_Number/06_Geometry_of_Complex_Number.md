### Q1. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 3)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The points represented by the complex number $1+i,-2+3 i, \frac{5}{3} i$ on the argand plane are

**Options:**

- **(A)** Vertices of an equilateral triangle
- **(B)** Vertical of an isosceles triangle
- **(C)** Collinear
- **(D)** None of the above

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) Collinear**

**Detailed Solution:**

According to question,
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/_0atlPd0xNFWe4Nv21YtL27zD-stVAdxmilFQ-sRn0Q.original.fullsize.png"/>
Let find slope of $A B, B C$ and $C A$
Slope of $A B=\frac{1-3}{1-(-2)}=\frac{-2}{3}$
Slope of $B C=\frac{3-\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)}{-2-0}=\frac{-2}{3}$
Slope of $C A=\frac{\frac{5}{3}-1}{0-1}=\frac{-2}{3}$
Since, slope of all lines are same, therefore, points are collinear.

### Q2. BITSAT 2024 (Memory Based Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $z, \bar{z},-z,-\bar{z}$ forms a rectangle of area $2 \sqrt{3}$ square units, then one such $z$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{3 i}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3 i}}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3 i}}{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\sqrt{3}+\sqrt{11 i}}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{3 i}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let a complex number, $z=x+i y$
$\Rightarrow \bar{z}=\bar{x}-i y$
Then, vertices of rectangle for $z, \bar{z},-z,-\bar{z}$ are $(x, y),(x,-y),(-x,-y),(-x, y)$
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/bitsat/NFU4Az-iHlpF5xPsxP1ZxykEOeV7R7l72OMXxo4Bn-A.original.fullsize.png"/>
Now, Area of rectangle $=(2 x)(2 y)=4 x y$
It is given that,
Area $=2 \sqrt{3}=4 x y \Rightarrow 2 x y=\sqrt{3}$
$\therefore x=\frac{1}{2}, y=\sqrt{3} \quad \therefore z=\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{3} i$

</details>

### Q4. BITSAT 2015

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If complex number $z_{1}, z_{2}$ and 0 are vertices of equilateral triangle, then $z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}-z_{1} z_{2}$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0$
- **(B)** $z_{1}-z_{2}$
- **(C)** $z_{1}+z_{2}$
- **(D)** 1

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$z_{1}, z_{2}, 0$ are vertices of an equilateral triangle, so we have $z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+0^{2}=z_{1} z_{2}+z_{2} \cdot 0+0 \cdot z_{1}$

$$

\Rightarrow z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}=z_{1} z_{2} \Rightarrow z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}-z_{1} z_{2}=0

$$

</details>

### Q5. BITSAT 2014

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The complex number $z=z+i y$ which satisfies

the equation $\left|\frac{z-3 i}{z+3 i}\right|=1,$ lies on

**Options:**

- **(A)** the $X$ -axis
- **(B)** the straight line $y=3$
- **(C)** a circle passing through origin
- **(D)** None of the above

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) the $X$ -axis**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\left|\frac{z-3 i}{z+3 i}\right|=1 \Rightarrow|z-3 i|=|z+3 i|$

[if $\left|z-z_{1}\right|=\left|z+z_{2}\right|,$ then it is a perpendicular bisector of $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ ] Hence, perpendicular bisector of (0,3) and (0,-3) is $X$ -axis.

</details>

### Q6. BITSAT 2013

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If the real part of $\frac{\bar{z}+2}{\bar{z}-1}$ is $4, z \neq 1,$ then the locus of the point representing $z$ in the complex plane is

**Options:**

- **(A)** a straight line parallel to $x$ -axis
- **(B)** a straight line equally inclined to axes
- **(C)** a circle with radius 2
- **(D)** a circle with radius $\frac{1}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) a circle with radius $\frac{1}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Real part of $\frac{\bar{z}+2}{\bar{z}-1}$ is given by

$$

\begin{array}{l}

\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\bar{z}+2}{\bar{z}-1}+\left(\frac{\bar{z}+2}{\bar{z}-1}\right)\right]=4 \\

\Rightarrow \frac{\bar{z}+2}{\bar{z}-1}+\frac{z+2}{z-1}=8 \\

\Rightarrow z \bar{z}-\bar{z}+2 z-2+z \bar{z}+2 \bar{z}-z-2 \\

=8(z \bar{z}-\bar{z}-z+1) \\

\Rightarrow z \bar{z}-\frac{3}{2} z-\frac{3}{2} \bar{z}+2=0

\end{array}

$$

Comparing with the equation $z \bar{z}+\bar{a} z+a \bar{z}+b=0,$ we get $a=-\frac{3}{2}$ and $b$

$=2$. Thus, the locus of $z$ given by the equation

(i) is a circle with centre $=\frac{1}{2}$

</details>
