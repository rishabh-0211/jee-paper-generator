### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The sum of the first ten terms of an A.P. is $160$ and the sum of the first two terms of a G.P. is $8$. If the first term of the A.P. is equal to the common ratio of the G.P. and the first term of the G.P. is equal to common difference of the A.P., then the sum of all possible values of the first term of the G.P. is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{34}{9}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{34}{13}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{32}{9}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{32}{13}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{34}{9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the first term of the A.P. be $a$ and its common difference be $d$.
Let the first term of the G.P. be $A$ and its common ratio be $R$.

Given that the sum of the first ten terms of the A.P. is $160$:
$\dfrac{10}{2} [2a + (10 - 1)d] = 160$
$5(2a + 9d) = 160$
$2a + 9d = 32$

Given that the sum of the first two terms of the G.P. is $8$:
$A + AR = 8$
$A(1 + R) = 8$

We are also given that $a = R$ and $A = d$. Substituting these into the first equation:
$2R + 9A = 32$
$R = \dfrac{32 - 9A}{2}$

Substitute $R$ into the second equation:
$A \left(1 + \dfrac{32 - 9A}{2}\right) = 8$
$A \left(\dfrac{2 + 32 - 9A}{2}\right) = 8$
$A(34 - 9A) = 16$
$34A - 9A^2 = 16$
$9A^2 - 34A + 16 = 0$

This is a quadratic equation in $A$. The discriminant is $\Delta = (-34)^2 - 4(9)(16) = 1156 - 576 = 580 > 0$, so there are two distinct real values for $A$.

The sum of all possible values of the first term of the G.P. ($A$) is the sum of the roots of this quadratic equation:
Sum of roots $= -\dfrac{\text{coefficient of } A}{\text{coefficient of } A^2} = \dfrac{34}{9}$

Answer: $\dfrac{34}{9}$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (22 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

Suppose $\mathrm{a}, \mathrm{b}, \mathrm{c}$ are in A.P. and $\mathrm{a}^{2}, 2 \mathrm{~b}^{2}, \mathrm{c}^{2}$ are in G.P. If $\mathrm{a}<\mathrm{b}<\mathrm{c}$ and $\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{b}+\mathrm{c}=1$, then $9\left(\mathrm{a}^{2}+\mathrm{b}^{2}+\mathrm{c}^{2}\right)$ is equal to $\_\_\_\_$ .

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **9**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since $a, b, c$ are in A.P., we have $2b = a + c$.
With $a + b + c = 1$, we get $3b = 1$, so $b = \frac{1}{3}$ and $a + c = \frac{2}{3}$.
Since $a^2, 2b^2, c^2$ are in G.P.: $(2b^2)^2 = a^2 c^2$, giving $4b^4 = a^2c^2$.
With $b = \frac{1}{3}$: $a^2c^2 = \frac{4}{81}$.
For real values with $a < b < c$, we need $ac = -\frac{2}{9}$.
Thus $a, c$ satisfy $t^2 - \frac{2}{3}t - \frac{2}{9} = 0$, giving $t = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{3}}{3}$. So $a = \frac{1-\sqrt{3}}{3}$, $c = \frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{3}$.
Calculating: $a^2 + c^2 = \frac{(1-\sqrt{3})^2 + (1+\sqrt{3})^2}{9} = \frac{4-2\sqrt{3}+4+2\sqrt{3}}{9} = \frac{8}{9}$.
Therefore $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = \frac{8}{9} + \frac{1}{9} = 1$, and $9(a^2 + b^2 + c^2) = 9$.

</details>

### Q29. JEE Main 2013 (25 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Given a sequence of 4 numbers, first three of which are in G.P. and the last three are in A.P. with common difference six. If first and last terms of this sequence are equal, then the last term is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** 16
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** 4
- **(D)** 2

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 8**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $a, b, c, d$ be four numbers of the sequence.
Now, according to the question $b^2=a c$ and $c-b=6$ and $a-c=6$
Also, given $a=d$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\therefore b^2=a c \Rightarrow b^2=a\left[\frac{a+b}{2}\right] \\
(\because 2 c=a+b)
\end{aligned}
$$
$\Rightarrow a^2-2 b^2+a b=0$
Now, $c-b=6$ and $a-c=6$,
gives $a-b=12$
$\Rightarrow b=a-12$
$\therefore a^2-2 b^2+a b=0$
$\Rightarrow a^2-2(a-12)^2+a(a-12)=0$
$\Rightarrow a^2-2 a^2-288+48 a+a^2-12 a=0$
$\Rightarrow 36 a=288 \Rightarrow a=8$

</details>

### Q30. JEE Main 2005

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $x=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a^n, y=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b^n, z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c^n$ where $a, b, c$ are in A.P. and $|a| < 1,|b| < 1,|c| < 1$, then $x, y, z$ are in

**Options:**

- **(A)** G.P.
- **(B)** A.P.
- **(C)** Arithmetic - Geometric Progression
- **(D)** H.P.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) H.P.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
x=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a^n=\frac{1}{1-a} & \Rightarrow a=1-\frac{1}{x} \\
y=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} b^n=\frac{1}{1-b} & \Rightarrow b=1-\frac{1}{y}
\end{array}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c^n=\frac{1}{1-c} \quad \Rightarrow c=1-\frac{1}{z} \\
& a, b, c \text { are in A.P. } \\
& 2 b=a+c \\
& 2\left(1-\frac{1}{y}\right)=1-\frac{1}{x}+1-\frac{1}{y} \\
& \frac{2}{y}=\frac{1}{x}+\frac{1}{z} \\
& \Rightarrow x, y, z \text { are in H.P. }
\end{aligned}
$$

### Q31. JEE Main 2003

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $R_1$ and $R_2$ respectively be the maximum ranges up and down an inclined plane and $R$ be the maximum range on the horizontal plane. Then $\mathrm{R}_1, \mathrm{R}, \mathrm{R}_2$ are in

**Options:**

- **(A)** Н.P
- **(B)** A.G.P
- **(C)** A.P
- **(D)** G.P.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Н.P**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\beta$ be the inclination of the plane to the horizontal and $u$ be the velocity of projection of the projectile
$\mathrm{R}_1=\frac{\mathrm{u}^2}{\mathrm{~g}(1+\sin \beta)}$ and $\mathrm{R}_2=\frac{\mathrm{u}^2}{\mathrm{~g}(1-\sin \beta)}$
$\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_1}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_2}=\frac{2 \mathrm{~g}}{\mathrm{u}^2}$ or $\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_1}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{R}_2}=\frac{2}{\mathrm{R}}\left[\because \mathrm{R}=\frac{\mathrm{u}^2}{\mathrm{~g}}\right]$
Therefore, $\mathrm{R}_1, \mathrm{R}, \mathrm{R}_2$ are in H.P.

</details>
