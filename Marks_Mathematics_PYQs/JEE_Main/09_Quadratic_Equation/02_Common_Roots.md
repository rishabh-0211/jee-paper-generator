<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **117**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & a(b-c) x^2+b(c-a) x+c(a-b)=0 \\ & x=1 \text { is root } \therefore \text { other root is } 1 \\ & \alpha+\beta=-\frac{b(c-a)}{a(b-c)}=2 \\ & \Rightarrow-\mathrm{bc}+\mathrm{ab}=2 \mathrm{ab}-2 \mathrm{ac} \\ & \Rightarrow 2 \mathrm{ac}=\mathrm{ab}+\mathrm{bc} \\ & \Rightarrow 2 \mathrm{ac}=\mathrm{b}(\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{c}) \\ & \Rightarrow 2 \mathrm{ac}=15 \mathrm{~b} \ldots(1) \\ & \Rightarrow 2 \mathrm{ac}=15\left(\frac{36}{5}\right)=108 \\ & \Rightarrow \mathrm{ac}=54 \\ & \mathrm{a}+\mathrm{c}=15 \\ & \mathrm{a}^2+\mathrm{c}^2+2 \mathrm{ac}=225 \\ & \mathrm{a}^2+\mathrm{c}^2=225-108=117\end{aligned}$

### Q12. JEE Main 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The quadratic equations $x^2-6 x+a=0$ and $x^2-c x+6=0$ have one root in common. The other roots of the first and second equations are integers in the ratio $4: 3$. Then the common root is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1
- **(B)** 4
- **(C)** 3
- **(D)** 2

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\alpha$ and $4 \beta$ be roots of $x^2-6 x+a=0$ and $\alpha, 3 \beta$ be the roots of $x^2-c x+6=0$, then $\alpha+4 \beta=6$ and $4 \alpha \beta=a$
$\alpha+3 \beta=c$ and $3 \alpha \beta=6$.
We get $\alpha \beta=2 \Rightarrow a=8$
So the first equation is $x^2-6 x+8=0 \Rightarrow x=2,4$
If $\alpha=2$ and $4 \beta=4$ then $3 \beta=3$
If $\alpha=4$ and $4 \beta=2$, then $3 \beta=3 / 2 \quad$ (non-integer)
$\therefore$ common root is $x=2$.
