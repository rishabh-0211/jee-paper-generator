### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let the line $L_1 : x + 3 = 0$ intersect the lines $L_2 : x - y = 0$ and $L_3 : 3x + y = 0$ at the points $A$ and $B$, respectively. Let the bisector of the obtuse angle between the lines $L_2$ and $L_3$ intersect the line $L_1$ at the point $C$. Then $BC^2 : AC^2$ is equal to:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5:1$
- **(B)** $1:5$
- **(C)** $2:3$
- **(D)** $3:2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $5:1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The intersection point of $L_1: x = -3$ and $L_2: x - y = 0$ is $A(-3, -3)$.

The intersection point of $L_1: x = -3$ and $L_3: 3x + y = 0$ is $B(-3, 9)$.

The lines $L_2$ and $L_3$ intersect at the origin $O(0, 0)$.

The distances from the origin to $A$ and $B$ are:
$OA = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + (-3)^2} = 3\sqrt{2}$
$OB = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 9^2} = 3\sqrt{10}$

The angle $\angle AOB$ between the segments $OA$ and $OB$ can be determined using the dot product of vectors $\overrightarrow{OA}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB}$:
$\overrightarrow{OA} \cdot \overrightarrow{OB} = (-3)(-3) + (-3)(9) = 9 - 27 = -18$

Since the dot product is negative, $\cos(\angle AOB) < 0$, which means $\angle AOB$ is an obtuse angle. Therefore, the internal bisector of $\angle AOB$ is the bisector of the obtuse angle between the lines $L_2$ and $L_3$.

According to the internal angle bisector theorem in $\triangle AOB$, the bisector of $\angle AOB$ divides the opposite side $AB$ at point $C$ in the ratio of the adjacent sides:
$\dfrac{AC}{BC} = \dfrac{OA}{OB} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{10}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{5}}$

Squaring both sides yields:
$\dfrac{AC^2}{BC^2} = \dfrac{1}{5} \Rightarrow \dfrac{BC^2}{AC^2} = 5$

Thus, $BC^2 : AC^2 = 5 : 1$.

Answer: $5:1$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $\mathrm{A}(1,0), \mathrm{B}(2,-1)$ and $\mathrm{C}\left(\frac{7}{3}, \frac{4}{3}\right)$ be three points. If the equation of the bisector of the angle ABC is $\alpha x+\beta y=5$, then the value of $\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 13
- **(B)** 10
- **(C)** 5
- **(D)** 8

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 10**

**Detailed Solution:**

For angle bisector of angle ABC where A(1,0), B(2,-1), C($\frac{7}{3}$, $\frac{4}{3}$).
Direction BA: $(1-2, 0-(-1)) = (-1, 1)$, unit vector $\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$
Direction BC: $\left(\frac{1}{3}, \frac{7}{3}\right)$, magnitude $\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{3}$, unit vector $\left(\frac{1}{5\sqrt{2}}, \frac{7}{5\sqrt{2}}\right)$
Angle bisector direction (sum of unit vectors): $\left(\frac{-4}{5\sqrt{2}}, \frac{12}{5\sqrt{2}}\right)$ → direction $(-1, 3)$
Normal to bisector: $(3, 1)$. Bisector passes through B(2, -1):
$3(x-2) + 1(y+1) = 0$ → $3x + y - 5 = 0$
Therefore $\alpha = 3$, $\beta = 1$, so $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = 9 + 1 = 10$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2007

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If one of the lines of $m y^2+\left(1-m^2\right) x y-m x^2=0$ is a bisector of the angle between the lines $x y=0$, then $m$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-1 / 2$
- **(B)** $-2$
- **(C)** $1$
- **(D)** $2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Equation of bisectors of lines $x y=0$ are $y=\pm x$ 
put $y=\pm x$ in $m y^2+\left(1-m^2\right) x y-m x^2=0$, we get $\left(1-m^2\right) x^2=0$ 
$\Rightarrow m=\pm 1$.

</details>
