### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (28 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Given below are two statements : 
 <b>Statement I:</b> $\quad 25^{13}+20^{13}+8^{13}+3^{13}$ is divisible by 7. 
 <b>Statement II:</b> The integral part of $(7+4 \sqrt{3})^{25}$ is an odd number. 
 In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below :

**Options:**

- **(A)** Statement I is true but Statement II is false
- **(B)** Both Statement I and Statement II are true
- **(C)** Statement I is false but Statement II is true
- **(D)** Both Statement I and Statement II are false

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Both Statement I and Statement II are true**

**Detailed Solution:**

Statement I:
$25^{13} + 3^{13}$ → divisible by $(25 + 3)$
$20^{13} + 8^{13}$ → divisible by $(20 + 8)$
$\therefore$ divisible by $7$. TRUE
Statement II:
For $(7+4\sqrt{3})^{25}$, let $\alpha = 7+4\sqrt{3}$ and $\beta = 7-4\sqrt{3}$ (conjugate).
Then $\alpha\beta = 1$ and $\alpha + \beta = 14$.
Since $0 < \beta < 1$, we have $\alpha^{25} + \beta^{25}$ is an integer.
The fractional part of $\alpha^{25}$ is $1-\beta^{25}$, making the integral part odd. TRUE.
Both Statement I and Statement II are true.

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2025 (8 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The number of integral terms in the expansion of $\left(5^{\frac{1}{2}}+7^{\frac{1}{8}}\right)^{1016}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 127
- **(B)** 130
- **(C)** 129
- **(D)** 128

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 128**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{r}}={ }^{1016} \mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{r}}(5)^{\frac{1016-\mathrm{r}}{2}} 7^{\frac{\mathrm{r}}{8}} \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{r}=0,8,16,24, \ldots ., 1016 \\
& 1016=0+(\mathrm{n}-1) 8 \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{n}-1=\frac{1016}{8}=127
\end{aligned}$
So, $\mathrm{n}=128$.

### Q5. JEE Main 2012 (Offline)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If $n$ is a positive integer, then $(\sqrt{3}+1)^{2 n}-(\sqrt{3}-1)^{2 n}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** an irrational number
- **(B)** an odd positive integer
- **(C)** an even positive integer
- **(D)** a rational number other than positive integers

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) an irrational number**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& (\sqrt{3}+1)^{2 n}-(\sqrt{3}-1)^{2 n}=\left[(\sqrt{3}+1)^2\right]^n-\left[(\sqrt{3}-1)^2\right]^n=(4+2 \sqrt{3})^n-(4-2 \sqrt{3})^n \\
& =2^n\left[(2+\sqrt{3})^n-(2-\sqrt{3})^n\right] \\
& =2^n\left\{\left[{ }^n C_0 2^n+{ }^n C_1 2^{n-1} \sqrt{3}+{ }^n C_2 2^{n-2} 3+\cdots \cdot\right]-\left[{ }^n C_0 2^n-{ }^n C_1 2^{n-1} \sqrt{3}+{ }^n C_2 2^{n-2} 3-\cdots \cdot\right]\right\} \\
& =2^{n+1}\left[{ }^n C_1 2^{n-1} \sqrt{3}+{ }^n C_3 2^{n-3} 3 \sqrt{3}+\cdots \cdot\right]=2^{n+1} \sqrt{3} \text { (some integer) }
\end{aligned}$
Which is irrational
