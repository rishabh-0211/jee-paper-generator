### Q1. JEE Main 2014 (11 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $\mathrm{A}(2,3,5), \mathrm{B}(-1,3,2)$ and $\mathrm{C}(\lambda, 5, \mu)$ be the vertices of a $\triangle \mathrm{ABC}$. If the median through $\mathrm{A}$ is equally inclined to the coordinate axes, then:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5 \lambda-8 \mu=0$
- **(B)** $8 \lambda-5 \mu=0$
- **(C)** $10 \lambda-7 \mu=0$
- **(D)** $7 \lambda-10 \mu=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $10 \lambda-7 \mu=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

If D be the mid-point of $B C$, then
$$
D=\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{2}, 4, \frac{\mu+2}{2}\right)
$$
<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/q5lWniA6fQkkSXh1i-0oL4yuBTNuYAEtTcR3cD752ls.original.fullsize.png">

Direction ratios of $\mathrm{AD}$ are $\frac{\lambda-5}{2}, 1, \frac{\mu-8}{2}$
Since median AD is equally inclined with coordinate axes, therefore direction ratios of $\mathrm{AD}$ will be equal, i.e,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{\left(\frac{\lambda-5}{2}\right)^2}{\left(\frac{\lambda-5}{2}\right)^2+1+\left(\frac{\mu-8}{2}\right)^2} \\
&=\frac{1}{\left(\frac{\lambda-5}{2}\right)^2+1+\left(\frac{\mu-8}{2}\right)^2} \\
&=\frac{\left(\frac{\mu-8}{2}\right)^2}{\left(\frac{\lambda-5}{2}\right)^2+1+\left(\frac{\mu-8}{2}\right)^2} \\
&\Rightarrow\left(\frac{\lambda-5}{2}\right)^2=1=\left(\frac{\mu-8}{2}\right)^2
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\Rightarrow\left(\frac{\lambda-5}{2}\right)^2=1=\left(\frac{\mu-8}{2}\right)^2 \\
&\Rightarrow \lambda=7,3 \text { and } \mu=10,6
\end{aligned}
$$
If $\lambda=7$ and $\mu=10$
Then $\frac{\lambda}{\mu}=\frac{7}{10} \Rightarrow 10 \lambda-7 \mu=0$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2013 (23 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If the extremities of the base of an isosceles triangle are the points $(2 a, 0)$ and $(0, a)$ and the equation of one of the sides is $x=2 a$, then the area of the triangle, in square units, is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{5}{4} a^2$
- **(B)** $\frac{5}{2} a^2$
- **(C)** $\frac{25 a^2}{4}$
- **(D)** $5 a^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{5}{2} a^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\text { Let } y \text {-coordinate of } \mathrm{C}=b
$$


<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/PyjTdQZH_iQNBERxnxSJ2LKdd13YqwWalzkJRPPuI1c.original.fullsize.png">



$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{AB}=\sqrt{4 a^2+a^2}=\sqrt{5} a \\
& \text { Now, } \mathrm{AC}=\mathrm{BC} \Rightarrow b=\sqrt{4 a^2+(b-a)^2} \\
& \Rightarrow b^2=4 a^2+b^2+a^2-2 a b \\
& \Rightarrow 2 a b=5 a^2 \Rightarrow b=\frac{5 a}{2} \\
& \therefore \mathrm{C}=\left(2 a, \frac{5 a}{2}\right)
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence area of the triangle
$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{1}{2}\left|\begin{array}{ccc}
2 a & 0 & 1 \\
0 & a & 1 \\
2 a & \frac{5 a}{2} & 1
\end{array}\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left|\begin{array}{ccc}
2 a & 0 & 1 \\
0 & a & 1 \\
0 & \frac{5 a}{2} & 0
\end{array}\right| \\
& =\frac{1}{2} \times 2 a\left(-\frac{5 a}{2}\right)=-\frac{5 a^2}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
Since area is always $+\mathrm{ve}$, hence area
$$
=\frac{5 a^2}{2} \text { sq. unit }
$$

### Q4. JEE Main 2012 (19 May Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If three distinct points $A, B, C$ are given in the 2dimensional coordinate plane such that the ratio of the distance of each one of them from the point $(1,0)$ to the distance from $(-1,0)$ is equal to $\frac{1}{2}$, then the circumcentre of the triangle $A B C$ is at the point

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\frac{5}{3}, 0\right)$
- **(B)** $(0,0)$
- **(C)** $\left(\frac{1}{3}, 0\right)$
- **(D)** $(3,0)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\left(\frac{5}{3}, 0\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $P(1,0)$ and $Q(-1,0), A(x, y)$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text { Given: } \frac{A P}{A Q}=\frac{B P}{B Q}=\frac{C P}{C Q}=\frac{1}{2} \\
& \Rightarrow 2 A P=A Q \\
& \Rightarrow 4(A P)^2=A Q^2
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow 4\left[(x-1)^2+y^2\right]=(x+1)^2+y^2 \\
& \Rightarrow 4\left(x^2+1-2 x\right)+4 y^2=x^2+1+2 x+y^2 \\
& \Rightarrow 3 x^2+3 y^2-8 x-2 x+4-1=0 \\
& \Rightarrow 3 x^2+3 y^2-10 x+3=0 \\
& \Rightarrow x^2+y^2-\frac{10}{3} x+1=0
\end{aligned}
$$
$\therefore$ A lies on the circle given by (1). As $B$ and $C$ also follow the same condition.
$\therefore \quad$ Centre of circumcircle of $\triangle A B C=$ centre of circle given by (1)
$$
=\left(\frac{5}{3}, 0\right) \text {. }
$$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

For a regular polygon, let $\mathrm{r}$ and $\mathrm{R}$ be the radii of the inscribed and the circumscribed circles. A false statement among the following is

**Options:**

- **(A)** There is a regular polygon with $\frac{r}{R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(B)** There is a regular polygon with $\frac{r}{R}=\frac{2}{3}$
- **(C)** There is a regular polygon with $\frac{r}{R}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$
- **(D)** There is a regular polygon with $\frac{r}{R}=\frac{1}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) There is a regular polygon with $\frac{r}{R}=\frac{2}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\mathrm{r}=\frac{\mathrm{a}}{2} \cot \frac{\pi}{\mathrm{n}}
$$
'a' is side of polygon.
$$
\begin{aligned}
& R=\frac{a}{2} \operatorname{cosec} \frac{\pi}{n} \\
& \frac{r}{R}=\frac{\cot \frac{\pi}{n}}{\operatorname{cosec} \frac{\pi}{n}}=\cos \frac{\pi}{n} \\
& \cos \frac{\pi}{n} \neq \frac{2}{3} \text { for any } n \in N
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q7. JEE Main 2005

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a triangle $\mathrm{PQR}, \angle \mathrm{R}=\frac{\pi}{2}$. If $\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}}{2}\right)$ and $\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}\right)$ are the roots of $a x^2+b x+c=0, a \neq 0$ then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $a=b+c$
- **(B)** $c=a+b$
- **(C)** $b=c$
- **(D)** $b=a+c$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $c=a+b$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}}{2}\right), \tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}\right)$ are the roots of $\mathrm{ax}^2+\mathrm{bx}+\mathrm{c}=0$
$$
\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}}{2}\right)+\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}\right)=-\frac{\mathrm{b}}{\mathrm{a}}
$$
$\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}}{2}\right) \tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{c}}{\mathrm{a}}$
$\frac{\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}}{2}\right)+\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}\right)}{1-\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}}{2}\right) \tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}\right)}=\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{P}}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{Q}}{2}\right)=1$
$\Rightarrow \frac{-\frac{\mathrm{b}}{\mathrm{a}}}{1-\frac{\mathrm{c}}{\mathrm{a}}}=1 \Rightarrow-\frac{\mathrm{b}}{\mathrm{a}}=\frac{\mathrm{a}}{\mathrm{a}}-\frac{\mathrm{c}}{\mathrm{a}} \Rightarrow-\mathrm{b}=\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{c}$
$\mathrm{c}=\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{b}$.

</details>

### Q10. JEE Main 2003

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

The sum of the radii of inscribed and circumscribed circles for an $\mathrm{n}$ sided regular polygon of side a, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{a}{4} \cot \left(\frac{\pi}{2 n}\right)$
- **(B)** $a \cot \left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)$
- **(C)** $\frac{a}{2} \cot \left(\frac{\pi}{2 n}\right)$
- **(D)** $\operatorname{acot}\left(\frac{\pi}{2 n}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\operatorname{acot}\left(\frac{\pi}{2 n}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\tan \left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)=\frac{a}{2 r} ; \sin \left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)=\frac{a}{2 R}$

$r+R=\frac{a}{2}\left[\cot \frac{\pi}{n}+\operatorname{cosec} \frac{\pi}{n}\right] \Rightarrow r+R=\frac{a}{2} \cdot \cot \left(\frac{\pi}{2 n}\right)$

</details>
