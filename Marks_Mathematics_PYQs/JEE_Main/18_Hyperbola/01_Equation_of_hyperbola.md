### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the eccentricity $e$ of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$, passing through $(6, 4\sqrt{3})$, satisfies $15(e^2 + 1) = 34e$, then the length of the latus rectum of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{b^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{2(a^2+1)} = 1$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$
- **(B)** $20$
- **(C)** $25$
- **(D)** $30$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $10$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the hyperbola is $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.

Since it passes through $(6, 4\sqrt{3})$, we have:

$\dfrac{36}{a^2} - \dfrac{48}{b^2} = 1$

The eccentricity $e$ satisfies $15(e^2 + 1) = 34e$.

$15e^2 - 34e + 15 = 0$

$(3e - 5)(5e - 3) = 0$

Since the eccentricity of a hyperbola is $e > 1$, we get $e = \dfrac{5}{3}$.

We know that $b^2 = a^2(e^2 - 1)$.

$b^2 = a^2 \left(\dfrac{25}{9} - 1\right) = \dfrac{16}{9}a^2$

Substituting $b^2$ into the first equation:

$\dfrac{36}{a^2} - \dfrac{48}{\dfrac{16}{9}a^2} = 1$

$\dfrac{36}{a^2} - \dfrac{27}{a^2} = 1$

$\dfrac{9}{a^2} = 1 \Rightarrow a^2 = 9$

Then, $b^2 = \dfrac{16}{9} \times 9 = 16$.

The second hyperbola is given by $\dfrac{x^2}{b^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{2(a^2+1)} = 1$.

Substituting the values of $a^2$ and $b^2$:

$\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{2(9+1)} = 1$

$\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{20} = 1$

Here, $A^2 = 16 \Rightarrow A = 4$ and $B^2 = 20$.

The length of the latus rectum is $\dfrac{2B^2}{A}$.

Length of latus rectum $= \dfrac{2 \times 20}{4} = 10$.

Answer: $10$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (05 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let the eccentricity $e$ of a hyperbola satisfy the equation $6e^2 - 11e + 3 = 0$. If the foci of the hyperbola are $(3, 5)$ and $(3, -4)$, then the length of its latus rectum is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{11}{3}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{17}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{15}{2}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{17}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{15}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the equation for eccentricity: $6e^2 - 11e + 3 = 0$

$6e^2 - 9e - 2e + 3 = 0$

$(3e - 1)(2e - 3) = 0$

$e = \dfrac{1}{3}$ or $e = \dfrac{3}{2}$

For a hyperbola, $e > 1$, so $e = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

The distance between the foci $(3, 5)$ and $(3, -4)$ is $2ae$.

$2ae = \sqrt{(3-3)^2 + (5 - (-4))^2} = 9$

$2a \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right) = 9 \Rightarrow 3a = 9 \Rightarrow a = 3$

Using the relation $b^2 = a^2(e^2 - 1)$:

$b^2 = 9 \left(\dfrac{9}{4} - 1\right) = 9 \left(\dfrac{5}{4}\right) = \dfrac{45}{4}$

The length of the latus rectum is $\dfrac{2b^2}{a}$:

$\dfrac{2 \left(\dfrac{45}{4}\right)}{3} = \dfrac{45}{6} = \dfrac{15}{2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{15}{2}$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

**Options:**

- **(A)** $12$
- **(B)** $16$
- **(C)** $24$
- **(D)** $25$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $16$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given distance between foci is $2ae = 6 \Rightarrow ae = 3$

Distance between directrices is $\dfrac{2a}{e} = \dfrac{8}{3}$

Multiplying the two equations, we get $4a^2 = 16 \Rightarrow a^2 = 4$

Dividing the two equations, we get $e^2 = \dfrac{9}{4}$

Using $b^2 = a^2(e^2 - 1)$, we get $b^2 = 4\left(\dfrac{9}{4} - 1\right) = 5$

The equation of the hyperbola is $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{5} = 1$

The line $x = \alpha$ intersects the hyperbola at $A$ and $B$. Substituting $x = \alpha$, we get $\dfrac{\alpha^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{5} = 1 \Rightarrow y = \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{5(\alpha^2 - 4)}}{2}$

The coordinates of $A$ and $B$ are $\left(\alpha, \dfrac{\sqrt{5(\alpha^2 - 4)}}{2}\right)$ and $\left(\alpha, -\dfrac{\sqrt{5(\alpha^2 - 4)}}{2}\right)$

The length of the base $AB$ is $\sqrt{5(\alpha^2 - 4)}$ and the height of the triangle $AOB$ from the origin is $|\alpha|$

Area of $\Delta AOB = \dfrac{1}{2} \times AB \times |\alpha| = 4\sqrt{15}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} \sqrt{5(\alpha^2 - 4)} |\alpha| = 4\sqrt{15}$

Squaring both sides, we get $\dfrac{1}{4} \times 5(\alpha^2 - 4) \alpha^2 = 240$

$\alpha^4 - 4\alpha^2 - 192 = 0$

$(\alpha^2 - 16)(\alpha^2 + 12) = 0$

Since $\alpha^2 > 0$, we have $\alpha^2 = 16$

Answer: $16$

### Q46. JEE Main 2012 (07 May Online)

If the eccentricity of a hyperbola $\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$, which passes through $(K, 2)$, is $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$, then the value of $K^2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 18
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** 1
- **(D)** 2

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 18**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given hyperbola is
$$
\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1
$$
Since this passes through $(K, 2)$, therefore
$$
\frac{K^2}{9}-\frac{4}{b^2}=1
$$
Also, given $e=\sqrt{1+\frac{b^2}{a^2}}=\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow \sqrt{1+\frac{b^2}{9}}=\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3} \Rightarrow 9+b^2=13 \\
& \Rightarrow b=\pm 2
\end{aligned}
$$
Now, from $\mathrm{eq}^{\mathrm{n}}$ (1), we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{K^2}{9}-\frac{4}{4}=1 \quad(\because b=\pm 2) \\
& \Rightarrow \quad K^2=18
\end{aligned}
$$

### Q47. JEE Main 2007

For the hyperbola $\frac{x^2}{\cos ^2 \alpha}-\frac{y^2}{\sin ^2 \alpha}=1$, which of the following remains constant when $\alpha$ varies?

**Options:**

- **(A)** eccentricity
- **(B)** directrix
- **(C)** abscissae of vertices
- **(D)** abscissae of foci

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) abscissae of foci**

**Detailed Solution:**

$a^2=\cos ^2 \alpha$ and $b^2=\sin ^2 \alpha$
Coordinates of focii are $(\pm a e, 0)$
$\therefore b^2=a^2\left(e^2-1\right) \Rightarrow e=\sec \alpha$.
Hence abscissae of foci remain constant when $\alpha$ varies.
