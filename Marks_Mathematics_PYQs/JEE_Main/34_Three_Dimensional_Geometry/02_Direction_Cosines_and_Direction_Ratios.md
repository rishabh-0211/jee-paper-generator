### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Let the direction cosines of two lines satisfy the equations : $4 l+m-n=0$ and $2 m n+10 n l+3 l m=0$. Then the cosine of the acute angle between these lines is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{20}{3 \sqrt{38}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{10}{3 \sqrt{38}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{10}{7 \sqrt{38}}$
- **(D)** $\frac{10}{\sqrt{38}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{10}{3 \sqrt{38}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

From $n = 4l + m$, substitute in $2mn + 10nl + 3lm = 0$:
$40l^2 + 21lm + 2m^2 = 0$. Let $t = l/m$: $40t^2 + 21t + 2 = 0$.
$t = \frac{-21 \pm 11}{80}$, giving $t = -1/8$ or $t = -2/5$.
$l/m = -1/8$: direction $(l,m,n) = (-1, 8, 4)$.
$l/m = -2/5$: direction $(l,m,n) = (-2, 5, -3)$.
$\cos\theta = \frac{|2+40-12|}{\sqrt{81}\sqrt{38}} = \frac{30}{9\sqrt{38}} = \frac{10}{3\sqrt{38}}$.

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2025 (3 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Each of the angles $\beta$ and $\gamma$ that a given line makes with the positive y - and z -axes, respectively, is half of the angle that this line makes with the positive x -axes. Then the sum of all possible values of the angle $\beta$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{3 \pi}{4}$
- **(B)** $\pi$
- **(C)** $\frac{\pi}{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{3 \pi}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{3 \pi}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& \beta=\frac{\alpha}{2}, \gamma=\frac{\alpha}{2} \\
& \cos ^2 \alpha+\cos ^2 \beta+\cos ^2 \gamma=1 \\
& \cos ^2 \alpha+2 \cos ^2 \frac{\alpha}{2}=1 \\
& \cos ^2 \alpha+\cos \alpha=0 \\
& \cos \alpha(\cos \alpha+1)=0 \\
& \cos \alpha=0,-1 \\
& \alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}, \pi
\end{aligned}$
Now $\beta=\frac{\alpha}{2} \Rightarrow \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{2}$
so sum is $\frac{3 \pi}{4}$

</details>

The direction ratios of normal to the plane through the

points (0,-1,0) and (0,0,1) and making an angle $\frac{\pi}{4}$

2,-1,1

$2, \sqrt{2}-\sqrt{2}$

$\sqrt{2}, 1,-1$

$2 \sqrt{3}, 1,-1$

**Options:**

- **(A)** option 1 and 2
- **(B)** option 2 and 3
- **(C)** option 3 and 4
- **(D)** all the options

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the d.r's of the normal be $\langle a, b, c\rangle$ Equation of the plane is $a(x-0)+b(y+1)+c(z-0)=0$

$\Rightarrow \quad b-c=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}$

$\Rightarrow \quad b=\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} a$

### Q12. JEE Main 2004

Let $\bar{u}, \bar{v}, \bar{w}$ be such that $|\bar{u}|=1,|\bar{v}|=2,|\bar{w}|=3$. If the projection $\bar{v}$ along $\bar{u}$ is equal to that of $\overline{\mathrm{w}}$ along $\overline{\mathrm{u}}$ and $\overline{\mathrm{v}}, \overline{\mathrm{w}}$ are perpendicular to each other then $|\overline{\mathrm{u}}-\overline{\mathrm{v}}+\overline{\mathrm{w}}|$ equals

- **(B)** $\sqrt{7}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{14}$
- **(D)** 14

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{14}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Projection of $\bar{v}$ along $\bar{u}$ and $\bar{w}$ along $\bar{u}$ is $\frac{\bar{v} \cdot \bar{u}}{|\bar{u}|}$ and $\frac{\bar{w} \cdot \bar{u}}{|\bar{u}|}$ respectively According to question $\frac{\overline{\mathrm{v}} \cdot \overline{\mathrm{u}}}{|\overline{\mathrm{u}}|}=\frac{\overline{\mathrm{w}} \cdot \overline{\mathrm{u}}}{|\overline{\mathrm{u}}|} \Rightarrow \overline{\mathrm{v}} \cdot \overline{\mathrm{u}}=\overline{\mathrm{w}} \cdot \overline{\mathrm{u}}$. and $\overline{\mathrm{v}} \cdot \overline{\mathrm{w}}=0$
$$
|\bar{u}-\bar{v}+\bar{w}|^2=|\bar{u}|^2+|\bar{v}|^2+|\bar{w}|^2-2 \bar{u} \cdot \bar{v}+2 \bar{u} \cdot \bar{w}-2 \bar{v} \cdot \bar{w}=14 \Rightarrow|\bar{u}-\bar{v}+\bar{w}|=\sqrt{14}

### Q13. JEE Main 2004

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A line with direction cosines proportional to $2,1,2$ meets each of the lines $x=y+a=z$ and $x+a=2 y=2 z$. The co-ordinates of each of the point of intersection are given by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(3 a, 3 a, 3 a),(a, a, a)$
- **(B)** $(3 a, 2 a, 3 a),(a, a, a)$
- **(C)** $(3 a, 2 a, 3 a),(a, a, 2 a)$
- **(D)** $(2 a, 3 a, 3 a),(2 a, a, a)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $(3 a, 2 a, 3 a),(a, a, a)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Any point on the line $\frac{x}{1}=\frac{y+a}{1}=\frac{z}{1}=t_1 \quad$ (say) is $\left(t_1, t_1-a, t_1\right)$ and any point on the line $\frac{x+a}{2}=\frac{y}{1}=\frac{z}{1}=t_2 \quad($ say $)$ is $\left(2 t_2-a, t_2, t_2\right)$.
Now direction cosine of the lines intersecting the above lines is proportional to $\left(2 t_2-a-t_1, t_2-t_1+a, t_2-t_1\right)$.
Hence $2 \mathrm{t}_2-\mathrm{a}-\mathrm{t}_1=2 \mathrm{k}, \mathrm{t}_2-\mathrm{t}_1+\mathrm{a}=\mathrm{k}$ and $\mathrm{t}_2-\mathrm{t}_1=2 \mathrm{k}$
On solving these, we get $t_1=3 a, t_2=a$.
Hence points are $(3 a, 2 a, 3 a)$ and $(a, a, a)$
