### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $C$ be a circle having centre in the first quadrant and touching the $x$-axis at a distance of $3$ units from the origin. If the circle $C$ has an intercept of length $6\sqrt{3}$ on $y$-axis, then the length of the chord of the circle $C$ on the line $x - y = 3$ is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $8$
- **(B)** $6$
- **(C)** $6\sqrt{2}$
- **(D)** $8\sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $6\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the centre of the circle be $(3, r)$ since it touches the $x$-axis at $(3, 0)$ and lies in the first quadrant.

The radius of the circle is $r$.

The length of the intercept on the $y$-axis is given by $2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$, where $d$ is the distance of the centre from the $y$-axis.

Here, $d = 3$.

Given that the intercept on the $y$-axis is $6\sqrt{3}$:

$2\sqrt{r^2 - 3^2} = 6\sqrt{3}$

$\sqrt{r^2 - 9} = 3\sqrt{3}$

$r^2 - 9 = 27$

$r^2 = 36 \Rightarrow r = 6$

The centre of the circle is $(3, 6)$ and its radius is $6$.

The given line is $x - y - 3 = 0$.

The perpendicular distance $p$ from the centre $(3, 6)$ to the line is:

$p = \dfrac{|3 - 6 - 3|}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} = \dfrac{6}{\sqrt{2}} = 3\sqrt{2}$

The length of the chord is $2\sqrt{r^2 - p^2}$.

Length of the chord $= 2\sqrt{36 - (3\sqrt{2})^2} = 2\sqrt{36 - 18} = 2\sqrt{18} = 6\sqrt{2}$

Answer: $6\sqrt{2}$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (06 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `2`

Let the centre of the circle $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + 25 = 0$ be in the first quadrant and lie on the line $2x - y = 4$. Let the area of an equilateral triangle inscribed in the circle be $27\sqrt{3}$. Then the square of the length of the chord of the circle on the line $x = 1$ is _______.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **80**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the circle is $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + 25 = 0$.

The centre of the circle is $(-g, -f)$. Since it lies in the first quadrant, $-g > 0$ and $-f > 0$, which implies $g < 0$ and $f < 0$.

The centre lies on the line $2x - y = 4$, so:

$-2g - (-f) = 4 \Rightarrow f = 2g + 4$

Let the radius of the circle be $R$. The side length $a$ of an equilateral triangle inscribed in the circle is $a = \sqrt{3}R$.

The area of the equilateral triangle is given as $27\sqrt{3}$:

$\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2 = 27\sqrt{3} \Rightarrow \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(3R^2) = 27\sqrt{3} \Rightarrow R^2 = 36$

The radius of the circle is also given by $R^2 = g^2 + f^2 - c$. Here $c = 25$, so:

$g^2 + f^2 - 25 = 36 \Rightarrow g^2 + f^2 = 61$

Substituting $f = 2g + 4$ into the equation:

$g^2 + (2g + 4)^2 = 61$

$5g^2 + 16g - 45 = 0$

$(5g - 9)(g + 5) = 0$

Since $g < 0$, we get $g = -5$.

Then, $f = 2(-5) + 4 = -6$.

The centre of the circle is $(5, 6)$ and its radius is $R = 6$.

The distance $d$ from the centre $(5, 6)$ to the line $x = 1$ is:

$d = |5 - 1| = 4$

The length of the chord $L$ on the line $x = 1$ is given by:

$L = 2\sqrt{R^2 - d^2} = 2\sqrt{36 - 16} = 2\sqrt{20}$

The square of the length of the chord is:

$L^2 = (2\sqrt{20})^2 = 4 \times 20 = 80$

Answer: $80$

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (04 April Shift 1)

Suppose that two chords, drawn from the point $(1, 2)$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 + x - 3y = 0$ are bisected by the $y$-axis. If the other ends of these chords are $R$ and $S$, and the mid point of the line segment $RS$ is $(\alpha, \beta)$, then $6(\alpha + \beta)$ is equal to:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $3$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $6$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the other end of the chord be $(x_1, y_1)$.

Since the chord is bisected by the $y$-axis, the midpoint of the chord lies on the $y$-axis.

The midpoint of the chord joining $(1, 2)$ and $(x_1, y_1)$ is $\left(\dfrac{x_1 + 1}{2}, \dfrac{y_1 + 2}{2}\right)$.

Since it lies on the $y$-axis, its $x$-coordinate must be zero:

$\dfrac{x_1 + 1}{2} = 0 \Rightarrow x_1 = -1$

Since the point $(x_1, y_1)$ lies on the given circle $x^2 + y^2 + x - 3y = 0$, we substitute $x_1 = -1$ into the equation:

$(-1)^2 + y_1^2 + (-1) - 3y_1 = 0$

$1 + y_1^2 - 1 - 3y_1 = 0$

$y_1^2 - 3y_1 = 0$

$y_1(y_1 - 3) = 0 \Rightarrow y_1 = 0$ or $y_1 = 3$

Thus, the other ends of the two chords are $R(-1, 0)$ and $S(-1, 3)$.

The midpoint of the line segment $RS$ is given as $(\alpha, \beta)$.

$\alpha = \dfrac{-1 + (-1)}{2} = -1$

$\beta = \dfrac{0 + 3}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$

We need to find the value of $6(\alpha + \beta)$:

$6(\alpha + \beta) = 6\left(-1 + \dfrac{3}{2}\right) = 6\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = 3$

Answer: $3$

</details>

### Q4. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let a circle pass through the origin and its centre be the point of intersection of two mutually perpendicular lines $x + (k-1)y + 3 = 0$ and $2x + k^2 y - 4 = 0$. If the line $x - y + 2 = 0$ intersects the circle at the points A and B, then $(AB)^2$ is equal to:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$
- **(B)** $27$
- **(C)** $18$
- **(D)** $34$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $18$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given lines are $x + (k-1)y + 3 = 0$ and $2x + k^2 y - 4 = 0$.

Since they are mutually perpendicular, the product of their slopes is $-1$:

$\left(\dfrac{-1}{k-1}\right) \left(\dfrac{-2}{k^2}\right) = -1$

$\dfrac{2}{k^2(k-1)} = -1 \Rightarrow k^3 - k^2 + 2 = 0$

By trial, $k = -1$ is a root. Factoring gives $(k+1)(k^2 - 2k + 2) = 0$. Since $k^2 - 2k + 2 = 0$ has no real roots, $k = -1$.

Substituting $k = -1$ into the equations of the lines, we get:

$x - 2y + 3 = 0$

$2x + y - 4 = 0$

Solving these two equations, we get the point of intersection as $x = 1$ and $y = 2$. Thus, the centre of the circle is $C(1, 2)$.

Since the circle passes through the origin $(0,0)$, the square of its radius $R$ is:

$R^2 = (1 - 0)^2 + (2 - 0)^2 = 5$

The perpendicular distance $d$ from the centre $C(1, 2)$ to the line $x - y + 2 = 0$ is:

$d = \dfrac{|1 - 2 + 2|}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

The length of the chord $AB$ is given by $2\sqrt{R^2 - d^2}$. Therefore, its square is:

$(AB)^2 = 4(R^2 - d^2) = 4\left(5 - \dfrac{1}{2}\right) = 4 \times \dfrac{9}{2} = 18$

Answer: $18$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (02 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

Let a circle $C$ have its centre in the first quadrant, intersect the coordinate axes at exactly three points and cut off equal intercepts from the coordinate axes. If the length of the chord of $C$ on the line $x + y = 1$ is $\sqrt{14}$, then the square of the radius of $C$ is _______.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **8**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the equation of the circle be $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$.

Since the circle intersects the coordinate axes at exactly three points, it must pass through the origin and intersect each axis at one other distinct point. Thus, $c = 0$.

The lengths of the intercepts on the x-axis and y-axis are $2\sqrt{g^2 - c} = 2|g|$ and $2\sqrt{f^2 - c} = 2|f|$.

Given that the intercepts are equal, we have $|g| = |f|$.

The centre of the circle is $(-g, -f)$. Since it lies in the first quadrant, $-g > 0$ and $-f > 0$. Therefore, $g = f = -a$ for some $a > 0$.

The centre is $(a, a)$ and the radius is $r = \sqrt{a^2 + a^2} = a\sqrt{2}$.

The distance $d$ from the centre $(a, a)$ to the line $x + y - 1 = 0$ is given by:

$d = \dfrac{|a + a - 1|}{\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2}} = \dfrac{|2a - 1|}{\sqrt{2}}$

The length of the chord on the given line is $2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2} = \sqrt{14}$.

Squaring both sides, we get:

$4(r^2 - d^2) = 14 \Rightarrow r^2 - d^2 = \dfrac{7}{2}$

Substituting $r^2 = 2a^2$ and $d^2 = \dfrac{(2a - 1)^2}{2}$:

$2a^2 - \dfrac{(2a - 1)^2}{2} = \dfrac{7}{2}$

$4a^2 - (4a^2 - 4a + 1) = 7$

$4a - 1 = 7 \Rightarrow 4a = 8 \Rightarrow a = 2$

The square of the radius of the circle is $r^2 = 2a^2 = 2(2)^2 = 8$.

Answer: $8$

</details>
