### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

Consider the circle $C: x^2+y^2-6x-8y-11=0$. Let a variable chord AB of the circle C subtend a right angle at the origin. If the locus of the foot of the perpendicular drawn from the origin on the chord AB is the circle $x^2+y^2-\alpha x - \beta y - \gamma = 0$, then $\alpha + \beta + 2\gamma$ is equal to ________.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **18**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the foot of the perpendicular from the origin to the chord $AB$ be $P(h, k)$.

The slope of $OP$ is $\dfrac{k}{h}$. Since the chord $AB$ is perpendicular to $OP$, its equation is given by:

$y - k = -\dfrac{h}{k}(x - h) \Rightarrow hx + ky = h^2 + k^2$

This can be rewritten as $\dfrac{hx + ky}{h^2 + k^2} = 1$.

The chord $AB$ subtends a right angle at the origin. We homogenize the equation of the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y - 11 = 0$ with the equation of the chord to find the joint equation of the lines $OA$ and $OB$:

$x^2 + y^2 - (6x + 8y)\left(\dfrac{hx + ky}{h^2 + k^2}\right) - 11\left(\dfrac{hx + ky}{h^2 + k^2}\right)^2 = 0$

Since the lines $OA$ and $OB$ are perpendicular, the sum of the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ in this homogenized equation must be zero.

Coefficient of $x^2$: $1 - \dfrac{6h}{h^2 + k^2} - \dfrac{11h^2}{(h^2 + k^2)^2}$

Coefficient of $y^2$: $1 - \dfrac{8k}{h^2 + k^2} - \dfrac{11k^2}{(h^2 + k^2)^2}$

Equating the sum of these coefficients to zero:

$2 - \dfrac{6h + 8k}{h^2 + k^2} - \dfrac{11(h^2 + k^2)}{(h^2 + k^2)^2} = 0$

$2 - \dfrac{6h + 8k}{h^2 + k^2} - \dfrac{11}{h^2 + k^2} = 0$

$2(h^2 + k^2) - 6h - 8k - 11 = 0$

$h^2 + k^2 - 3h - 4k - \dfrac{11}{2} = 0$

Replacing $(h, k)$ with $(x, y)$, the locus of the foot of the perpendicular is:

$x^2 + y^2 - 3x - 4y - \dfrac{11}{2} = 0$

Comparing this with the given locus equation $x^2 + y^2 - \alpha x - \beta y - \gamma = 0$, we get:

$\alpha = 3$, $\beta = 4$, $\gamma = \dfrac{11}{2}$

Therefore, the value of $\alpha + \beta + 2\gamma$ is:

$3 + 4 + 2\left(\dfrac{11}{2}\right) = 7 + 11 = 18$

Answer: $18$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let a circle of radius 4 pass through the origin O, the points $\mathrm{A}(-\sqrt{3} a, 0)$ and $\mathrm{B}(0,-\sqrt{2} b)$, where $a$ and $b$ are real parameters and $a b \neq 0$. Then the locus of the centroid of $\triangle O A B$ is a circle of radius

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\frac{7}{3}}{3}$
- **(B)** $\frac{8}{3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{11}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{5}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{8}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Circle of radius 4 passes through O and points A($-\sqrt{3a}$, 0), B(0, $-\sqrt{2b}$).
Let center be C(h, k) on circle: $h^2 + k^2 = 16$.
Since A, B lie on circle: $3a^2 + 2\sqrt{3a}h + h^2 + k^2 = 16$ gives $h = -\frac{\sqrt{3a}}{2}$
And $h^2 + 2b + 2\sqrt{2b}k + k^2 = 16$ gives $k = -\frac{\sqrt{2b}}{2}$
Centroid G = $\left(\frac{-\sqrt{3a}}{3}, \frac{-\sqrt{2b}}{3}\right)$. Setting $x = \frac{-\sqrt{3a}}{3}$ and $y = \frac{-\sqrt{2b}}{3}$:
$a = 3x^2$ and $b = \frac{9y^2}{2}$
From $h^2 + k^2 = 16$: $\frac{3a}{4} + \frac{2b}{4} = 16$ → $3a + 2b = 64$
Substituting: $9x^2 + 9y^2 = 64$ → $x^2 + y^2 = \frac{64}{9}$
Radius = $\frac{8}{3}$

</details>

- **(D)** $x^{2}+y^{2}-18 x-12 y-25=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $3\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)-12 x-18 y-25=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Circle: $(x-2)^2 + (y-3)^2 = 16$, center O = (2, 3), radius $r = 4$.

In quadrilateral OAPB: $\angle APB = \pi - \pi/3 = 2\pi/3$, so $\angle OPA = \pi/3$.
In right triangle OAP: $\sin(\pi/3) = \frac{4}{OP}$.
$OP = \frac{8}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{8\sqrt{3}}{3}$.
Locus: $(x-2)^2 + (y-3)^2 = \frac{64}{3}$.
$3(x^2 + y^2) - 12x - 18y - 25 = 0$.

### Q19. JEE Main 2019 (11 Jan Shift 2)

A circle cuts a chord of length 4 a on the $x$ -axis and passes through a point on the $y$ -axis, distant $2 \mathrm{~b}$ from the origin. Then the locus of the centre of this circle, is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** a hyperbola
- **(B)** an ellipse
- **(C)** a straight line
- **(D)** a parabola

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) a parabola**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/jee_main/jcChSBnovtRTzVISRP-iGRFAGiX9hts05s5_-2erfPM.original.fullsize.png">


Let centre be $C(h, k)$

$C Q=C P=r$

$\Rightarrow C Q^{2}=C P^{2}$

$(h-0)^{2}+(k \pm 0)^{2}=C M^{2}+M P^{2}$

$h^{2}+(k \pm 2 \mathrm{~b})^{2}=k^{2}+4 a^{2}$

$h^{2}+k^{2}+4 b^{2} \pm 4 b k=k^{2}+4 a^{2}$

Then, the locus of centre $C(h, k)$

$x^{2}+4 b^{2} \pm 4 b y=4 a^{2}$

Hence, the above locus of the centre of circle is a

parabola.

### Q22. JEE Main 2005

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the pair of lines $a x^2+2(a+b) x y+b y^2=0$ lie along diameters of a circle and divide the circle into four sectors such that the area of one of the sectors is thrice the area of another sector then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 a^2-10 a b+3 b^2=0$
- **(B)** $3 a^2-2 a b+3 b^2=0$
- **(C)** $3 a^2+10 a b+3 b^2=0$
- **(D)** $3 a^2+2 a b+3 b^2=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $3 a^2+2 a b+3 b^2=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left|\frac{2 \sqrt{(a+b)^2-a b}}{a+b}\right|=1 \\
& \Rightarrow(a+b)^2=4\left(a^2+b^2+a b\right) \\
& \Rightarrow 3 a^2+3 b^2+2 a b=0 .
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
