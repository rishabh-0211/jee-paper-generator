### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (08 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let O be the vertex of the parabola $y^2=4x$ and its chords OP and OQ are perpendicular to each other. If the locus of the mid-point of the line segment PQ is a conic C, then the length of its latus rectum is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the vertex of the parabola $y^2 = 4x$ be $O(0, 0)$.

Let the coordinates of the points $P$ and $Q$ on the parabola be $(t_1^2, 2t_1)$ and $(t_2^2, 2t_2)$ respectively.

The slope of the chord $OP$ is $m_1 = \dfrac{2t_1 - 0}{t_1^2 - 0} = \dfrac{2}{t_1}$.

The slope of the chord $OQ$ is $m_2 = \dfrac{2t_2 - 0}{t_2^2 - 0} = \dfrac{2}{t_2}$.

Since $OP$ and $OQ$ are perpendicular to each other, $m_1 m_2 = -1$.

$\Rightarrow \left(\dfrac{2}{t_1}\right) \left(\dfrac{2}{t_2}\right) = -1$

$\Rightarrow t_1 t_2 = -4$

Let $M(h, k)$ be the mid-point of the line segment $PQ$. Then,

$h = \dfrac{t_1^2 + t_2^2}{2} \Rightarrow t_1^2 + t_2^2 = 2h$

$k = \dfrac{2t_1 + 2t_2}{2} \Rightarrow t_1 + t_2 = k$

Squaring the equation for $k$, we get:

$k^2 = (t_1 + t_2)^2 = t_1^2 + t_2^2 + 2t_1 t_2$

Substituting the values of $t_1^2 + t_2^2$ and $t_1 t_2$, we obtain:

$k^2 = 2h + 2(-4)$

$k^2 = 2h - 8$

$k^2 = 2(h - 4)$

Replacing $h$ with $x$ and $k$ with $y$, the locus of the mid-point is:

$y^2 = 2(x - 4)$

This represents a parabola of the form $Y^2 = 4AX$, where the length of the latus rectum is $4A = 2$.

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q2. JEE Main 2026 (24 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let the image of parabola $x^{2}=4 y$, in the line $x-y=1$ be $(y+a)^{2}=b(x-c)$, $a, b, c \in \mathrm{~N}$. Then $a+b+c$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** 8
- **(B)** 6
- **(C)** 12
- **(D)** 4

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 6**

**Detailed Solution:**

Reflection of point $(h, k)$ in line $x - y - 1 = 0$:
$\frac{x-h}{1} = \frac{y-k}{-1} = \frac{-2(h-k-1)}{2} = k - h + 1$.
Image: $(k+1,\ h-1)$.
Let $(X, Y) = (k+1,\ h-1)$, so $h = Y+1,\ k = X-1$.
From $h^2 = 4k$: $(Y+1)^2 = 4(X-1)$.
Comparing with $(y+a)^2 = b(x-c)$: $a = 1,\ b = 4,\ c = 1$.
$a + b + c = 1 + 4 + 1 = 6$.

</details>

### Q3. JEE Main 2026 (23 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

An equilateral triangle OAB is inscribed in the parabola $y^{2}=4 x$ with the vertex O at the vertex of the parabola. Then the minimum distance of the circle having $A B$ as a diameter from the origin is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2(8-3 \sqrt{3})$
- **(B)** $2(3+\sqrt{3})$
- **(C)** $4(6+\sqrt{3})$
- **(D)** $4(3-\sqrt{3})$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $4(3-\sqrt{3})$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Parabola $y^2 = 4x$ has vertex $O = (0,0)$. By symmetry, let $A = (t^2, 2t)$, $B = (t^2, -2t)$.
$OA = t\sqrt{t^2+4}$, $AB = 4t$.
For equilateral: $OA = AB \Rightarrow t\sqrt{t^2+4} = 4t \Rightarrow t^2 = 12 \Rightarrow t = 2\sqrt{3}$.
$A = (12, 4\sqrt{3})$, $B = (12, -4\sqrt{3})$.
Circle with $AB$ as diameter: centre $(12, 0)$, radius $= 4\sqrt{3}$.
Minimum distance from origin $= 12 - 4\sqrt{3} = 4(3 - \sqrt{3})$.

</details>

### Q5. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $y^{2}=12 x$ be the parabola with its vertex at O. Let P be a point on the parabola and A be a point on the $x$-axis such that $\angle \mathrm{OPA}=90^{\circ}$. Then the locus of the centroid of such triangles OPA is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $y^{2}-2 x+8=0$
- **(B)** $y^{2}-9 x+6=0$
- **(C)** $y^{2}-4 x+8=0$
- **(D)** $y^{2}-6 x+4=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $y^{2}-2 x+8=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Parabola $y^2 = 12x$, $a = 3$. Let $P = (3t^2, 6t)$, $O = (0,0)$, $A = (h, 0)$.
$\angle OPA = 90^\circ \Rightarrow \vec{PO} \cdot \vec{PA} = 0$:
$(-3t^2)(h - 3t^2) + (-6t)(-6t) = 0 \Rightarrow h = 3t^2 + 12$.
Centroid $G = \left(\frac{0 + 3t^2 + h}{3}, \frac{0 + 6t + 0}{3}\right) = (2t^2 + 4, \ 2t)$.
Let $G = (X, Y)$: $t = Y/2$, $X = Y^2/2 + 4$.
Locus: $y^2 - 2x + 8 = 0$.

</details>
