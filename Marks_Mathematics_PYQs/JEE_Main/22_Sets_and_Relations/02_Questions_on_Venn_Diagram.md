### Q1. JEE Main 2026 (21 January Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $\mathrm{A}=\left\{x:\left|x^{2}-10\right| \leq 6\right\}$ and $\mathrm{B}=\{x:|x-2|>1\}$. Then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B}=(-\infty, 1] \cup(2, \infty)$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{B}-\mathrm{A}=(-\infty,-4) \cup(-2,1) \cup(4, \infty)$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{A}-\mathrm{B}=[2,3)$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{~B}=[-4,-2] \cup[3,4]$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\mathrm{B}-\mathrm{A}=(-\infty,-4) \cup(-2,1) \cup(4, \infty)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$A = \{x : |x^2 - 10| \le 6\}$: $-6 \le x^2 - 10 \le 6 \Rightarrow 4 \le x^2 \le 16$, so $A = [-4, -2] \cup [2, 4]$.
$B = \{x : |x - 2| > 1\} = (-\infty, 1) \cup (3, \infty)$.
$A^c = (-\infty, -4) \cup (-2, 2) \cup (4, \infty)$.
$B - A = B \cap A^c = [(-\infty, 1) \cap A^c] \cup [(3, \infty) \cap A^c]$
$= [(-\infty, -4) \cup (-2, 1)] \cup [(4, \infty)]$
$= (-\infty, -4) \cup (-2, 1) \cup (4, \infty)$.

</details>

### Q16. JEE Main 2012 (07 May Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $X$ and $Y$ are two events such that $P(X \cup Y=) P X \cap(Y . \quad)$
Statement 1: $P\left(X \cap Y^{\prime}=\dot{P} X^{\prime} \cap(Y=0 \quad)\right.$
Statement 2: $P(X) P Y \in 2) P X \cap Y(\quad)$

**Options:**

- **(A)** Statement 1 is false, Statement 2 is true.
- **(B)** Statement 1 is true, Statement 2 is true, Statement 2 is not a correct explanation of Statement 1.
- **(C)** Statement 1 is true, Statement 2 is false.
- **(D)** Statement 1 is true, Statement 2 is true; Statement 2 is a correct explanation of Statement 1.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Statement 1 is true, Statement 2 is true, Statement 2 is not a correct explanation of Statement 1.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $X$ and $Y$ be two events such that
$$
P(X \cup Y)=P(X \cap Y)
$$
We know
$$
\begin{aligned}
& P(X \cup Y)=P(X)+P(Y)-P(X \cap Y) \\
& P(X \cap Y)=P(X)+P(Y)-P(X \cap Y) \\
& \text { (from }(1) \\
& \Rightarrow P(X)+P(Y)=2 P(X \cap Y)
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, Statement $-2$ is true.
Now, $P\left(X \cap Y^{\prime}\right)=P(X)-P(X \cap Y)$
and $P\left(X^{\prime} \cap Y\right)=P(Y)-P(X \cap Y)$
This implies statement-1 is also true.

</details>

### Q17. JEE Main 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $S$ be a non-empty subset of R. Consider the following statement:
$\mathrm{P}$ : There is a rational number $\mathrm{x} \in \mathrm{S}$ such that $\mathrm{x}>0$.
Which of the following statements is the negation of the statement $P$ ?

**Options:**

- **(A)** There is no rational number $x \in S$ such that $x \leq 0$
- **(B)** Every rational number $x \in S$ satisfies $x \leq 0$
- **(C)** $x \in S$ and $x \leq 0 \Rightarrow x$ is not rational
- **(D)** There is a rational number $x \in S$ such that $x \leq 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) Every rational number $x \in S$ satisfies $x \leq 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$P$ : there is a rational number $x \in S$ such that $x>0$
$\sim P$ : Every rational number $\mathrm{x} \in \mathrm{S}$ satisfies $\mathrm{x} \leq 0$
