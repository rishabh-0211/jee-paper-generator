### Q1. JEE Main 2024 (06 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The mean and standard deviation of 20 observations are found to be 10 and 2 . respectively. On rechecking, it was found that an observation by mistake was taken 8 instead of 12. The correct standard deviation is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1.8
- **(B)** 1.94
- **(C)** $\sqrt{3.96}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{3.86}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{3.96}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& \text { Mean }(\overline{\mathrm{x}})=10 \\
& \Rightarrow \frac{\Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}}{20}=10 \\
& \Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}=10 \times 20=200
\end{aligned}$
If 8 is replaced by 12, then $\Sigma x_i=200-8+12=204$
$\therefore$ Correct mean $(\overline{\mathrm{x}})=\frac{\Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}}{20}$
$=\frac{204}{20}=10.2$
$\because$ Standard deviation $=2$
$\therefore$ Variance $=(\text { S.D. })^2=2^2=4$
$\Rightarrow \frac{\Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}^2}{20}-\left(\frac{\Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}}{20}\right)^2=4$
$\Rightarrow \frac{\Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}^2}{20}-(10)^2=4$
$\Rightarrow \frac{\Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}^2}{20}=104$
$\Rightarrow \Sigma x_i^2=2080$
Now, replaced ' 8 ' observations by ' 12 '
Then, $\Sigma \mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}^2=2080-8^2+12^2=2160$
$\therefore$ Variance of removing observations
$\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow \frac{\Sigma x_i^2}{20}-\left(\frac{\Sigma x_i}{20}\right)^2 \\
& \Rightarrow \frac{2160}{20}-(10.2)^2 \\
& \Rightarrow 108-104.04 \\
& \Rightarrow 3.96
\end{aligned}$
Correct standard deviation
$=\sqrt{3.96}$

</details>

### Q6. JEE Main 2014 (12 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $\bar{X}$ and M.D. be the mean and the mean deviation about $\bar{X}$ of $n$ observations $\mathrm{x}_{\mathrm{i}}, \mathrm{i}=1,2$,n. If each of the observations is increased by 5 , then the new mean and the mean deviation about the new mean, respectively, are :

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\bar{X}$, M.D.
- **(B)** $\overline{\mathrm{X}}+5$, M.D.
- **(C)** $\bar{X}$, M.D. $+5$
- **(D)** $\overline{\mathrm{X}}+5$, M.D. $+5$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\overline{\mathrm{X}}+5$, M.D.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $x_i$ be $n$ observations, $i=1,2, \ldots n$ Let $\bar{X}$ be the mean and M.D be the mean deviation about $\bar{X}$. If each observation is increased by 5 then new mean will be $\bar{X}+5$ and new M.D. about new mean will be M.D.
$$
\left(\because \text { Mean }=\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{x_i}{n}\right)
$$

</details>

### Q7. JEE Main 2013 (25 Apr Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In a set of $2 n$ observations, half of them are equal to ' $\mathrm{a}$ ' and the remaining hall are equal to ' $-\mathrm{a}^{\prime}$ '. If the standard deviation of all the observations is 2 ; then the value of $|a|$ is :

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** 4
- **(D)** $2 \sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Clearly mean $\mathrm{A}=0$
Now, standard deviation $\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\Sigma(x-\mathrm{A})^2}{2 n}}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
2=& \sqrt{\frac{(a-0)^2+(a-0)^2+\ldots \ldots+(0-a)^2+\ldots . .}{2 n}} \\
&=\sqrt{\frac{a^2 \cdot 2 n}{2 n}}=|a|
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, $|a|=2$

</details>

### Q8. JEE Main 2012 (12 May Online)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the mean of $4,7,2,8,6$ and a is 7 , then the mean deviation from the median of these observations is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 8
- **(B)** 5
- **(C)** 1
- **(D)** 3

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 3**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given observations are 4, 7, 2, 8, 6, $a$ and mean is 7.
We know
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text { Mean }=\frac{4+7+2+8+6+a}{6} \\
& \Rightarrow 7=\frac{4+7+2+8+6+a}{6} \Rightarrow a=15
\end{aligned}
$$
Now, given observations can be written in ascending order which is $2,4,6,7,8,15$ Since, No. of observation is even
$\therefore$ Median
$=\frac{\left(\frac{6}{2}\right) \text { th observation }+\left(\frac{6}{2}+1\right) \text { th observation }}{2}$
$=\frac{\text { 3rd observation }+4 \text { th observation }}{2}$
$=\frac{6+7}{2}=\frac{13}{2}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text { Now, Mean deviation }=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^6\left|x_i-\frac{13}{2}\right|}{6} \\
& =\frac{\left|4-\frac{13}{2}\right|+\left|7-\frac{13}{2}\right|+\left|2-\frac{13}{2}\right|+\left|8-\frac{13}{2}\right|+\left|6-\frac{13}{2}\right|+\left|15-\frac{13}{2}\right|}{6} \\
& =\frac{\frac{5}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{9}{2}+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{17}{2}}{6}=\frac{18}{6}=3 \\
&
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

If the mean deviation about the median of the numbers $\mathrm{a}, 2 \mathrm{a}, \ldots, 50 \mathrm{a}$ is 50 , then $|\mathrm{a}|$ equals

$\Rightarrow 625 \mathrm{a}=2500 \Rightarrow \mathrm{a}=4$

### Q10. JEE Main 2009

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the mean deviation of number $1,1+d, 1+2 d, \ldots ., 1+100 d$ from their mean is 255 , then the $d$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10.0$
- **(B)** $20.0$
- **(C)** $10.1$
- **(D)** $20.2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $10.1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \text { Mean }(\bar{x})=\frac{\text { sum of quantities }}{n}=\frac{\frac{n}{2}(a+1)}{\not n}=\frac{1}{2}[1+1+100 d]=1+50 d \\
& \text { M.D. }=\frac{1}{n} \sum\left|x_i-\bar{x}\right| \Rightarrow 255=\frac{1}{101}[50 d+49 d+48 d+\ldots .+d+0+d+\ldots \ldots+50 d]=\frac{2 d}{101}\left[\frac{50 \times 51}{2}\right] \\
& \Rightarrow d=\frac{255 \times 101}{50 \times 51}=10.1
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q11. JEE Main 2004

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

In a series of $2 n$ observations, half of them equal a and remaining half equal $-a$. If the standard deviation of the observations is 2 , then $|a|$ equals

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{n}$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** 2
- **(D)** $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{n}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

$x_i=a$ for $i=1,2, \ldots, n$ and $x_i=-a$ for $i=n, \ldots ., 2 n$
S.D. $=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2 n} \sum_{i=1}^{2 n}\left(x_i-\bar{x}\right)^2} \Rightarrow 2=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2 n} \sum_{i=1}^{2 n} x_i^2} \quad\left(\right.$ Since $\left.\sum_{i=1}^{2 n} x_i=0\right) \Rightarrow 2=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2 n} \cdot 2 n a^2} \Rightarrow|a|=2$

</details>
