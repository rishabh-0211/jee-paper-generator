### Q6. JEE Advanced 2008 (Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

<strong>Paragraph:</strong>
Let $A, B, C$ be three sets of complex numbers as defined below.
$A=\{z ; \operatorname{lm} z \geq 1\} ; B=\{z:|z-2-i|=3\} ; C=\{z: \operatorname{Re}((1-i) z)=\sqrt{2}\}$.<strong>Question:</strong>
The number of elements in the set $A \cap B \cap C$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0
- **(B)** 1
- **(C)** 2
- **(D)** $\infty$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $z=x+i y$
Set $A$ corresponds to the region $y \geq 1$.
Set $B$ consists of points lying on the circle, centred at $(2,1)$ and radius 3 , i.e., $x^2+y^2-4 x-2 y=4$

$$
\text { Set } C \text { consists of points lying on the } x+y=\sqrt{2}
$$

Clearly, there is only one point of intersection of the line
$$
x+y=\sqrt{2} \text { and circle } x^2+y^2-4 x-2 y=4 .

### Q7. JEE Advanced 2007 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $|z|=1$ and $z \neq \pm 1$, then all the values of $\frac{z}{1-z^2}$ lie on

**Options:**

- **(A)** a line not passing through the origin
- **(B)** $|z|=\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** the X-axis
- **(D)** the Y-axis

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) the Y-axis**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $z=\cos \theta+i \sin \theta$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{z}{1-z^2} & =\frac{\cos \theta+i \sin \theta}{1-(\cos 2 \theta+i \sin 2 \theta)} \\
& =\frac{\cos \theta+i \sin \theta}{2 \sin ^2 \theta-2 i \sin \theta \cos \theta} \\
& =\frac{\cos \theta+i \sin \theta}{-2 i \sin \theta(\cos \theta+i \sin \theta)} \\
& =\frac{i}{2 \sin \theta}
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence, $\frac{z}{1-z^2}$ lies on the imaginary axis, i.e. $x=0$.
ALITER
Let
$E=\frac{z}{1-z^2}=\frac{z}{z \bar{z}-z^2}=\frac{1}{\bar{z}-z}$ which is imaginary.

</details>

### Q8. JEE Advanced 2007 (Paper 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A man walks a distance of 3 units from the origin towards the North-East $\left(\mathrm{N} 45^{\circ} \mathrm{E}\right)$ direction. From there, he walks a distance of 4 units towards the North-West ( $\mathrm{N} 45^{\circ} \mathrm{W}$ ) direction to reach a point $P$. Then, the position of $P$ in the Argand plane is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 e^{i \pi / 4}+4 i$
- **(B)** $(3-4 i) e^{i \pi / 4}$
- **(C)** $(4+3 i) e^{i \pi / 4}$
- **(D)** $(3+4 i) e^{i \pi / 4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $(3+4 i) e^{i \pi / 4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $O A=3$, so that the complex number associated with $A$ is $3 e^{i \pi / 4}$. If $z$ is the complex number associated with $P$, then
$$
\begin{array}{rlrl} 
& & \frac{z-3 e^{i \pi / 4}}{0-3 e^{i \pi / 4}} & =\frac{4}{3} e^{-i \pi / 2}=-\frac{4 i}{3} \\
\Rightarrow & 3 z-9 e^{i \pi / 4} & =12 i^{i \pi / 4} \\
\Rightarrow & z & z & =(3+4 i) e^{i \pi / 4}
\end{array}
$$
