### Q4. JEE Advanced 2010 (Paper 1)

*Type:* `numerical` | *Level:* `3`

Let $S_k, k=1,2, \ldots, 100$, denote the sum of the infinite geometric series whose first term is $\frac{k-1}{k !}$ and the common ratio is $\frac{1}{k}$. Then the value of $\frac{100^2}{100 !}+\sum_{k=1}^{100}\left|\left(k^2-3 k+1\right) S_k\right|$ is

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Value:** **4**

**Detailed Solution:**

We have, $S_k=\frac{\frac{k-1}{k !}}{1-\frac{1}{k}}=\frac{1}{(k-1) !}$
Now,
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \left(k^2-3 k+1\right) S_k=\{(k-2)(k-1)-1\} \\
& =\frac{1}{(k-3) !}-\frac{1}{(k-1) !} \\
& \Rightarrow \sum_{k=1}^{100}\left|\left(k^2-3 k+1\right) S_k\right| \\
& =1+1+2-\left(\frac{1}{99 !}+\frac{1}{98 !}\right)=4-\frac{100^2}{100 !}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\Rightarrow \quad \frac{100^2}{100 !}+\sum_{k=1}^{100}\left|\left(k^2-3 k+1\right) S_k\right|=4
$$

</details>

### Q5. JEE Advanced 2009 (Paper 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the sum of first $n$ terms of an $\mathrm{AP}$ is $\mathrm{cn}^2$, then the sum of squares of these $n$ terms is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{n\left(4 n^2-1\right) c^2}{6}$
- **(B)** $\frac{n\left(4 n^2+1\right) c^2}{3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{n\left(4 n^2-1\right) c^2}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{n\left(4 n^2+1\right) c^2}{6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{n\left(4 n^2-1\right) c^2}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $S_n=c^2$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& S_{n-1}=c(n-1)^2=c n^2+c-2 c n \\
& \therefore T_n=2 c n-c \quad\left(\because T_n=S_n-S_{n-1}\right) \\
& T_n^2=(2 c n-c)^2=4 c^2 n^2+c^2-4 c^2 n \\
& \therefore \text { Sum }=\Sigma T_n^2=\frac{4 c^2 \cdot n(n+1)(2 n+1)}{6} \\
& =\frac{2 c^2 n(n+1)(2 n+1)+3 n c^2-6 c^2 n(n+1)}{3} \\
& =\frac{n c^2\left[4 n^2+6 n+2+3-6 n-6\right]}{3} \\
& =\frac{n c^2\left(4 n^2-1\right)}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
