### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The coordinates of the point on the curve $x^2 + y^2 = 8$ which are nearest to the point $(2, 0)$ are:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(2,\,2)$
- **(B)** $(\sqrt{2},\,\sqrt{6})$
- **(C)** $(2\sqrt{2},\,0)$
- **(D)** $(\sqrt{6},\,\sqrt{2})$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $(2\sqrt{2},\,0)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the point on the curve be $(x, y)$.

The square of the distance from $(x, y)$ to $(2, 0)$ is given by $D = (x - 2)^2 + y^2$.

Since the point lies on the curve $x^2 + y^2 = 8$, we can substitute $y^2 = 8 - x^2$ into the distance equation.

$D = (x - 2)^2 + 8 - x^2$

$D = x^2 - 4x + 4 + 8 - x^2$

$D = 12 - 4x$

To find the nearest point, we need to minimize the distance $D$. This requires maximizing the value of $x$.

For the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 8$, the maximum possible value of $x$ is the radius of the circle, which is $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$.

Substituting $x = 2\sqrt{2}$ into the equation of the curve, we get $y^2 = 8 - (2\sqrt{2})^2 = 0$, which gives $y = 0$.

Thus, the coordinates of the nearest point are $(2\sqrt{2}, 0)$.

Answer: $(2\sqrt{2},\,0)$

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The least distance of the point $A(10,7)$ from the circle $x^2+\mathrm{y}^2-4 x-2 \mathrm{y}-20=0$ is length of seg. AM . If $\mathrm{MM}^{\prime}$ is the diameter of the circle, then the lengths of AM and $\mathrm{AM}^{\prime}$ are respectively _______ , ______ units

**Options:**

- **(A)** 5,10
- **(B)** 4,15
- **(C)** 5,15
- **(D)** 2,10

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 5,15**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the given circle is $x^2+y^2-4x-2y-20=0$.

Comparing with the standard equation $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$, we get $g = -2$, $f = -1$, and $c = -20$.

The center of the circle is $C(-g, -f) \equiv (2, 1)$.

The radius of the circle is $r = \sqrt{g^2+f^2-c} = \sqrt{(-2)^2+(-1)^2-(-20)} = \sqrt{4+1+20} = 5$.

The distance of point $A(10, 7)$ from the center $C(2, 1)$ is given by:

$AC = \sqrt{(10-2)^2 + (7-1)^2} = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64+36} = 10$

Since $AC > r$, the point $A$ lies outside the circle.

The least distance of point $A$ from the circle is $AM = AC - r = 10 - 5 = 5$.

The greatest distance of point $A$ from the circle is $AM' = AC + r = 10 + 5 = 15$.

Therefore, the lengths of $AM$ and $AM'$ are $5$ and $15$ units respectively.

Answer: 5,15

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The minimum distance and maximum distance of the point $\mathrm{P}(2,-7)$ from the circle $x^2+y^2-14 x-10 y-151=0$ are respectively _______ units

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2,28
- **(B)** 5,25
- **(C)** 6,24
- **(D)** 3,27

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 2,28**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the circle is $x^2+y^2-14x-10y-151=0$.

Comparing with the standard equation $x^2+y^2+2gx+2fy+c=0$, we get $2g = -14 \Rightarrow g = -7$ and $2f = -10 \Rightarrow f = -5$.

The center of the circle is $C(-g, -f) \equiv (7, 5)$.

The radius of the circle is $r = \sqrt{g^2+f^2-c} = \sqrt{(-7)^2+(-5)^2-(-151)} = \sqrt{49+25+151} = \sqrt{225} = 15$.

The distance of the point $P(2, -7)$ from the center $C(7, 5)$ is $d = \sqrt{(7-2)^2 + (5-(-7))^2} = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25+144} = 13$.

Since $d < r$, the point $P$ lies inside the circle.

The minimum distance of the point $P$ from the circle is $|r - d| = |15 - 13| = 2$ units.

The maximum distance of the point $P$ from the circle is $r + d = 15 + 13 = 28$ units.

Answer: 2,28

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\left(\mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{i}}, \dfrac{1}{\mathrm{~m}_{\mathrm{i}}}\right), \mathrm{m}_{\mathrm{i}}\gt0, \mathrm{i}=1,2,3,4$ are four distinct points on a circle, then the product $\mathrm{m}_1 \mathrm{~m}_2 \mathrm{~m}_3 \mathrm{~m}_4$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** -1
- **(B)** 1
- **(C)** 0
- **(D)** 2

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the equation of the circle be $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$.

Since the points $\left(m_i, \dfrac{1}{m_i}\right)$ lie on the circle, substituting $x = m$ and $y = \dfrac{1}{m}$ gives:

$m^2 + \dfrac{1}{m^2} + 2gm + 2f\left(\dfrac{1}{m}\right) + c = 0$

Multiplying the entire equation by $m^2$, we get:

$m^4 + 1 + 2gm^3 + 2fm + cm^2 = 0$

Rearranging the terms in descending powers of $m$:

$m^4 + 2gm^3 + cm^2 + 2fm + 1 = 0$

This is a polynomial equation of degree 4 in $m$. Since the four points are distinct, $m_1, m_2, m_3, m_4$ are the four roots of this equation.

By the theory of equations, the product of the roots of a polynomial $am^4 + bm^3 + cm^2 + dm + e = 0$ is given by $\dfrac{e}{a}$.

Therefore, the product of the roots is $m_1 m_2 m_3 m_4 = \dfrac{1}{1} = 1$.

Answer: 1

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $(3, \lambda \lambda \lambda \lambda)$ and $(5,6)$ are the conjugate points to the curve $x^{2}+y^{2}=3$, then $\lambda \lambda \lambda \lambda$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-1$
- **(B)** 1
- **(C)** $-2$
- **(D)** 2

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $-2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

If the $(3, \lambda \lambda \lambda \lambda)$ and $(5,6)$ are the conjugate points to the curve $x^{2}+y^{2}=3$, then
$\begin{aligned} x_{1} x_{2}+y_{1} y_{2} &=3 \\ \Rightarrow \quad(3)(5)+(\lambda \lambda \lambda \lambda) \cdot(6) &=3 \end{aligned}$
$\Rightarrow \quad 15+6(\lambda \lambda \lambda \lambda)=3$
$\Rightarrow 6(\lambda \lambda \lambda \lambda)=-12$
$\Rightarrow \lambda \lambda \lambda \lambda=-2$

</details>
