### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the length of the tangent segment from the point $(8, -3)$ to the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 2x + ky - 23 = 0$ is $\sqrt{10}$, then the value of $k$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$
- **(B)** $8$
- **(C)** $24$
- **(D)** $34$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $8$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The length of the tangent from a point $(x_1, y_1)$ to the circle $S = 0$ is given by $\sqrt{S_1}$.

Given the point $(8, -3)$ and the circle $S \equiv x^2 + y^2 - 2x + ky - 23 = 0$.

Substituting the point into the circle's equation to find $S_1$:

$S_1 = (8)^2 + (-3)^2 - 2(8) + k(-3) - 23$

$S_1 = 64 + 9 - 16 - 3k - 23$

$S_1 = 34 - 3k$

The length of the tangent is given as $\sqrt{10}$.

$\sqrt{34 - 3k} = \sqrt{10}$

Squaring both sides:

$34 - 3k = 10$

$3k = 24$

$k = 8$

Answer: $8$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the tangent to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 5$ at point $(1, -2)$ also touches the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 6y + k = 0$, then $\dfrac{k}{5} = \cdots\cdots\cdots$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $20$
- **(B)** $4$
- **(C)** $5$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{19}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the tangent to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 5$ at the point $(1, -2)$ is given by $T = 0$:

$x(1) + y(-2) = 5 \Rightarrow x - 2y - 5 = 0$

This line also touches the second circle $x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 6y + k = 0$.

The center of the second circle is $(4, -3)$ and its radius is $r = \sqrt{4^2 + (-3)^2 - k} = \sqrt{25 - k}$.

For the line to be a tangent to the second circle, the perpendicular distance from the center $(4, -3)$ to the line $x - 2y - 5 = 0$ must be equal to the radius of the circle.

$\dfrac{|4 - 2(-3) - 5|}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-2)^2}} = \sqrt{25 - k}$

$\dfrac{|4 + 6 - 5|}{\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{25 - k}$

$\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{5}} = \sqrt{25 - k}$

$\sqrt{5} = \sqrt{25 - k}$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$5 = 25 - k \Rightarrow k = 20$

Therefore, $\dfrac{k}{5} = \dfrac{20}{5} = 4$.

Answer: $4$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The length of the tangent segment drawn from point $P$ to the circle, $x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 2y - 2 = 0$ is $4\sqrt{2}$ units. If $\lambda_{max}$ and $\lambda_{min}$ are the maximum and minimum distances of point $P$ from the circle, then $\lambda_{max} + \lambda_{min}$ is equal to....

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6$
- **(B)** $8$
- **(C)** $12$
- **(D)** $16$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $12$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the circle is $x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 2y - 2 = 0$

The center of the circle is $C(1, 1)$ and its radius is $r = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2 - (-2)} = 2$

Let $d$ be the distance of point $P$ from the center $C$. The length of the tangent $L$ from $P$ to the circle is given by $L = \sqrt{d^2 - r^2}$

Given $L = 4\sqrt{2}$, we have $(4\sqrt{2})^2 = d^2 - 2^2$

$32 = d^2 - 4 \Rightarrow d^2 = 36 \Rightarrow d = 6$

The maximum distance of point $P$ from the circle is $\lambda_{max} = d + r$

The minimum distance of point $P$ from the circle is $\lambda_{min} = d - r$

Therefore, $\lambda_{max} + \lambda_{min} = (d + r) + (d - r) = 2d$

$\lambda_{max} + \lambda_{min} = 2(6) = 12$

Answer: $12$

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let PA and PB be the tangent segments drawn from point P$(6, 8)$ to the circle with the centre at origin O. The radius of circle for which the area of quadrilateral PAOB is maximum, is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5$
- **(B)** $5\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{5}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $5\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the radius of the circle be $r$.

The centre of the circle is $O(0, 0)$ and the external point is $P(6, 8)$.

The distance between $O$ and $P$ is:

$OP = \sqrt{(6-0)^2 + (8-0)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$

The length of the tangent segments $PA$ and $PB$ from $P$ to the circle is:

$PA = PB = \sqrt{OP^2 - r^2} = \sqrt{100 - r^2}$

The quadrilateral $PAOB$ consists of two congruent right-angled triangles, $\triangle OAP$ and $\triangle OBP$.

The area of the quadrilateral $PAOB$ is:

$A = 2 \times \text{Area of } \triangle OAP = 2 \times \left( \dfrac{1}{2} \times OA \times PA \right) = r \sqrt{100 - r^2}$

To maximize the area $A$, we can maximize its square, $A^2$:

$f(r) = A^2 = r^2 (100 - r^2) = 100r^2 - r^4$

Differentiating $f(r)$ with respect to $r$:

$f'(r) = 200r - 4r^3$

Setting the derivative to zero for maximum/minimum:

$200r - 4r^3 = 0$

$4r(50 - r^2) = 0$

Since $r > 0$, we have $50 - r^2 = 0 \Rightarrow r^2 = 50$

$r = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$

To verify it is a maximum, check the second derivative:

$f''(r) = 200 - 12r^2$

At $r = 5\sqrt{2}$, $f''(5\sqrt{2}) = 200 - 12(50) = 200 - 600 = -400 < 0$

Thus, the area is maximum when the radius is $5\sqrt{2}$.

</details>

$\dfrac{y+1}{2} = x(1) \Rightarrow 2x - y - 1 = 0$

The equation of the family of circles touching the line $2x - y - 1 = 0$ at $(1,1)$ is:

$(x - 1)^2 + (y - 1)^2 + \lambda(2x - y - 1) = 0$

Since the circle passes through the point $(0,1)$, substituting $x = 0$ and $y = 1$ gives:

$(0 - 1)^2 + (1 - 1)^2 + \lambda(2(0) - 1 - 1) = 0$

$1 - 2\lambda = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$x^2 + y^2 - x - \dfrac{5}{2}y + \dfrac{3}{2} = 0$

The coordinates of the centre of the circle $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$ are $(-g, -f)$.

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The number of circles passing through the origin and touching the lines $x + y = 1$ and $x - y = 1$ is $\ldots$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $3$
- **(D)** $4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given lines are $x + y - 1 = 0$ and $x - y - 1 = 0$.

The center of a circle touching both lines must lie on one of their angle bisectors.

The equations of the angle bisectors are given by:

$\dfrac{x + y - 1}{\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2}} = \pm \dfrac{x - y - 1}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}}$

$\dfrac{x + y - 1}{\sqrt{2}} = \pm \dfrac{x - y - 1}{\sqrt{2}}$

Taking the positive sign, we get $2y = 0 \Rightarrow y = 0$.

Taking the negative sign, we get $2x - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 1$.

Case 1: Let the center lie on the bisector $y = 0$. Let the center be $(h, 0)$.

Since the circle passes through the origin $(0, 0)$, its radius is $r = \sqrt{(h-0)^2 + (0-0)^2} = |h|$.

The perpendicular distance from the center $(h, 0)$ to the line $x + y - 1 = 0$ must be equal to the radius $r$.

$\dfrac{|h + 0 - 1|}{\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2}} = |h|$

$\dfrac{|h - 1|}{\sqrt{2}} = |h|$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$\dfrac{(h - 1)^2}{2} = h^2$

$h^2 - 2h + 1 = 2h^2$

$h^2 + 2h - 1 = 0$

This is a quadratic equation in $h$ and its discriminant is $\Delta = 2^2 - 4(1)(-1) = 8 > 0$. Thus, it has two distinct real roots ($h = -1 \pm \sqrt{2}$). This gives $2$ such circles.

Case 2: Let the center lie on the bisector $x = 1$. Let the center be $(1, k)$.

Since the circle passes through the origin $(0, 0)$, its radius is $r = \sqrt{(1-0)^2 + (k-0)^2} = \sqrt{1 + k^2}$.

The perpendicular distance from the center $(1, k)$ to the line $x + y - 1 = 0$ must be equal to the radius $r$.

$\dfrac{|1 + k - 1|}{\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2}} = \sqrt{1 + k^2}$

$\dfrac{|k|}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{1 + k^2}$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$\dfrac{k^2}{2} = 1 + k^2$

$k^2 = 2 + 2k^2$

$k^2 = -2$

This equation has no real solution for $k$. Thus, no circle exists with its center on $x = 1$.

Therefore, the total number of such circles is $2$.

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The area of the region (in sq. unit) bounded by x-axis, the tangent and normal to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, drawn at a point $(1, \sqrt{3})$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{3}$
- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $2\sqrt{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2\sqrt{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Equation of the circle is $x^2 + y^2 = 4$.

Equation of the tangent at the point $(1, \sqrt{3})$ is given by $xx_1 + yy_1 = r^2$, which is $x + \sqrt{3}y = 4$.

Equation of the normal at $(1, \sqrt{3})$ passes through the origin $(0,0)$, so its equation is $y = \sqrt{3}x$.

The region bounded by the tangent, normal, and the x-axis ($y=0$) forms a triangle. We find its vertices by solving the equations of the lines pairwise.

Intersection of the normal and the x-axis is $(0, 0)$.

Intersection of the tangent and the x-axis is obtained by putting $y=0$ in $x + \sqrt{3}y = 4$, which gives $x=4$. The point is $(4, 0)$.

Intersection of the tangent and the normal is the point of contact $(1, \sqrt{3})$.

The base of the triangle lies on the x-axis with length $4 - 0 = 4$.

The height of the triangle is the y-coordinate of the point $(1, \sqrt{3})$, which is $\sqrt{3}$.

Area of the triangle $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times \sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3}$ sq. units.

### Q15. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A pair of tangents are drawn to the circle $x^2+y^2+6 x-4 y-12=0$ from a point $\mathrm{P}(-4,-5)$, then the area enclosed between these tangents and the area of the circle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $25\left(\frac{4+\pi}{4}\right)$ sq. units
- **(B)** $25\left(\frac{4+\pi}{2}\right)$ sq. units
- **(C)** $25\left(\frac{4-\pi}{2}\right)$ sq. units
- **(D)** $25\left(\frac{4-\pi}{4}\right)$ sq. units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $25\left(\frac{4-\pi}{4}\right)$ sq. units**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the circle is $x^2+y^2+6x-4y-12=0$.

The center of the circle is $C(-3, 2)$ and its radius is $r = \sqrt{3^2 + (-2)^2 - (-12)} = \sqrt{9 + 4 + 12} = 5$.

$PA = \sqrt{PC^2 - r^2} = \sqrt{50 - 25} = \sqrt{25} = 5$

In the right-angled $\triangle PAC$, we have $PA = 5$ and $AC = r = 5$.
The area of the quadrilateral $PACB$ formed by the two tangents and the two radii is:
Area of $PACB = 2 \times \text{Area of } \triangle PAC = 2 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2} \times PA \times AC\right) = 5 \times 5 = 25$ sq. units.

Since $PA = AC = 5$, $\triangle PAC$ is an isosceles right-angled triangle, which gives $\angle PCA = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
By symmetry, $\angle PCB = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
The total angle subtended by the minor arc $AB$ at the center is $\angle ACB = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

The area of the sector $ACB$ is:
Area of sector = $\dfrac{1}{2} r^2 \theta = \dfrac{1}{2} \times (5)^2 \times \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \dfrac{25\pi}{4}$ sq. units.

The area enclosed between the tangents and the circle is the area of the quadrilateral $PACB$ minus the area of the sector $ACB$:
Required Area = $25 - \dfrac{25\pi}{4} = 25\left(\dfrac{4 - \pi}{4}\right)$ sq. units.

Answer: $25\left(\frac{4-\pi}{4}\right)$ sq. units
