### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The angle between the tangents drawn from the point $(1, 1)$ to the circle $2x^2 + 2y^2 - 8x + 4y + 5 = 0$ is …………

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the circle is $2x^2 + 2y^2 - 8x + 4y + 5 = 0$.

Dividing by $2$, we get the standard form:

$x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 2y + \dfrac{5}{2} = 0$

The center of the circle is $C(2, -1)$.

The radius of the circle is $r = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + (1)^2 - \dfrac{5}{2}} = \sqrt{5 - \dfrac{5}{2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}$.

The point from which tangents are drawn is $P(1, 1)$.

The distance between the point $P$ and the center $C$ is $PC = \sqrt{(2 - 1)^2 + (-1 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4} = \sqrt{5}$.

Let the angle between the tangents be $2\theta$. From the right-angled triangle formed by the center, the point $P$, and the point of tangency, we have:

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{r}{PC} = \dfrac{\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}}}{\sqrt{5}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

This gives $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

The angle between the tangents is $2\theta = 2 \times \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The angle between the tangents drawn from the origin to the circle $(x-7)^2 + (y+1)^2 = 25$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $45^\circ$
- **(B)** $90^\circ$
- **(C)** $60^\circ$
- **(D)** $30^\circ$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $90^\circ$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the circle is $(x-7)^2 + (y+1)^2 = 25$.

The center of the circle is $C(7, -1)$ and its radius is $r = 5$.

Let the tangents be drawn from the origin $O(0, 0)$ and the angle between them be $2\theta$.

The distance between the origin and the center of the circle is $OC = \sqrt{(7-0)^2 + (-1-0)^2} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$.

From the right-angled triangle formed by the origin, the center, and the point of contact, we have $\sin \theta = \dfrac{r}{OC}$.

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{5}{5\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$\theta = 45^\circ$

The angle between the tangents is $2\theta = 90^\circ$.

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The locus of point of intersection of the tangents to the circle $x^2+y^2=16$, such that the angle between them is $60^{\circ}$, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x^2+y^2=4$
- **(B)** $x^2+y^2=64$
- **(C)** $x^2+y^2=32$
- **(D)** $x^2+y^2=48$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $x^2+y^2=64$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the point of intersection of the tangents be $P(h, k)$.

The equation of the given circle is $x^2+y^2=16$, which has its center at $O(0,0)$ and radius $a=4$.

The angle between the tangents from $P$ is given as $\theta = 60^{\circ}$. The line joining the center $O$ to $P$ bisects the angle between the tangents.

In the right-angled triangle formed by the center $O$, the point of contact $T$, and $P$, we have:

$\sin\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2}\right) = \dfrac{OT}{OP}$

$\sin(30^{\circ}) = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2}}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{4}{\sqrt{h^2+k^2}}$

$\sqrt{h^2+k^2} = 8$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$h^2+k^2 = 64$

Replacing $(h, k)$ with $(x, y)$, the locus of the point is $x^2+y^2 = 64$.

Answer: $x^2+y^2=64$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let PQ and RS be tangents at the extremities of the diameter PR of a circle of radius r. If PS and RQ intersect at a point $X$ on the circumference of the circle, then 2 r equals

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{\mathrm{PQ} \cdot \mathrm{RS}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\mathrm{PQ}+\mathrm{RS}}{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2 \cdot \mathrm{PQ} \cdot \mathrm{RS}}{\mathrm{PQ}+\mathrm{RS}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathrm{PQ}^2+\mathrm{RS}^2}{2}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\sqrt{\mathrm{PQ} \cdot \mathrm{RS}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the equation of the circle be $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$.

Let the extremities of the diameter $PR$ be $P(0, -r)$ and $R(0, r)$.

The equations of the tangents at $P$ and $R$ are $y = -r$ and $y = r$ respectively.

Let the coordinates of $Q$ on the tangent at $P$ be $(a, -r)$ and $S$ on the tangent at $R$ be $(b, r)$. Thus, $PQ = a$ and $RS = b$.

The equation of the line $PS$ passing through $P(0, -r)$ and $S(b, r)$ is:

$y + r = \dfrac{r - (-r)}{b - 0}(x - 0) \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{2r}{b}x - r$

The equation of the line $RQ$ passing through $R(0, r)$ and $Q(a, -r)$ is:

$y - r = \dfrac{-r - r}{a - 0}(x - 0) \Rightarrow y = -\dfrac{2r}{a}x + r$

To find the intersection point $X$, equate the expressions for $y$:

$\dfrac{2r}{b}x - r = -\dfrac{2r}{a}x + r$

$2rx \left( \dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b} \right) = 2r$

$x \left( \dfrac{a+b}{ab} \right) = 1 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{ab}{a+b}$

Substituting $x$ into the equation of $PS$ gives $y$:

$y = \dfrac{2r}{b} \left( \dfrac{ab}{a+b} \right) - r = \dfrac{2ar}{a+b} - r = r \left( \dfrac{a-b}{a+b} \right)$

Since the point $X$ lies on the circle $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$, substitute the coordinates of $X$:

$\left( \dfrac{ab}{a+b} \right)^2 + r^2 \left( \dfrac{a-b}{a+b} \right)^2 = r^2$

$a^2b^2 + r^2(a-b)^2 = r^2(a+b)^2$

$a^2b^2 = r^2 \left[ (a+b)^2 - (a-b)^2 \right]$

$a^2b^2 = r^2(4ab)$

Since $a$ and $b$ are non-zero lengths, dividing by $ab$ yields:

$ab = 4r^2$

$(2r)^2 = ab$

$2r = \sqrt{ab}$

Substituting $a = PQ$ and $b = RS$, we get:

$2r = \sqrt{PQ \cdot RS}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\mathrm{PQ} \cdot \mathrm{RS}}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two tangents drawn from $\mathrm{P}(1,7)$ to the circle $x^2+y^2=25$, touch the circle at Q and R respectively. The area of the quadrilateral PQOR is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 16 sq. units
- **(B)** 36 sq. units
- **(C)** 25 sq. units
- **(D)** 49 sq. units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 25 sq. units**

**Detailed Solution:**

The center of the given circle $x^2+y^2=25$ is $O(0,0)$ and its radius is $r = 5$.

The length of the tangent from $P(1,7)$ to the circle is given by $L = \sqrt{x_1^2 + y_1^2 - r^2}$.

$L = \sqrt{1^2 + 7^2 - 25} = \sqrt{1 + 49 - 25} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

The quadrilateral $PQOR$ is formed by two congruent right-angled triangles, $\triangle PQO$ and $\triangle PRO$.

The area of $\triangle PQO$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} \times OQ \times PQ = \dfrac{1}{2} \times r \times L = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 5 = \dfrac{25}{2}$.

The total area of the quadrilateral $PQOR$ is $2 \times \dfrac{25}{2} = 25$ sq. units.

Answer: 25 sq. units

</details>
