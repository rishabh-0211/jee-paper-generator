### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The centre and radius of the circle $(a+1)x^2 + 3y^2 - 6x + 9y + a + 4 = 0$ are respectively ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(-1, \dfrac{3}{2}\right), \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$
- **(B)** $\left(-1, -\dfrac{3}{2}\right), \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$
- **(C)** $\left(1, -\dfrac{3}{2}\right), \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$
- **(D)** $\left(1, \dfrac{3}{2}\right), \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\left(1, -\dfrac{3}{2}\right), \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the given equation to represent a circle, the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ must be equal.

$a + 1 = 3 \Rightarrow a = 2$

Substituting $a = 2$ into the given equation:

$3x^2 + 3y^2 - 6x + 9y + 6 = 0$

Dividing by $3$, we get the standard form of the circle:

$x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 3y + 2 = 0$

Comparing with $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$, we have $2g = -2$, $2f = 3$, and $c = 2$.

The centre of the circle is $(-g, -f) = \left(1, -\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$.

The radius of the circle is $r = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c} = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2 - 2} = \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{9}{4} - 2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{5}{4}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$.

Answer: $\left(1, -\dfrac{3}{2}\right), \dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{2}$

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the equation $3x^2 + (3 - p)xy + qy^2 - 2px = 8pq$ represents a circle, then the area (in sq. units) of this circle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5\pi$
- **(B)** $9\pi$
- **(C)** $25\pi$
- **(D)** $81\pi$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $25\pi$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For a general second-degree equation $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$ to represent a circle, the coefficient of $xy$ must be zero and the coefficients of $x^2$ and $y^2$ must be equal.

From the given equation $3x^2 + (3 - p)xy + qy^2 - 2px = 8pq$, we have:

Coefficient of $xy = 0 \Rightarrow 3 - p = 0 \Rightarrow p = 3$

Coefficient of $x^2 = \text{Coefficient of } y^2 \Rightarrow q = 3$

Substituting $p = 3$ and $q = 3$ into the given equation:

$3x^2 + 3y^2 - 6x = 72$

Dividing by $3$, we get:

$x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 24 = 0$

Comparing this with the standard equation of a circle $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$, we get $g = -1$, $f = 0$, and $c = -24$.

The radius of the circle is $r = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c} = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 0^2 - (-24)} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

The area of the circle is $\pi r^2 = \pi (5)^2 = 25\pi$.

Answer: $25\pi$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\lambda$ is the perpendicular distance of a point $\mathrm{P}$ on the circle $x^2+y^2+2 x+2 y-3=0$, from the line $2 x+y+13=0$, then maximum possible value of $\lambda$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \sqrt{5}$
- **(B)** $3 \sqrt{5}$
- **(C)** $4 \sqrt{5}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{5}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3 \sqrt{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation of the circle is $x^2+y^2+2 x+2 y-3=0$
Which can be written as: $(x+1)^2+(y+1)^2=5$ It is a circle with centre $(-1,-1)$ and radius $\sqrt{5}$ Given line is: $2 x+y+13=0$
To find the required distance, we find the equation of a line perpendicular to the given line, and passing through the centre of the given circle.
$\therefore \quad$ Equation of this line is: $(y+1)=\frac{1}{2}(x+1)$ i.e., $x=2 y+1$
Now, we find the points where line $x=2 y+1$ intersects the circle $x^2+y^2+2 x+2 y-3=0$
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore & (2 y+1)^2+y^2+2(2 y+1)+2 y-3=0 \\
\therefore & 4 y^2+4 y+1+y^2+4 y+2+2 y-3=0 \\
\therefore & 5 y^2+10 y=0 \\
\therefore & y(y+2)=0 \\
\therefore & y=0 \text { or } y=-2 \\
\therefore & x=1 \text { or } x=-3
\end{array}
$$
$\therefore \quad(1,0)$ and $(-3,-2)$ are the points on the circle, and one of them is at the maximum distance from the given line.
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore & \mathrm{d}_1=\left|\frac{2(1)+(0)+13}{\sqrt{4+1}}\right| \text { and } \mathrm{d}_2=\left|\frac{2(-3)+(-2)+13}{\sqrt{4+1}}\right| \\
\therefore & \mathrm{d}_1=\frac{15}{\sqrt{5}}=3 \sqrt{5} \quad \text { and } \mathrm{d}_2=\frac{5}{\sqrt{5}}=\sqrt{5} \\
\therefore & \lambda=3 \sqrt{5}
\end{array}
$$

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If a circle passes through points $(4,0)$ and $(0,2)$ and its centre lies on $\mathrm{Y}$-axis. If the radius of the circle is $r$, then the value of $r^2-r+1$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $25$
- **(B)** $21$
- **(C)** $20$
- **(D)** $10$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $21$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $(0, y)$ be the centre of the circle.
$\begin{aligned}
& \therefore \quad \sqrt{(0-4)^2+(y-0)^2}=\sqrt{(0-0)^2+(y-2)^2} \\
& \therefore \quad 16+y^2=(y-2)^2 \\
& \therefore \quad 16+y^2=y^2-4 y+4 \\
& \begin{aligned}
\therefore & y=-3 \\
\therefore & \text { centre of the circle is }(0,-3) . \\
\therefore & \text { Radius of the circle }=\mathrm{r}=\sqrt{(0-0)^2+(-3-2)^2} \\
& =5 \text { units } \\
\therefore & \mathrm{r}^2-\mathrm{r}+1=25-5+1=21
\end{aligned}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2023 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The parametric equations of the circle $x^2+y^2+2 x-4 y-4=0$ are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x=-1+3 \cos \theta, y=2+3 \sin \theta$
- **(B)** $x=1+3 \cos \theta, y=-2+3 \sin \theta$
- **(C)** $x=-1+3 \sin \theta, y=-2+3 \cos \theta$
- **(D)** $x=1+3 \sin \theta, y=-2+3 \cos \theta$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $x=-1+3 \cos \theta, y=2+3 \sin \theta$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation of circle is
$\begin{aligned}
& x^2+y^2+2 x-4 y-4=0 \\
& \Rightarrow\left(x^2+2 x+1\right)+\left(y^2-4 y+4\right)-4-5=0 \\
& \Rightarrow(x+1)^2+(y-2)^2=3^2
\end{aligned}$
$\therefore \quad$ Parametric equation of circle is
$x=-1+3 \cos \theta, y=2+3 \sin \theta$

</details>
