### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The maximum number of points of intersection between $4$ circles and $4$ straight lines is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $66$
- **(B)** $50$
- **(C)** $32$
- **(D)** $18$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $50$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The maximum number of points of intersection between $4$ circles is $2 \times ^{4}C_{2} = 2 \times 6 = 12$.

The maximum number of points of intersection between $4$ straight lines is $1 \times ^{4}C_{2} = 6$.

The maximum number of points of intersection between $4$ circles and $4$ straight lines is $2 \times (4 \times 4) = 32$.

The total maximum number of points of intersection is $12 + 6 + 32 = 50$.

Answer: $50$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2023 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The circles $x^2+y^2+2 \mathrm{a} x+\mathrm{c}=0$ and $x^2+y^2+2 \mathrm{~b} y+\mathrm{c}=0$ touch each other externally, if

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{a^2}-\frac{1}{b^2}=\frac{1}{c}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}^2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{~b}^2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{c}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{a^2}+\frac{1}{b^2}=\frac{1}{c^2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{a^2}-\frac{1}{b^2}=\frac{1}{c^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{\mathrm{a}^2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{~b}^2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{c}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& x^2+y^2+2 \mathrm{a} x+\mathrm{c}=0 \\
& \Rightarrow x^2+2 \mathrm{a} x+\mathrm{a}^2+y^2=\mathrm{a}^2-\mathrm{c} \\
& \Rightarrow(x+\mathrm{a})^2+y^2=\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{a}^2-\mathrm{c}}\right)^2
\end{aligned}
$$
i.e., it is a circle with centre $(-a, 0)$ and radius
$$
\sqrt{\mathrm{a}^2-\mathrm{c}}
$$

Simiłarly,
$$
\begin{aligned}
& x^2+y^2+2 \mathrm{~b} y+\mathrm{c}=0 \\
& \Rightarrow x^2+(y+\mathrm{b})^2=\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{b}^2-\mathrm{c}}\right)^2
\end{aligned}
$$
i.e., it is a circle with centre $(0,-b)$ and
$$
\text { radius }=t \sqrt{b^2-c}
$$
$\therefore \quad$ If circles touch externally, then we get Sum of radii $=$ Distance between centres
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow \sqrt{a^2-c}+\sqrt{b^2-c}=\sqrt{a^2+b^2} \\
& \Rightarrow a^2-c+b^2-c+2 \sqrt{a^2-c} \sqrt{b^2-c}=a^2+b^2 \\
& \Rightarrow\left(a^2-c\right)\left(b^2-c\right)=c^2 \\
& \Rightarrow a^2 b^2-c b^2-c a^2+c^2=c^2 \\
& \Rightarrow \frac{1}{a^2}+\frac{1}{b^2}=\frac{1}{c}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the circles $x^2+y^2=9$ and $x^2+y^2+2 \alpha x+2 y+1=0$ touch each other internally, then the value of $\alpha^3$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{27}{64}$
- **(B)** $\frac{125}{27}$
- **(C)** $\frac{27}{125}$
- **(D)** $\frac{64}{27}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{64}{27}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& x^2+y^2=9 \\
& \mathrm{C}_1=(0,0), \mathrm{r}_1=3 \\
& x^2+y^2+2 \alpha x+2 y+1=0 \\
& \mathrm{C}_2=(-\alpha,-1), \\
& \mathrm{r}_2=\sqrt{\alpha^2+1-1}=\alpha
\end{aligned}$
Since the given circles touch each other internally,
$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{C}_1 \mathrm{C}_2=\left|\mathrm{r}_1-\mathrm{r}_2\right| \\
& \Rightarrow \sqrt{\mathrm{a}^2+1}=|3-\alpha| \\
& \Rightarrow \alpha^2+1=9+\alpha^2-6 \alpha \\
& \Rightarrow 6 \alpha=8 \\
& \Rightarrow \alpha=\frac{4}{3} \\
& \Rightarrow \alpha^3=\frac{64}{27}
\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2022 (11 Aug Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Given two circles $\quad x^2+y^2+8 x-6 y-24=0 \quad$ and $x^2+y^2-4 x+10 y+20=0$. Then they are

**Options:**

- **(A)** Disjoint.
- **(B)** Concentric,
- **(C)** Touching internally
- **(D)** Touching externally.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Touching externally.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& x^2+y^2+8 x-6 y-24=0, C_1 \equiv(-4,3), r_1=7 \\
& x^2+y^2-4 x+10 y+20=0, C_2 \equiv(2,-5), r_2=3 \\
& C_1 C_2=\sqrt{(2+4)^2+(-5-3)^2}=10 \text { and } r_1+r_2=7+3=10
\end{aligned}$
$\because C_1 C_2=r_1+r_2$ hence, the tow circles touching externally

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the circles $x^{2}+y^{2}+2 x+2 k y+6=0$, $x^{2}+y^{2}+2 k y+k=0$ intersect orthogonally, then $k$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2 or $-3 / 2$
- **(B)** $-2$ or $-3 / 2$
- **(C)** 2 or $3 / 2$
- **(D)** $-2$ or $3 / 2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 2 or $-3 / 2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since, circles cut orthogonally, if
$$
\begin{aligned}
& & 2 g g^{\prime}+2 f f^{\prime}=c+c^{\prime} \\
\Rightarrow & & \quad 0+2 k^{2}=6+k \\
\Rightarrow & & 2 k^{2}-k-6=0 \\
\Rightarrow & &(2 k+3)(k-2)=0
\end{aligned}
$$
$\Rightarrow \quad k=2$ or $-3 / 2$

</details>
