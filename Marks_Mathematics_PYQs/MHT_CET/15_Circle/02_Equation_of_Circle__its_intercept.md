### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equations of the lines passing through the center of circle $x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 10y - 7 = 0$ and intersecting the line $3x - y - 1 = 0$ at an angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2y = x + 8$ or $y = 9 - 2x$
- **(B)** $x + 2y = 8$ or $2x - y = 9$
- **(C)** $x + 2y + 8 = 0$ or $y - 2x = 9$
- **(D)** $2y - x + 8 = 0$ or $2x - y = 9$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2y = x + 8$ or $y = 9 - 2x$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The center of the given circle $x^2 + y^2 - 4x - 10y - 7 = 0$ is $(2, 5)$.

The slope of the given line $3x - y - 1 = 0$ is $m_1 = 3$.

Let the slope of the required line be $m$. Since the angle between the required line and the given line is $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, we have:

$\tan\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \left| \dfrac{m - m_1}{1 + m m_1} \right|$

$1 = \left| \dfrac{m - 3}{1 + 3m} \right|$

$m - 3 = \pm (1 + 3m)$

Taking the positive sign:
$m - 3 = 1 + 3m \Rightarrow 2m = -4 \Rightarrow m = -2$

Taking the negative sign:
$m - 3 = -1 - 3m \Rightarrow 4m = 2 \Rightarrow m = \dfrac{1}{2}$

The required lines pass through the center $(2, 5)$.

Equation of the line with slope $m = -2$:
$y - 5 = -2(x - 2)$
$y = 9 - 2x$

Equation of the line with slope $m = \dfrac{1}{2}$:
$y - 5 = \dfrac{1}{2}(x - 2)$
$2y - 10 = x - 2$
$2y = x + 8$

The equations of the required lines are $2y = x + 8$ and $y = 9 - 2x$.

Answer: $2y = x + 8$ or $y = 9 - 2x$

</details>

**Detailed Solution:**

$d = \dfrac{|12(3) - 5(-4) - 17|}{\sqrt{12^2 + (-5)^2}}$

$d = \dfrac{|36 + 20 - 17|}{13} = \dfrac{39}{13} = 3$

$\sqrt{r^2 - 3^2} = 3$

$r^2 - 9 = 9 \Rightarrow r^2 = 18$

The equation of the circle is:

$(x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 18$

$x^2 - 6x + 9 + y^2 + 8y + 16 = 18$

$x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y + 7 = 0$

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of a circle with origin as centre and passing through the vertices of an equilateral triangle whose median is of length $3a$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x^2 + y^2 = 9a^2$
- **(B)** $x^2 + y^2 = 16a^2$
- **(C)** $x^2 + y^2 = 4a^2$
- **(D)** $x^2 + y^2 = a^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $x^2 + y^2 = 4a^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For an equilateral triangle, the circumcentre and the centroid coincide.

The centroid divides the median in the ratio $2:1$.

The radius of the circumcircle, $R$, is the distance from the centroid to any vertex, which is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the length of the median.

Given the length of the median is $3a$.

$R = \dfrac{2}{3} \times 3a = 2a$

The centre of the circle is the origin $(0, 0)$.

The equation of the circle is $x^2 + y^2 = R^2$.

Substituting $R = 2a$, we get $x^2 + y^2 = 4a^2$.

Answer: $x^2 + y^2 = 4a^2$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The Cartesian coordinates of a point P with parameter $\theta = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ on the circle $x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y + 1 = 0$ are...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(1, 0)$
- **(B)** $(0, -2 + \sqrt{3})$
- **(C)** $(-1, -2)$
- **(D)** $(-\sqrt{3} + 1, -1)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $(-\sqrt{3} + 1, -1)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the circle is $x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 4y + 1 = 0$.

Rewriting it in standard form by completing the square:

$(x^2 - 2x + 1) + (y^2 + 4y + 4) = 1 + 4 - 1$

$(x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 4$

The center of the circle is $(h, k) = (1, -2)$ and the radius is $r = 2$.

The parametric equations of the circle are given by:

$x = h + r \cos \theta$

$y = k + r \sin \theta$

Substituting $h = 1$, $k = -2$, $r = 2$, and $\theta = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$:

$x = 1 + 2 \cos\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right) = 1 + 2 \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = 1 - \sqrt{3}$

$y = -2 + 2 \sin\left(\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right) = -2 + 2 \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = -1$

The Cartesian coordinates of the point P are $(-\sqrt{3} + 1, -1)$.

Answer: $(-\sqrt{3} + 1, -1)$

Diameter $= \dfrac{|8 - (-28)|}{\sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2}} = \dfrac{36}{5}$

The center of the circle lies on the line parallel to and midway between the two given lines. The equation of this midway line is:

$3x - 4y + \dfrac{8 + (-28)}{2} = 0 \Rightarrow 3x - 4y - 10 = 0$

Solving $3x - 4y - 10 = 0$ and $x + 2y = 0$, we substitute $x = -2y$:

$3(-2y) - 4y - 10 = 0 \Rightarrow -10y = 10 \Rightarrow y = -1$

Substituting $y = -1$ in $x = -2y$, we get $x = 2$.

The center of the circle is $(2, -1)$.

The equation of the circle is:

$(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = \left(\dfrac{18}{5}\right)^2$

$(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = \dfrac{324}{25}$

$25(x - 2)^2 + 25(y + 1)^2 = 324$

Answer: $25(x - 2)^2 + 25(y + 1)^2 = 324$

### Q14. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The number of integral values of $K$ for which $x^2+\mathrm{y}^2+\mathrm{k} x+(1-\mathrm{k}) \mathrm{y}+5=0$ represents a circle whose radius cannot exceed 5, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $10$
- **(B)** $15$
- **(C)** $14$
- **(D)** $12$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $10$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the circle is $x^2 + y^2 + kx + (1-k)y + 5 = 0$.

Comparing this with the general equation of a circle $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$, we get:
$g = \dfrac{k}{2}$, $f = \dfrac{1-k}{2}$, and $c = 5$.

The radius of the circle is given by $r = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c}$.
For the equation to represent a real circle, we must have $r^2 > 0$.
Also, it is given that the radius cannot exceed $5$, so $r \le 5 \Rightarrow r^2 \le 25$.

Thus, we have the inequality:
$0 < g^2 + f^2 - c \le 25$

Substituting the values of $g$, $f$, and $c$:
$0 < \left(\dfrac{k}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{1-k}{2}\right)^2 - 5 \le 25$

$0 < \dfrac{k^2 + 1 - 2k + k^2}{4} - 5 \le 25$

$0 < \dfrac{2k^2 - 2k - 19}{4} \le 25$

Multiplying the entire inequality by $4$:
$0 < 2k^2 - 2k - 19 \le 100$

This gives us two separate inequalities to solve:

1) $2k^2 - 2k - 19 > 0$
The roots of the equation $2k^2 - 2k - 19 = 0$ are $k = \dfrac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 4(2)(-19)}}{4} = \dfrac{1 \pm \sqrt{39}}{2}$.
Since $\sqrt{39} \approx 6.24$, the roots are approximately $-2.62$ and $3.62$.
For the quadratic to be positive, $k < -2.62$ or $k > 3.62$.
Since $k$ is an integer, $k \le -3$ or $k \ge 4$.

2) $2k^2 - 2k - 19 \le 100$
$2k^2 - 2k - 119 \le 0$
The roots of the equation $2k^2 - 2k - 119 = 0$ are $k = \dfrac{2 \pm \sqrt{4 - 4(2)(-119)}}{4} = \dfrac{1 \pm \sqrt{239}}{2}$.
Since $\sqrt{239} \approx 15.46$, the roots are approximately $-7.23$ and $8.23$.
For the quadratic to be less than or equal to zero, $-7.23 \le k \le 8.23$.
Since $k$ is an integer, $-7 \le k \le 8$.

Taking the intersection of the two conditions for integer $k$:
$k \in \{-7, -6, -5, -4, -3\} \cup \{4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$

The number of integral values of $k$ is $5 + 5 = 10$.

Answer: $10$

</details>

### Q44. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If one end of the diameter is $(1,1)$ and the other end lies on the line $x+y=3$, then locus of centre of circle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x+y=1$
- **(B)** $2(x-y)=5$
- **(C)** $2 x+2 y=5$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2 x+2 y=5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the other end be $(t, 3-t)$. 
So, the equation of the variable circle is
$$
\begin{array}{l}
(x-1)(x-t)+(y-1)(y-3+t)=0 \\
\Rightarrow x^{2}+y^{2}-(1+t) x-(4-t) y+3=0
\end{array}
$$
$\therefore$ The centre $(\alpha, \beta)$ is given by
$$
\alpha=\frac{1+t}{2}, \beta=\frac{4-t}{2}
$$
$$
\Rightarrow \quad 2 \alpha+2 \beta=5
$$
Hence, the locus is $2 x+2 y=5$

</details>
