### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the slopes of the lines given by $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ are in the ratio $2 : 3$, then $h^2 = $

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2ab}{3}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3ab}{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{24ab}{25}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{25ab}{24}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{25ab}{24}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation of pair of straight lines is $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$.

Let the slopes of the lines be $m_1$ and $m_2$.

We have $m_1 + m_2 = -\dfrac{2h}{b}$ and $m_1 m_2 = \dfrac{a}{b}$.

Given that the slopes are in the ratio $2 : 3$, let $m_1 = 2m$ and $m_2 = 3m$.

Then, $m_1 + m_2 = 5m = -\dfrac{2h}{b} \Rightarrow m = -\dfrac{2h}{5b}$

And $m_1 m_2 = 6m^2 = \dfrac{a}{b}$

Substituting the value of $m$, we get:

$6 \left(-\dfrac{2h}{5b}\right)^2 = \dfrac{a}{b}$

$\dfrac{24h^2}{25b^2} = \dfrac{a}{b}$

$24h^2 = 25ab$

$h^2 = \dfrac{25ab}{24}$

Answer: $\dfrac{25ab}{24}$

</details>

- **(B)** $[0, 1]$

- **(D)** $[-3, 0)$

**Correct Option:** **(D) $[-3, 0)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the pair of straight lines is $6x^2 - xy + 4ky^2 = 0$.

One of the lines is $3x + 4y = 0$, which gives $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x$.

Substituting $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x$ into the given equation:

$6x^2 - x\left(-\dfrac{3}{4}x\right) + 4k\left(-\dfrac{3}{4}x\right)^2 = 0$

$6x^2 + \dfrac{3}{4}x^2 + 4k\left(\dfrac{9}{16}x^2\right) = 0$

Dividing by $x^2$ (for $x \neq 0$):

$6 + \dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{9k}{4} = 0$

Multiplying the entire equation by $4$:

$24 + 3 + 9k = 0$

$27 + 9k = 0 \Rightarrow k = -3$

The value $k = -3$ lies in the interval $[-3, 0)$.

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

If the sum of the cubes of slopes of two lines represented by the equation $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ is $1$, then

**Options:**

- **(B)** $b^3 + 2h(4h^2 - ab) = 0$
- **(C)** $3h^3 = 4ab^2$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $8h^3 - 6abh + b^3 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the slopes of the lines represented by $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ be $m_1$ and $m_2$.

We know that $m_1 + m_2 = -\dfrac{2h}{b}$ and $m_1 m_2 = \dfrac{a}{b}$.

Given that $m_1^3 + m_2^3 = 1$.

Using the identity $m_1^3 + m_2^3 = (m_1 + m_2)^3 - 3m_1 m_2 (m_1 + m_2)$, we get:

$\left(-\dfrac{2h}{b}\right)^3 - 3\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)\left(-\dfrac{2h}{b}\right) = 1$

$-\dfrac{8h^3}{b^3} + \dfrac{6ah}{b^2} = 1$

Multiplying by $b^3$, we get:

$-8h^3 + 6abh = b^3$

$8h^3 - 6abh + b^3 = 0$

Answer: $8h^3 - 6abh + b^3 = 0$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If two lines represented by the equation $x^2 + bcy^2 = (b+c)xy$ are coincident, then ____

**Options:**

- **(A)** $b = c^2$
- **(B)** $b^2 = c$
- **(C)** $b = c$
- **(D)** $b = -c$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $b = c$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation is $x^2 - (b+c)xy + bcy^2 = 0$.

Comparing this with the general equation of a pair of straight lines passing through the origin, $Ax^2 + 2Hxy + By^2 = 0$, we get $A = 1$, $2H = -(b+c)$, and $B = bc$.

For the two lines to be coincident, the condition is $H^2 = AB$.

Substituting the values, we get $\left(\dfrac{-(b+c)}{2}\right)^2 = 1 \cdot bc$

$\dfrac{(b+c)^2}{4} = bc$

$(b+c)^2 = 4bc$

$b^2 + c^2 + 2bc - 4bc = 0$

$b^2 + c^2 - 2bc = 0$

$(b-c)^2 = 0$

$b = c$

Answer: $b = c$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The product of the lengths of the perpendiculars drawn from the point $(3, 4)$ to the lines represented by $3x^2 - 5xy + 2y^2 = 0$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{26}}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{26}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{26}$
- **(D)** $26$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{26}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the pair of straight lines is $3x^2 - 5xy + 2y^2 = 0$.

Factorizing the equation, we get:

$3x^2 - 3xy - 2xy + 2y^2 = 0$

$3x(x - y) - 2y(x - y) = 0$

$(3x - 2y)(x - y) = 0$

The two lines are $3x - 2y = 0$ and $x - y = 0$.

The length of the perpendicular from the point $(3, 4)$ to the line $3x - 2y = 0$ is:

$d_1 = \dfrac{|3(3) - 2(4)|}{\sqrt{3^2 + (-2)^2}} = \dfrac{|9 - 8|}{\sqrt{13}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{13}}$

The length of the perpendicular from the point $(3, 4)$ to the line $x - y = 0$ is:

$d_2 = \dfrac{|3 - 4|}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}} = \dfrac{|-1|}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

The product of the lengths of the perpendiculars is:

$d_1 \times d_2 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{13}} \times \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{26}}$

Alternatively, using the direct formula for the product of perpendiculars from $(x_1, y_1)$ to $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$:

Product $= \dfrac{|ax_1^2 + 2hx_1y_1 + by_1^2|}{\sqrt{(a-b)^2 + 4h^2}}$

Substituting $a = 3$, $2h = -5$, $b = 2$, $x_1 = 3$, and $y_1 = 4$:

Product $= \dfrac{|3(3)^2 - 5(3)(4) + 2(4)^2|}{\sqrt{(3 - 2)^2 + (-5)^2}} = \dfrac{|27 - 60 + 32|}{\sqrt{1 + 25}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{26}}$

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{26}}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the angle made by the lines represented by the equation $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ with X-axis are $\alpha$ and $\beta$, then $\tan(\alpha + \beta)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{h}{a + b}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{2h}{a - b}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2h}{a + b}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{h}{a - b}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{2h}{a - b}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the slopes of the lines represented by $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ be $m_1$ and $m_2$.

Since the lines make angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with the X-axis, we have $m_1 = \tan \alpha$ and $m_2 = \tan \beta$.

From the given homogeneous equation, dividing by $x^2$ gives $b\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)^2 + 2h\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right) + a = 0$.

The roots of this quadratic equation in $m = \dfrac{y}{x}$ are $m_1$ and $m_2$.

Sum of the roots: $m_1 + m_2 = \tan \alpha + \tan \beta = -\dfrac{2h}{b}$

Product of the roots: $m_1 m_2 = \tan \alpha \tan \beta = \dfrac{a}{b}$

Using the trigonometric identity for the tangent of a sum:

$\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \dfrac{\tan \alpha + \tan \beta}{1 - \tan \alpha \tan \beta}$

Substituting the values of the sum and product of the slopes:

$\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \dfrac{-\dfrac{2h}{b}}{1 - \dfrac{a}{b}}$

$\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \dfrac{-\dfrac{2h}{b}}{\dfrac{b - a}{b}}$

$\tan(\alpha + \beta) = \dfrac{-2h}{b - a} = \dfrac{2h}{a - b}$

Answer: $\dfrac{2h}{a - b}$

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $4ab = 3h^2$, then the ratio of the slopes of the lines represented by $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{2} : 1$
- **(B)** $2 : 1$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{3} : 1$
- **(D)** $1 : 3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $1 : 3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the slopes of the lines represented by $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ be $m_1$ and $m_2$.

We know that:

$m_1 + m_2 = -\dfrac{2h}{b}$

$m_1 m_2 = \dfrac{a}{b}$

Squaring the sum of the slopes and dividing by their product, we get:

$\dfrac{(m_1 + m_2)^2}{m_1 m_2} = \dfrac{\left(-\dfrac{2h}{b}\right)^2}{\dfrac{a}{b}} = \dfrac{4h^2}{ab}$

We are given that $4ab = 3h^2$, which can be rewritten as $\dfrac{h^2}{ab} = \dfrac{4}{3}$.

Substituting this value into the equation:

$\dfrac{(m_1 + m_2)^2}{m_1 m_2} = 4 \left(\dfrac{4}{3}\right) = \dfrac{16}{3}$

Let the ratio of the slopes be $m_1 : m_2 = k : 1$, which means $m_1 = k m_2$. Substituting this into the above relation:

$\dfrac{(k m_2 + m_2)^2}{k m_2^2} = \dfrac{16}{3}$

$\dfrac{m_2^2(k + 1)^2}{k m_2^2} = \dfrac{16}{3}$

$\dfrac{(k + 1)^2}{k} = \dfrac{16}{3}$

$3(k^2 + 2k + 1) = 16k$

$3k^2 + 6k + 3 - 16k = 0$

$3k^2 - 10k + 3 = 0$

$3k^2 - 9k - k + 3 = 0$

$3k(k - 3) - 1(k - 3) = 0$

$(3k - 1)(k - 3) = 0$

$k = 3$ or $k = \dfrac{1}{3}$

Therefore, the ratio of the slopes is $3 : 1$ or $1 : 3$.

Answer: $1 : 3$

</details>

- **(B)** $\frac{2 h}{a-b}$
- **(C)** $\frac{h}{a+b}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 h}{a+b}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{2 h}{a-b}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We have $\tan \alpha+\tan \beta=\frac{-2 \mathrm{~h}}{\mathrm{~b}}$ and $\tan \alpha \cdot \tan \beta=\frac{\mathrm{a}}{\mathrm{b}}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \tan (\alpha+\beta)=\frac{\tan \alpha+\tan \beta}{1-\tan \alpha \tan \beta} \\
& =\frac{\left(\frac{-2 h}{b}\right)}{1-\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)}=\frac{-2 h}{b} \times \frac{b}{b-a}=\frac{2 h}{a-b}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
