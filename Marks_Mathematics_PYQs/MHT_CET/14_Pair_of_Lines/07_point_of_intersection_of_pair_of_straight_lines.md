### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the equation $ax^2 + 4xy - 2y^2 + 4x + 8y + 1 = 0$ represents a pair of straight lines, then the coordinates of their point of intersection are.........

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{3}{2}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- **(C)** $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$
- **(D)** $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation is $S = ax^2 + 4xy - 2y^2 + 4x + 8y + 1 = 0$.

Comparing with the general equation of second degree $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$, we get:

$a = a$, $h = 2$, $b = -2$, $g = 2$, $f = 4$, $c = 1$.

For the equation to represent a pair of straight lines, the condition is $\Delta = abc + 2fgh - af^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 = 0$.

Substituting the values:

$a(-2)(1) + 2(4)(2)(2) - a(4)^2 - (-2)(2)^2 - 1(2)^2 = 0$

$-2a + 32 - 16a + 8 - 4 = 0$

$-18a + 36 = 0 \Rightarrow a = 2$

The point of intersection of the pair of straight lines is obtained by solving $\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x} = 0$ and $\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial y} = 0$.

$\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x} = 2ax + 4y + 4 = 0$

Substituting $a = 2$:

$4x + 4y + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow x + y + 1 = 0$

$\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial y} = 4x - 4y + 8 = 0 \Rightarrow x - y + 2 = 0$

Adding the two equations:

$2x + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\dfrac{3}{2}$

Subtracting the second equation from the first:

$2y - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{1}{2}$

The coordinates of the point of intersection are $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$.

Answer: $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The number of integer values of $m$, for which $x$-coordinate of the point of intersection of the lines $3 x+4 y=9$ and $y=m x+1$ is also an integer, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** 0
- **(C)** 4
- **(D)** 1

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equations of lines are $3x + 4y = 9$ and $y = mx + 1$.

Substituting $y$ in the first equation, we get:

$3x + 4(mx + 1) = 9$

$x(3 + 4m) = 5$

$x = \dfrac{5}{3 + 4m}$

For $x$ to be an integer, $3 + 4m$ must be an integer divisor of $5$.

The divisors of $5$ are $1, -1, 5, -5$.

Equating $3 + 4m$ to these divisors:

$3 + 4m = 1 \Rightarrow m = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

$3 + 4m = -1 \Rightarrow m = -1$

$3 + 4m = 5 \Rightarrow m = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$3 + 4m = -5 \Rightarrow m = -2$

Since $m$ must be an integer, the possible values of $m$ are $-1$ and $-2$.

Thus, there are $2$ integer values of $m$.

Answer: 2

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2021 (22 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Area of the triangle formed by the line $y^2-9 x y+18 x^2=0$ and $y=9$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{27}{3}$ sq. units
- **(B)** $\frac{27}{2}$ sq. units
- **(C)** $\frac{27}{4}$ sq. units
- **(D)** 27 sq. units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{27}{4}$ sq. units**

**Detailed Solution:**

$$
\begin{aligned}
& y^2-9 x y+18 x^2=0 \\
& \therefore(y-3 x)(y-6 x)=0
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus three lines forming triangle are $y=3 x, y=6 x, y=9$

### Q4. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The joint equation of pair of lines passing through point of intersection of lines
$2 x^{2}-x y-15 y^{2}-7 x+32 y-9=0$ and parallel to co-ordinate axes is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x y-x-2 y+2=0$
- **(B)** $x y+x+2 y-2=0$
- **(C)** $x y+x+2 y+2=0$
- **(D)** $x y-x-2 y-2=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $x y-x-2 y+2=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\phi=2 x^{2}-x y-15 y^{2}-7 x+32 y-9=0$ ...(1)
$\quad \frac{d \phi}{d x}=4 x-y-7=0 \Rightarrow 4 x-y-7=0$ ...(2)
$\quad \frac{d \phi}{d y}=-x-30 y+32=0 \Rightarrow x+30 y-32=0$ ...(3)
Solving equation $(2) \&(3)$ we get $x=2, y=1$
$\therefore(2,1)$ is the point of intersection of given lines.
Lines passing through $(2,1)$ and parallel to co-ordinate axes are $x=2$ and $y=1$.
Hence required equation is
$\quad(x-2)(y-1)=0 \Rightarrow x y-2 y-x+2=0$
Note : Point of intersection can also be calculated as follows :
$2 x^{2}-x y-15 y^{2}-7 x+32 y-9=0 \text { gives } a=2, h=\frac{-1}{2}, b=-15, g=\frac{-7}{2}, f=16, c=-9$
$\begin{aligned} \text { Point of intersection } &=\left(\frac{\mathrm{bg}-\mathrm{hf}}{\mathrm{h}^{2}-\mathrm{ab}}, \frac{\mathrm{af}-\mathrm{gh}}{\mathrm{h}^{2}-\mathrm{ab}}\right) \\ & \equiv\left[\frac{(-15)\left(-\frac{7}{2}\right)-\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)(16)}{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-(2)(-15)}, \frac{(2)(16)-\left(-\frac{7}{2}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-(2)(-15)}\right], \\ & \equiv\left[\frac{\frac{105}{2}+\frac{16}{2}}{\frac{1}{4}+30}, \frac{32-\frac{7}{4}}{\frac{1}{4}+30}\right] \equiv\left[\frac{121}{2} \times \frac{4}{121}, \frac{121}{4} \times \frac{4}{121}\right] \\ & \equiv(2,1) \end{aligned}$

</details>
