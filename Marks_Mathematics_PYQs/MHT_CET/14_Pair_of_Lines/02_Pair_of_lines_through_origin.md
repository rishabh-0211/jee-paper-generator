### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the sum of the slopes of the line represented by $x^2+kxy-3y^2=0$ is twice their product, then the acute angle between the lines $x^2+k\,xy\cos ec\,\alpha+y^2=0$ is ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\alpha$
- **(B)** $2\alpha$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\pi}{2}+\alpha$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\alpha$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\alpha$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the equation $x^2+kxy-3y^2=0$, comparing with $ax^2+2hxy+by^2=0$, we have $a=1$, $2h=k$, and $b=-3$.

Let $m_1$ and $m_2$ be the slopes of the lines. The sum and product of the slopes are given by:

$m_1+m_2 = -\dfrac{2h}{b} = \dfrac{k}{3}$

$m_1m_2 = \dfrac{a}{b} = -\dfrac{1}{3}$

Given that the sum of the slopes is twice their product:

$m_1+m_2 = 2m_1m_2$

$\dfrac{k}{3} = 2\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) \Rightarrow k = -2$

Substituting $k = -2$ into the second equation $x^2+kxy\csc\alpha+y^2=0$, we get:

$x^2-2xy\csc\alpha+y^2=0$

Comparing with $Ax^2+2Hxy+By^2=0$, we have $A=1$, $H=-\csc\alpha$, and $B=1$.

The angle $\theta$ between the lines is given by:

$\tan\theta = \left|\dfrac{2\sqrt{H^2-AB}}{A+B}\right|$

$\tan\theta = \left|\dfrac{2\sqrt{(-\csc\alpha)^2-(1)(1)}}{1+1}\right|$

$\tan\theta = \sqrt{\csc^2\alpha-1} = |\cot\alpha|$

For an acute angle $\theta$, we have $\tan\theta = \cot\alpha = \tan\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\alpha\right)$.

$\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}-\alpha$

Answer: $\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\alpha$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The combined equation of the pair of lines passing through the origin and making an angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ with the line $3x + y - 6 = 0$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$
- **(B)** $2x^2 - 3xy + y^2 = 0$
- **(C)** $2x^2 - xy + 3y^2 = 0$
- **(D)** $2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Slope of the given line $3x + y - 6 = 0$ is $m_1 = -3$.

Let $m$ be the slope of the required line. Since the angle between the lines is $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, we have:

$\tan\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \left| \dfrac{m - m_1}{1 + m m_1} \right|$

$1 = \left| \dfrac{m - (-3)}{1 + m(-3)} \right| = \left| \dfrac{m + 3}{1 - 3m} \right|$

This gives two cases:

$\dfrac{m + 3}{1 - 3m} = 1 \Rightarrow m + 3 = 1 - 3m \Rightarrow 4m = -2 \Rightarrow m = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

$\dfrac{m + 3}{1 - 3m} = -1 \Rightarrow m + 3 = -1 + 3m \Rightarrow 2m = 4 \Rightarrow m = 2$

The equations of the lines passing through the origin with these slopes are $y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x$ and $y = 2x$, which can be written as $x + 2y = 0$ and $2x - y = 0$.

The combined equation of the pair of lines is:

$(x + 2y)(2x - y) = 0$

$2x^2 - xy + 4xy - 2y^2 = 0$

$2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$

Answer: $2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The line $x + y = 3$ intersects the pair of straight lines $x^2 - 3xy + y^2 = 0$ at points A and B. Then the co-ordinates of the mid-point of AB are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\dfrac{5}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(\dfrac{1}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- **(C)** $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}\right)$
- **(D)** $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the equation of the line $x + y = 3 \Rightarrow y = 3 - x$.

Substituting $y = 3 - x$ into the equation of the pair of straight lines $x^2 - 3xy + y^2 = 0$:

$x^2 - 3x(3 - x) + (3 - x)^2 = 0$

$x^2 - 9x + 3x^2 + 9 - 6x + x^2 = 0$

$5x^2 - 15x + 9 = 0$

Let $x_1$ and $x_2$ be the roots of this quadratic equation, which represent the x-coordinates of the intersection points A and B.

The sum of the roots is $x_1 + x_2 = \dfrac{15}{5} = 3$.

The x-coordinate of the mid-point of AB is $\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

Since the mid-point lies on the line $x + y = 3$, its y-coordinate is $y = 3 - \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

The coordinates of the mid-point of AB are $\left(\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{3}{2}\right)$.

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The joint equation of the pair of lines through the origin and making an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ with the line $3x + y - 6 = 0$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$
- **(B)** $2x^2 + 3xy + 2y^2 = 0$
- **(C)** $x^2 + 3xy - y^2 = 0$
- **(D)** $x^2 - 3xy + y^2 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Slope of the given line $3x + y - 6 = 0$ is $m_1 = -3$.

Let $m$ be the slope of a line passing through the origin and making an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ with the given line.

Using the angle formula, $\tan\left(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = \left| \dfrac{m - m_1}{1 + m m_1} \right|$

$1 = \left| \dfrac{m - (-3)}{1 + m(-3)} \right| = \left| \dfrac{m + 3}{1 - 3m} \right|$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$(1 - 3m)^2 = (m + 3)^2$

$1 - 6m + 9m^2 = m^2 + 6m + 9$

$8m^2 - 12m - 8 = 0$

Dividing by $4$, we get $2m^2 - 3m - 2 = 0$.

Since the lines pass through the origin, their equations are of the form $y = mx$, which implies $m = \dfrac{y}{x}$.

Substituting $m = \dfrac{y}{x}$ in the quadratic equation:

$2\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)^2 - 3\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right) - 2 = 0$

$2y^2 - 3xy - 2x^2 = 0$

Multiplying by $-1$, we obtain the joint equation:

$2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$.

Answer: $2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the slope of one of the two lines given by $\dfrac{x^2}{a} + \dfrac{2xy}{h} + \dfrac{y^2}{b} = 0$ is twice that of the other, then $h^2 : ab = $ ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1:2$
- **(B)** $2:1$
- **(C)** $8:9$
- **(D)** $9:8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $8:9$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation is $\dfrac{x^2}{a} + \dfrac{2xy}{h} + \dfrac{y^2}{b} = 0$

Dividing by $x^2$, we get $\dfrac{1}{b}\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)^2 + \dfrac{2}{h}\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right) + \dfrac{1}{a} = 0$

Let $m = \dfrac{y}{x}$ represent the slope of the lines. The equation becomes $\dfrac{m^2}{b} + \dfrac{2m}{h} + \dfrac{1}{a} = 0$

Let the slopes of the two lines be $m_1$ and $m_2$. From the quadratic equation in $m$, the sum and product of the roots are:

$m_1 + m_2 = -\dfrac{2b}{h}$

$m_1 m_2 = \dfrac{b}{a}$

Given that the slope of one line is twice that of the other, $m_2 = 2m_1$

Substituting $m_2$ in the sum of the roots:

$m_1 + 2m_1 = -\dfrac{2b}{h} \Rightarrow 3m_1 = -\dfrac{2b}{h} \Rightarrow m_1 = -\dfrac{2b}{3h}$

Substituting $m_2$ in the product of the roots:

$m_1(2m_1) = \dfrac{b}{a} \Rightarrow 2m_1^2 = \dfrac{b}{a}$

Substituting the value of $m_1$ into the product equation:

$2\left(-\dfrac{2b}{3h}\right)^2 = \dfrac{b}{a}$

$2\left(\dfrac{4b^2}{9h^2}\right) = \dfrac{b}{a}$

$\dfrac{8b^2}{9h^2} = \dfrac{b}{a}$

$8ab = 9h^2$

$\dfrac{h^2}{ab} = \dfrac{8}{9}$

Thus, $h^2 : ab = 8 : 9$

Answer: $8:9$

</details>

$9x^2 + 6xy + y^2 = x^2 - 6xy + 9y^2$

$8x^2 + 12xy - 8y^2 = 0$

$2x^2 + 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$

### Q36. MHT CET 2010

The equation of the lines passing through the origin and having slopes 3 and $-\frac{1}{3}$, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 y^{2}+8 x y-3 x^{2}=0$
- **(B)** $3 x^{2}+8 x y+3 y^{2}=0$
- **(C)** $3 y^{2}-8 x y-3 x^{2}=0$
- **(D)** $3 x^{2}+8 x y-3 y^{2}=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $3 x^{2}+8 x y-3 y^{2}=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Here, $m_{1}=3, m_{2}=\frac{-1}{3}$
Hence, the lines are $y=3 x, y=\frac{-1}{3} x$
On multiplying both the lines, we get $(y-3 x)(3 y+x)=0$
$\Rightarrow \quad 3 x^{2}+8 x y-3 y^{2}=0$
