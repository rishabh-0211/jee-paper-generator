### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$ are the acute angles between the lines $3x^2 - 7xy + 4y^2 = 0$ and $6x^2 - 5xy + y^2 = 0$ respectively, then ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\theta_1 = \theta_2$
- **(B)** $\theta_1 = 2\theta_2$
- **(C)** $2\theta_1 = \theta_2$
- **(D)** $\theta_1 + \theta_2 = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\theta_1 = \theta_2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The acute angle $\theta$ between the lines represented by $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$ is given by $\tan \theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{a + b} \right|$.

For the first pair of lines $3x^2 - 7xy + 4y^2 = 0$, we have $a = 3$, $h = -\dfrac{7}{2}$, and $b = 4$.

$\tan \theta_1 = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\left(-\dfrac{7}{2}\right)^2 - (3)(4)}}{3 + 4} \right| = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\dfrac{49}{4} - 12}}{7} \right| = \dfrac{1}{7}$

For the second pair of lines $6x^2 - 5xy + y^2 = 0$, we have $a = 6$, $h = -\dfrac{5}{2}$, and $b = 1$.

$\tan \theta_2 = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\left(-\dfrac{5}{2}\right)^2 - (6)(1)}}{6 + 1} \right| = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\dfrac{25}{4} - 6}}{7} \right| = \dfrac{1}{7}$

Since $\tan \theta_1 = \tan \theta_2$ and both are acute angles, we get $\theta_1 = \theta_2$.

Answer: $\theta_1 = \theta_2$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The measure of the acute angle between the pair of lines $2x^2 + xy - y^2 - x + 2y - 1 = 0$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\tan^{-1}\dfrac{1}{3}$
- **(B)** $\tan^{-1}1$
- **(C)** $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$
- **(D)** $\cos^{-1}\sqrt{10}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Comparing the given equation $2x^2 + xy - y^2 - x + 2y - 1 = 0$ with the general equation $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$, we get:

$a = 2$, $h = \dfrac{1}{2}$, $b = -1$

The acute angle $\theta$ between the pair of lines is given by:

$\tan \theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{a + b} \right|$

Substituting the values:

$\tan \theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 - (2)(-1)}}{2 + (-1)} \right|$

$\tan \theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{4} + 2}}{1} \right| = 2\sqrt{\dfrac{9}{4}} = 3$

Since $\tan \theta = 3$, we can find $\cos \theta$ using the relation $\cos \theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \tan^2 \theta}}$:

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + 3^2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$

$\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\right)$

Answer: $\cos^{-1}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{10}}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (16 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\theta$ is the acute angle between the lines $2x^2 + 7xy + 3y^2 = 0$, then the value of $\dfrac{2\cos\theta - 3\sin\theta}{4\sin\theta + 5\cos\theta} = \ldots$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{5}{9}$
- **(C)** $-\dfrac{1}{9}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $-\dfrac{1}{9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation of the pair of straight lines is $2x^2 + 7xy + 3y^2 = 0$.

Comparing with $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$, we get $a = 2$, $h = \dfrac{7}{2}$, and $b = 3$.

The acute angle $\theta$ between the lines is given by:

$\tan\theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{a + b} \right|$

$\tan\theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{7}{2}\right)^2 - (2)(3)}}{2 + 3} \right|$

Dividing the numerator and the denominator by $\cos\theta$, we get:

$\dfrac{2 - 3\tan\theta}{4\tan\theta + 5}$

Substituting $\tan\theta = 1$:

$\dfrac{2 - 3(1)}{4(1) + 5} = \dfrac{-1}{9} = -\dfrac{1}{9}$

Answer: $-\dfrac{1}{9}$

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\theta$ is the acute angle between the lines represented by the equation $x^2 - 3xy + 2y^2 = 0$, then $\dfrac{3\sin\theta + 2\cos\theta}{3\sin\theta - 2\cos\theta} = $

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(C)** $-3$
- **(D)** $3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $-3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the pair of straight lines is $x^2 - 3xy + 2y^2 = 0$.

Comparing with the standard form $ax^2 + 2hxy + by^2 = 0$, we get $a = 1$, $2h = -3$, and $b = 2$.

The acute angle $\theta$ between the lines is given by $\tan\theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{h^2 - ab}}{a + b} \right|$.

Substituting the values, we get:

$\tan\theta = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2 - (1)(2)}}{1 + 2} \right| = \left| \dfrac{2\sqrt{\dfrac{9}{4} - 2}}{3} \right| = \dfrac{2\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}{3} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

Substituting $\tan\theta = \dfrac{1}{3}$:

$\dfrac{3\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right) + 2}{3\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right) - 2} = \dfrac{1 + 2}{1 - 2} = \dfrac{3}{-1} = -3$

Answer: $-3$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The triangle formed by the lines $2x^2 - 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$ and $3x - y = 7$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** Right angled but not isosceles
- **(B)** isosceles with base angle $30^\circ$
- **(C)** Right angled with one angle $60^\circ$
- **(D)** Right angled isosceles

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) Right angled isosceles**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the pair of straight lines is $2x^2 - 3xy - 2y^2 = 0$.

Factorizing the equation:

$2x^2 - 4xy + xy - 2y^2 = 0$

$2x(x - 2y) + y(x - 2y) = 0$

$(2x + y)(x - 2y) = 0$

The equations of the two lines are $L_1: 2x + y = 0$ and $L_2: x - 2y = 0$.

The slopes of these lines are $m_1 = -2$ and $m_2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Since $m_1 m_2 = -2 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = -1$, the lines $L_1$ and $L_2$ are perpendicular to each other. Thus, the triangle is right-angled.

The equation of the third line is $L_3: 3x - y = 7$, which has a slope $m_3 = 3$.

Let $\theta_1$ be the angle between $L_1$ and $L_3$:

$\tan \theta_1 = \left| \dfrac{m_1 - m_3}{1 + m_1 m_3} \right| = \left| \dfrac{-2 - 3}{1 + (-2)(3)} \right| = \left| \dfrac{-5}{-5} \right| = 1$

$\Rightarrow \theta_1 = 45^\circ$

Let $\theta_2$ be the angle between $L_2$ and $L_3$:

$\tan \theta_2 = \left| \dfrac{m_2 - m_3}{1 + m_2 m_3} \right| = \left| \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2} - 3}{1 + \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)(3)} \right| = \left| \dfrac{-\dfrac{5}{2}}{\dfrac{5}{2}} \right| = 1$

$\Rightarrow \theta_2 = 45^\circ$

Since two angles of the right-angled triangle are $45^\circ$, the triangle is right-angled isosceles.

Answer: Right angled isosceles

</details>

$\left(-\dfrac{2h}{5}\right)^2 = 1$

If a pair of lines $x^{2}-2 p x y-y^{2}=0$ and $x^{2}-2 q x y-y^{2}=0$ is such that each pair bisects the angle between the other pair, then

**Options:**

- **(B)** $p q=1$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=0$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $p q=-1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since, $x^{2}+\frac{2 x y}{p}-y^{2}=0$, is the angle bisectors of $x^{2}-2 p x y-y^{2}=0$
But given that angle bisectors are
$\therefore$
$$
\begin{array}{c}
x^{2}-2 q x y-y^{2}=0 \\
-2 q=2 / p
\end{array}
$$
$$
\Rightarrow \quad p q=-1
$$
