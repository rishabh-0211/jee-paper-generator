### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The area of the triangle (in sq. units) formed by the pair of lines $x^2 - 4y^2 - 8x + 16 = 0$ and the line $x = 2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the pair of lines is $x^2 - 4y^2 - 8x + 16 = 0$.

This can be rewritten as:

$(x^2 - 8x + 16) - 4y^2 = 0$

$(x - 4)^2 - (2y)^2 = 0$

$(x - 4 - 2y)(x - 4 + 2y) = 0$

The two lines are $x - 2y - 4 = 0$ and $x + 2y - 4 = 0$.

The point of intersection of these two lines is $(4, 0)$.

The third line is $x = 2$.

Finding the points of intersection of $x = 2$ with the pair of lines:

For $x - 2y - 4 = 0$, substituting $x = 2$ gives $2 - 2y - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow y = -1$. The point is $(2, -1)$.

For $x + 2y - 4 = 0$, substituting $x = 2$ gives $2 + 2y - 4 = 0 \Rightarrow y = 1$. The point is $(2, 1)$.

The vertices of the triangle are $(4, 0)$, $(2, -1)$, and $(2, 1)$.

The length of the base formed by the points $(2, -1)$ and $(2, 1)$ on the line $x = 2$ is $1 - (-1) = 2$.

The height of the triangle is the perpendicular distance from the vertex $(4, 0)$ to the line $x = 2$, which is $|4 - 2| = 2$.

Area of the triangle $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 2 = 2$ sq. units.

Answer: $2$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the equation $16x^2 - 24xy + 9y^2 - 8x + 6y - 35 = 0$ represents a pair of straight lines, then the equation of the locus of points equidistant from these two lines is..........

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4x - 3y - 1 = 0$
- **(B)** $4x - 3y + 1 = 0$
- **(C)** $8x - 6y - 1 = 0$
- **(D)** $8x - 6y + 1 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $4x - 3y - 1 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation is $16x^2 - 24xy + 9y^2 - 8x + 6y - 35 = 0$.

This can be rewritten by grouping the terms as:

$(4x - 3y)^2 - 2(4x - 3y) - 35 = 0$

Let $t = 4x - 3y$. The equation becomes:

$t^2 - 2t - 35 = 0$

$(t - 7)(t + 5) = 0$

$t = 7$ or $t = -5$

Substituting back $t = 4x - 3y$, the equation represents two parallel straight lines:

$4x - 3y - 7 = 0$ and $4x - 3y + 5 = 0$

The locus of points equidistant from these two parallel lines is a line parallel to them and lying exactly midway between them. Its equation is given by the arithmetic mean of their constant terms:

$4x - 3y + \dfrac{-7 + 5}{2} = 0$

$4x - 3y - 1 = 0$

Answer: $4x - 3y - 1 = 0$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The combined equation of lines parallel to the coordinate axes and passing through the point of intersection of lines represented by $x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 + 10x - 14y + 9 = 0$ is $\ldots$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $xy + 2x + y + 2 = 0$
- **(B)** $xy + 2x - y - 2 = 0$
- **(C)** $xy - 2x + y - 2 = 0$
- **(D)** $xy - 2x - y + 2 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $xy - 2x - y + 2 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the given equation be $S = x^2 - 6xy + 5y^2 + 10x - 14y + 9 = 0$.

The point of intersection of the pair of straight lines represented by $S = 0$ can be found by solving the partial derivatives $\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x} = 0$ and $\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial y} = 0$.

$\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial x} = 2x - 6y + 10 = 0 \Rightarrow x - 3y + 5 = 0$

$\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial y} = -6x + 10y - 14 = 0 \Rightarrow 3x - 5y + 7 = 0$

Solving the two linear equations:
From the first equation, $x = 3y - 5$.
Substituting into the second equation:
$3(3y - 5) - 5y + 7 = 0$
$9y - 15 - 5y + 7 = 0$
$4y - 8 = 0 \Rightarrow y = 2$

Substituting $y = 2$ back into $x = 3y - 5$, we get $x = 3(2) - 5 = 1$.

The point of intersection is $(1, 2)$.

The equations of the lines parallel to the coordinate axes and passing through $(1, 2)$ are $x = 1$ and $y = 2$, which can be written as $x - 1 = 0$ and $y - 2 = 0$.

The combined equation of these lines is:
$(x - 1)(y - 2) = 0$
$xy - 2x - y + 2 = 0$

Answer: $xy - 2x - y + 2 = 0$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Two lines are given by $x^2 - 4xy + 4y^2 + kx - 2ky = 0$, then the value of k so that the distance between them is 3 is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{5}$
- **(B)** $3\sqrt{3}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{3}$
- **(D)** $3\sqrt{5}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $3\sqrt{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation is $x^2 - 4xy + 4y^2 + kx - 2ky = 0$

This can be rewritten as $(x - 2y)^2 + k(x - 2y) = 0$

$(x - 2y)(x - 2y + k) = 0$

The two lines are $x - 2y = 0$ and $x - 2y + k = 0$

The distance between these parallel lines is given by $d = \dfrac{|c_1 - c_2|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}$

$d = \dfrac{|k - 0|}{\sqrt{1^2 + (-2)^2}} = \dfrac{|k|}{\sqrt{5}}$

Given that the distance is $3$, we have:

$\dfrac{|k|}{\sqrt{5}} = 3$

$|k| = 3\sqrt{5}$

Answer: $3\sqrt{5}$

</details>
