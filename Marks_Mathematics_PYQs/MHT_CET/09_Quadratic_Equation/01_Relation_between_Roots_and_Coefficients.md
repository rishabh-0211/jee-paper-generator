### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If two sides of a triangle are the roots of the equation $x^2 - \sqrt{10}\,x + 2 = 0$ and angle between them is $60^\circ$, then the third side is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** $2$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{3}$
- **(D)** $3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the two sides be $a$ and $b$.

Since $a$ and $b$ are the roots of the equation $x^2 - \sqrt{10}x + 2 = 0$, we have:

$a + b = \sqrt{10}$

$ab = 2$

Let the angle between the sides $a$ and $b$ be $C = 60^\circ$.

Using the cosine rule for the third side $c$:

$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab \cos C$

$c^2 = (a + b)^2 - 2ab - 2ab \cos 60^\circ$

Substituting the values:

$c^2 = (\sqrt{10})^2 - 2(2) - 2(2) \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

$c^2 = 10 - 4 - 2$

$c^2 = 4$

Since $c > 0$, we get $c = 2$.

Answer: $2$

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $a$ be an integer selected at random from the set $\{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots, 9\}$. The probability that the equation $ax^2 - ax + 1 = 0$ has real roots is ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{5}{9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{3}{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The total number of possible values for $a$ from the set $\{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots, 9\}$ is $10$.

For the equation $ax^2 - ax + 1 = 0$ to have real roots, we consider two cases:

If $a = 0$, the equation becomes $1 = 0$, which has no roots.

If $a \neq 0$, the equation is a quadratic equation. For it to have real roots, its discriminant must be non-negative.

$D = (-a)^2 - 4(a)(1) \geq 0$

$a^2 - 4a \geq 0$

$a(a - 4) \geq 0$

Since $a \in \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 9\}$, the condition $a(a - 4) \geq 0$ is satisfied when $a \geq 4$.

The favorable values of $a$ are $4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9$.

The number of favorable outcomes is $6$.

The required probability is $\dfrac{6}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{3}{5}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

The quadratic polynomial $p(x)$ has roots $1$ and $\alpha$, while quadratic polynomial $q(x)$ has roots $1$ and $\beta$. Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be the roots of $r(x) = p(x) + q(x)$. Then $\lim_{x\to\infty}\left[\sqrt{p(x)} - \sqrt{q(x)}\right] =$

- **(B)** $-1$
- **(C)** $1$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $r(x) = p(x) + q(x)$, we can evaluate $r(x)$ at $x = 1$:
$r(1) = p(1) + q(1) = 0 + 0 = 0$

Since the roots of $r(x)$ are given as $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and $r(1) = 0$, one of the roots must be $1$. Without loss of generality, let $\alpha = 1$.

Then $p(x) = a(x-1)^2$.
Since the roots of $r(x)$ are $1$ and $\beta$, we can write $r(x) = (a+b)(x-1)(x-\beta)$.

Substitute $p(x)$, $q(x)$, and $r(x)$ into $r(x) = p(x) + q(x)$:
$(a+b)(x-1)(x-\beta) = a(x-1)^2 + b(x-1)(x-\beta)$

$(a+b)(x-\beta) = a(x-1) + b(x-\beta)$
$ax - a\beta + bx - b\beta = ax - a + bx - b\beta$
$-a\beta = -a \Rightarrow a(\beta - 1) = 0$

Thus, $\alpha = \beta = 1$.

This implies $p(x) = a(x-1)^2$ and $q(x) = b(x-1)^2$.

We are required to find:
$\lim_{x\to\infty} \left[\sqrt{p(x)} - \sqrt{q(x)}\right] = \lim_{x\to\infty} \left[\sqrt{a(x-1)^2} - \sqrt{b(x-1)^2}\right] = \lim_{x\to\infty} (\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})(x-1)$

For this limit to exist and be a finite value (as per the given options), we must have $a = b$. 
Therefore, $\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b} = 0$, and the limit evaluates to $0$.

$\dfrac{\tan \dfrac{\mathrm{A}}{2} + \tan \dfrac{\mathrm{C}}{2}}{1 - \tan \dfrac{\mathrm{A}}{2} \tan \dfrac{\mathrm{C}}{2}} = 1$

- **(B)** $b_1+c_1=a_1$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{a}_1+\mathrm{c}_1-\mathrm{b}_1$
- **(D)** $b_1=c_1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $a_1+b_1=c_1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{array}{ll}

& \angle \mathrm{A}+\angle \mathrm{B}+\angle \mathrm{C}=180^{\circ} \\
\therefore \quad & \angle \mathrm{A}+\frac{\pi}{2}+\angle \mathrm{B}=180^{\circ} \\
\therefore \quad & \angle \mathrm{A}+\angle \mathrm{B}=\frac{\pi}{2} \\
\therefore \quad & \frac{\angle \mathrm{A}}{2}+\frac{\angle \mathrm{B}}{2}=\frac{\pi}{4}
\end{array}$
$\tan \left(\frac{A}{2}\right)$ and $\tan \left(\frac{B}{2}\right)$ are roots of equation $\mathrm{a}_1 x^2+\mathrm{b}_1 x+\mathrm{c}_1=0 ... [Given]$
$\begin{aligned} & \therefore \quad \text { Sum of roots }=\frac{-b_1}{a_1} \\ & \tan \left(\frac{A}{2}\right)+\tan \left(\frac{B}{2}\right)=\frac{-b_1}{a_1}\end{aligned}$
Also, $\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{A}}{2}\right) \cdot \tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{B}}{2}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{c}_1}{\mathrm{a}_1}$
Using $\tan \left(\frac{\mathrm{A}}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{B}}{2}\right)=\frac{\tan \frac{\mathrm{A}}{2}+\tan \frac{\mathrm{B}}{2}}{1-\tan \frac{\mathrm{A}}{2} \tan \frac{\mathrm{B}}{2}}$, we get
$\begin{aligned}

& 1=\frac{-b_1}{a_1-c_1} \\

\end{aligned}$
