### Q1. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

The Number of values of $C$ that satisfy the conclusion of Rolle's theorem in case of following function $\mathrm{f}(x)=\sin 2 \pi x, x \in[-1,1]$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 02
- **(B)** 04
- **(C)** 03
- **(D)** zero

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 04**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $f(x) = \sin 2\pi x$ on $[-1, 1]$.

$f(x)$ is continuous on $[-1, 1]$ and differentiable on $(-1, 1)$.

$f(-1) = \sin(-2\pi) = 0$ and $f(1) = \sin(2\pi) = 0$, so $f(-1) = f(1)$.

By Rolle's theorem, there exists at least one $c \in (-1, 1)$ such that $f'(c) = 0$.

$f'(x) = 2\pi \cos 2\pi x$

$f'(c) = 2\pi \cos 2\pi c = 0$

$\cos 2\pi c = 0$

$2\pi c = \pm \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pm \dfrac{3\pi}{2}, \pm \dfrac{5\pi}{2}, \dots$

$c = \pm \dfrac{1}{4}, \pm \dfrac{3}{4}, \pm \dfrac{5}{4}, \dots$

Since $c \in (-1, 1)$, the possible values of $c$ are $-\dfrac{3}{4}, -\dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{1}{4}, \dfrac{3}{4}$.

Thus, there are $4$ values of $c$ that satisfy Rolle's theorem.

Answer: 04

</details>
