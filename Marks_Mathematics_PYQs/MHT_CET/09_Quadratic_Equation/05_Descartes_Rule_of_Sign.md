### Q1. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation $\mathrm{e}^{\sin x}-\mathrm{e}^{-\sin x}=4$ has _______ solutions.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** 4
- **(C)** 3
- **(D)** no

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) no**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $y = \mathrm{e}^{\sin x}$. The given equation becomes $y - \dfrac{1}{y} = 4$.

$y^2 - 4y - 1 = 0$

$y = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 4(1)(-1)}}{2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{5}$

Since $y = \mathrm{e}^{\sin x} > 0$, we must have $y = 2 + \sqrt{5}$.

We know that $-1 \le \sin x \le 1$, which implies $\dfrac{1}{\mathrm{e}} \le \mathrm{e}^{\sin x} \le \mathrm{e}$.

The maximum possible value of $\mathrm{e}^{\sin x}$ is $\mathrm{e} \approx 2.718$.

However, $2 + \sqrt{5} \approx 4.236$, which is strictly greater than $\mathrm{e}$.

Therefore, there is no real value of $x$ satisfying the equation, meaning it has no solution.

Answer: no

</details>
