### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the line $\dfrac{x-2}{3} = \dfrac{y-1}{-5} = \dfrac{z+2}{2}$ lies in the plane $x + 3y + \alpha z + \beta = 0$, then $(\alpha, \beta)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(6, 7)$
- **(B)** $(-6, 7)$
- **(C)** $(6, -7)$
- **(D)** $(-6, -7)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $(6, 7)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The line $\dfrac{x-2}{3} = \dfrac{y-1}{-5} = \dfrac{z+2}{2}$ lies in the plane $x + 3y + \alpha z + \beta = 0$.

The direction ratios of the line are $(3, -5, 2)$ and the normal to the plane has direction ratios $(1, 3, \alpha)$.

Since the line lies in the plane, it is perpendicular to the normal of the plane.

$3(1) - 5(3) + 2(\alpha) = 0$

$3 - 15 + 2\alpha = 0$

$2\alpha = 12 \Rightarrow \alpha = 6$

Also, the point $(2, 1, -2)$ on the line must satisfy the equation of the plane.

$2 + 3(1) + \alpha(-2) + \beta = 0$

$5 - 2(6) + \beta = 0$

$5 - 12 + \beta = 0 \Rightarrow \beta = 7$

Thus, $(\alpha, \beta) = (6, 7)$.

Answer: $(6, 7)$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The angle between the line $\dfrac{x+1}{1} = \dfrac{y-2}{2} = \dfrac{z-3}{3}$ and the plane $3x + 4y + z + 5 = 0$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{7}}{3}\right)$
- **(B)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{26}}\right)$
- **(C)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{21}}{14}\right)$
- **(D)** $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{28}}{21}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{26}}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The direction ratios of the line are $1, 2, 3$.

The direction vector of the line is $\vec{b} = \hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 3\hat{k}$.

The direction ratios of the normal to the plane are $3, 4, 1$.

The normal vector to the plane is $\vec{n} = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + \hat{k}$.

The angle $\theta$ between the line and the plane is given by:

$\sin \theta = \left| \dfrac{\vec{b} \cdot \vec{n}}{|\vec{b}| |\vec{n}|} \right|$

$\vec{b} \cdot \vec{n} = (1)(3) + (2)(4) + (3)(1) = 3 + 8 + 3 = 14$

$|\vec{b}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 9} = \sqrt{14}$

$|\vec{n}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 1} = \sqrt{26}$

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{14}{\sqrt{14} \sqrt{26}}$

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{26}}$

$\theta = \sin^{-1} \left( \dfrac{\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{26}} \right)$

</details>

- **(D)** $\dfrac{29}{24}$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $-\dfrac{29}{24}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The direction ratios of the line are $1, 2, k$.

The direction ratios of the normal to the plane are $2, -3, 3$.

Let $\theta$ be the angle between the line and the plane. The sine of the angle is given by:

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{|1(2) + 2(-3) + k(3)|}{\sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + k^2} \sqrt{2^2 + (-3)^2 + 3^2}} = \dfrac{|3k - 4|}{\sqrt{5 + k^2} \sqrt{22}}$

$\sin \theta = \sqrt{1 - \cos^2 \theta} = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{13}{22}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{9}{22}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{22}}$

$\dfrac{|3k - 4|}{\sqrt{5 + k^2} \sqrt{22}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{22}}$

Squaring both sides:

$(3k - 4)^2 = 9(5 + k^2)$

$9k^2 - 24k + 16 = 45 + 9k^2$

$k = -\dfrac{29}{24}$
