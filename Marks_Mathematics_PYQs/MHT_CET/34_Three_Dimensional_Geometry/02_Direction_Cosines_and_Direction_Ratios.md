### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The direction cosines $l, m, n$ of the two lines are connected by the relations $l + m + n = 0$ and $lm = 0$, then the angle between the lines is....

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0$
- **(B)** $45^\circ$
- **(C)** $60^\circ$
- **(D)** $90^\circ$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $60^\circ$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given relations are $l + m + n = 0$ and $lm = 0$.

From $lm = 0$, we have two cases: $l = 0$ or $m = 0$.

Case 1: $l = 0$
Substituting $l = 0$ in $l + m + n = 0$, we get $m = -n$.
The direction ratios of the first line are proportional to $(0, -n, n)$, which simplifies to $(0, -1, 1)$.

Case 2: $m = 0$
Substituting $m = 0$ in $l + m + n = 0$, we get $l = -n$.
The direction ratios of the second line are proportional to $(-n, 0, n)$, which simplifies to $(-1, 0, 1)$.

The angle $\theta$ between the two lines is given by:
$\cos \theta = \dfrac{|a_1 a_2 + b_1 b_2 + c_1 c_2|}{\sqrt{a_1^2 + b_1^2 + c_1^2} \sqrt{a_2^2 + b_2^2 + c_2^2}}$

Substituting the values:
$\cos \theta = \dfrac{|(0)(-1) + (-1)(0) + (1)(1)|}{\sqrt{0^2 + (-1)^2 + 1^2} \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 0^2 + 1^2}}$

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\theta = 60^\circ$

Answer: $60^\circ$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The position vector of a point $P$, such that $OP$ inclined to the X axis at $45^\circ$ and to the Y axis at $60^\circ$ and $|OP| = 12$ units is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\vec{r} = 6\sqrt{2}\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}$
- **(B)** $\vec{r} = 6\sqrt{2}\hat{i} \pm 6\hat{j} \pm 6\hat{k}$
- **(C)** $\vec{r} = 6\sqrt{2}\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} \pm 6\hat{k}$
- **(D)** $\vec{r} = 6\sqrt{2}\hat{i} - 6\hat{j} - 6\hat{k}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\vec{r} = 6\sqrt{2}\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} \pm 6\hat{k}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the direction cosines of the vector $\vec{OP}$ be $l, m, n$.

Given that the vector is inclined at $45^\circ$ to the X-axis and $60^\circ$ to the Y-axis.

$l = \cos 45^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$m = \cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Using the relation $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$:

$\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + n^2 = 1$

$\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4} + n^2 = 1$

$n^2 = 1 - \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{1}{4}$

$n = \pm \dfrac{1}{2}$

The position vector $\vec{r}$ is given by $\vec{r} = |\vec{OP}| (l\hat{i} + m\hat{j} + n\hat{k})$.

Substituting $|\vec{OP}| = 12$ and the values of $l, m, n$:

$\vec{r} = 12 \left( \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{i} + \dfrac{1}{2}\hat{j} \pm \dfrac{1}{2}\hat{k} \right)$

$\vec{r} = 6\sqrt{2}\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} \pm 6\hat{k}$

Answer: $\vec{r} = 6\sqrt{2}\hat{i} + 6\hat{j} \pm 6\hat{k}$

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $l, m, n$ are direction cosines of a vector $\bar{r}$ such that $l + m + n = 1$ and $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$, then:
(where $l,\,m,\,n \geq 0$)

**Options:**

- **(A)** the vector lies along one of the coordinate axes.
- **(B)** the vector $\bar{r}$ is equally inclined to the three coordinate axes.
- **(C)** The vector $\bar{r}$ is perpendicular to the vector $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$
- **(D)** The vector $\bar{r}$ makes an angle of $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ with each of the coordinate axes.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) the vector lies along one of the coordinate axes.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $l + m + n = 1$

Squaring both sides:

$(l + m + n)^2 = 1$

$l^2 + m^2 + n^2 + 2(lm + mn + nl) = 1$

Since $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$ for direction cosines:

$1 + 2(lm + mn + nl) = 1$

$lm + mn + nl = 0$

Given $l, m, n \geq 0$, the sum of non-negative terms can be zero only if at least two of $l, m, n$ are zero.

If $m = 0$ and $n = 0$, then $l = 1$.

Thus, the possible direction cosines are $(1, 0, 0)$, $(0, 1, 0)$, or $(0, 0, 1)$.

This implies that the vector $\bar{r}$ lies along one of the coordinate axes.

Answer: the vector lies along one of the coordinate axes.

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

A line makes the same angle $\alpha$ with each of the $X$ and $Z$ axes. If the angle $\beta$ which it makes with the $Y$-axis is such that $\sin^2 \beta = 2 \sin^2 \alpha$, then the value of $\cos^2 \alpha$ is ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{4}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{3}{5}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{4}{5}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the direction cosines of the line be $l, m, n$.

Given that the line makes an angle $\alpha$ with the $X$ and $Z$ axes, and an angle $\beta$ with the $Y$-axis, we have:

$l = \cos \alpha$, $m = \cos \beta$, $n = \cos \alpha$

Using the property of direction cosines, $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$:

$\cos^2 \alpha + \cos^2 \beta + \cos^2 \alpha = 1$

$2 \cos^2 \alpha + \cos^2 \beta = 1$

We are given that $\sin^2 \beta = 2 \sin^2 \alpha$. Expressing this in terms of cosine:

$1 - \cos^2 \beta = 2(1 - \cos^2 \alpha)$

$\cos^2 \beta = 2 \cos^2 \alpha - 1$

Substituting this value of $\cos^2 \beta$ into the first equation:

$2 \cos^2 \alpha + (2 \cos^2 \alpha - 1) = 1$

$4 \cos^2 \alpha = 2$

$\cos^2 \alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{2}$

</details>

The angle between a diagonal and one of its edges of a cube is ...................

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$
- **(B)** $\cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)$
- **(C)** $\cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)$
- **(D)** $\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{2}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{2}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Consider a cube of side length $a$ with one of its vertices at the origin $(0,0,0)$ and edges along the coordinate axes.

The vector representing an edge along the x-axis is $\vec{e} = a\hat{i}$.

The vector representing the diagonal of the cube from the origin is $\vec{d} = a\hat{i} + a\hat{j} + a\hat{k}$.

The angle $\theta$ between the diagonal and the edge is given by $\cos\theta = \dfrac{\vec{d} \cdot \vec{e}}{|\vec{d}| |\vec{e}|}$.

$\vec{d} \cdot \vec{e} = (a)(a) + (a)(0) + (a)(0) = a^2$

$|\vec{d}| = \sqrt{a^2 + a^2 + a^2} = a\sqrt{3}$

$|\vec{e}| = a$

$\cos\theta = \dfrac{a^2}{a\sqrt{3} \times a} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$

Using trigonometric identities, if $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, then the adjacent side is $1$ and the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{3}$.

The opposite side is $\sqrt{(\sqrt{3})^2 - 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$.

Therefore, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{1} = \sqrt{2}$.

$\theta = \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{2})$

Answer: $\tan^{-1}\left(\sqrt{2}\right)$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

$5\left(\dfrac{l}{n}\right)^2 + 6\left(\dfrac{l}{n}\right) - 2 = 0$

### Q73. MHT CET 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The angle between a line with direction ratio $2: 2: 1$ and a line joining $(3,1,4)$ to $(7,2,12)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\cos ^{-1}(2 / 3)$
- **(B)** $\cos ^{-1}(3 / 2)$
- **(C)** $\tan ^{-1}(-2 / 3)$
- **(D)** None of the above

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\cos ^{-1}(2 / 3)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Direction ratios of the line joining the points $(3,1,4)$ and $(7,2,12)$ are
$$
\begin{array}{l}
=\langle 7-3,2-1,12-4\rangle \\
= < 4,1,8> \\
=\left\langle a_{1}, a_{2}, a_{3}\right\rangle
\end{array}
$$
And the direction ratio of given line is
$$
\begin{array}{l}
= < 2,2,1> \\
= < b_{1}, b_{2}, b_{3}>
\end{array}
$$
Let $Q$ be the angle between the lines,
$$
\text { then } \cos \theta=\frac{a_{1} b_{1}+a_{2} b_{2}+a_{3} b_{3}}{\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}} \sqrt{b_{1}^{2}+b_{2}^{2}+b_{3}^{2}}}
$$
$$
\begin{array}{l}
\Rightarrow \quad \cos \theta=\frac{(4)(2)+(1)(2)+(8)(1)}{\sqrt{16+1+64} \sqrt{4+4+1}} \\
\Rightarrow \quad \cos \theta=\frac{18}{\sqrt{81} \sqrt{9}}=\frac{18}{9 \times 3}=\frac{2}{3} \\
\Rightarrow \quad \theta=\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)
\end{array}
$$

</details>

### Q74. MHT CET 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\alpha, \beta$ and $\gamma$ are the angles which a half ray makes with the positive direction of the axes, then $\sin ^{2} \alpha+\sin ^{2} \beta+\sin ^{2} \gamma$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1
- **(B)** 2
- **(C)** 0
- **(D)** $-1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given expression, $\sin ^{2} \alpha+\sin ^{2} \beta+\sin ^{2} \gamma$
$$
\begin{array}{r}
=\left(1-\cos ^{2} \alpha\right)+\left(1-\cos ^{2} \beta\right)+\left(1-\cos ^{2} \gamma\right) \\
=3-\cos ^{2} \alpha+\cos ^{2} \beta+\cos ^{2} \gamma=3-1=2 \\
\left(\because \cos ^{2} \alpha+\cos ^{2} \beta+\cos ^{2} \gamma=1\right)
\end{array}
$$

</details>
