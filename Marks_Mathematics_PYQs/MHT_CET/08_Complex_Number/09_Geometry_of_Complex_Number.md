### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A particle $P$ starts from the point $Z_0=1+2 i$ where $i=\sqrt{-1}$. It moves first horizontally away from the origin by 5 units and then vertically upwards parallel to positive $y$-axis by 3 units to reach a point $Z_1$. From $Z_1$ the particle moves $\sqrt{2}$ units in the direction of vector $\hat{i}+\hat{j}$ and then it moves through an angle $\frac{\pi}{2}$ in anticlockwise direction on a circle with centre at origin to reach at point $Z_2$, then $Z_2=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $6+7 i$
- **(B)** $-7+6 i$
- **(C)** $-6+7 i$
- **(D)** $7-6 i$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $-6+7 i$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Initial position of the particle is $Z_0 = 1 + 2i$.

The particle moves horizontally away from the origin by 5 units. Since the real part of $Z_0$ is positive, moving away from the origin horizontally means moving in the positive real direction. The new position is $(1 + 5) + 2i = 6 + 2i$.

It then moves vertically upwards by 3 units. The new position is $Z_1 = 6 + (2 + 3)i = 6 + 5i$.

From $Z_1$, the particle moves $\sqrt{2}$ units in the direction of the vector $\hat{i} + \hat{j}$. The unit vector in this direction is $\dfrac{1 + i}{\sqrt{2}}$.

The displacement is $\sqrt{2} \times \dfrac{1 + i}{\sqrt{2}} = 1 + i$.

The position after this displacement is $Z_1' = (6 + 5i) + (1 + i) = 7 + 6i$.

Finally, the particle moves through an angle $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ in the anticlockwise direction on a circle with its centre at the origin. This corresponds to a rotation of the position vector by $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ about the origin, which is equivalent to multiplying by $e^{i\pi/2} = i$.

Therefore, $Z_2 = (7 + 6i) \times i = 7i + 6i^2 = -6 + 7i$.

Answer: $-6+7 i$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\frac{z-1}{2 z+1}$ is an imaginary number and if it represents a circle then its radius is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{9}{16}$ units
- **(B)** $\frac{3}{4}$ units
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{4}$ units
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{2}$ units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{3}{4}$ units**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $z = x + iy$.

The given complex number is $w = \dfrac{z-1}{2z+1}$.

Substituting $z = x + iy$ into the expression:

$w = \dfrac{x + iy - 1}{2(x + iy) + 1} = \dfrac{(x-1) + iy}{(2x+1) + i(2y)}$

To find the real part, multiply the numerator and denominator by the complex conjugate of the denominator:

$w = \dfrac{((x-1) + iy)((2x+1) - i(2y))}{(2x+1)^2 + (2y)^2}$

The real part of $w$ is:

$\text{Re}(w) = \dfrac{(x-1)(2x+1) + y(2y)}{(2x+1)^2 + 4y^2}$

Since $w$ is an imaginary number, its real part must be zero:

$(x-1)(2x+1) + 2y^2 = 0$

$2x^2 - x - 1 + 2y^2 = 0$

Dividing by $2$, we get the equation of the circle:

$x^2 + y^2 - \dfrac{1}{2}x - \dfrac{1}{2} = 0$

Comparing this with the general equation of a circle $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$, we have:

$2g = -\dfrac{1}{2} \Rightarrow g = -\dfrac{1}{4}$

$2f = 0 \Rightarrow f = 0$

$c = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

The radius of the circle is given by $r = \sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c}$:

$r = \sqrt{\left(-\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^2 + 0^2 - \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}$

$r = \sqrt{\dfrac{1}{16} + \dfrac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{1 + 8}{16}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{9}{16}} = \dfrac{3}{4}$ units

Answer: $\frac{3}{4}$ units

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $\mathrm{z}=x+\mathrm{i} y$ be a complex number, where $x$ and $y$ are integers and $\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}$. Then the area of the rectangle whose vertices are the roots of the equation $z \bar{z}^3+\bar{z} z^3=350$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 48
- **(B)** 32
- **(C)** 40
- **(D)** 80

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 48**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the equation $z \bar{z}^3 + \bar{z} z^3 = 350$

Factoring out $z \bar{z}$, we get:

$z \bar{z} (\bar{z}^2 + z^2) = 350$

Let $z = x + \mathrm{i}y$. Then $z \bar{z} = x^2 + y^2$.

Also, $z^2 + \bar{z}^2 = (x + \mathrm{i}y)^2 + (x - \mathrm{i}y)^2 = (x^2 - y^2 + 2\mathrm{i}xy) + (x^2 - y^2 - 2\mathrm{i}xy) = 2(x^2 - y^2)$

Substituting these into the equation:

$(x^2 + y^2) \cdot 2(x^2 - y^2) = 350$

$(x^2 + y^2)(x^2 - y^2) = 175$

Since $x$ and $y$ are integers, $x^2 + y^2$ and $x^2 - y^2$ must be integer factors of $175$. Also, $x^2 + y^2 \geq x^2 - y^2$ and both are positive.

The factor pairs of $175$ are $(175, 1)$, $(35, 5)$, and $(25, 7)$.

Checking the pairs for $(x^2 + y^2, x^2 - y^2)$:

If $x^2 + y^2 = 175$ and $x^2 - y^2 = 1$, adding them gives $2x^2 = 176 \Rightarrow x^2 = 88$ (not a perfect square).

If $x^2 + y^2 = 35$ and $x^2 - y^2 = 5$, adding them gives $2x^2 = 40 \Rightarrow x^2 = 20$ (not a perfect square).

If $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ and $x^2 - y^2 = 7$, adding them gives $2x^2 = 32 \Rightarrow x^2 = 16$.

Substituting $x^2 = 16$ into $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ gives $y^2 = 9$.

Since $x^2 = 16$ and $y^2 = 9$, we have $x = \pm 4$ and $y = \pm 3$.

The roots of the equation are the four vertices of a rectangle in the complex plane: $(4, 3)$, $(-4, 3)$, $(-4, -3)$, and $(4, -3)$.

The length of the rectangle is $4 - (-4) = 8$.

The width of the rectangle is $3 - (-3) = 6$.

The area of the rectangle is $8 \times 6 = 48$.

Answer: 48

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the complex number $\mathrm{z}=x+\mathrm{i} y$, where $\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}$, satisfies the condition $|z+1|=1$, then $z$ lies on

**Options:**

- **(A)** X -axis.
- **(B)** circle with centre $(1,0)$ and radius 1 unit.
- **(C)** circle with centre $(-1,0)$ and radius 1 unit.
- **(D)** Y-axis.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) circle with centre $(-1,0)$ and radius 1 unit.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the condition $|z+1|=1$.

Substituting $z = x + \mathrm{i}y$, we get:

$|x + \mathrm{i}y + 1| = 1$

$|(x + 1) + \mathrm{i}y| = 1$

Using the definition of the modulus of a complex number, we have:

$\sqrt{(x + 1)^2 + y^2} = 1$

Squaring both sides gives:

$(x + 1)^2 + y^2 = 1$

This represents the equation of a circle in the form $(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$, where $(h, k)$ is the centre and $r$ is the radius.

Comparing the equations, we get the centre as $(-1, 0)$ and the radius as $1$ unit.

Therefore, $z$ lies on a circle with centre $(-1, 0)$ and radius $1$ unit.

Answer: circle with centre $(-1,0)$ and radius 1 unit.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If $\left|\dfrac{\mathrm{z}}{1+\mathrm{i}}\right|=2$, where $\mathrm{z}=x+\mathrm{i} y, \mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}$ represents a circle, then centre ' $C$ ' and radius ' $r$ ' of the circle are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{C} \equiv(3,0), \mathrm{r}=4$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{C} \equiv(6,0), \mathrm{r}=2$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{C} \equiv(0,3), \mathrm{r}=8$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{C} \equiv(0,0), \mathrm{r}=2 \sqrt{2}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mathrm{C} \equiv(0,0), \mathrm{r}=2 \sqrt{2}$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation is $\left|\dfrac{z}{1+\mathrm{i}}\right| = 2$

Using the property of modulus, $\left|\dfrac{z_1}{z_2}\right| = \dfrac{|z_1|}{|z_2|}$

$\dfrac{|z|}{|1+\mathrm{i}|} = 2$

Since $|1+\mathrm{i}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$

$\dfrac{|z|}{\sqrt{2}} = 2$

$|z| = 2\sqrt{2}$

Substituting $z = x + \mathrm{i}y$, we get $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = 2\sqrt{2}$

$x^2 + y^2 = 8$

This represents a circle with centre at $(0,0)$ and radius $2\sqrt{2}$.

Therefore, $\mathrm{C} \equiv (0,0)$ and $\mathrm{r} = 2\sqrt{2}$.

Answer: $\mathrm{C} \equiv(0,0), \mathrm{r}=2 \sqrt{2}$.

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2023 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $|z-2+i| \leq 2$, then the difference between the greatest and least value of $|z|$ is $(\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1})$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2 \sqrt{5}+4$
- **(B)** $2 \sqrt{5}$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $8$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Note that $\left|z_1-z_2\right| \geq|| z_1|-| z_2||$
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\therefore \quad & |z-2+i| \leq 2 \\
& \Rightarrow|| z|-| 2-i|| \leq 2 \\
& \Rightarrow-2 \leq|z|-|2-i| \leq 2
\end{array}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow-2 \leq|z|-\sqrt{4+1} \leq 2 \\
& \Rightarrow-2 \leq|z|-\sqrt{5} \leq 2 \\
& \Rightarrow \sqrt{5}-2 \leq|z| \leq 2+\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$
$\Rightarrow$ Largest value of $|z|$ is ' $2+\sqrt{5}$ ' and the least value is ' $\sqrt{5}-2$ '
$\therefore \quad$ Required difference $=2+\sqrt{5}-(\sqrt{5}-2)=4$

### Q7. MHT CET 2023 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If $\mathrm{w}=\frac{\mathrm{z}}{\mathrm{z}-\frac{1}{3} \mathrm{i}}$ and $|\mathrm{w}|=1, \mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}$, then $\mathrm{z}$ lies on

**Options:**

- **(A)** circle.
- **(B)** line.
- **(C)** parabola.
- **(D)** ellipse.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) line.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned}
& w=\frac{z}{z-\frac{1}{3} i} \\
& \Rightarrow w=\frac{3 z}{3 z-i}
\end{aligned}$
Applying mod on both sides, we get
$\begin{aligned}
& |w|=\frac{3|z|}{|3 z-i|} \\
& \Rightarrow 3|z|=|3 z-i| \quad \ldots[|w|=1]
\end{aligned}$
Consider $\mathrm{z}=\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{ib}$
$\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow 3|a+i b|=|3 a+3 i b-i| \\
& \Rightarrow 3|a+i b|=|3 a+(3 b-1) i| \\
& \Rightarrow 3\left(\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\right)=\left(\sqrt{9 a^2+(3 b-1)^2}\right) \\
& \Rightarrow 9 a^2+9 b^2=9 a^2+9 b^2-6 b+1 \\
& \Rightarrow 6 b-1=0
\end{aligned}$
$\therefore \quad$ The above equation represents a straight line.

</details>

### Q8. MHT CET 2021 (24 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If amplitude of $(z-2-3 i)$ is $\frac{3 \pi}{4}$, then locus of $z$ is (where $\mathrm{z}=\mathrm{x}+$ iy)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x+y=1$
- **(B)** $x+y=5$
- **(C)** $x-y=-5$
- **(D)** $x-y=1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $x+y=5$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Amplitude of $(z-2-3 i)$ is $\frac{3 \pi}{4}$ and we have $z=x+$ iy
$\therefore \operatorname{Amp}[(\mathrm{x}-2)+\mathrm{i}(\mathrm{y}-3)]$ is $\frac{3 \pi}{4}$
$\therefore \tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{y}-3}{\mathrm{x}-2}\right)=\frac{3 \pi}{4} \Rightarrow \tan \left(\frac{3 \pi}{4}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{y}-3}{\mathrm{x}-2}$
$\therefore-1=\frac{y-3}{x-2} \Rightarrow-x+2=y-3 \Rightarrow x+y=5$

</details>
