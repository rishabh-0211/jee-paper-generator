### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $|z - 5i| = |z + 3|$, where $z$ is a complex number, then the equation of the locus of $z$ in Cartesian coordinates is ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3x + 5y - 8 = 0$
- **(B)** $3x - 5y - 8 = 0$
- **(C)** $-3x + 5y - 8 = 0$
- **(D)** $3x + 5y + 8 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $3x + 5y - 8 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $z = x + iy$.

Substituting $z$ in the given equation $|z - 5i| = |z + 3|$, we get:

$|x + iy - 5i| = |x + iy + 3|$

$|x + i(y - 5)| = |(x + 3) + iy|$

Squaring both sides:

$x^2 + (y - 5)^2 = (x + 3)^2 + y^2$

$x^2 + y^2 - 10y + 25 = x^2 + 6x + 9 + y^2$

$-10y + 25 = 6x + 9$

$6x + 10y - 16 = 0$

Dividing by $2$, we get the locus of $z$ as:

$3x + 5y - 8 = 0$

Answer: $3x + 5y - 8 = 0$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\mathrm{z}=x+$ iy is a complex number, then the equation $\left|\frac{\mathrm{z}+i}{\mathrm{z}-i}\right|=\sqrt{3}$ represents the

**Options:**

- **(A)** circle with centre $(2,0)$ and radius $\sqrt{3}$
- **(B)** circle with centre $(0,2)$ and radius $\sqrt{3}$
- **(C)** circle with centre $(0,0)$ and radius $\sqrt{3}$
- **(D)** circle with centre $(0,-2)$ and radius $\sqrt{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) circle with centre $(0,2)$ and radius $\sqrt{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation is $\left|\dfrac{z+i}{z-i}\right| = \sqrt{3}$

Substituting $z = x + iy$:

$\left|\dfrac{x + i(y+1)}{x + i(y-1)}\right| = \sqrt{3}$

$|x + i(y+1)|^2 = 3|x + i(y-1)|^2$

$x^2 + (y+1)^2 = 3[x^2 + (y-1)^2]$

$x^2 + y^2 + 2y + 1 = 3(x^2 + y^2 - 2y + 1)$

$2x^2 + 2y^2 - 8y + 2 = 0$

$x^2 + y^2 - 4y + 1 = 0$

$x^2 + (y-2)^2 = 3$

This represents a circle with centre $(0, 2)$ and radius $\sqrt{3}$.

Answer: circle with centre $(0,2)$ and radius $\sqrt{3}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The locus of the points represented by $|z+3|-|z-3|=6$, where $z$ is a complex number, is ....

**Options:**

- **(A)** Circle with radius 1 unit
- **(B)** Straight line with slope 1.
- **(C)** Parabola with focus $(1,0)$
- **(D)** A ray on the X-axis

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) A ray on the X-axis**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $z = x + iy$.

The given equation is $|z+3| - |z-3| = 6$.

This represents the difference of distances of a point $P(z)$ from two fixed points $A(-3, 0)$ and $B(3, 0)$.

The distance between $A$ and $B$ is $AB = |3 - (-3)| = 6$.

The given equation can be written as $PA - PB = AB$.

This condition is satisfied if and only if the points $P, B, A$ are collinear and $B$ lies between $P$ and $A$.

Since $A$ is at $(-3, 0)$ and $B$ is at $(3, 0)$, $P$ must lie on the X-axis to the right of $B(3, 0)$.

Therefore, the locus of $z$ is a ray on the X-axis starting from $(3, 0)$ and extending towards positive infinity.

Answer: A ray on the X-axis

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The equation $|z+1-i|=|z-1+i|$ represents a (where $z$ is a complex number)

**Options:**

- **(A)** Straight line passing through the origin and the first and third quadrant.
- **(B)** Straight line passing through the origin and the second and fourth quadrant.
- **(C)** Straight line passing through the point $(1,-1)$ and having slope -1 .
- **(D)** Straight line passing through the point $(2,1)$ and having slope $\frac{1}{2}$.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) Straight line passing through the origin and the first and third quadrant.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $z = x + iy$.

The given equation is $|z+1-i|=|z-1+i|$.

Substituting $z = x + iy$, we get:

$|x + iy + 1 - i| = |x + iy - 1 + i|$

$|(x + 1) + i(y - 1)| = |(x - 1) + i(y + 1)|$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$(x + 1)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = (x - 1)^2 + (y + 1)^2$

$x^2 + 2x + 1 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 + 2y + 1$

$2x - 2y = -2x + 2y$

$4x = 4y$

$y = x$

This represents a straight line passing through the origin with a slope of $1$. Since the slope is positive, the line passes through the first and third quadrants.

Answer: Straight line passing through the origin and the first and third quadrant.

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $\mathrm{P}(x, y)$ denotes $\mathrm{z}=x+\mathrm{i} y x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ and $\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}$ in Argand's plane and $\left|\dfrac{z-1}{z+2 i}\right|=1$, then the locus of $P$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** parabola
- **(B)** hyperbola
- **(C)** circle
- **(D)** straight line

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) straight line**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the equation $\left|\dfrac{z-1}{z+2 i}\right|=1$

This can be rewritten as $|z-1| = |z+2i|$

Let $z = x + iy$. Substituting this into the equation, we get:

$|x + iy - 1| = |x + iy + 2i|$

$|(x-1) + iy| = |x + i(y+2)|$

Squaring both sides:

$(x-1)^2 + y^2 = x^2 + (y+2)^2$

$x^2 - 2x + 1 + y^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 4y + 4$

$-2x + 1 = 4y + 4$

$2x + 4y + 3 = 0$

This is a linear equation in $x$ and $y$, which represents a straight line.

Alternatively, $|z-z_1| = |z-z_2|$ represents the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the points $z_1$ and $z_2$ in the Argand plane, which is always a straight line.

Answer: straight line

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2021 (23 Sep Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\mathrm{z}=\mathrm{x}+$ iy satisfies the condition $|\mathrm{z}+1|=1$, then $\mathrm{z}$ lies on the

**Options:**

- **(A)** parabola with vertex $(0,0)$
- **(B)** circle with centre $(-1,0)$ and radius 1
- **(C)** circle with centre $(1,0)$ and radius 1
- **(D)** Y-axis

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) circle with centre $(-1,0)$ and radius 1**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\mathrm{z}=\mathrm{x}+$ iy and $|\mathrm{z}+1|=1$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \therefore|(\mathrm{x}+1)+\mathrm{iy}|=1 \\
& \therefore \sqrt{(\mathrm{x}+1)^2+\mathrm{y}^2}=1 \Rightarrow(\mathrm{x}+1)^2+\mathrm{y}^2=1
\end{aligned}
$$
This is an equation of a circle with centre $(-1,0)$ and radius 1 .

</details>
