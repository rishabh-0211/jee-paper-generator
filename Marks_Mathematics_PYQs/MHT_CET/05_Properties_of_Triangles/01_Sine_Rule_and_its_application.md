### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In $\triangle ABC$, with usual notations, if $A = 60^\circ$ and $a = 4$, then the maximum possible area (in sq. units) of $\triangle ABC$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $4\sqrt{3}$
- **(C)** $8$
- **(D)** $8\sqrt{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4\sqrt{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the cosine rule, $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \cos A$.

Substituting $a = 4$ and $A = 60^\circ$:

$16 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$

$16 = b^2 + c^2 - bc$

This can be rewritten as:

$16 = (b-c)^2 + bc$

The area of the triangle is given by $\Delta = \dfrac{1}{2}bc \sin A$.

To maximize the area, the product $bc$ must be maximum.

Since $(b-c)^2 \ge 0$, the maximum value of $bc$ is $16$, which occurs when $b = c$.

Substituting $bc = 16$ and $A = 60^\circ$ into the area formula:

$\Delta = \dfrac{1}{2}(16)\sin 60^\circ = 8 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 4\sqrt{3}$

Answer: $4\sqrt{3}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In $\Delta ABC$ with the usual notations, angles $A, B, C$ of are in A.P. and $b : c = \sqrt{3} : \sqrt{2}$. The ratio of the largest and the smallest angle of the triangle will be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2:1$
- **(B)** $3:1$
- **(C)** $5:3$
- **(D)** $4:3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5:3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given that the angles $A, B, C$ are in A.P., we have $2B = A + C$.

Since $A + B + C = 180^{\circ}$, we get $3B = 180^{\circ} \Rightarrow B = 60^{\circ}$.

Using the Sine Rule, $\dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$.

Substituting the given ratio $b : c = \sqrt{3} : \sqrt{2}$ and $B = 60^{\circ}$:

$\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sin 60^{\circ}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sin C}$

$\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sin C}$

$2 = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sin C} \Rightarrow \sin C = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

Since $C$ is an angle of a triangle and $B = 60^{\circ}$, $C$ must be $45^{\circ}$ (as $C = 135^{\circ}$ would make $B + C > 180^{\circ}$).

The third angle $A$ is $180^{\circ} - (60^{\circ} + 45^{\circ}) = 75^{\circ}$.

The largest angle is $75^{\circ}$ and the smallest angle is $45^{\circ}$.

The ratio of the largest to the smallest angle is $\dfrac{75^{\circ}}{45^{\circ}} = \dfrac{5}{3}$.

Answer: $5:3$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In $\Delta ABC$, with usual notations, if $\sin B-\cos C=\cos A$, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $a^2, b^2, c^2$ form an Arithmetic Progression
- **(B)** $b^2, c^2, a^2$ form a Geometric Progression
- **(C)** $a^2=b^2+c^2$
- **(D)** $b^2=a^2+c^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $a^2=b^2+c^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $\sin B - \cos C = \cos A$

$\Rightarrow \sin B = \cos A + \cos C$

Using the sum-to-product formula for cosines and the double angle formula for sine:

$2 \sin\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right) = 2 \cos\left(\dfrac{A+C}{2}\right) \cos\left(\dfrac{A-C}{2}\right)$

In $\Delta ABC$, $A+B+C = \pi$, which gives $\dfrac{A+C}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{B}{2}$.

$\Rightarrow \cos\left(\dfrac{A+C}{2}\right) = \cos\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - \dfrac{B}{2}\right) = \sin\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right)$

Substituting this into the equation:

$2 \sin\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right) = 2 \sin\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\dfrac{A-C}{2}\right)$

Since $0 < B < \pi$, $\sin\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right) \neq 0$. Dividing both sides by $2 \sin\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right)$ yields:

$\cos\left(\dfrac{B}{2}\right) = \cos\left(\dfrac{A-C}{2}\right)$

This implies $\dfrac{B}{2} = \dfrac{A-C}{2}$ or $\dfrac{B}{2} = \dfrac{C-A}{2}$.

Case 1: $B = A - C \Rightarrow A = B + C$

Since $A + B + C = \pi$, we get $2A = \pi \Rightarrow A = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

By Pythagoras theorem, $a^2 = b^2 + c^2$.

Case 2: $B = C - A \Rightarrow C = A + B$

Since $A + B + C = \pi$, we get $2C = \pi \Rightarrow C = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

By Pythagoras theorem, $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$.

Since $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$ is not present in the given options, the valid relation from the options is $a^2 = b^2 + c^2$.

Answer: $a^2=b^2+c^2$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In $\triangle ABC$, with usual notation, if cot A, cot B, cot C are in arithmetic progression, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** sin A, sin B, sin C are in arithmetic progression.
- **(B)** $a^2, b^2, c^2$ are in arithmetic progression.
- **(C)** cos A, cos B, cos C are in arithmetic progression.
- **(D)** a, b, c are in arithmetic progression.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $a^2, b^2, c^2$ are in arithmetic progression.**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $\cot A, \cot B, \cot C$ are in A.P.

We know that $\cot A = \dfrac{\cos A}{\sin A}$.

Using the cosine rule, $\cos A = \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$ and using the sine rule, $\sin A = \dfrac{a}{2R}$.

Therefore, $\cot A = \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} \times \dfrac{2R}{a} = \dfrac{R(b^2 + c^2 - a^2)}{abc}$.

Similarly, $\cot B = \dfrac{R(c^2 + a^2 - b^2)}{abc}$ and $\cot C = \dfrac{R(a^2 + b^2 - c^2)}{abc}$.

Since $\cot A, \cot B, \cot C$ are in A.P., we have:

$2 \cot B = \cot A + \cot C$

Substituting the expressions, we get:

$2 \left( \dfrac{R(c^2 + a^2 - b^2)}{abc} \right) = \dfrac{R(b^2 + c^2 - a^2)}{abc} + \dfrac{R(a^2 + b^2 - c^2)}{abc}$

Cancelling the common term $\dfrac{R}{abc}$ from both sides:

$2(c^2 + a^2 - b^2) = (b^2 + c^2 - a^2) + (a^2 + b^2 - c^2)$

$2c^2 + 2a^2 - 2b^2 = 2b^2$

$2a^2 + 2c^2 = 4b^2$

$a^2 + c^2 = 2b^2$

This implies that $a^2, b^2, c^2$ are in arithmetic progression.

Answer: $a^2, b^2, c^2$ are in arithmetic progression.

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $P_1$, $P_2$, $P_3$ be the altitudes of a triangle ABC from the vertices A, B, C respectively. If $\triangle$ denotes the area of the triangle and s is the semi-perimeter of the triangle, then $\dfrac{\cos A}{P_1} + \dfrac{\cos B}{P_2} + \dfrac{\cos C}{P_3} =$

- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{R}$
- **(C)** $R^2$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{R^2}$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Detailed Solution:**

The area of the triangle is given by $\Delta = \dfrac{1}{2} a P_1 = \dfrac{1}{2} b P_2 = \dfrac{1}{2} c P_3$.

This gives $P_1 = \dfrac{2\Delta}{a}$, $P_2 = \dfrac{2\Delta}{b}$, and $P_3 = \dfrac{2\Delta}{c}$.

Substituting these into the given expression:

$\dfrac{\cos A}{P_1} + \dfrac{\cos B}{P_2} + \dfrac{\cos C}{P_3} = \dfrac{a \cos A + b \cos B + c \cos C}{2\Delta}$

Using the sine rule $a = 2R \sin A$, $b = 2R \sin B$, $c = 2R \sin C$, the numerator becomes:

$a \cos A + b \cos B + c \cos C = 2R \sin A \cos A + 2R \sin B \cos B + 2R \sin C \cos C$

$= R(\sin 2A + \sin 2B + \sin 2C)$

The denominator is $2\Delta = 4R^2 \sin A \sin B \sin C$.

Dividing the numerator by the denominator:

$\dfrac{4R \sin A \sin B \sin C}{4R^2 \sin A \sin B \sin C} = \dfrac{1}{R}$

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{R}$

In a triangle $\mathrm{ABC}$ with usual notations, if $\tan \mathrm{A}, \tan \mathrm{B}, \tan \mathrm{C}$ are in H.P., then $a^{2}, b^{2}, c^{2}$
are in

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathbf{n}} \mathbf{P}$.
- **(B)** Not in A. P.
- **(C)** $\mathrm{H}. \mathrm{P}$
- **(D)** $\boldsymbol{G}.\mathrm{P}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\boldsymbol{A}_{\mathbf{n}} \mathbf{P}$.**

**Detailed Solution:**

If $\tan A, \tan B, \tan C$ are in H.P., then
$$
\frac{2}{\tan B}=\frac{1}{\tan A}+\frac{1}{\tan C} \Rightarrow \frac{2 \cos B}{\sin B}=\frac{\cos A}{\sin A}+\frac{\cos C}{\sin C}
$$
We know that $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$
$\therefore \frac{2\left(\frac{a^{2}+c^{2}-b^{2}}{2 a c}\right)}{b k}=\frac{\frac{b^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2}}{2 b c}}{a k}+\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}}{2 a b}$
$\Rightarrow \frac{2\left(c^{2}+a^{2}-b^{2}\right)}{2 a b c k}=\frac{b^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2}}{2 a b c k}+\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}}{2 a b c k}$
$2\left(a^{2}+c^{2}-b^{2}\right)=b^{2}+c^{2}-a^{2}+a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}$
$2 a^{2}+2 c^{2}-2 b^{2}=2 b^{2} \Rightarrow 4 b^{2}=2 a^{2}+2 c^{2} \Rightarrow 2 b^{2}=a^{2}+c^{2}$
$\Rightarrow a^{2}, b^{2}, c^{2} a r e$ in $A \cdot P .$

</details>

### Q49. MHT CET 2020 (12 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If two angles of $\triangle \mathrm{ABC}$ are $\frac{\pi}{4}$ and $\frac{\pi}{3}$, then the ratio of the smallest and greatest side is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{3}: \sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** $(\sqrt{3}-1): 1$
- **(C)** $(\sqrt{3}+1):(\sqrt{3}-1)$
- **(D)** $(\sqrt{3}+1): 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $(\sqrt{3}-1): 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Two angles of triangle are $\frac{\pi}{4}$ and $\frac{\pi}{3}$ Let the third angle be $\alpha$.
$\begin{array}{l}
\therefore \frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\pi}{3}+\alpha=\pi \\
\therefore 45^{\circ}+60^{\circ}+\alpha=180^{\circ} \Rightarrow \alpha=75^{\circ}
\end{array}$
We know that side opposite to smallest angle is the smallest side and side opposite to largest angle is the largest side.
$\therefore \frac{\mathrm{c}}{\sin 75^{\circ}}=\frac{\mathrm{a}}{\sin 45^{\circ}}$
We know that $\sin 75^{\circ}=\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2 \sqrt{2}}$
$\therefore \frac{\mathrm{c}(2 \sqrt{2})}{\sqrt{3}+1}=\mathrm{a} \sqrt{2} \Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{a}}{\mathrm{c}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}+1}$
By rationalizing, we get $\frac{a}{c}=\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{1}$
Note : $\sin 75^{\circ}$ can be calculated as follows :
$\begin{aligned}
\sin 75^{\circ} &=\sin \left(45^{\circ}+30^{\circ}\right) \\
&=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \times \frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2 \sqrt{2}}
\end{aligned}$

</details>
