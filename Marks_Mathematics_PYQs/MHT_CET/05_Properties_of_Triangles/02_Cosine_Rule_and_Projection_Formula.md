### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In a triangular plot $ABC$, the side lengths are $BC = 7\,m$, $CA = 8\,m$, and $AB = 9\,m$. A vertical lamp post is located at the midpoint $E$ of side $AC$. If the angle of elevation of the top of the lamp post from point $B$ is $\tan^{-1}(4)$, then the height of the lamp post is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $22$
- **(B)** $24$
- **(C)** $26$
- **(D)** $28$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $28$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the length of the median from $B$ to $AC$ be $BE$. Using Apollonius's theorem in triangle $ABC$:

$AB^2 + BC^2 = 2(BE^2 + AE^2)$

Given $AB = 9$, $BC = 7$, and $E$ is the midpoint of $AC$, so $AE = \dfrac{AC}{2} = \dfrac{8}{2} = 4$. Substituting these values:

$9^2 + 7^2 = 2(BE^2 + 4^2)$

$81 + 49 = 2(BE^2 + 16)$

$130 = 2(BE^2 + 16)$

$65 = BE^2 + 16$

$BE^2 = 49 \Rightarrow BE = 7$

Let $h$ be the height of the vertical lamp post at $E$. The angle of elevation of the top of the lamp post from $B$ is $\theta = \tan^{-1}(4)$, which gives $\tan \theta = 4$.

From the right-angled triangle formed by the lamp post and the segment $BE$:

$\tan \theta = \dfrac{h}{BE}$

$4 = \dfrac{h}{7}$

$h = 28$

Answer: $28$

</details>

In a triangle, the lengths of the two largest sides are $10$ cm. and $9$ cm. If the angles of the triangle are in arithmatic progression (A. P.), then the length of the third side (in cm.) is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 \pm \sqrt{6}$
- **(B)** $4 \pm \sqrt{6}$
- **(C)** $5 \pm \sqrt{6}$
- **(D)** $6 \pm \sqrt{6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $5 \pm \sqrt{6}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the angles of the triangle be $A, B, C$. Since they are in arithmetic progression, we have $2B = A + C$.

The sum of the angles is $A + B + C = 180^\circ$, which gives $3B = 180^\circ \Rightarrow B = 60^\circ$.

The angles are $60^\circ - d, 60^\circ, 60^\circ + d$. The side opposite to the largest angle is the largest side, and the side opposite to the middle angle $60^\circ$ is the second largest side.

Given the two largest sides are $10$ and $9$, the largest side must be $10$ and the middle side must be $9$. Thus, the side opposite to $B = 60^\circ$ is $b = 9$, and the largest side is $c = 10$. Let the third (smallest) side be $a$.

Using the cosine rule for angle $B$:

$\cos B = \dfrac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac}$

$\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{a^2 + 10^2 - 9^2}{2 \cdot a \cdot 10}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{a^2 + 100 - 81}{20a}$

$10a = a^2 + 19$

$a^2 - 10a + 19 = 0$

Solving the quadratic equation for $a$:

$a = \dfrac{10 \pm \sqrt{100 - 4(19)}}{2}$

$a = \dfrac{10 \pm \sqrt{24}}{2}$

$a = 5 \pm \sqrt{6}$

Both values $5 + \sqrt{6}$ and $5 - \sqrt{6}$ are less than $9$, making them valid lengths for the smallest side.

Answer: $5 \pm \sqrt{6}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In $\triangle ABC$ with usual notations, if $a, c, b$ are in Arithmetic Progression (A.P.), then the value of $\dfrac{a \cos B - b \cos A}{a - b}$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0$
- **(B)** $1$
- **(C)** $2$
- **(D)** $3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Using the cosine rule, we have:

$\cos B = \dfrac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac}$

$\cos A = \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$

Substituting these into the numerator of the given expression:

$a \cos B - b \cos A = a \left( \dfrac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2ac} \right) - b \left( \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} \right)$

$= \dfrac{a^2 + c^2 - b^2}{2c} - \dfrac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2c}$

$= \dfrac{2a^2 - 2b^2}{2c} = \dfrac{a^2 - b^2}{c}$

Substituting this back into the original expression:

$\dfrac{a \cos B - b \cos A}{a - b} = \dfrac{\dfrac{a^2 - b^2}{c}}{a - b} = \dfrac{(a - b)(a + b)}{c(a - b)} = \dfrac{a + b}{c}$

Since $a, c, b$ are in Arithmetic Progression (A.P.), we have:

$2c = a + b$

Therefore, the value of the expression is:

$\dfrac{a + b}{c} = \dfrac{2c}{c} = 2$

Answer: $2$

</details>

$\dfrac{\cos 3A}{\cos A} + 2 = \dfrac{4\cos^3 A - 3\cos A}{\cos A} + 2 = 4\cos^2 A - 3 + 2 = 4\cos^2 A - 1$

By the cosine rule in $\triangle ABC$, we have:

$= \dfrac{(b^2 - c^2)^2}{4b^2 c^2}$

### Q13. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $p_1, p_2, p_3$ are altitudes of a triangle $A B C$ from the vertices $A, B, C$ respectively and if $\Delta$ is the area of the triangle, $S$ is the semi perimeter of the triangle, then
$\frac{\operatorname{Cos} \mathrm{A}}{\mathrm{p}_1}+\frac{\operatorname{Cos} \mathrm{B}}{\mathrm{p}_2}+\frac{\operatorname{Cos} \mathrm{C}}{\mathrm{p}_3}$ is equal to (where $R$ is the circumradius)

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1/R
- **(B)** R
- **(C)** 2R
- **(D)** 2/R

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 1/R**

**Detailed Solution:**

The area of the triangle is given by $\Delta = \dfrac{1}{2} a p_1 = \dfrac{1}{2} b p_2 = \dfrac{1}{2} c p_3$.

From this, we get $p_1 = \dfrac{2\Delta}{a}$, $p_2 = \dfrac{2\Delta}{b}$, and $p_3 = \dfrac{2\Delta}{c}$.

Substituting these into the given expression:

$\dfrac{\cos A}{p_1} + \dfrac{\cos B}{p_2} + \dfrac{\cos C}{p_3} = \dfrac{a \cos A}{2\Delta} + \dfrac{b \cos B}{2\Delta} + \dfrac{c \cos C}{2\Delta}$

$= \dfrac{1}{2\Delta} (a \cos A + b \cos B + c \cos C)$

Using the sine rule, $a = 2R \sin A$, $b = 2R \sin B$, and $c = 2R \sin C$. Substituting these into the numerator:

$a \cos A + b \cos B + c \cos C = 2R \sin A \cos A + 2R \sin B \cos B + 2R \sin C \cos C$

$= R (\sin 2A + \sin 2B + \sin 2C)$

In any triangle $ABC$, the identity $\sin 2A + \sin 2B + \sin 2C = 4 \sin A \sin B \sin C$ holds true.

So, $a \cos A + b \cos B + c \cos C = 4R \sin A \sin B \sin C$

We also know that the area of the triangle is $\Delta = \dfrac{abc}{4R}$.

Substituting the sine rule expressions for $a, b, c$ into the area formula:

$\Delta = \dfrac{(2R \sin A)(2R \sin B)(2R \sin C)}{4R} = 2R^2 \sin A \sin B \sin C$

From this, we can write $4R \sin A \sin B \sin C = \dfrac{2\Delta}{R}$.

Therefore, the numerator is $a \cos A + b \cos B + c \cos C = \dfrac{2\Delta}{R}$.

Substituting this back into the original expression:

$\dfrac{1}{2\Delta} \times \dfrac{2\Delta}{R} = \dfrac{1}{R}$

Answer: 1/R

</details>
