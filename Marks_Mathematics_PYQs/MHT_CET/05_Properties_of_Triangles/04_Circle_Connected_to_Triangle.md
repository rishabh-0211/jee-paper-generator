### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $P$ and $P'$ denote respectively the circumcenter and the orthocenter of $\triangle ABC$, then $\overline{PA} + \overline{PB} + \overline{PC} =$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\overline{PP'}$
- **(B)** $\overline{P'P}$
- **(C)** $2\overline{P'P}$
- **(D)** $2\overline{PP'}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\overline{PP'}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the circumcenter $P$ be the origin of the position vectors.

The position vectors of the vertices $A, B, C$ are $\overline{PA}, \overline{PB}, \overline{PC}$ respectively.

The position vector of the centroid $G$ of $\triangle ABC$ is given by $\overline{PG} = \dfrac{\overline{PA} + \overline{PB} + \overline{PC}}{3}$.

In any triangle, the circumcenter $P$, centroid $G$, and orthocenter $P'$ are collinear, and the centroid $G$ divides the line segment joining the circumcenter $P$ and the orthocenter $P'$ in the ratio $1:2$.

Using the section formula, the position vector of $G$ is $\overline{PG} = \dfrac{1 \cdot \overline{PP'} + 2 \cdot \overline{PP}}{1 + 2}$.

Since $\overline{PP} = \vec{0}$, we get $\overline{PG} = \dfrac{\overline{PP'}}{3}$.

Equating the two expressions for $\overline{PG}$, we obtain $\dfrac{\overline{PA} + \overline{PB} + \overline{PC}}{3} = \dfrac{\overline{PP'}}{3}$.

$\overline{PA} + \overline{PB} + \overline{PC} = \overline{PP'}$

Answer: $\overline{PP'}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The circumradius of a triangle whose sides are 10 units, 8 units and 6 units is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 4 units
- **(B)** 2 units
- **(C)** 3 units
- **(D)** 5 units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) 5 units**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the sides of the triangle be $a = 10$, $b = 8$, and $c = 6$.

Checking the sum of the squares of the two smaller sides:
$b^2 + c^2 = 8^2 + 6^2 = 64 + 36 = 100$

Since $a^2 = 10^2 = 100$, we have $a^2 = b^2 + c^2$.

The triangle is a right-angled triangle with the hypotenuse of length $10$ units.

For a right-angled triangle, the circumcenter lies at the midpoint of the hypotenuse, so the circumradius $R$ is half the length of the hypotenuse.

$R = \dfrac{10}{2} = 5$ units.

Answer: 5 units

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the angles of a triangle are in the ratio $4: 1: 1$, then the ratio of the longest side to its perimeter is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{3}:(2+\sqrt{3})$
- **(B)** $2:(1+\sqrt{3})$
- **(C)** $1:(2+\sqrt{3})$
- **(D)** $2: 3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\sqrt{3}:(2+\sqrt{3})$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the angles of the triangle be $4 x, x$ and $x$.
$\begin{aligned}
\therefore \quad & 4 x+x+x=180^{\circ} \Rightarrow 6 x=180^{\circ} \Rightarrow x=30^{\circ} \\
& \frac{\sin 120^{\circ}}{\mathrm{a}}=\frac{\sin 30^{\circ}}{\mathrm{b}}=\frac{\sin 30^{\circ}}{\mathrm{c}} \\
\therefore \quad & \mathrm{a}:(\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{b}+\mathrm{c}) \\
& =\left(\sin 120^{\circ}\right):\left(\sin 120^{\circ}+\sin 30^{\circ}+\sin 30^{\circ}\right) \\
& =\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}: \frac{\sqrt{3}+2}{2}=\sqrt{3}:(\sqrt{3}+2)
\end{aligned}$

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (09 May Shift 2)

- **(A)** its altitudes are in A.P.
- **(B)** its altitudes are in H.P.
- **(C)** its medians are in G.P.
- **(D)** its medians are in A.P.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) its altitudes are in H.P.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{array}{ll}

$\therefore \quad \mathrm{a}, \mathrm{b}, \mathrm{c}$ are in A.P.
$\therefore \quad \frac{1}{a}, \frac{1}{b}, \frac{1}{\mathrm{c}}$ are in H.P. ... (iv)
From equations (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv), we get $\mathrm{p}_1, \mathrm{p}_2$ and $\mathrm{p}_3$ are in H.P.
