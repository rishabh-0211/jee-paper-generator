### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the line $4x - 3y + k = 0$ touches the ellipse $5x^2 + 9y^2 = 45$, then the value of $k$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $21$
- **(B)** $\pm 3\sqrt{21}$
- **(C)** $\pm 3$
- **(D)** $63$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\pm 3\sqrt{21}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the given line is $4x - 3y + k = 0$, which can be rewritten as $y = \dfrac{4}{3}x + \dfrac{k}{3}$.

The equation of the given ellipse is $5x^2 + 9y^2 = 45$, which can be rewritten as $\dfrac{x^2}{9} + \dfrac{y^2}{5} = 1$.

Comparing with the standard forms $y = mx + c$ and $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$, we get $m = \dfrac{4}{3}$, $c = \dfrac{k}{3}$, $a^2 = 9$, and $b^2 = 5$.

The condition for a line to touch an ellipse is $c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2$.

Substituting the values, we get:

$\left(\dfrac{k}{3}\right)^2 = 9\left(\dfrac{4}{3}\right)^2 + 5$

$\dfrac{k^2}{9} = 9\left(\dfrac{16}{9}\right) + 5$

$\dfrac{k^2}{9} = 16 + 5 = 21$

$k^2 = 189$

$k = \pm \sqrt{189} = \pm 3\sqrt{21}$

Answer: $\pm 3\sqrt{21}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A tangent having slope $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ to the ellipse $3x^2 + 4y^2 = 12$ intersects the X-axis and Y-axis at the points A and B respectively. if O is the origin, then the area of $\triangle AOB$ is ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$ sq. units
- **(B)** $8$ sq. units
- **(C)** $12$ sq. units
- **(D)** $16$ sq. units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $4$ sq. units**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the given ellipse can be rewritten as $\dfrac{x^2}{4} + \dfrac{y^2}{3} = 1$

Here, $a^2 = 4$ and $b^2 = 3$

The equation of a tangent to the ellipse with slope $m$ is given by $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 + b^2}$

Given the slope $m = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, substituting the values we get:

$y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x \pm \sqrt{4\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + 3}$

$y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x \pm \sqrt{1 + 3}$

$y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x \pm 2$

$x + 2y = \pm 4$

The tangent intersects the X-axis at $A(\pm 4, 0)$ and the Y-axis at $B(0, \pm 2)$

The area of $\triangle AOB$ is $\dfrac{1}{2} \times OA \times OB = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 2 = 4$ sq. units

Answer: $4$ sq. units

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the line $3x + 4y + k = 0$ touches the ellipse $9x^2 + 16y^2 = 144$, then the value of k is.

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\pm 3\sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** $\pm 4\sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** $\mp 8\sqrt{2}$
- **(D)** $\mp 12\sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\mp 12\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the ellipse is $9x^2 + 16y^2 = 144$.

Dividing by $144$, we get $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$.

Here, $a^2 = 16$ and $b^2 = 9$.

The equation of the line is $3x + 4y + k = 0$, which can be written as $y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x - \dfrac{k}{4}$.

Comparing with $y = mx + c$, we get $m = -\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $c = -\dfrac{k}{4}$.

The condition for the line $y = mx + c$ to touch the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ is $c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2$.

Substituting the values, we get:

$\left(-\dfrac{k}{4}\right)^2 = 16\left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 + 9$

$\dfrac{k^2}{16} = 16\left(\dfrac{9}{16}\right) + 9$

$\dfrac{k^2}{16} = 9 + 9 = 18$

$k^2 = 18 \times 16 = 288$

$k = \pm 12\sqrt{2}$

Answer: $\mp 12\sqrt{2}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equations of the tangents to the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$ making an inclination of $30^\circ$ with the major axis are

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x + \sqrt{3}\,y \pm \sqrt{43} = 0$
- **(B)** $x - \sqrt{3}\,y \pm \sqrt{43} = 0$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{3}\,x - y \pm \sqrt{43} = 0$
- **(D)** $x - \sqrt{3}\,y \pm \sqrt{3} = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $x - \sqrt{3}\,y \pm \sqrt{43} = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the ellipse is $\dfrac{x^2}{16} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$.

Here, $a^2 = 16$ and $b^2 = 9$.

The tangent makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the major axis (x-axis), so the slope of the tangent is $m = \tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$.

The equation of the tangent to the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ with slope $m$ is given by $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2 m^2 + b^2}$.

Substituting the values of $a^2$, $b^2$, and $m$, we get:

$y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}x \pm \sqrt{16\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^2 + 9}$

$y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}x \pm \sqrt{\dfrac{16}{3} + 9}$

$y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}x \pm \sqrt{\dfrac{16 + 27}{3}}$

$y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}x \pm \sqrt{\dfrac{43}{3}}$

Multiplying the entire equation by $\sqrt{3}$, we obtain:

$\sqrt{3}y = x \pm \sqrt{43}$

$x - \sqrt{3}y \pm \sqrt{43} = 0$

Answer: $x - \sqrt{3}\,y \pm \sqrt{43} = 0$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The tangent to the ellipse $9 x^2+16 y^2=288$ making equal intercepts on the co-ordinate axes intersects the X -axis and the Y -axis in the points $A$ and $B$ respectively. Then $A(\triangle O A B)=$ (where O is origin)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{25}{2}$ sq. units
- **(B)** 25 sq. units
- **(C)** $\frac{25 \sqrt{5}}{2}$ sq. units
- **(D)** $25 \sqrt{5}$ sq. units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 25 sq. units**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the ellipse is $9x^2 + 16y^2 = 288$.

Dividing by $288$, we get:

$\dfrac{x^2}{32} + \dfrac{y^2}{18} = 1$

Here, $a^2 = 32$ and $b^2 = 18$.

Let the equation of the tangent making equal intercepts on the coordinate axes be $\dfrac{x}{c} + \dfrac{y}{c} = 1$.

This can be written as $y = -x + c$, which has a slope $m = -1$.

The condition for the line $y = mx + c$ to be a tangent to the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ is $c^2 = a^2m^2 + b^2$.

Substituting the values, we get:

$c^2 = 32(-1)^2 + 18 = 50$

The tangent intersects the X-axis at $A(c, 0)$ and the Y-axis at $B(0, c)$.

The area of $\triangle OAB$ is given by:

$A(\triangle OAB) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times OA \times OB = \dfrac{1}{2} |c| |c| = \dfrac{1}{2} c^2$

$A(\triangle OAB) = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 50 = 25$ sq. units.

Answer: 25 sq. units

</details>
