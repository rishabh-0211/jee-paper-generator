### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The point on the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$ which is equidistant from its foci can be

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(0, 3)$
- **(B)** $(5, 0)$
- **(C)** $(3, 0)$
- **(D)** $(0, 5)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $(0, 3)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the given ellipse is $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$.

Here, $a^2 = 25$ and $b^2 = 9$.

The eccentricity $e = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{b^2}{a^2}} = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{9}{25}} = \dfrac{4}{5}$.

The coordinates of the foci are $(\pm ae, 0) = (\pm 4, 0)$.

The locus of a point equidistant from the foci is the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the foci. Since the foci lie on the x-axis symmetrically about the origin, the perpendicular bisector is the y-axis, whose equation is $x = 0$.

Substituting $x = 0$ in the equation of the ellipse, we get:

$\dfrac{0}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$

$y^2 = 9 \Rightarrow y = \pm 3$

Thus, the points on the ellipse equidistant from the foci are $(0, 3)$ and $(0, -3)$.

Answer: $(0, 3)$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

In an ellipse with semi-minor axis OB, S and S' are its foci, $a > b$ and $\angle SBS'$ is a right angle, then the eccentricity of the ellipse is..

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the equation of the ellipse be $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.

The coordinates of the foci are $S(ae, 0)$ and $S'(-ae, 0)$.

The coordinates of the end of the semi-minor axis are $B(0, b)$.

The slope of the line $BS$ is $m_1 = \dfrac{0 - b}{ae - 0} = -\dfrac{b}{ae}$.

The slope of the line $BS'$ is $m_2 = \dfrac{0 - b}{-ae - 0} = \dfrac{b}{ae}$.

Since $\angle SBS'$ is a right angle, the product of the slopes is $-1$.

$m_1 m_2 = -1$

$\left(-\dfrac{b}{ae}\right) \left(\dfrac{b}{ae}\right) = -1$

$\dfrac{b^2}{a^2 e^2} = 1 \Rightarrow b^2 = a^2 e^2$

For an ellipse, we know that $b^2 = a^2(1 - e^2)$.

Substituting this into the above equation, we get:

$a^2(1 - e^2) = a^2 e^2$

$1 - e^2 = e^2$

$2e^2 = 1 \Rightarrow e^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$

Since eccentricity $e > 0$, we have $e = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

</details>

Here, $a^2 = 25$ and $b^2 = 16$.

If $P$ be any point on the ellipse $16x^2 + 25y^2 = 400$ with foci $S$ and $S'$ and area of $\triangle PSS'$ is 9 square units, then the abscissa of point $P$ is...........

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{7\sqrt{5}}{4}$

- **(C)** $\dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{10}{7}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the ellipse is $16x^2 + 25y^2 = 400$, which can be written as $\dfrac{x^2}{25} + \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$.

Here, $a^2 = 25$ and $b^2 = 16$.

The eccentricity $e = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{b^2}{a^2}} = \sqrt{1 - \dfrac{16}{25}} = \dfrac{3}{5}$.

The foci $S$ and $S'$ are at $(\pm ae, 0) = (\pm 3, 0)$.

The distance between the foci is $SS' = 6$.

Let the coordinates of point $P$ be $(x, y)$. The area of $\triangle PSS'$ is given by $\dfrac{1}{2} \times SS' \times |y|$.

Given that the area is $9$ square units, we have:

$\dfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times |y| = 9 \Rightarrow 3|y| = 9 \Rightarrow |y| = 3$

Substituting $y^2 = 9$ into the equation of the ellipse:

$16x^2 + 25(9) = 400$

$16x^2 + 225 = 400$

$16x^2 = 175$

$x^2 = \dfrac{175}{16}$

$x = \pm \dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{4}$

The abscissa of point $P$ is $\pm \dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{4}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{5\sqrt{7}}{4}$

</details>

$3x + 0 - 6 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2$

$a \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right) = 2 \Rightarrow a = 3$

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (5 May Shift 2)

The eccentric angle of the point $\mathrm{P}(-6,2)$ of the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{48}+\frac{y^2}{16}=1$ is

**Options:**

- **(B)** $135^{\circ}$
- **(C)** $150^{\circ}$
- **(D)** $120^{\circ}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

### Q15. MHT CET 2025 (19 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

An ellipse has $O B$ as semi-minor axis, $S$ and $S^{\prime}$ are foci and angle $S B S^{\prime}$ is a right angle. Then the eccentricity of the ellipse is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the equation of the ellipse be $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.

The coordinates of the end of the semi-minor axis $B$ are $(0, b)$ and the foci are $S(ae, 0)$ and $S'(-ae, 0)$.

The slope of the line $BS$ is $m_1 = \dfrac{0 - b}{ae - 0} = -\dfrac{b}{ae}$.

The slope of the line $BS'$ is $m_2 = \dfrac{0 - b}{-ae - 0} = \dfrac{b}{ae}$.

Since the angle $SBS'$ is a right angle, the product of their slopes is $-1$.

$m_1 m_2 = -1$

$\left(-\dfrac{b}{ae}\right) \left(\dfrac{b}{ae}\right) = -1$

$\dfrac{b^2}{a^2 e^2} = 1$

$b^2 = a^2 e^2$

For an ellipse, we have the standard relation $b^2 = a^2(1 - e^2)$.

Substituting $b^2 = a^2 e^2$ into the relation, we get:

$a^2 e^2 = a^2(1 - e^2)$

$e^2 = 1 - e^2$

$2e^2 = 1$

$e^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$e = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

Answer: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

</details>
