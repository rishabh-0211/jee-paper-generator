### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The area (in sq.units) of a triangle formed by joining the vertex and the endpoints of the latus rectum of the parabola $x^2 = -20y$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $100$
- **(B)** $200$
- **(C)** $50$
- **(D)** $10$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $50$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the parabola is $x^2 = -20y$.

Comparing this with the standard form $x^2 = -4ay$, we get $4a = 20$, which gives $a = 5$.

The vertex of the parabola is $O(0, 0)$.

The endpoints of the latus rectum for $x^2 = -4ay$ are $(2a, -a)$ and $(-2a, -a)$.

Substituting $a = 5$, the endpoints of the latus rectum are $L(10, -5)$ and $L'(-10, -5)$.

The length of the base of the triangle formed by the vertex and the latus rectum is the length of the latus rectum, which is $4a = 20$.

The height of the triangle is the perpendicular distance from the vertex to the latus rectum, which is $a = 5$.

The area of the triangle is $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 20 \times 5 = 50$ sq. units.

Answer: $50$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of the directrix of the parabola $x^2 - 4x - 5y - 1 = 0$ is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4x + 9 = 0$
- **(B)** $4y + 9 = 0$
- **(C)** $4y - 9 = 0$
- **(D)** $4x - 9 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $4y + 9 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation of the parabola is $x^2 - 4x - 5y - 1 = 0$.

Completing the square for the $x$ terms:

$x^2 - 4x + 4 = 5y + 1 + 4$

$(x - 2)^2 = 5(y + 1)$

Comparing this with the standard form $(x - h)^2 = 4a(y - k)$, we get:

$h = 2$, $k = -1$, and $4a = 5 \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{5}{4}$

The equation of the directrix for this parabola is $y = k - a$.

Substituting the values of $k$ and $a$:

$y = -1 - \dfrac{5}{4}$

$y = -\dfrac{9}{4}$

$4y + 9 = 0$

Answer: $4y + 9 = 0$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

One of the tangents at the end of the latus rectum of the parabola $y^2 = 12x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x - y + 3 = 0$
- **(B)** $2x + y = 0$
- **(C)** $3x + y - 3 = 0$
- **(D)** $x + 3y - 3 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $x - y + 3 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the parabola is $y^2 = 12x$.

Comparing this with the standard form $y^2 = 4ax$, we get $4a = 12 \Rightarrow a = 3$.

The coordinates of the ends of the latus rectum for $y^2 = 4ax$ are $(a, 2a)$ and $(a, -2a)$.

Substituting $a = 3$, the ends of the latus rectum are $(3, 6)$ and $(3, -6)$.

The equation of the tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ at a point $(x_1, y_1)$ is given by $y y_1 = 2a(x + x_1)$.

For the point $(3, 6)$, the equation of the tangent is:

$y(6) = 2(3)(x + 3)$

$6y = 6(x + 3)$

$x - y + 3 = 0$

For the point $(3, -6)$, the equation of the tangent is:

$y(-6) = 2(3)(x + 3)$

$-6y = 6(x + 3)$

$x + y + 3 = 0$

Among the given options, $x - y + 3 = 0$ is the correct equation.

Answer: $x - y + 3 = 0$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A parabola has its focus on the positive X-axis and the Y-axis as its directrix. If $P(\alpha , 4)$ is a point on this parabola such that the tangent to the parabola at point P passes through the origin, then the distance of P from origin is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{20}$
- **(C)** $5$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{32}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{32}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the focus of the parabola be $F(a, 0)$ with $a > 0$.

Since the directrix is the Y-axis ($x = 0$), the distance of any point $(x, y)$ on the parabola from the focus is equal to its distance from the directrix.

$\sqrt{(x - a)^2 + y^2} = x$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$(x - a)^2 + y^2 = x^2$

$x^2 - 2ax + a^2 + y^2 = x^2$

$y^2 = 2ax - a^2$

The point $P(\alpha, 4)$ lies on the parabola, so:

$16 = 2a\alpha - a^2$

The equation of the tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 2ax - a^2$ at $P(\alpha, 4)$ is given by $T = 0$:

$4y = a(x + \alpha) - a^2$

Since the tangent passes through the origin $(0, 0)$, we substitute $x = 0$ and $y = 0$:

$0 = a(0 + \alpha) - a^2$

$a\alpha = a^2$

Since $a > 0$, we get $\alpha = a$.

Substituting $\alpha = a$ into the equation $16 = 2a\alpha - a^2$:

$16 = 2a(a) - a^2$

$16 = a^2$

Since $a > 0$, we have $a = 4$, which gives $\alpha = 4$.

The coordinates of point $P$ are $(4, 4)$.

The distance of $P$ from the origin is:

$OP = \sqrt{4^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{16 + 16} = \sqrt{32}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The distance of the focus of the parabola $y^2 = 16x$ from its directrix is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $8$
- **(C)** $12$
- **(D)** $16$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $8$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the parabola is $y^2 = 16x$.

Comparing this with the standard form $y^2 = 4ax$, we get $4a = 16$, which gives $a = 4$.

The coordinates of the focus are $(a, 0) = (4, 0)$.

The equation of the directrix is $x = -a$, which is $x = -4$.

The distance between the focus and the directrix is the perpendicular distance from the point $(4, 0)$ to the line $x + 4 = 0$.

Distance $= |4 - (-4)| = 8$.

Alternatively, the distance from the focus to the directrix for a parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ is always $2a$.

Distance $= 2 \times 4 = 8$.

Answer: $8$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A point on the parabola $y^2 = \dfrac{36}{5}x$ at which the ordinate increases at thrice the rate of the abscissa is .....

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\dfrac{6}{5}, \dfrac{1}{5}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(\dfrac{1}{5}, \dfrac{6}{5}\right)$
- **(C)** $(5, 6)$
- **(D)** $\left(\dfrac{3}{5}, \dfrac{9}{5}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\left(\dfrac{1}{5}, \dfrac{6}{5}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the equation of the parabola:

$y^2 = \dfrac{36}{5}x$

Differentiating both sides with respect to time $t$:

$2y \dfrac{dy}{dt} = \dfrac{36}{5} \dfrac{dx}{dt}$

It is given that the ordinate increases at thrice the rate of the abscissa, so:

$\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 3 \dfrac{dx}{dt}$

Substituting this into the differentiated equation:

$2y \left(3 \dfrac{dx}{dt}\right) = \dfrac{36}{5} \dfrac{dx}{dt}$

$6y = \dfrac{36}{5}$

$y = \dfrac{6}{5}$

Substituting $y = \dfrac{6}{5}$ into the equation of the parabola to find $x$:

$\left(\dfrac{6}{5}\right)^2 = \dfrac{36}{5}x$

$\dfrac{36}{25} = \dfrac{36}{5}x$

$x = \dfrac{1}{5}$

The required point is $\left(\dfrac{1}{5}, \dfrac{6}{5}\right)$.

</details>

### Q9. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The length of latus rectum of the parabola whose focus is $(3,3)$ and directrix is $3 x-4 y-2=0$ is ______ units.

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** 6
- **(D)** $\frac{1}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

The length of the latus rectum of a parabola is twice the perpendicular distance from the focus to the directrix.

Let $d$ be the perpendicular distance from the focus $(3,3)$ to the directrix $3x - 4y - 2 = 0$.

$d = \dfrac{|3(3) - 4(3) - 2|}{\sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2}}$

$d = \dfrac{|9 - 12 - 2|}{\sqrt{9 + 16}}$

$d = \dfrac{|-5|}{5} = 1$

The length of the latus rectum is $2d = 2 \times 1 = 2$ units.

Answer: 2

</details>
