### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of the common tangent to the parabolas $y^2 = 4x$ and $x^2 = 32y$ is ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x + 2y = 0$
- **(B)** $x + 2y - 4 = 0$
- **(C)** $x + 2y + 4 = 0$
- **(D)** $x - 2y + 4 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $x + 2y + 4 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of a tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ with slope $m$ is $y = mx + \dfrac{a}{m}$.
For $y^2 = 4x$, $a = 1$, so the tangent is $y = mx + \dfrac{1}{m}$.

The equation of a tangent to the parabola $x^2 = 4by$ with slope $m$ is $y = mx - bm^2$.
For $x^2 = 32y$, $b = 8$, so the tangent is $y = mx - 8m^2$.

For a common tangent, both equations must represent the same line, which means their y-intercepts must be equal:
$\dfrac{1}{m} = -8m^2$

$m^3 = -\dfrac{1}{8}$

$m = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

Substituting $m = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ into the equation of the tangent:
$y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x + \dfrac{1}{-1/2}$

$y = -\dfrac{1}{2}x - 2$

$2y = -x - 4$

$x + 2y + 4 = 0$

Answer: $x + 2y + 4 = 0$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Angle between the parabola $y^2=4(x-1)$ and $x^2+4(y-3)=0$ at the common end of their latus rectum is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\pi}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\pi}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\pi}{6}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\pi}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{\pi}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For the first parabola $y^2 = 4(x-1)$, the vertex is $(1, 0)$ and $a = 1$.

The focus is $(1+1, 0) = (2, 0)$.

Substituting $x = 2$ into the equation to find the endpoints of the latus rectum:

$y^2 = 4(2-1) = 4 \Rightarrow y = \pm 2$

The endpoints of its latus rectum are $(2, 2)$ and $(2, -2)$.

For the second parabola $x^2 = -4(y-3)$, the vertex is $(0, 3)$ and $a = 1$.

The focus is $(0, 3-1) = (0, 2)$.

Substituting $y = 2$ into the equation to find the endpoints of the latus rectum:

$x^2 = -4(2-3) = 4 \Rightarrow x = \pm 2$

The endpoints of its latus rectum are $(2, 2)$ and $(-2, 2)$.

The common end of their latus rectum is the point $(2, 2)$.

To find the angle between the parabolas at $(2, 2)$, we find the slopes of their tangents at this point.

Differentiating $y^2 = 4(x-1)$ with respect to $x$:

$2y \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4 \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{2}{y}$

At $(2, 2)$, the slope is $m_1 = \dfrac{2}{2} = 1$.

Differentiating $x^2 = -4(y-3)$ with respect to $x$:

$2x = -4 \dfrac{dy}{dx} \Rightarrow \dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{x}{2}$

At $(2, 2)$, the slope is $m_2 = -\dfrac{2}{2} = -1$.

Since $m_1 m_2 = (1)(-1) = -1$, the tangents are perpendicular to each other.

Therefore, the angle between the parabolas is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Answer: $\frac{\pi}{2}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The Y-intercept of the common tangent to the parabola $y^2=32 x$ and $x^2=108 y$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3$
- **(B)** $-12$
- **(C)** $-3$
- **(D)** $2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $-12$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of a tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ is given by $y = mx + \dfrac{a}{m}$.

For the parabola $y^2 = 32x$, we have $4a = 32 \Rightarrow a = 8$.
Thus, the tangent is $y = mx + \dfrac{8}{m}$.

The equation of a tangent to the parabola $x^2 = 4by$ is given by $y = mx - bm^2$.

For the parabola $x^2 = 108y$, we have $4b = 108 \Rightarrow b = 27$.
Thus, the tangent is $y = mx - 27m^2$.

For a common tangent, the Y-intercepts of both equations must be equal:

$\dfrac{8}{m} = -27m^2$

$m^3 = -\dfrac{8}{27}$

$m = -\dfrac{2}{3}$

The Y-intercept of the common tangent is $c = \dfrac{8}{m}$.

Substituting the value of $m$:

$c = \dfrac{8}{-\dfrac{2}{3}} = -12$

Answer: $-12$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of the common tangent touching the circle $(x-3)^{2}+y^{2}=9$ and the parabola $y^{2}=4 x$ above the $x$ -axis is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{2} y=3 x+1$
- **(B)** $\sqrt{3} y=-(x+3)$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{3} y=x+3$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{3} y=-(3 x+1)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\sqrt{3} y=x+3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the common tangent to the parabola
$$
\begin{array}{l}
y^{2}=4 x \text { be } \\
y=m x+\frac{1}{m}
\end{array}
$$
It should be also touch the circle
$$
(x-3)^{2}+y^{2}=9
$$
whose centre is $(3,0)$ and radius $=3$, then
$$
\begin{array}{cc} 
& \frac{|3 m+1 / m|}{\sqrt{1+m^{2}}}=3 \\
\Rightarrow & 3 m^{2}=1 \\
\Rightarrow & \quad m=\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
\end{array}
$$
But $m>0$, then equation of common tangent is
$$
y=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot x+\sqrt{3}
$$
or $\sqrt{3} \cdot y=x+3$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of common tangent to the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=2$ and the parabola $y^{2}=8 x$ is $x+y-k$. Then value of $k$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 1
- **(B)** $-1$
- **(C)** $\underline{2}$
- **(D)** $-2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $-2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given parabola is, $y^{2}=8 x$
Here,
$$
4 a=8 \Rightarrow a=2
$$
Any tangent to the parabola is $y=m x+\frac{a}{m}$
or
$$
m x-y+\frac{2}{m}=0
$$
If it is a tangent to the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=2$, then length of perpendicular from centre $(0,0)$ is equal to radius $\sqrt{2}$.
$$
\begin{array}{lr}
\therefore & \frac{2 / m}{\sqrt{m^{2}+1}}=\sqrt{2} \\
\Rightarrow & \frac{4}{m^{2}}=2\left(m^{2}+1\right) \\
\Rightarrow & m^{4}+m^{2}-2=0 \\
\Rightarrow & \left(m^{2}+2\right)\left(m^{2}-1\right)=0
\end{array}
$$
$\Rightarrow \quad m^{2}=\pm 1$
Hence, the common tangent are $y=\pm(x+2)$. But given equation of tangent is $x+y=k$ On comparing, we get $k=-2$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2008

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of common tangent to the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=2$ and parabola $y^{2}=8 x$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $y=x+1$
- **(B)** $y=x+2$
- **(C)** $y=x-2$
- **(D)** $y=-x+2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $y=x+2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given, $y^{2}=8 x$
$$
\therefore \quad 4 a=8 \Rightarrow a=2
$$
Any tangent of parabola is,
$$
\begin{aligned}
y &=m x+\frac{a}{m} \\
\Rightarrow \quad & m x-y+\frac{2}{m}=0
\end{aligned}
$$
If it is a tangent to the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=2$, then perpendicular from centre $(0,0)$ is equal to radius $\sqrt{2}$.
$$
\begin{array}{lr}
\therefore & \frac{\frac{2}{m}}{\sqrt{m^{2}+1}}=\sqrt{2} \\
\Rightarrow & \frac{4}{m^{2}}=2\left(m^{2}+1\right) \\
\Rightarrow & \quad m^{4}+m^{2}-2=0 \\
\Rightarrow & \left(m^{2}+2\right)\left(m^{2}-1\right)=0
\end{array}
$$
$\Rightarrow$
$m=\pm 1$
Hence, the common tangents are $y=\pm(x+2)$.

</details>
