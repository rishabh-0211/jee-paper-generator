### Q1. MHT CET 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $2 x+y+\lambda=0$ is normal to the parabola $y^{2}=8 x$, then $\lambda$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-24$
- **(B)** 8
- **(C)** $-16$
- **(D)** 24

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $-24$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given, parabola, $y^{2}=8 x$
$\begin{array}{lr}\Rightarrow & 2 y \frac{d y}{d x}=8 \\ \Rightarrow & \frac{d y}{d x}=\frac{4}{y}\end{array}$
Slope of normal of parabola $=-\frac{y}{4}$ $\ldots(\mathrm{i})$
Given, $2 x+y+\lambda=0$ is a equation of normal of the parabola $y^{2}=8 x$.
$\therefore$ Slope of normal $=-2$ $\ldots$ (ii)
From Eqs. (i) and (ii), we get
$\therefore$
$-\frac{y}{4}=-2 \Rightarrow y=8$
$(8)^{2}=8 x \Rightarrow x=8$
Now, putting the values of $x$ and $y$ in the equation of normal
$\begin{aligned} 2(8)+8+\lambda &=0 \\\Rightarrow \lambda &=-24 \end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2011

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equations of the normals at the ends of the latusrectum of the parabola $y^{2}=4 a x$ are given by

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x^{2}-y^{2}-6 a x+9 a^{2}=0$
- **(B)** $x^{2}-y^{2}-6 a x-6 a y+9 a^{2}=0$
- **(C)** $x^{2}-y^{2}-6 a y+9 a^{2}=0$
- **(D)** None of the above

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $x^{2}-y^{2}-6 a x+9 a^{2}=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The coordinates of the ends of the latusrectum of the parabola $y^{2}=4 a x$ are $(a, 2 a)$ respectively. The equation of the normal at $(a, 2 a)$ to $y^{2}=4 a x$ is
$$
y-2 a=\frac{-2 a}{2 a}(x-a)
$$
or $x+y-3 a=0$
Similarly, the equation of the normal $(a,-2 a)$ is
$$
x-y-3 a=0
$$
The combined equation is
$$
x^{2}-y^{2}-6 a x+9 a^{2}=0
$$

</details>
