### Q1. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{C}$ are the angles of a triangle with $\tan \dfrac{A}{2}=\dfrac{1}{3}, \tan \dfrac{B}{2}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ then the value of $\tan \dfrac{C}{2}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{-7}{9}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{7}{9}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{9}{7}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{-9}{7}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{7}{9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

In a triangle $A + B + C = \pi$, which gives $\dfrac{A}{2} + \dfrac{B}{2} + \dfrac{C}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

Using the identity for the half-angles of a triangle:

$\tan \dfrac{A}{2} \tan \dfrac{B}{2} + \tan \dfrac{B}{2} \tan \dfrac{C}{2} + \tan \dfrac{C}{2} \tan \dfrac{A}{2} = 1$

Substituting $\tan \dfrac{A}{2} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\tan \dfrac{B}{2} = \dfrac{2}{3}$:

$\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right) + \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)\tan \dfrac{C}{2} + \tan \dfrac{C}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right) = 1$

$\dfrac{2}{9} + \tan \dfrac{C}{2} \left(\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{3}\right) = 1$

$\dfrac{2}{9} + \tan \dfrac{C}{2} = 1$

$\tan \dfrac{C}{2} = 1 - \dfrac{2}{9} = \dfrac{7}{9}$

Answer: $\dfrac{7}{9}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The sides of a triangle are $\sin \theta, \cos \theta$ and $\sqrt{1+\sin \theta \cos \theta}$ for some $0 \lt \theta \lt \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, then the greatest angle of a triangle is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{2 \pi}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{5 \pi}{6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{2 \pi}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the sides of the triangle be $a = \sin \theta$, $b = \cos \theta$, and $c = \sqrt{1 + \sin \theta \cos \theta}$.

Since $0 < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, both $\sin \theta$ and $\cos \theta$ are positive.

We have $a^2 + b^2 = \sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta = 1$.

Also, $c^2 = 1 + \sin \theta \cos \theta$.

Since $\sin \theta \cos \theta > 0$, $c^2 > 1 = a^2 + b^2$. This implies $c > a$ and $c > b$, making $c$ the largest side of the triangle.

The greatest angle $C$ is opposite to the largest side $c$. Using the cosine rule:

$\cos C = \dfrac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$

Substituting the values of $a$, $b$, and $c$:

$\cos C = \dfrac{\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta - (1 + \sin \theta \cos \theta)}{2 \sin \theta \cos \theta}$

$\cos C = \dfrac{1 - 1 - \sin \theta \cos \theta}{2 \sin \theta \cos \theta}$

$\cos C = \dfrac{-\sin \theta \cos \theta}{2 \sin \theta \cos \theta} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

Since $C$ is an angle of a triangle, $0 < C < \pi$. Therefore, $C = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

Answer: $\dfrac{2 \pi}{3}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2022 (08 Aug Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

With usual notations in $\triangle A B C$ if $a^2+b^2-c^2=a b$, then measure of angle $C$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{\pi}{4}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\pi}{6}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\pi}{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\pi}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{\pi}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & a^2+b^2-c^2=a b \\ & \Rightarrow \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2 a b}=\frac{1}{2} \\ & \Rightarrow \cos c=\frac{1}{2} \\ & \Rightarrow c=\frac{\pi}{3}\end{aligned}$

</details>
