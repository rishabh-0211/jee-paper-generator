### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The lengths of the shadows of a tree of height p, thrown by sun's rays at three different moments are p, 2p and 3p. The sum of the angles of elevation of the sun's rays at these three moments is equal to...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the angles of elevation of the sun's rays at the three moments be $\theta_1$, $\theta_2$, and $\theta_3$.

From the given information, the height of the tree is $p$ and the lengths of the shadows are $p$, $2p$, and $3p$.

$\tan \theta_1 = \dfrac{p}{p} = 1 \Rightarrow \theta_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$

$\tan \theta_2 = \dfrac{p}{2p} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\tan \theta_3 = \dfrac{p}{3p} = \dfrac{1}{3}$

Using the tangent addition formula for $\theta_2$ and $\theta_3$:

$\tan(\theta_2 + \theta_3) = \dfrac{\tan \theta_2 + \tan \theta_3}{1 - \tan \theta_2 \tan \theta_3}$

Substituting the values:

$\tan(\theta_2 + \theta_3) = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3}}{1 - \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{3}} = \dfrac{\dfrac{5}{6}}{\dfrac{5}{6}} = 1$

Since $\theta_2$ and $\theta_3$ are acute angles, $\theta_2 + \theta_3 = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

Therefore, the sum of the angles of elevation is:

$\theta_1 + \theta_2 + \theta_3 = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2023 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A ladder, 5 meters long; rests against a vertical wall. If its top slides downwards at the rate of $10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}$, then the angle between the ladder and the floor is decreasing at the rate of radians/second when it's lower end is $\overline{4 \mathrm{~m} \text { away from the wall. }}$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-0.1$
- **(B)** $-0.025$
- **(C)** $0.1$
- **(D)** $0.025$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $0.025$**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/mht_cet/anbQ_IuBUWUDuDpRxTKPp_JiC34UZdrTbXHEqN56LpI.original.fullsize.png">

According to the figure, $x^2+y^2=25$
Note that $\cos \theta=\frac{\mathrm{OB}}{\mathrm{AB}}=\frac{x}{5}$
$\therefore \quad x=5 \cos \theta$
$\therefore \quad$ (i) $\Rightarrow 25 \cos ^2 \theta+y^2=25$
Differentiating w.r.t. ' $t$ ', we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \quad-50 \cos \theta \sin \theta \frac{\mathrm{d} \theta}{\mathrm{dt}}+2 y \frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{dt}}=0 \\
& 25 \sin \theta \cos \theta \frac{\mathrm{d} \theta}{\mathrm{dt}}=y \frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{dt}} \\
& \therefore \quad 25 \sin \theta \cos \theta \frac{\mathrm{d} \theta}{\mathrm{dt}}=y(-0.1) \\
& \quad \ldots\left[\because \frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{~d} x}=-10 \mathrm{~cm} / \mathrm{s}=-0.1 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}\right] \\
& \therefore \quad 25 \sin \theta \cos \theta \frac{\mathrm{d} \theta}{\mathrm{dt}}=-(0.1) y \\
& \quad \text { at } x=4, \cos \theta=\frac{4}{5}, \sin \theta=\frac{3}{5} \text { and } y=3 \\
& \therefore \quad \text { (ii) } \Rightarrow 25 \times \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{4}{5} \times \frac{\mathrm{d} \theta}{\mathrm{dt}}=-0.3 \\
& \Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{d} \theta}{\mathrm{dt}}=-0.025
\end{aligned}
$$
i.e., the angle is decreasing at the rate of $0.025 \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

$A$ rod $A B, 13$ feet long moves with its ends $A$ and $B$ on two perpendicular lines $O X$ and $O Y$ respectively. When $\mathrm{A}$ is 5 feet from $\mathrm{O}$, it is moving away at the rate of $3 \mathrm{feet} / \mathrm{sec}$. At this instant, B is moving at the rate

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{5}{4} \mathrm{ft} / \mathrm{sec}$ upwards.
- **(B)** $\frac{4}{5} \mathrm{ft} / \mathrm{sec}$ upwards.
- **(C)** $\frac{5}{4} \mathrm{ft} / \mathrm{sec}$ downwards.
- **(D)** $\frac{4}{5} \mathrm{ft} / \mathrm{sec}$ downwards.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{5}{4} \mathrm{ft} / \mathrm{sec}$ downwards.**

**Detailed Solution:**

<img src="https://cdn-question-pool.getmarks.app/pyq/mht_cet/r3TdmvhqJPmWx4iMcQAFPswBoUp-nW2Td9OeQcGoNSU.original.fullsize.png">

Note that $\triangle \mathrm{OAB}$ is a right angled triangle. Let $\mathrm{OA}=x \mathrm{ft}$ and $\mathrm{OB}=y \mathrm{ft}$.
$\therefore \quad y^2=169-x^2$
Now, differentiating above function w.r.t. time 't', we get
$2 y \frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{dt}}=-2 x \frac{\mathrm{d} x}{\mathrm{dt}}$
At $x=5, \frac{\mathrm{d} x}{\mathrm{dt}}=3 \mathrm{ft} / \mathrm{sec}$... [Given]
Also, at $x=5, y=12$
$\therefore \quad$ (i) $\Rightarrow 2(12) \frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{dt}}=-2(5)(3)$
$\Rightarrow \frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{~d} x}=\frac{-5}{4}$
Negative sign indicates that B is moving downwards.
$\therefore \quad$ B is moving at the rate $\frac{5}{4} \mathrm{ft} / \mathrm{sec}$ downwards.

</details>
