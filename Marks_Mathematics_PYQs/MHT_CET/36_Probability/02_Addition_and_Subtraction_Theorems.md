### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the probabilities of a student succeeding in the entrance tests for institutes A, B and C are $0.6$, $0.5$ and $0.4$ respectively, while the probability of succeeding in both A and B is $0.3$, in both B and C is $0.2$, in both A and C is $0.2$, and in all three is $0.1$, then the probability that the student succeeds in exactly one of these tests is......

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0.3$
- **(B)** $0.4$
- **(C)** $0.5$
- **(D)** $0.6$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $0.4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $A$, $B$, and $C$ be the events that the student succeeds in the entrance tests for institutes A, B, and C respectively.

Given:
$P(A) = 0.6$
$P(B) = 0.5$
$P(C) = 0.4$
$P(A \cap B) = 0.3$
$P(B \cap C) = 0.2$
$P(A \cap C) = 0.2$
$P(A \cap B \cap C) = 0.1$

The probability that the student succeeds in exactly one of the tests is given by the formula:
$P(\text{exactly one}) = P(A) + P(B) + P(C) - 2[P(A \cap B) + P(B \cap C) + P(A \cap C)] + 3P(A \cap B \cap C)$

Substituting the given values into the formula:
$P(\text{exactly one}) = (0.6 + 0.5 + 0.4) - 2(0.3 + 0.2 + 0.2) + 3(0.1)$

$P(\text{exactly one}) = 1.5 - 2(0.7) + 0.3$

$P(\text{exactly one}) = 1.5 - 1.4 + 0.3$

$P(\text{exactly one}) = 0.4$

Answer: $0.4$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If A, B, C are mutually exclusive and exhaustive events of a sample space $S$ such that $P(B)=\frac{3}{2} P(A)$ and $P(C)=\frac{1}{2} P(B)$, then $P(A)=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{4}{13}$
- **(B)** $\frac{6}{13}$
- **(C)** $\frac{8}{13}$
- **(D)** $\frac{3}{13}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{4}{13}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since A, B, C are mutually exclusive and exhaustive events, $P(A) + P(B) + P(C) = 1$.

Given $P(B) = \dfrac{3}{2} P(A)$ and $P(C) = \dfrac{1}{2} P(B)$.

Substituting $P(B)$ in $P(C)$, we get $P(C) = \dfrac{1}{2} \left( \dfrac{3}{2} P(A) \right) = \dfrac{3}{4} P(A)$.

Substituting these values in the first equation:

$P(A) + \dfrac{3}{2} P(A) + \dfrac{3}{4} P(A) = 1$

$P(A) \left( 1 + \dfrac{3}{2} + \dfrac{3}{4} \right) = 1$

$P(A) \left( \dfrac{4 + 6 + 3}{4} \right) = 1$

$P(A) \left( \dfrac{13}{4} \right) = 1$

$P(A) = \dfrac{4}{13}$

Answer: $\frac{4}{13}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (10 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

There are three events $\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{C}$, one of which must and only one can happen. The odds are 8:3 against $\mathrm{A}, 5: 2$ against B and the odds against C is $43: 17 \mathrm{k}$, then value of $k$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** 2
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{3}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) 2**

**Detailed Solution:**

Since events $A, B, C$ are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, $P(A) + P(B) + P(C) = 1$.

Given odds against $A$ are $8:3$, $P(A) = \dfrac{3}{8+3} = \dfrac{3}{11}$.

Given odds against $B$ are $5:2$, $P(B) = \dfrac{2}{5+2} = \dfrac{2}{7}$.

Substituting the probabilities into the equation:

$\dfrac{3}{11} + \dfrac{2}{7} + P(C) = 1$

$P(C) = 1 - \left(\dfrac{3}{11} + \dfrac{2}{7}\right) = 1 - \dfrac{21+22}{77} = \dfrac{34}{77}$

The odds against $C$ are $P(C') : P(C) = \left(1 - \dfrac{34}{77}\right) : \dfrac{34}{77} = \dfrac{43}{77} : \dfrac{34}{77} = 43 : 34$.

Comparing this with the given odds $43 : 17k$, we get:

$17k = 34$

$k = 2$

Answer: 2

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{B}$ are two events such that $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})=\frac{1}{3}$, $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B})=\frac{1}{5}, \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B})=\frac{1}{3}$, then the value of $\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{A}^{\prime} / \mathrm{B}^{\prime}\right)+\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{B}^{\prime} / \mathrm{A}^{\prime}\right)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{5}{6}$
- **(B)** $1$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{6}$
- **(D)** $\frac{11}{6}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{11}{6}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} \mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{A}^{\prime} / \mathrm{B}^{\prime}\right) & =\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{A}^{\prime} \cap \mathrm{B}^{\prime}\right)}{\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{B}^{\prime}\right)} \\ & =\frac{1-\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B})}{1-\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B})} \\ & =\frac{1-\frac{1}{3}}{1-\frac{1}{5}}=\frac{\frac{2}{3}}{\frac{3}{5}}=\frac{5}{6} \\ \mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{B}^{\prime} / \mathrm{A}^{\prime}\right) & =\frac{\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{A}^{\prime} \cap \mathrm{B}^{\prime}\right)}{\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{A}^{\prime}\right)} \\ & =\frac{1-\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cup \mathrm{B})}{1-\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})} \\ & =\frac{\frac{2}{3}}{1-\frac{1}{3}}=\frac{\frac{2}{3}}{\frac{2}{3}}=1 \\ \mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{A}^{\prime} / \mathrm{B}^{\prime}\right) & +\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{B}^{\prime} / \mathrm{A}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{5}{6}+1=\frac{11}{6}\end{aligned}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2020 (14 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Two dice are thrown together. The probability that sum of the numbers is divisible
by 2 or 3 is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{6}$
- **(B)** $\frac{3}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{2}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Two dice are thrown together. Then sum of $2,3,4,6,8,9,10,12$ is obtained in following ways.
Let $A=\{(2,2),(1,2),(1,1),(1,3),(1,5),(2,1),(2,4),(2,6),(3,1),(3,3),(3,5),(3,6)$,
$(4,5),(4,2),(4,4),(4,6),(5,1),(5,3),(5,4),(5,5),(6,2),(6,3),(6,4),(6,6)\}$
Thus $n(A)=24$ and $n(S)=6 \times 6=36$
$\therefore \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})=\frac{\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{A})}{\mathrm{n}(\mathrm{S})}=\frac{24}{36}=\frac{2}{3}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2020 (14 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})=\frac{2}{5}, \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B})=\frac{1}{4}$ and $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{AUB})=\frac{1}{2}$, then $\mathrm{P}\left(\mathrm{A}^{\prime} \cup \mathrm{B}^{\prime}\right)=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{3}{20}$
- **(D)** $\frac{17}{20}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\frac{17}{20}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$P(A \cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A \cap B)$
$P(A \cap B)=\frac{3}{20}$
$P(A \cap B)+P(\overline{A \cap B})=1$
$P(\overline{A \cap B})=\frac{17}{20}$
$P\left(A^{\prime} \cup B^{\prime}\right)=\frac{17}{20}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $A$ and $B$ are independent events such that odds in favour of $A$ is $2: 3$ and odds
against $B$ is $4: 5$, then $P(A \cap B)=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{9}$
- **(B)** $\frac{4}{5}$
- **(C)** $\frac{2}{9}$
- **(D)** $\frac{3}{9}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{2}{9}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $P(A)=\frac{2}{5} \Rightarrow P\left(A^{\prime}\right)=\frac{3}{5}$ and $P\left(B^{\prime}\right)=\frac{4}{9} \Rightarrow P(B)=\frac{5}{9}$ 
Since $A$ and $B$ are independent events, 
$\therefore \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B})=\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A}) \cdot \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B})=\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{5}{9}=\frac{2}{9}$

</details>
