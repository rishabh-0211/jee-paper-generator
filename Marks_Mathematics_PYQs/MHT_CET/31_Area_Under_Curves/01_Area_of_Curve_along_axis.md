### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The area between the parabola $y = 2x - x^2$ and the X-axis is .....

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2}{3}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{4}{3}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{16}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{4}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

To find the area between the parabola $y = 2x - x^2$ and the X-axis, we first determine the points of intersection with the X-axis by setting $y = 0$.

$2x - x^2 = 0$

$x(2 - x) = 0$

$x = 0, x = 2$

The required area is given by the definite integral of the curve from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$.

$\text{Area} = \int_{0}^{2} (2x - x^2) dx$

$\text{Area} = \left[ x^2 - \dfrac{x^3}{3} \right]_{0}^{2}$

$\text{Area} = \left( 2^2 - \dfrac{2^3}{3} \right) - (0 - 0)$

$\text{Area} = 4 - \dfrac{8}{3} = \dfrac{4}{3}$

Answer: $\dfrac{4}{3}$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The parabolas $y^2=4x$ and $x^2=4y$ divide the square region bounded by the lines $x=4, y=4$ and the coordinate axes. If $A1, A2, A3$ are respectively the areas of these parts numbered from top to bottom, then $A1:A2:A3$ is ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1 : 2 : 3$
- **(B)** $3 : 2 : 1$
- **(C)** $1 : 1 : 1$
- **(D)** $1 : 1 : 2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1 : 1 : 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The square region is bounded by the lines $x=0$, $x=4$, $y=0$, and $y=4$. The total area of the square is $4 \times 4 = 16$.

The parabolas $y^2=4x$ and $x^2=4y$ intersect at $(0,0)$ and $(4,4)$.

The area $A_3$ is the region below the parabola $x^2=4y$ (or $y=\dfrac{x^2}{4}$):

$A_3 = \int_{0}^{4} \dfrac{x^2}{4} dx = \left[ \dfrac{x^3}{12} \right]_{0}^{4} = \dfrac{64}{12} = \dfrac{16}{3}$

The area $A_2$ is the region between the two parabolas $y=2\sqrt{x}$ and $y=\dfrac{x^2}{4}$:

$A_2 = \int_{0}^{4} \left( 2\sqrt{x} - \dfrac{x^2}{4} \right) dx = \left[ \dfrac{4}{3}x^{3/2} - \dfrac{x^3}{12} \right]_{0}^{4} = \dfrac{32}{3} - \dfrac{16}{3} = \dfrac{16}{3}$

The area $A_1$ is the region above the parabola $y^2=4x$ (or $y=2\sqrt{x}$), which can be found by subtracting $A_2$ and $A_3$ from the total area of the square:

$A_1 = 16 - A_2 - A_3 = 16 - \dfrac{16}{3} - \dfrac{16}{3} = \dfrac{16}{3}$

Therefore, the ratio of the areas is:

$A_1 : A_2 : A_3 = \dfrac{16}{3} : \dfrac{16}{3} : \dfrac{16}{3} = 1 : 1 : 1$

Answer: $1 : 1 : 1$

</details>

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $4\pi$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The region bounded by the curve, $x = 0$, $x = 4$, and above the X-axis lies entirely in the first quadrant.

The required area is given by the integral:

$A = \int_{0}^{4} \sqrt{16 - x^2} dx$

Using the standard integral formula $\int \sqrt{a^2 - x^2} dx = \dfrac{x}{2}\sqrt{a^2 - x^2} + \dfrac{a^2}{2}\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{a}\right)$, we get:

$A = \left[ \dfrac{x}{2}\sqrt{16 - x^2} + 8\sin^{-1}\left(\dfrac{x}{4}\right) \right]_{0}^{4}$

$A = \left( 0 + 8\sin^{-1}(1) \right) - \left( 0 + 8\sin^{-1}(0) \right)$

$A = 8 \times \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 4\pi$ sq. units.

Answer: $4\pi$

$A = \int_{-2}^{0} -(x^3 + ax) dx + \int_{0}^{1} (x^3 + ax) dx$

$\dfrac{17}{4} + \dfrac{5a}{2} = \dfrac{37}{4}$

The area of the region (in sq.units) bounded by the curve $y = 2\sqrt{1 - x^2}$ and the X-axis is _____

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\pi^2}{2}$
- **(C)** $\pi$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\pi$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given curve is $y = 2\sqrt{1 - x^2}$.

Squaring both sides, we get $y^2 = 4(1 - x^2) \Rightarrow x^2 + \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$.

This represents an ellipse with semi-minor axis $a = 1$ and semi-major axis $b = 2$.

Since $y \ge 0$, the given curve represents the upper half of the ellipse.

The area of the complete ellipse is $\pi a b = \pi(1)(2) = 2\pi$.

Therefore, the area of the upper half of the ellipse is $\dfrac{1}{2}(2\pi) = \pi$.

Alternatively, the area can be found by integration:

Area $= \int_{-1}^{1} 2\sqrt{1 - x^2} dx$

Area $= 2 \left[ \dfrac{x}{2}\sqrt{1 - x^2} + \dfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}x \right]_{-1}^{1}$

Area $= 2 \left[ \dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) - \dfrac{1}{2}\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) \right] = \pi$ sq. units.

Answer: $\pi$

### Q19. MHT CET 2026 (11 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The area bounded by the curve $y = x\left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right|$ and the X-axis between the lines $x = 0$ and $x = 4\pi$ (in square units), is....

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4\pi$
- **(B)** $8\pi$
- **(C)** $12\pi$
- **(D)** $16\pi$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $16\pi$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The required area is given by the integral:

$A = \int_{0}^{4\pi} x\left|\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right| dx$

Since $\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) \ge 0$ for $x \in [0, 2\pi]$ and $\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) \le 0$ for $x \in [2\pi, 4\pi]$, the integral can be split as:

$A = \int_{0}^{2\pi} x\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) dx - \int_{2\pi}^{4\pi} x\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) dx$

Using integration by parts:

$\int x\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) dx = x\left(-2\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right) - \int 1 \cdot \left(-2\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right) dx$

$= -2x\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + 4\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)$

Evaluating the first integral:

$\int_{0}^{2\pi} x\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) dx = \left[-2x\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + 4\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right]_{0}^{2\pi} = (-4\pi(-1) + 0) - (0 + 0) = 4\pi$

Evaluating the second integral:

$\int_{2\pi}^{4\pi} x\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) dx = \left[-2x\cos\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + 4\sin\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)\right]_{2\pi}^{4\pi} = (-8\pi(1) + 0) - (-4\pi(-1) + 0) = -8\pi - 4\pi = -12\pi$

Substituting these values back into the area expression:

$A = 4\pi - (-12\pi) = 16\pi$

Answer: $16\pi$

</details>
