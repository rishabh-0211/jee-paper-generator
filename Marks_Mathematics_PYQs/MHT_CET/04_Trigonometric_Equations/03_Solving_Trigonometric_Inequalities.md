### Q1. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The number of values of $x$ in the interval $(0,5 \pi)$ satisfying the equation $3 \sin ^2 x-7 \sin x+2=0$

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0
- **(B)** 5
- **(C)** 6
- **(D)** 10

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) 6**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation: $3 \sin^2 x - 7 \sin x + 2 = 0$

Factorizing the quadratic equation in terms of $\sin x$:

$3 \sin^2 x - 6 \sin x - \sin x + 2 = 0$

$3 \sin x (\sin x - 2) - 1 (\sin x - 2) = 0$

$(3 \sin x - 1)(\sin x - 2) = 0$

$\sin x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ or $\sin x = 2$

Since the range of $\sin x$ is $[-1, 1]$, $\sin x = 2$ is not possible. Thus, we only consider $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

We need to find the number of solutions in the interval $(0, 5\pi)$.

In the interval $(0, \pi)$, $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ has $2$ solutions (one in the first quadrant and one in the second quadrant).

In the interval $(\pi, 2\pi)$, $\sin x$ is negative, so there are $0$ solutions.

In the interval $(2\pi, 3\pi)$, $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ has $2$ solutions.

In the interval $(3\pi, 4\pi)$, $\sin x$ is negative, so there are $0$ solutions.

In the interval $(4\pi, 5\pi)$, $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{3}$ has $2$ solutions.

Total number of solutions in $(0, 5\pi)$ is $2 + 0 + 2 + 0 + 2 = 6$.

Answer: 6

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

Let $P=\{\theta / \sin \theta-\cos \theta=\sqrt{2} \cos \theta\}$ and $Q=\{\theta / \sin \theta+\cos \theta=\sqrt{2} \sin \theta\}$ be two sets, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{P} \subset \mathrm{Q}$ and $\mathrm{Q}-\mathrm{P} \neq \phi$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{Q} \not \subset \mathrm{P}$
- **(C)** $\mathrm{P} \not \subset \mathrm{Q}$
- **(D)** $\quad \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{Q}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\quad \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{Q}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For set $P$:

$\sin \theta - \cos \theta = \sqrt{2} \cos \theta$

$\sin \theta = (\sqrt{2} + 1) \cos \theta$

$\tan \theta = \sqrt{2} + 1$

For set $Q$:

$\sin \theta + \cos \theta = \sqrt{2} \sin \theta$

$\cos \theta = (\sqrt{2} - 1) \sin \theta$

$\tan \theta = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2} - 1}$

Rationalizing the denominator:

$\tan \theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{2} + 1}{(\sqrt{2} - 1)(\sqrt{2} + 1)} = \sqrt{2} + 1$

Since the conditions for both sets simplify to $\tan \theta = \sqrt{2} + 1$, the elements of both sets are identical.

Therefore, $P = Q$.

Answer: $\quad \mathrm{P}=\mathrm{Q}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

The solution set of the equation $\tan x+\sec x=2 \cos x$, in the interval $[0,2 \pi]$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left\{\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{7 \pi}{6}\right\}$
- **(B)** $\left\{\frac{5 \pi}{6}, \frac{7 \pi}{6}\right\}$
- **(C)** $\left\{\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5 \pi}{6}\right\}$
- **(D)** $\left\{\frac{5 \pi}{6}, \frac{11 \pi}{6}\right\}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\left\{\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5 \pi}{6}\right\}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation is $\tan x + \sec x = 2 \cos x$.

Rewriting in terms of sine and cosine:

$\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x} + \dfrac{1}{\cos x} = 2 \cos x$

$\dfrac{\sin x + 1}{\cos x} = 2 \cos x$

Assuming $\cos x \neq 0$, multiplying both sides by $\cos x$ gives:

$\sin x + 1 = 2 \cos^2 x$

Using the identity $\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$:

$\sin x + 1 = 2(1 - \sin^2 x)$

$\sin x + 1 = 2 - 2 \sin^2 x$

$2 \sin^2 x + \sin x - 1 = 0$

Factorizing the quadratic equation:

$(2 \sin x - 1)(\sin x + 1) = 0$

This gives $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ or $\sin x = -1$.

If $\sin x = -1$, then $\cos x = 0$, which makes $\tan x$ and $\sec x$ undefined. Thus, $\sin x = -1$ is rejected.

For $\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}$ in the interval $[0, 2\pi]$:

$x = \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

The solution set is $\left\{\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right\}$.

Answer: $\left\{\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5 \pi}{6}\right\}$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Let $2 \sin ^2 x+3 \sin x-2\gt0$ and $x^2-x-2 \lt 0$ ( $x$ is measured in radians).
Then $x$ lies in the interval

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5 \pi}{6}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(-1, \dfrac{5 \pi}{6}\right)$
- **(C)** $(-1,2)$
- **(D)** $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, 2\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, 2\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the inequality $x^2 - x - 2 < 0$

$(x - 2)(x + 1) < 0$

$x \in (-1, 2)$

Now, considering the trigonometric inequality $2 \sin^2 x + 3 \sin x - 2 > 0$

$2 \sin^2 x + 4 \sin x - \sin x - 2 > 0$

$2 \sin x (\sin x + 2) - 1 (\sin x + 2) > 0$

$(2 \sin x - 1)(\sin x + 2) > 0$

Since $-1 \le \sin x \le 1$, we have $\sin x + 2 > 0$ for all real $x$.

Therefore, $2 \sin x - 1 > 0$

$\sin x > \dfrac{1}{2}$

The general solution for this inequality is $x \in \left(2n\pi + \dfrac{\pi}{6}, 2n\pi + \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$ for any integer $n$.

For $n = 0$, the interval is $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$.

We need to find the intersection of $x \in (-1, 2)$ and $x \in \left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$.

Since $\dfrac{\pi}{6} \approx 0.52$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} \approx 2.62$, the value $2$ lies between $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

Taking the intersection of $(-1, 2)$ and $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$, we get $x \in \left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, 2\right)$.

Answer: $\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}, 2\right)$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The value of $\cos \left(2 \cos ^{-1} x+\sin ^{-1} x\right)$ at $x=\dfrac{1}{5}$, where $0 \leq \cos ^{-1} x \leq \pi$ and $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq \sin ^{-1} x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{5}$
- **(B)** $-\dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{5}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2 \sqrt{6}}{5}$
- **(D)** $-\dfrac{2 \sqrt{6}}{5}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $-\dfrac{2 \sqrt{6}}{5}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given expression is $\cos \left(2 \cos ^{-1} x+\sin ^{-1} x\right)$

Using the inverse trigonometric identity $\cos^{-1} x + \sin^{-1} x = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, the expression can be rewritten as:

$\cos \left(\cos ^{-1} x + \left(\cos ^{-1} x + \sin ^{-1} x\right)\right)$

$= \cos \left(\cos ^{-1} x + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$

Using $\cos \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} + \theta\right) = -\sin \theta$, we get:

$= -\sin \left(\cos ^{-1} x\right)$

Since $\sin(\cos^{-1} x) = \sqrt{1 - x^2}$ for $x \in [-1, 1]$, the expression becomes:

$= -\sqrt{1 - x^2}$

Substituting $x = \dfrac{1}{5}$:

$= -\sqrt{1 - \left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^2}$

$= -\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{1}{25}}$

$= -\sqrt{\dfrac{24}{25}}$

$= -\dfrac{2\sqrt{6}}{5}$

Answer: $-\dfrac{2 \sqrt{6}}{5}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2023 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\mathrm{a}$ and $\mathrm{b}$ are positive number such that $\mathrm{a}>\mathrm{b}$, then the minimum value of $a \sec \theta-b \tan \theta\left(0 < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}$
- **(B)** $\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}$
- **(C)** $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{a^2-b^2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{a^2-b^2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & \text { let } \mathrm{f}(\theta)=\mathrm{a} \sec \theta-b \tan \theta \\ & \therefore \quad \mathrm{f}^{\prime}(\theta)=\mathrm{a} \sec \theta \tan \theta-b \sec ^2 \theta \\ & \quad=\sec \theta(\mathrm{a} \tan \theta-\mathrm{b} \sec \theta) \\ & \therefore \quad \mathrm{f}^{\prime}(\theta)=0 \Rightarrow \sec \theta(\mathrm{a} \tan \theta-\mathrm{b} \sec \theta)=0 \\ & \Rightarrow \mathrm{a} \tan \theta-\mathrm{b} \sec \theta=0 \quad \ldots\left[\text { As } 0 < \pi < \frac{\pi}{2}, \sec \theta \neq 0\right] \\ & \\ & \Rightarrow \mathrm{a} \sin \theta-b=0 \quad \ldots\left[\text { As } 0 < \pi < \frac{\pi}{2}, \cos \theta \neq 0\right]\end{aligned}$
$\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow \sin \theta=\frac{b}{a} \\
& \Rightarrow \sec \theta=\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}} \text { and } \tan \theta=\frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}
\end{aligned}$
Now,
$\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{f}^{\prime \prime}(\theta)=\sec \theta \tan \theta(\mathrm{a} \tan \theta-\mathrm{b} \sec \theta) \\
& +\sec \theta\left(a \sec ^2 \theta-b \sec \theta \tan \theta\right) \\
& =\mathrm{a} \tan ^2 \theta \sec \theta-\mathrm{b} \sec ^2 \theta \tan \theta \\
& +a \sec ^3 \theta-b \sec ^2 \theta \tan \theta \\
& =a \sec \theta\left(\tan ^2 \theta+\sec ^2 \theta\right) \\
& =a \sec \theta\left(1+2 \tan ^2 \theta\right) \\
& >0 \quad \ldots\left[\because \text { a is positive and } 0 < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2}\right] \\
&
\end{aligned}$
$\therefore \\f(\theta)$ is minimum when $\sin \theta=\frac{b}{a}$.
$\begin{aligned}\therefore \
& \text { Minimum value of } f(\theta) \\
& =a\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\right)-b\left(\frac{b}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\right) \quad \ldots[\text { From (i) }] \\
& =\frac{a^2-b^2}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}} \\
& =\sqrt{a^2-b^2}
\end{aligned}$

</details>
