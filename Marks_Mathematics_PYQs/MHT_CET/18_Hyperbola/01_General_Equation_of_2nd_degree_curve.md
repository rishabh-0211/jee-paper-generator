### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation of the hyperbola formed by the locus of the point of intersection of the lines $\sqrt{5}x - y - 2\sqrt{5}\mu = 0$, and $\sqrt{5}\mu x + \mu y - 2\sqrt{5} = 0$, where $\mu$ is a variable parameter, is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{20} = 1$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{x^2}{16} - \dfrac{y^2}{20} = 1$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{x^2}{20} - \dfrac{y^2}{4} = 1$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{x^2}{20} - \dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{20} = 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equations of the lines are:

$\sqrt{5}x - y = 2\sqrt{5}\mu$ ... (1)

$\sqrt{5}\mu x + \mu y = 2\sqrt{5}$

$\Rightarrow \mu(\sqrt{5}x + y) = 2\sqrt{5}$ ... (2)

Multiplying equation (1) and equation (2), we get:

$(\sqrt{5}x - y) \cdot \mu(\sqrt{5}x + y) = 2\sqrt{5}\mu \cdot 2\sqrt{5}$

$\mu(5x^2 - y^2) = 20\mu$

For $\mu \neq 0$, dividing both sides by $\mu$ gives:

$5x^2 - y^2 = 20$

Dividing by $20$, we get the equation of the hyperbola:

$\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{20} = 1$

Answer: $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{20} = 1$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2020 (15 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $a x^{2}+2 h x y+b y^{2}+2 g x+2 f y+c=0$ represents a joint equation of directrices
of the hyperbola $16 x^{2}-9 y^{2}=144$, then $g+f-c=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-81$
- **(B)** $-25$
- **(C)** $81$
- **(D)** $25$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $81$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Equation of the hyperbola: $16 x^{2}-9 y^{2}=-144$
This can be rewritten in the following way:
$$
\frac{x^{2}}{9}-\frac{y^{2}}{16}=-1
$$
This is the standard equation of a hyperbola, where
$$
\begin{array}{l}
a^{2}=9 \text { and } b^{2}=16 \\
\Rightarrow a^{2}=b^{2}\left(e^{2}-1\right) \\
\Rightarrow 9=16\left(e^{2}-1\right) \\
\Rightarrow e^{2}-1=\frac{9}{16} \\
\Rightarrow e^{2}=\frac{25}{16} \\
\Rightarrow e=\frac{5}{4}
\end{array}
$$
Coordinates of foci are given by $(0, \pm a e)$, i.e.
$(0, \pm 5)$.
Equation of the directrices: $y=\pm \frac{a}{e}$
$\Rightarrow y=\pm \frac{4}{\frac{5}{4}}$ $\Rightarrow 5 y \pm 16=0$
Length of the latus rectum of the hyperbola $=\frac{2 a^{2}}{b}$
Length of the latus rectum $=\frac{2 \times 9}{4}=\frac{9}{2}$.

</details>
