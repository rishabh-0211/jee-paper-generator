### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The equation $16x^2 - 3y^2 - 32x + 12y - 44 = 0$ represents a hyperbola whose...

**Options:**

- **(A)** centre is $(-1, 2)$
- **(B)** eccentricity is $\sqrt{\dfrac{19}{3}}$
- **(C)** length of its transverse axis is $4\sqrt{3}$
- **(D)** length of its conjugate axis is $4$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) eccentricity is $\sqrt{\dfrac{19}{3}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given equation of the hyperbola is $16x^2 - 3y^2 - 32x + 12y - 44 = 0$

$16(x^2 - 2x) - 3(y^2 - 4y) = 44$

$16(x^2 - 2x + 1) - 3(y^2 - 4y + 4) = 44 + 16 - 12$

$16(x - 1)^2 - 3(y - 2)^2 = 48$

$\dfrac{(x - 1)^2}{3} - \dfrac{(y - 2)^2}{16} = 1$

Comparing with the standard equation $\dfrac{(x - h)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(y - k)^2}{b^2} = 1$, we get:

Centre $(h, k) = (1, 2)$

$a^2 = 3 \Rightarrow a = \sqrt{3}$

$b^2 = 16 \Rightarrow b = 4$

Length of transverse axis $= 2a = 2\sqrt{3}$

Length of conjugate axis $= 2b = 8$

Eccentricity $e = \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{b^2}{a^2}} = \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{16}{3}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{19}{3}}$

Answer: eccentricity is $\sqrt{\dfrac{19}{3}}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (25 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The eccentricity of the standard hyperbola which passes through the points $(3,0)$ and $(3 \sqrt{2}, 2)$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\sqrt{13}$
- **(B)** $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$
- **(D)** $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the equation of the standard hyperbola be $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.

Since it passes through the point $(3, 0)$, we have:

$\dfrac{3^2}{a^2} - 0 = 1 \Rightarrow a^2 = 9$

The hyperbola also passes through the point $(3 \sqrt{2}, 2)$. Substituting these coordinates, we get:

$\dfrac{(3 \sqrt{2})^2}{9} - \dfrac{2^2}{b^2} = 1$

$\dfrac{18}{9} - \dfrac{4}{b^2} = 1$

$2 - \dfrac{4}{b^2} = 1 \Rightarrow \dfrac{4}{b^2} = 1 \Rightarrow b^2 = 4$

The eccentricity $e$ of the hyperbola is given by:

$e = \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{b^2}{a^2}}$

$e = \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{4}{9}}$

$e = \sqrt{\dfrac{13}{9}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$

Answer: $\frac{\sqrt{13}}{3}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The area of the region bounded by hyperbola $x^2-y^2=9$ and its latus recta is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9[\sqrt{2}-\log (\sqrt{2}+1)]$ sq. units
- **(B)** $4[\sqrt{2}-\log (\sqrt{2}+1)]$ sq. units
- **(C)** $3[\sqrt{2}-\log (\sqrt{2}+1)]$ sq. units
- **(D)** $18[\sqrt{2}-\log (\sqrt{2}+1)]$ sq. units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $18[\sqrt{2}-\log (\sqrt{2}+1)]$ sq. units**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the hyperbola is $x^2 - y^2 = 9$, which can be rewritten as $\dfrac{x^2}{9} - \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$.

Here, $a^2 = 9$ and $b^2 = 9$, giving $a = 3$ and $b = 3$.

The eccentricity of the hyperbola is $e = \sqrt{1 + \dfrac{b^2}{a^2}} = \sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$.

The foci of the hyperbola are at $(\pm ae, 0) = (\pm 3\sqrt{2}, 0)$.

The latus recta are the lines passing through the foci and perpendicular to the transverse axis, so their equations are $x = 3\sqrt{2}$ and $x = -3\sqrt{2}$.

The region bounded by the hyperbola and its latus recta is symmetric about both the x-axis and the y-axis. The total area $A$ is $4$ times the area in the first quadrant bounded by the hyperbola, the x-axis, and the line $x = 3\sqrt{2}$.

$A = 4 \int_{3}^{3\sqrt{2}} y \, dx$

From the equation of the hyperbola, $y = \sqrt{x^2 - 9}$ in the first quadrant.

$A = 4 \int_{3}^{3\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{x^2 - 9} \, dx$

Using the standard integral formula $\int \sqrt{x^2 - a^2} \, dx = \dfrac{x}{2}\sqrt{x^2 - a^2} - \dfrac{a^2}{2} \log|x + \sqrt{x^2 - a^2}|$, we get:

$A = 4 \left[ \dfrac{x}{2}\sqrt{x^2 - 9} - \dfrac{9}{2} \log|x + \sqrt{x^2 - 9}| \right]_{3}^{3\sqrt{2}}$

Substituting the upper limit $x = 3\sqrt{2}$:

Value at upper limit $= \dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}\sqrt{18 - 9} - \dfrac{9}{2} \log|3\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{18 - 9}| = \dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{2} - \dfrac{9}{2} \log(3\sqrt{2} + 3)$

Substituting the lower limit $x = 3$:

Value at lower limit $= 0 - \dfrac{9}{2} \log|3 + 0| = -\dfrac{9}{2} \log 3$

Subtracting the lower limit value from the upper limit value:

$A = 4 \left( \dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{2} - \dfrac{9}{2} \log(3\sqrt{2} + 3) + \dfrac{9}{2} \log 3 \right)$

$A = 4 \left( \dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{2} - \dfrac{9}{2} \log\left(\dfrac{3(\sqrt{2} + 1)}{3}\right) \right)$

$A = 4 \left( \dfrac{9\sqrt{2}}{2} - \dfrac{9}{2} \log(\sqrt{2} + 1) \right)$

$A = 18[\sqrt{2} - \log(\sqrt{2} + 1)]$ sq. units

Answer: $18[\sqrt{2}-\log (\sqrt{2}+1)]$ sq. units

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2020 (19 Oct Shift 1)

**Options:**

- **(A)** 2
- **(B)** $2 \sqrt{2}$
- **(C)** 1
- **(D)** $\sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\lim _{x \rightarrow \pi} \frac{(1+\cos x)-\sin x}{(1+\cos x)+\sin x}$
$=\lim _{x \rightarrow \pi} \frac{2 \cos ^{2} x / 2-2(\sin x / 2) \cos x / 2}{2 \cos ^{2} x / 2+2(\sin x / 2) \cos x / 2}$
$=\lim _{x \rightarrow \pi} \frac{2 \cos ^{2} \frac{x}{2}\left(1-\tan \frac{x}{2}\right)}{2 \cos ^{2} \frac{x}{2}\left(x+\tan \frac{x}{2}\right)}$
$=\lim _{x \rightarrow \pi} \tan \left(\frac{\pi}{4}-\frac{x}{2}\right)=-1$
$\therefore f(\pi)=\lim _{x \rightarrow \pi} f(x)=-1$
