### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (15 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the line $4x + 3y = 7$ touches the hyperbola $x^2 - y^2 = 7$, then the sum of the co-ordinates of the point of contact is...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $4$
- **(B)** $7$
- **(C)** $1$
- **(D)** $0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the given hyperbola is $x^2 - y^2 = 7$.

Let the point of contact be $(x_1, y_1)$. The equation of the tangent to the hyperbola at this point is given by $x x_1 - y y_1 = 7$.

The given equation of the line is $4x + 3y = 7$.

Since both equations represent the same tangent line, we can compare their corresponding coefficients:

$x_1 = 4$

$-y_1 = 3 \Rightarrow y_1 = -3$

The point of contact is $(4, -3)$.

The sum of the coordinates of the point of contact is $4 + (-3) = 1$.

Answer: $1$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the tangent at the point $(2 \sec \theta, 3 \tan \theta)$ to the hyperbola $\frac{x^2}{4}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1$ is parallel to $3 x-y+4=0$, then the value of $\theta$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $45^{\circ}$
- **(B)** $60^{\circ}$
- **(C)** $30^{\circ}$
- **(D)** $90^{\circ}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $30^{\circ}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the hyperbola is $\dfrac{x^2}{4} - \dfrac{y^2}{9} = 1$.

The equation of the tangent at the point $(2 \sec \theta, 3 \tan \theta)$ is given by:

$\dfrac{x(2 \sec \theta)}{4} - \dfrac{y(3 \tan \theta)}{9} = 1$

$\dfrac{x \sec \theta}{2} - \dfrac{y \tan \theta}{3} = 1$

The slope of this tangent is:

$m = \dfrac{\dfrac{\sec \theta}{2}}{\dfrac{\tan \theta}{3}} = \dfrac{3 \sec \theta}{2 \tan \theta} = \dfrac{3}{2 \sin \theta}$

The tangent is parallel to the line $3x - y + 4 = 0$, which has a slope of $3$.

Equating the slopes, we get:

$\dfrac{3}{2 \sin \theta} = 3$

$\dfrac{1}{2 \sin \theta} = 1$

$\sin \theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\theta = 30^{\circ}$

Answer: $30^{\circ}$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The X and Y intercepts of the tangent to the hyperbola $\frac{x^2}{20}-\frac{\mathrm{y}^2}{5}=1$ which is perpendicular to the line $4 x+3 y=7$, are respectively

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{-10}{3}, \frac{-5}{3}$
- **(B)** $\frac{10}{3}, \frac{-5}{2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{10}{3}, \frac{5}{2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{10}{3}, \frac{5}{3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{10}{3}, \frac{-5}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the hyperbola is $\dfrac{x^2}{20} - \dfrac{y^2}{5} = 1$.

Here, $a^2 = 20$ and $b^2 = 5$.

The slope of the given line $4x + 3y = 7$ is $m_1 = -\dfrac{4}{3}$.

Since the tangent is perpendicular to this line, the slope of the tangent is $m = \dfrac{-1}{m_1} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

The equation of the tangent to the hyperbola is given by $y = mx \pm \sqrt{a^2m^2 - b^2}$.

Substituting the values of $a^2$, $b^2$, and $m$:

$y = \dfrac{3}{4}x \pm \sqrt{20\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 - 5}$

$y = \dfrac{3}{4}x \pm \sqrt{20\left(\dfrac{9}{16}\right) - 5}$

$y = \dfrac{3}{4}x \pm \sqrt{\dfrac{45}{4} - 5}$

$y = \dfrac{3}{4}x \pm \sqrt{\dfrac{25}{4}}$

$y = \dfrac{3}{4}x \pm \dfrac{5}{2}$

Taking the negative sign, the equation of the tangent is:

$y = \dfrac{3}{4}x - \dfrac{5}{2}$

$3x - 4y - 10 = 0$

To find the X-intercept, put $y = 0$:

$3x - 10 = 0 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{10}{3}$

To find the Y-intercept, put $x = 0$:

$-4y - 10 = 0 \Rightarrow y = -\dfrac{5}{2}$

Thus, the X and Y intercepts are $\dfrac{10}{3}$ and $-\dfrac{5}{2}$ respectively.

Answer: $\frac{10}{3}, \frac{-5}{2}$

</details>
