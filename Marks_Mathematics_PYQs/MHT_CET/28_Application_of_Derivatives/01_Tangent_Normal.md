### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let the curve $y = e^{2x} + x^2$ cuts the Y-axis at the point P. If the tangent and normal to the curve at point P intersect the X-axis at points R and Q respectively, the area of triangle PQR is.....

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{3}{4}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{3}{2}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{5}{4}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{5}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $\dfrac{5}{4}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the curve is given by $y = e^{2x} + x^2$.

To find the point of intersection with the Y-axis, we substitute $x = 0$ into the equation of the curve:
$y = e^{0} + 0 = 1$
Thus, the point P is $(0, 1)$.

Next, we find the slope of the tangent to the curve at P by differentiating $y$ with respect to $x$:
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2e^{2x} + 2x$

At point P$(0, 1)$, the slope of the tangent is:
$m = \left. \dfrac{dy}{dx} \right|_{x=0} = 2e^{0} + 0 = 2$

The equation of the tangent at P$(0, 1)$ is:
$y - 1 = 2(x - 0)$
$y = 2x + 1$
The tangent intersects the X-axis at R. Substituting $y = 0$, we get $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
So, R is $\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}, 0\right)$.

The slope of the normal at P is $-\dfrac{1}{m} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.
The equation of the normal at P$(0, 1)$ is:
$y - 1 = -\dfrac{1}{2}(x - 0)$
$y = -\dfrac{x}{2} + 1$
The normal intersects the X-axis at Q. Substituting $y = 0$, we get $x = 2$.
So, Q is $(2, 0)$.

The base of the triangle PQR lies on the X-axis along the segment RQ. The length of the base is:
$RQ = 2 - \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = \dfrac{5}{2}$

The height of the triangle is the y-coordinate of point P, which is $1$.

The area of triangle PQR is:
$\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{5}{2} \times 1 = \dfrac{5}{4}$

Answer: $\dfrac{5}{4}$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 1)

The tangent to the curve $y = e^{2x}$ is drawn at the point $P(0,1)$. If this tangent intersects the $x$-axis at point $A$ and the $y$-axis at point $B$, then the area of $\triangle OAB$ (where $O$ is the origin) in square units is:

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

At point $P(0,1)$, the slope of the tangent is $m = 2e^{0} = 2$.

The equation of the tangent at $P(0,1)$ is $y - 1 = 2(x - 0)$, which simplifies to $y = 2x + 1$.

The equation of the line tangent to the curve $y^2 = 4x$ with an inclination of $60^\circ$ is:

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $3x - \sqrt{3}y + 1 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Comparing, we get $a = 1$.

The inclination of the tangent is $60^{\circ}$, so its slope is $m = \tan 60^{\circ} = \sqrt{3}$.

The equation of a tangent to the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$ with slope $m$ is given by $y = mx + \dfrac{a}{m}$.

Substituting $a = 1$ and $m = \sqrt{3}$, we get:

$y = \sqrt{3}x + \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$

Multiplying the entire equation by $\sqrt{3}$, we obtain:

$\sqrt{3}y = 3x + 1$

$3x - \sqrt{3}y + 1 = 0$

Answer: $3x - \sqrt{3}y + 1 = 0$

The coordinates of the point on the curve $y = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{2}\right)$ at which the normal is parallel to the Y- axis are:

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$

$x = 2n + 1$

$y = \sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1$

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

A tangent is drawn from the origin $(0,0)$ to the curve $y = \sin x$. If $(x, y)$ is the point of contact and $x \neq 0$, then which of the following relations is true?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{1}{y^2} - \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 1$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{1}{y^2} + \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 1$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{1}{x^2} - \dfrac{1}{y^2} = 1$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{x^2} + \dfrac{1}{y^2} = -1$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\dfrac{1}{y^2} - \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the point of contact be $(x, y)$.

The equation of the curve is $y = \sin x$.

Differentiating with respect to $x$, we get the slope of the tangent:

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \cos x$

Since the tangent passes through the origin $(0,0)$ and the point of contact $(x, y)$, its slope can also be written as:

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{y - 0}{x - 0} = \dfrac{y}{x}$

Equating the two expressions for the slope:

$\cos x = \dfrac{y}{x}$

Since the point $(x, y)$ lies on the curve, we have:

$\sin x = y$

Squaring and adding the equations for $\sin x$ and $\cos x$:

$\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = y^2 + \dfrac{y^2}{x^2}$

$1 = y^2 \left(1 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}\right)$

Dividing both sides by $y^2$:

$\dfrac{1}{y^2} = 1 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}$

$\dfrac{1}{y^2} - \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 1$

Answer: $\dfrac{1}{y^2} - \dfrac{1}{x^2} = 1$

</details>

### Q15. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The tangent to the curve intersects the Y-axis at point P. A line drawn through point P is perpendicular to this tangent and passes through another point $(1, 0)$. The differential equation of the curve is...

- **(A)** $y\dfrac{dy}{dx} - x\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 1$
- **(B)** $x\dfrac{dy}{dx} - y\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 1$
- **(C)** $y\dfrac{dy}{dx} + x = 1$
- **(D)** $x\dfrac{dy}{dx} + y = 1$

<details>

The equation of the tangent at $(x, y)$ is $Y - y = \dfrac{dy}{dx}(X - x)$.

To find the intersection with the Y-axis (point P), substitute $X = 0$:

$Y = y - x\dfrac{dy}{dx}$

Thus, the coordinates of point P are $\left(0, y - x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)$.

The slope of the tangent is $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, so the slope of the line perpendicular to it is $-\dfrac{dx}{dy}$.

This perpendicular line passes through P $\left(0, y - x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)$ and $(1, 0)$. The slope of this line is also given by:

$\dfrac{0 - \left(y - x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)}{1 - 0} = x\dfrac{dy}{dx} - y$

$x\dfrac{dy}{dx} - y = -\dfrac{dx}{dy}$

Multiplying both sides by $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$:

$x\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 - y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -1$

$y\dfrac{dy}{dx} - x\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 1$

Answer: $y\dfrac{dy}{dx} - x\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^2 = 1$
