### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (19 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the slope of the tangent to the curve $2y^2 = ax^2 + b$ at $(1, -1)$ is $-1$, then ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $a = 2$ and $b = 0$
- **(B)** $a = 2$ and $b = 1$
- **(C)** $a = 0$ and $b = 1$
- **(D)** $a = 1$ and $b = 2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $a = 2$ and $b = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the curve is $2y^2 = ax^2 + b$.

Since the point $(1, -1)$ lies on the curve, substituting $x = 1$ and $y = -1$ gives:
$2(-1)^2 = a(1)^2 + b$
$a + b = 2$

Differentiating the equation of the curve with respect to $x$, we get:
$4y \dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2ax$
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{ax}{2y}$

The slope of the tangent at $(1, -1)$ is given as $-1$. Substituting $x = 1$ and $y = -1$ into the derivative:
$\dfrac{a(1)}{2(-1)} = -1$
$-\dfrac{a}{2} = -1$
$a = 2$

Substituting $a = 2$ into the equation $a + b = 2$, we get:
$2 + b = 2$
$b = 0$

Thus, $a = 2$ and $b = 0$.

Answer: $a = 2$ and $b = 0$
