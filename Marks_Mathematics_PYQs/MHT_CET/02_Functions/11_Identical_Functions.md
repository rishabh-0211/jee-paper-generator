### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (21 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

Which of the following is not a homogeneous function?

**Options:**

- **(A)** $y^2+2 x y$
- **(B)** $2 x-3 y$
- **(C)** $\sin \left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$
- **(D)** $\cos x+\sin y$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\cos x+\sin y$**

**Detailed Solution:**

A function $f(x, y)$ is said to be a homogeneous function of degree $n$ if $f(tx, ty) = t^n f(x, y)$.

For $f(x, y) = y^2 + 2xy$, $f(tx, ty) = t^2(y^2 + 2xy) = t^2 f(x, y)$. It is homogeneous of degree $2$.

For $f(x, y) = 2x - 3y$, $f(tx, ty) = t(2x - 3y) = t^1 f(x, y)$. It is homogeneous of degree $1$.

For $f(x, y) = \sin\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right)$, $f(tx, ty) = \sin\left(\dfrac{ty}{tx}\right) = t^0 \sin\left(\dfrac{y}{x}\right) = t^0 f(x, y)$. It is homogeneous of degree $0$.

For $f(x, y) = \cos x + \sin y$, $f(tx, ty) = \cos(tx) + \sin(ty) \neq t^n f(x, y)$ for any $n$.

Thus, $\cos x + \sin y$ is not a homogeneous function.

Answer: $\cos x+\sin y$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2024 (15 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If $[x]^2-5[x]+6=0$, where [.] denotes the greatest integer function, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\quad x \in(2,4]$
- **(B)** $x \in[2,4]$
- **(C)** $x \in[2,4)$
- **(D)** $\quad x \in(2,4)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $x \in[2,4)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $[x] = y$. The given equation becomes $y^2 - 5y + 6 = 0$.

Factoring the quadratic equation, we get $(y - 2)(y - 3) = 0$.

This gives $y = 2$ or $y = 3$.

Substituting back $y = [x]$, we have $[x] = 2$ or $[x] = 3$.

For $[x] = 2$, the values of $x$ lie in the interval $[2, 3)$.

For $[x] = 3$, the values of $x$ lie in the interval $[3, 4)$.

Taking the union of both solutions, we get $x \in [2, 3) \cup [3, 4) = [2, 4)$.

Answer: $x \in[2,4)$

</details>
