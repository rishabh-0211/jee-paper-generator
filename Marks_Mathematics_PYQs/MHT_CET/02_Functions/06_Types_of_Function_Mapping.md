### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The function defined by $\mathrm{f}(x)=\frac{2 x+3}{3 x+4}, x \neq-\frac{4}{3}$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** only one one
- **(B)** only onto
- **(C)** onto for $y \neq \frac{2}{3}$ and one - one
- **(D)** neither one-one nor onto

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) onto for $y \neq \frac{2}{3}$ and one - one**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $f(x_1) = f(x_2)$

$\dfrac{2x_1+3}{3x_1+4} = \dfrac{2x_2+3}{3x_2+4}$

$(2x_1+3)(3x_2+4) = (2x_2+3)(3x_1+4)$

$6x_1x_2 + 8x_1 + 9x_2 + 12 = 6x_1x_2 + 9x_1 + 8x_2 + 12$

$8x_1 + 9x_2 = 9x_1 + 8x_2$

$x_1 = x_2$

Therefore, $f(x)$ is one-one.

To find the range, let $y = f(x)$

$y = \dfrac{2x+3}{3x+4}$

$3xy + 4y = 2x + 3$

$x(3y - 2) = 3 - 4y$

$x = \dfrac{3-4y}{3y-2}$

For $x$ to be a real number, the denominator must not be zero, so $3y - 2 \neq 0 \Rightarrow y \neq \dfrac{2}{3}$.

Thus, for every $y \in \mathbb{R} - \left\{\dfrac{2}{3}\right\}$, there exists a real $x \neq -\dfrac{4}{3}$ such that $f(x) = y$.

Hence, the function is onto for $y \neq \dfrac{2}{3}$ and one-one.

Answer: onto for $y \neq \frac{2}{3}$ and one - one

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2022 (10 Aug Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If the function $f: R-\{-1,1\} \rightarrow A$ defined by $f(x)=\frac{x^2}{1-x^2}$ is surjective, then $A$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $R-[-1,0)$
- **(B)** $R-\{-1\}$
- **(C)** $\{0, \infty)$
- **(D)** $R-(-1,0)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $R-[-1,0)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

For $f(x)$ to be a surjective function $A=\operatorname{range}$ of $f(x)$
now, $y=\frac{x^2}{1-x^2}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow x^2=\frac{y}{1+y} \\
& \Rightarrow x=\sqrt{\frac{y}{1+y}}
\end{aligned}
$$
for $x$ to be real $\frac{y}{1+y} \geq 0$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \Rightarrow y \in(-\infty,-1) \cup[0, \infty) \\
& \Rightarrow \text { Range of } f(x) \text { is } R \sim[-1,0) \\
& \Rightarrow A \text { is } R \sim[-1,0)
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2022 (10 Aug Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Let $A=\{x \in R / x$ is not a positive integer). Let a function $f$ be defined as $f: A \rightarrow R$ so that $f(x)=\frac{2 x}{x-1}$, then $f$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** Not injective.
- **(B)** surjective but not injective.
- **(C)** neither injective nor surjective.
- **(D)** injective but not surjective.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) injective but not surjective.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & f(x)=\frac{2 x}{x-1} \\ & \Rightarrow f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{(x-1) 2-2 x(1-0)}{(x-1)^2}=\frac{-2}{(x-1)^2}<0\end{aligned}$
$f(x)$ is strictly decreasing so $f(x)$ is injective
But $f(x)=4$
$\Rightarrow \frac{2 x}{x-1}=4$
$\Rightarrow x=2$ (which is a positive integer)
i.e., $4 \in R$ (co-domain of $f$ ) has no pre-image in $A$ (domain of $f$ )
So, ' $f$ ' is not surjective

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2022 (05 Aug Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\mathrm{R}$ denotes the set of all real numbers then the function $\mathrm{f}: \mathrm{R} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}$ defined by $f(x)=|x|$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** injective and surjective.
- **(B)** neither injective nor surjective.
- **(C)** injective.
- **(D)** surjective.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) neither injective nor surjective.**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{aligned} & \mathrm{f}: \mathrm{R} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}, \mathrm{f}(\mathrm{x})=|\mathrm{x}| \\ & \because \mathrm{f}(-1)=\mathrm{f}(1)=1\end{aligned}$
i.e. not injection
and range of $\mathrm{f}(\mathrm{x})$ is $[0, \infty]$
Hence, not surjection

</details>
