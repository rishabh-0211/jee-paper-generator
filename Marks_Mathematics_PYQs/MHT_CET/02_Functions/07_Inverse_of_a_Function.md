### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $f(x) = \dfrac{2x - 1}{x + 5}$, $x \neq -5$ then $f^{-1}(x)$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{x + 5}{2x - 1}$, $x \neq \dfrac{1}{2}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{5x + 1}{2 - x}$, $x \neq 2$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{5x - 1}{2 - x}$, $x \neq 2$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{x - 5}{2x + 1}$, $x \neq -\dfrac{1}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\dfrac{5x + 1}{2 - x}$, $x \neq 2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $y = \dfrac{2x - 1}{x + 5}$

$y(x + 5) = 2x - 1$

$xy + 5y = 2x - 1$

$x(y - 2) = -5y - 1$

$x = \dfrac{5y + 1}{2 - y}$

Therefore, $f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{5x + 1}{2 - x}$, $x \neq 2$

Answer: $\dfrac{5x + 1}{2 - x}$, $x \neq 2$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (13 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $g(x)$ is the inverse function of $f(x)$, where $f(x) = \dfrac{5x+3}{4x-1}$, then $g(1) =$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-4$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{-1}{4}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{8}{3}$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{3}{8}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $-4$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $f(x) = \dfrac{5x+3}{4x-1}$ and $g(x)$ is the inverse function of $f(x)$.

By definition of inverse functions, if $g(1) = x$, then $f(x) = 1$.

Substituting $f(x) = 1$ into the given function:

$\dfrac{5x+3}{4x-1} = 1$

$5x + 3 = 4x - 1$

$5x - 4x = -1 - 3$

$x = -4$

Therefore, $g(1) = -4$.

Answer: $-4$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (11 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $\mathrm{f}:[1, \infty) \rightarrow[2, \infty)$ is given by $\mathrm{f}(x)=x+\frac{1}{x}$ then $\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)$ equals

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2-4}}{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{2}{1+x^2}$
- **(C)** $\frac{x-\sqrt{x^2-4}}{2}$
- **(D)** $1+\sqrt{x^2-4}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2-4}}{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $y = f(x) = x + \dfrac{1}{x}$

$y = \dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x}$

$x^2 - yx + 1 = 0$

Solving for $x$ using the quadratic formula, we get:

$x = \dfrac{y \pm \sqrt{y^2 - 4}}{2}$

Since the domain of $f(x)$ is $[1, \infty)$, we have $x \ge 1$.

If we take $x = \dfrac{y - \sqrt{y^2 - 4}}{2}$, then for $y > 2$, $x < 1$, which is not in the domain.

Therefore, we must take the positive sign:

$x = \dfrac{y + \sqrt{y^2 - 4}}{2}$

Replacing $y$ with $x$, the inverse function is:

$f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{x + \sqrt{x^2 - 4}}{2}$

Answer: $\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2-4}}{2}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2023 (14 May Shift 2)

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{4 x-3}{3 x-2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{3 x-2}{4 x+3}$
- **(C)** $\frac{3 x-4}{4 x-2}$
- **(D)** $\frac{2 x+3}{4 x-3}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{4 x-3}{3 x-2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let $\mathrm{f}(x)=y \Rightarrow x=\mathrm{f}^{-1}(y)$
$\begin{aligned}
& y=\frac{2 x-3}{3 x-4} \\
& \Rightarrow 3 x y-4 y=2 x-3 \\
& \Rightarrow x(3 y-2)=4 y-3 \\
& \Rightarrow x=\frac{4 y-3}{3 y-2} \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{f}^{-1}(y)=\frac{4 y-3}{3 y-2} \\
& \Rightarrow \mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)=\frac{4 x-3}{3 x-2}
\end{aligned}$

- **(D)** $\frac{1}{2} \log _a\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)$

### Q7. MHT CET 2020 (20 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

$f(x)=\frac{3 x+2}{5 x-3}, x \in R-\left\{\frac{3}{5}\right\}$, then

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)=\mathrm{f}(x)$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)$ does not exist.
- **(C)** $\mathrm{f}[\mathrm{f}(x)]=-x$
- **(D)** $\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)=-\mathrm{f}(x)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\mathrm{f}^{-1}(x)=\mathrm{f}(x)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

$\begin{array}{l}
\text { Let } y=f(x)=\frac{3 x+2}{5 x-3} \\
\therefore y(5 x-3)=3 x+2 \Rightarrow 5 x y-3 y=3 x+2 \Rightarrow(5 y-3) x=3 y+2 \\
x=\frac{3 y+2}{5 y-3} \\
\Rightarrow f^{-1}(y)=\frac{3 y+2}{5 y-3} \\
\Rightarrow f^{-1}(x)=\frac{3 x+2}{5 x-3}
\end{array}$
Thus $f^{-1}(x)=f(x)$

### Q10. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $f: \mathrm{R} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}, \mathrm{g}: \mathrm{R} \rightarrow \mathrm{R}$ are two functions defined by $f(x)=2 x-3, \mathrm{~g}(x)=x^{3}+5$
then $(\operatorname{fog})^{-1}(x)=$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\frac{2 x+3}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}$
- **(B)** $\left(\frac{x-7}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$
- **(C)** $\left(\frac{x-7}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}$
- **(D)** $\left(\frac{x+7}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\left(\frac{x-7}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given $\mathrm{f}(\mathrm{x})=2 \mathrm{x}-3, \quad \mathrm{~g}(\mathrm{x})=\mathrm{x}^{3}+5$
$\begin{aligned} \therefore \quad(\mathrm{fog})(\mathrm{x}) &=\mathrm{f}[\mathrm{g}(\mathrm{x})]=\mathrm{f}\left(\mathrm{x}^{3}+5\right) \\ &=2\left(\mathrm{x}^{3}+5\right)-3=2 \mathrm{x}^{3}+7 \end{aligned}$

### Q11. MHT CET 2010

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $g$ is the inverse of $f$ and $f^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}$, then $g^{\prime}(x)$ is equal to

**Options:**

- **(A)** $1+[g(x)]^{2}$
- **(B)** $\frac{-1}{1+[g(x)]^{2}}$
- **(C)** $\frac{1}{2\left(1+x^{2}\right)}$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $1+[g(x)]^{2}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given, $g=$ inverse of $f=f^{-1}$
$$
\Rightarrow \quad g(x)=f^{-1}(x)
$$
$$
\Rightarrow \quad f[g(x)]=x
$$
On differentiating w.r.t. $x$, we get
$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{\prime}[g(x)] \cdot g^{\prime}(x) &=1 \\
\Rightarrow \quad g^{\prime}(x) &=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}[g(x)]} \\
&=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{1+[g(x)]^{2}}} \\
\Rightarrow & g^{\prime}(x)=1+[g(x)]^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
