### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (20 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $p_1$ and $p_2$ are the lengths of perpendiculars from the origin to the lines $x\sec\theta + y\csc\theta = a$ and $x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta = a\cos(2\theta)$ respectively, then $4p_1^2 + p_2^2 =$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $2a^2$
- **(B)** $4a^2$
- **(C)** $a^2$
- **(D)** $3a^2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $a^2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The length of the perpendicular from the origin $(0,0)$ to the line $x\sec\theta + y\csc\theta - a = 0$ is given by

$p_1 = \dfrac{|-a|}{\sqrt{\sec^2\theta + \csc^2\theta}}$

Since $\sec^2\theta + \csc^2\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos^2\theta} + \dfrac{1}{\sin^2\theta} = \dfrac{1}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta}$,

$p_1 = |a\sin\theta\cos\theta| = \dfrac{|a\sin(2\theta)|}{2}$

Squaring both sides and multiplying by $4$,

$4p_1^2 = a^2\sin^2(2\theta)$

The length of the perpendicular from the origin to the line $x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta - a\cos(2\theta) = 0$ is given by

$p_2 = \dfrac{|-a\cos(2\theta)|}{\sqrt{\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta}}$

Since $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$,

$p_2 = |a\cos(2\theta)|$

Squaring both sides,

$p_2^2 = a^2\cos^2(2\theta)$

Adding the two expressions,

$4p_1^2 + p_2^2 = a^2\sin^2(2\theta) + a^2\cos^2(2\theta)$

$4p_1^2 + p_2^2 = a^2(\sin^2(2\theta) + \cos^2(2\theta)) = a^2$

Answer: $a^2$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (15 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The sum of the ordinates of the points on the line $4x + 3y = 10$ whose distance from the line $x + y - 4 = 0$ is $\sqrt{2}$ is equal to ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $-4$
- **(B)** $-10$
- **(C)** $12$
- **(D)** $18$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $12$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the point on the line $4x + 3y = 10$ be $(x_1, y_1)$.

We have $4x_1 + 3y_1 = 10 \Rightarrow x_1 = \dfrac{10 - 3y_1}{4}$.

The distance of $(x_1, y_1)$ from the line $x + y - 4 = 0$ is given as $\sqrt{2}$.

$\dfrac{|x_1 + y_1 - 4|}{\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2}} = \sqrt{2}$

$|x_1 + y_1 - 4| = 2$

This gives two possible cases:

$x_1 + y_1 - 4 = 2 \Rightarrow x_1 + y_1 = 6$

$x_1 + y_1 - 4 = -2 \Rightarrow x_1 + y_1 = 2$

Substituting $x_1 = \dfrac{10 - 3y_1}{4}$ in the first equation:

$\dfrac{10 - 3y_1}{4} + y_1 = 6$

$10 - 3y_1 + 4y_1 = 24$

$10 + y_1 = 24 \Rightarrow y_1 = 14$

Substituting $x_1 = \dfrac{10 - 3y_1}{4}$ in the second equation:

$\dfrac{10 - 3y_1}{4} + y_1 = 2$

$10 - 3y_1 + 4y_1 = 8$

$10 + y_1 = 8 \Rightarrow y_1 = -2$

The ordinates of the points are $14$ and $-2$.

The sum of the ordinates is $14 + (-2) = 12$.

Answer: $12$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The points on the line $x + y - 4 = 0$ which are at one unit distance from the line $4x + 3y - 10 = 0$ are...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(5, -1)$ and $(-2, 6)$

<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $(3, 1)$ and $(-7, 11)$**

Let the point on the line $x + y - 4 = 0$ be $(a, 4 - a)$.

Using the perpendicular distance formula, we have:

$\dfrac{|4a + 3(4 - a) - 10|}{\sqrt{4^2 + 3^2}} = 1$

$\dfrac{|4a + 12 - 3a - 10|}{5} = 1$

$|a + 2| = 5$

$a + 2 = -5 \Rightarrow a = -7$

For $a = -7$, the $y$-coordinate is $4 - (-7) = 11$. The point is $(-7, 11)$.

### Q4. MHT CET 2026 (13 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular from the point $(2, 3)$ on the line $y=3x+4$ is given by ...

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\dfrac{37}{10}, \dfrac{-1}{10}\right)$
- **(B)** $\left(\dfrac{-1}{10}, \dfrac{37}{10}\right)$
- **(C)** $\left(\dfrac{10}{37}, -10\right)$
- **(D)** $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}, -\dfrac{1}{3}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\left(\dfrac{-1}{10}, \dfrac{37}{10}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given line is $3x - y + 4 = 0$.

Let the foot of the perpendicular from the point $(2, 3)$ to the line be $(h, k)$.

Using the formula for the foot of the perpendicular:

$\dfrac{h - x_1}{a} = \dfrac{k - y_1}{b} = -\dfrac{ax_1 + by_1 + c}{a^2 + b^2}$

Substituting the values $x_1 = 2$, $y_1 = 3$, $a = 3$, $b = -1$, $c = 4$:

$\dfrac{h - 2}{3} = \dfrac{k - 3}{-1} = -\dfrac{3(2) - 1(3) + 4}{3^2 + (-1)^2}$

$\dfrac{h - 2}{3} = \dfrac{k - 3}{-1} = -\dfrac{6 - 3 + 4}{10}$

$\dfrac{h - 2}{3} = \dfrac{k - 3}{-1} = -\dfrac{7}{10}$

Solving for $h$:

$h - 2 = -\dfrac{21}{10} \Rightarrow h = 2 - \dfrac{21}{10} = -\dfrac{1}{10}$

Solving for $k$:

$k - 3 = \dfrac{7}{10} \Rightarrow k = 3 + \dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{37}{10}$

The coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular are $\left(\dfrac{-1}{10}, \dfrac{37}{10}\right)$.

Answer: $\left(\dfrac{-1}{10}, \dfrac{37}{10}\right)$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2025 (23 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The distance of the point $(1,2)$ from the line $x+y=0$ measured parallel to the line $3 x-y=2$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{3 \sqrt{2}}{8}$ units
- **(B)** $\frac{3 \sqrt{10}}{4}$ units
- **(C)** 10 units
- **(D)** $5 \sqrt{5}$ units

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\frac{3 \sqrt{10}}{4}$ units**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the given point be $P(1, 2)$.

The line along which the distance is measured is parallel to $3x - y = 2$.

The equation of the line passing through $P(1, 2)$ and parallel to $3x - y = 2$ is:

$3x - y = 3(1) - 2$

$3x - y = 1$

Let this line intersect the given line $x + y = 0$ at point $Q$.

Solving $3x - y = 1$ and $x + y = 0$, we get:

$3x - (-x) = 1 \Rightarrow 4x = 1 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{1}{4}$

$y = -\dfrac{1}{4}$

So, $Q = \left(\dfrac{1}{4}, -\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$.

The required distance is $PQ$:

$PQ = \sqrt{\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{4}\right)^2 + \left(2 - \left(-\dfrac{1}{4}\right)\right)^2}$

$PQ = \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{9}{4}\right)^2}$

$PQ = \sqrt{\dfrac{9}{16} + \dfrac{81}{16}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{90}{16}} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{10}}{4}$ units.

Answer: $\frac{3 \sqrt{10}}{4}$ units

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2025 (22 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

From the following options, the nearest line to the origin is ....

**Options:**

- **(A)** $3 x-4 y+4=0$
- **(B)** $2 x-3 y=5$
- **(C)** $4 x-3 y+12=0$
- **(D)** $5 x-2 y=3$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $5 x-2 y=3$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The perpendicular distance of a line $ax + by + c = 0$ from the origin $(0,0)$ is given by $d = \dfrac{|c|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}$.

For $3x - 4y + 4 = 0$, $d_1 = \dfrac{|4|}{\sqrt{3^2 + (-4)^2}} = \dfrac{4}{5} = 0.8$

For $2x - 3y - 5 = 0$, $d_2 = \dfrac{|-5|}{\sqrt{2^2 + (-3)^2}} = \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{13}} \approx 1.38$

For $4x - 3y + 12 = 0$, $d_3 = \dfrac{|12|}{\sqrt{4^2 + (-3)^2}} = \dfrac{12}{5} = 2.4$

For $5x - 2y - 3 = 0$, $d_4 = \dfrac{|-3|}{\sqrt{5^2 + (-2)^2}} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{29}} \approx 0.55$

Since $d_4$ is the minimum, the line $5x - 2y = 3$ is nearest to the origin.

Answer: $5 x-2 y=3$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

If two lines $x+(a-1) y=1$ and $2 x+\mathrm{a}^2 y=1(\mathrm{a} \in \mathrm{R}-\{0,1\})$ are perpendicular, then the distance of their point of intersection from the origin is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\dfrac{2}{5}$
- **(B)** $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{5}$
- **(C)** $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}}$
- **(D)** $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{5}}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{5}}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given lines are $x + (a-1)y = 1$ and $2x + a^2 y = 1$.

The slope of the first line is $m_1 = -\dfrac{1}{a-1}$ and the slope of the second line is $m_2 = -\dfrac{2}{a^2}$.

Since the lines are perpendicular, $m_1 m_2 = -1$.

$\left(-\dfrac{1}{a-1}\right) \left(-\dfrac{2}{a^2}\right) = -1$

$\dfrac{2}{a^2(a-1)} = -1$

$a^3 - a^2 + 2 = 0$

$(a+1)(a^2 - 2a + 2) = 0$

Since $a \in \mathbb{R}$, the only real root is $a = -1$.

Substituting $a = -1$ into the equations of the lines, we get:

$x - 2y = 1$

$2x + y = 1$

Solving these two equations, we multiply the second equation by $2$ and add it to the first equation:

$x - 2y + 4x + 2y = 1 + 2$

$5x = 3 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{3}{5}$

Substituting $x = \dfrac{3}{5}$ into $2x + y = 1$ gives $y = -\dfrac{1}{5}$.

The point of intersection is $\left(\dfrac{3}{5}, -\dfrac{1}{5}\right)$.

The distance of this point from the origin is:

$d = \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^2 + \left(-\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{9}{25} + \dfrac{1}{25}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{10}{25}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2}{5}}$

Answer: $\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{5}}$

</details>
