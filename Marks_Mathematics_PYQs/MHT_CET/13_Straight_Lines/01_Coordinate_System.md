### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Point C and D divide segment AB respectively internally and externally, in the ratio m:n then B divides CD internally in the ratio --------

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(m + n) : m$
- **(B)** $(m + n) : n$
- **(C)** $(m - n) : (m + n)$
- **(D)** $(m + n) : (m - n)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $(m - n) : (m + n)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Let the length of the line segment AB be $d$.

Since point C divides AB internally in the ratio $m:n$, the distance CB is given by:

$CB = \dfrac{n}{m+n} d$

Since point D divides AB externally in the ratio $m:n$, the distance BD is given by:

$BD = \dfrac{n}{m-n} d$

The ratio in which B divides CD internally is the ratio of the distance CB to the distance BD:

$\dfrac{CB}{BD} = \dfrac{\dfrac{n}{m+n} d}{\dfrac{n}{m-n} d}$

$\dfrac{CB}{BD} = \dfrac{m-n}{m+n}$

Thus, B divides CD internally in the ratio $(m-n) : (m+n)$.

Answer: $(m - n) : (m + n)$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2026 (14 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the polar coordinates of the point P are $\left(1, \dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right)$, then the sum of the squares of abscissa and ordinate of Cartesian coordinates of point P is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $0$
- **(B)** $1$
- **(C)** $4$
- **(D)** $\dfrac{1}{2}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $1$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given polar coordinates $(r, \theta) = \left(1, \dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right)$.

The Cartesian coordinates $(x, y)$ are given by $x = r \cos \theta$ and $y = r \sin \theta$.

The sum of the squares of the abscissa and ordinate is $x^2 + y^2$.

Using the relation between Cartesian and polar coordinates:

$x^2 + y^2 = (r \cos \theta)^2 + (r \sin \theta)^2 = r^2 (\cos^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta) = r^2$

Substituting $r = 1$, we get:

$x^2 + y^2 = 1^2 = 1$

Alternatively, calculating the coordinates explicitly:

$x = 1 \cdot \cos\left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$y = 1 \cdot \sin\left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

$x^2 + y^2 = \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 + \left(-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 1$

Answer: $1$

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2026 (18 April Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The coordinates of the orthocenter of the triangle whose sides are represented by the lines $4x - 7y + 10 = 0$, $x + y = 5$ and $7x + 4y = 15$ are $\ldots$

**Options:**

- **(A)** $(1,2)$
- **(B)** $(1,-2)$
- **(C)** $(-1,2)$
- **(D)** $(-1,-2)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $(1,2)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given lines forming the triangle are $4x - 7y + 10 = 0$, $x + y = 5$, and $7x + 4y = 15$.

The slopes of the lines $4x - 7y + 10 = 0$ and $7x + 4y = 15$ are $\dfrac{4}{7}$ and $-\dfrac{7}{4}$ respectively.

Since the product of their slopes is $\dfrac{4}{7} \times \left(-\dfrac{7}{4}\right) = -1$, these two lines are perpendicular to each other.

The triangle formed by the given lines is a right-angled triangle. In a right-angled triangle, the orthocenter is the vertex where the right angle is formed.

Thus, the orthocenter is the point of intersection of the perpendicular lines $4x - 7y + 10 = 0$ and $7x + 4y = 15$.

Solving the equations:
$4x - 7y = -10$
$7x + 4y = 15$

Multiplying the first equation by $4$ and the second by $7$, we get:
$16x - 28y = -40$
$49x + 28y = 105$

Adding these equations gives:
$65x = 65 \Rightarrow x = 1$

Substituting $x = 1$ in $7x + 4y = 15$, we get:
$7(1) + 4y = 15 \Rightarrow 4y = 8 \Rightarrow y = 2$

The coordinates of the orthocenter are $(1, 2)$.

### Q8. MHT CET 2026 (17 April Shift 1)

If $A(2, -3)$ and $C(-6, 7)$ are opposite vertices of a rhombus ABCD, then the equation of diagonal BD is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $5x - 4y + 2 = 0$
- **(B)** $5x + 4y + 2 = 0$
- **(C)** $4x - 5y + 18 = 0$
- **(D)** $4x + 5y + 18 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) $4x - 5y + 18 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles. Thus, diagonal $BD$ is the perpendicular bisector of diagonal $AC$.

The midpoint of diagonal $AC$ is $\left(\dfrac{2 - 6}{2}, \dfrac{-3 + 7}{2}\right) = (-2, 2)$.

The slope of diagonal $AC$ is $m_{AC} = \dfrac{7 - (-3)}{-6 - 2} = \dfrac{10}{-8} = -\dfrac{5}{4}$.

Since $BD \perp AC$, the slope of diagonal $BD$ is $m_{BD} = -\dfrac{1}{m_{AC}} = \dfrac{4}{5}$.

The equation of diagonal $BD$, which passes through the midpoint $(-2, 2)$ with slope $\dfrac{4}{5}$, is:

$y - 2 = \dfrac{4}{5}(x - (-2))$

$5(y - 2) = 4(x + 2)$

$5y - 10 = 4x + 8$

$4x - 5y + 18 = 0$
