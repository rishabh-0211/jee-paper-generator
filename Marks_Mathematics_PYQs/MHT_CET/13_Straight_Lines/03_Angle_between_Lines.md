### Q1. MHT CET 2026 (12 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

If $(2, 8)$ is one vertex and $x - y + 3 = 0$ is one diagonal of a square, then the equation of the other diagonal is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x + y - 10 = 0$
- **(B)** $x + y + 10 = 0$
- **(C)** $x - y + 10 = 0$
- **(D)** $x - y - 10 = 0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $x + y - 10 = 0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Substituting the coordinates of the given vertex $(2, 8)$ into the equation of the given diagonal $x - y + 3 = 0$, we get $2 - 8 + 3 = -3 \neq 0$.

Since the vertex does not lie on the given diagonal, the other diagonal must pass through $(2, 8)$.

The diagonals of a square are perpendicular to each other. The slope of the given diagonal $x - y + 3 = 0$ is $1$.

The slope of the other diagonal is $-1$.

The equation of the line passing through $(2, 8)$ with slope $-1$ is

$y - 8 = -1(x - 2)$

$x + y - 10 = 0$

Answer: $x + y - 10 = 0$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The acute angle between the line $4 x-2 y+13=0$ and the line which makes equal intercepts with the co-ordinate axes is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\tan ^{-1}(2)$
- **(B)** $\tan ^{-1}(3)$
- **(C)** $\tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)$
- **(D)** $\tan ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $\tan ^{-1}(3)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The slope of the given line $4x - 2y + 13 = 0$ is $m_1 = -\dfrac{4}{-2} = 2$.

A line making equal intercepts on the coordinate axes has the equation $\dfrac{x}{a} + \dfrac{y}{a} = 1$, which simplifies to $x + y = a$.

The slope of this line is $m_2 = -1$.

The acute angle $\theta$ between the two lines is given by:

$\tan \theta = \left| \dfrac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right|$

Substituting the values of $m_1$ and $m_2$:

$\tan \theta = \left| \dfrac{2 - (-1)}{1 + (2)(-1)} \right| = \left| \dfrac{3}{-1} \right| = 3$

$\theta = \tan^{-1}(3)$

Answer: $\tan ^{-1}(3)$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2025 (20 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The acute angle between the lines
\(x=-2+2 \mathrm{t}, \mathrm{y}=3-4 \mathrm{t}, \mathrm{z}=-4+\mathrm{t}\) and \(x=-2-\mathrm{t}, \mathrm{y}=3+2 \mathrm{t}, \mathrm{z}=-4+3 \mathrm{t}\) is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right)$
- **(B)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\right)$
- **(C)** $\sin ^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\right)$
- **(D)** $\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\right)$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The direction ratios of the first line are $a_1 = 2$, $b_1 = -4$, $c_1 = 1$.

The direction ratios of the second line are $a_2 = -1$, $b_2 = 2$, $c_2 = 3$.

The acute angle $\theta$ between the two lines is given by:

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{|a_1 a_2 + b_1 b_2 + c_1 c_2|}{\sqrt{a_1^2 + b_1^2 + c_1^2} \sqrt{a_2^2 + b_2^2 + c_2^2}}$

Substituting the values:

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{|(2)(-1) + (-4)(2) + (1)(3)|}{\sqrt{2^2 + (-4)^2 + 1^2} \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 2^2 + 3^2}}$

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{|-2 - 8 + 3|}{\sqrt{4 + 16 + 1} \sqrt{1 + 4 + 9}}$

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{|-7|}{\sqrt{21} \sqrt{14}}$

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{7}{\sqrt{3 \times 7 \times 2 \times 7}}$

$\cos \theta = \dfrac{7}{7\sqrt{6}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}$

$\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right)$

Answer: $\cos ^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right)$

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

Suppose that the points $(\mathrm{h}, \mathrm{k}),(1,2)$ and $(-3,4)$ lie on the line $l_1$. If a line $l_2$ passing through the points $(h, k)$ and $(4,3)$ is perpendicular to $l_1$, then $\left(\frac{k}{h}\right)$ equals

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\frac{1}{3}$
- **(B)** 0
- **(C)** 3
- **(D)** $-\frac{1}{7}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\frac{1}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The slope of line $l_1$ passing through $(1, 2)$ and $(-3, 4)$ is given by:

$m_1 = \dfrac{4 - 2}{-3 - 1} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

Since the point $(h, k)$ lies on $l_1$, the slope of the line segment joining $(h, k)$ and $(1, 2)$ is also $-\dfrac{1}{2}$.

$\dfrac{k - 2}{h - 1} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

$2k - 4 = -h + 1$

$h + 2k = 5 \quad \dots (1)$

The slope of line $l_2$ passing through $(h, k)$ and $(4, 3)$ is:

$m_2 = \dfrac{3 - k}{4 - h}$

Since $l_2$ is perpendicular to $l_1$, we have $m_1 m_2 = -1$.

$\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) \left(\dfrac{3 - k}{4 - h}\right) = -1$

$\dfrac{3 - k}{4 - h} = 2$

$3 - k = 8 - 2h$

$2h - k = 5 \quad \dots (2)$

Solving equations (1) and (2):

Multiply equation (2) by 2:

$4h - 2k = 10$

Adding this to equation (1):

$(h + 2k) + (4h - 2k) = 5 + 10$

$5h = 15 \Rightarrow h = 3$

Substituting $h = 3$ into equation (1):

$3 + 2k = 5 \Rightarrow 2k = 2 \Rightarrow k = 1$

Therefore, $\dfrac{k}{h} = \dfrac{1}{3}$.

Answer: $\frac{1}{3}$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (04 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The acute angle between the lines $x \cos 30^{\circ}+y \sin 30^{\circ}=3$ and $x \cos 60^{\circ}+y \sin 60^{\circ}=5$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $75^{\circ}$
- **(B)** $30^{\circ}$
- **(C)** $60^{\circ}$
- **(D)** $45^{\circ}$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $30^{\circ}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equations of the lines are in the normal form $x \cos \alpha + y \sin \alpha = p$.

For the first line, the angle made by the normal with the positive x-axis is $\alpha_1 = 30^{\circ}$.

For the second line, the angle made by the normal with the positive x-axis is $\alpha_2 = 60^{\circ}$.

The angle between two lines is equal to the angle between their normals.

The acute angle between the lines is $|\alpha_1 - \alpha_2| = |30^{\circ} - 60^{\circ}| = 30^{\circ}$.

Answer: $30^{\circ}$

</details>

### Q7. MHT CET 2021 (23 Sep Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If the angle between the lines is $\frac{\pi^c}{4}$ and slope of one of the lines is $\frac{1}{2}$, then slope of the other line is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 3 or $-\frac{1}{3}$
- **(B)** 4 or $-\frac{1}{4}$
- **(C)** 2 or $-\frac{1}{2}$
- **(D)** 3 or -3

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 3 or $-\frac{1}{3}$**

**Detailed Solution:**

We have $\theta=45^{\circ}$ and $\mathrm{m}_1=\frac{1}{2}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
& \tan \theta=\left|\frac{\mathrm{m}_1-\mathrm{m}_2}{1+\mathrm{m}_1 \mathrm{~m}_2}\right| \\
& \therefore \quad \tan 45^{\circ}=\left|\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\mathrm{m}_2}{1+\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \mathrm{m}_2}\right| \Rightarrow\left|\frac{1-2 \mathrm{~m}_2}{2+\mathrm{m}_2}\right|= \pm 1 \\
& \therefore \quad 1-2 \mathrm{~m}_2=2+\mathrm{m}_2 \text { or } 1-2 \mathrm{~m}_2=-2 \\
& \therefore \mathrm{m}_2=\frac{-1}{3} \text { or } \mathrm{m}_2=3
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

### Q10. MHT CET 2020 (13 Oct Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The acute angle included between the lines $x \sin \theta-y \cos \theta=5$ and
$x \sin \propto-y \cos \propto+11=0$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $|\theta-\propto|$
- **(B)** $\frac{\pi}{4}$
- **(C)** $\frac{\pi}{3}$
- **(D)** $\theta+\propto$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $|\theta-\propto|$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Slope of line $x \sin \theta-y \cos \theta=5$ is $m_{1}=\frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}=\tan \theta$ Slope of line $x \sin \alpha-y \sin \alpha+11=0$ is $m_{2}=\frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha}=\tan \alpha$ Let $\beta$ be the angle between the lines
$\begin{aligned}
\tan \beta &=\left|\frac{\tan \theta-\tan \alpha}{1+\tan \alpha \tan \alpha}\right| \Rightarrow \tan \beta=\tan (\theta-\alpha) \\
\beta &=|\theta-\alpha|
\end{aligned}$

</details>
