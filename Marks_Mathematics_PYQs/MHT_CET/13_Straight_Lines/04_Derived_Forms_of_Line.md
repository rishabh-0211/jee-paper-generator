### Q1. MHT CET 2025 (27 Apr Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The equation of the line passing through the point of intersection of the lines $x+2 y+6=0$ and $2 x-y=2$ and making an intercept 5 on the $y$-axis is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $39 x-2 y+10=0$
- **(B)** $39 x+2 y+10=0$
- **(C)** $39 x-2 y-10=0$
- **(D)** $39 x+2 y-10=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $39 x-2 y+10=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given lines are $x+2y+6=0$ and $2x-y-2=0$.

Solving these equations, we get the point of intersection.

From the second equation, $y = 2x-2$. Substituting in the first equation:

$x + 2(2x-2) + 6 = 0$

$5x + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\dfrac{2}{5}$

$y = 2\left(-\dfrac{2}{5}\right) - 2 = -\dfrac{14}{5}$

The point of intersection is $\left(-\dfrac{2}{5}, -\dfrac{14}{5}\right)$.

The required line makes a $y$-intercept of $5$, so it passes through $(0, 5)$.

The slope of the line passing through $\left(-\dfrac{2}{5}, -\dfrac{14}{5}\right)$ and $(0, 5)$ is:

$m = \dfrac{5 - \left(-\dfrac{14}{5}\right)}{0 - \left(-\dfrac{2}{5}\right)} = \dfrac{\dfrac{39}{5}}{\dfrac{2}{5}} = \dfrac{39}{2}$

The equation of the line is $y = mx + c$:

$y = \dfrac{39}{2}x + 5$

$2y = 39x + 10$

$39x - 2y + 10 = 0$

Answer: $39 x-2 y+10=0$

</details>

### Q2. MHT CET 2025 (26 Apr Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The line MN whose equation is $x-y-2=0$ cuts the X-axis at M and co-ordinates of N are $(4,2)$. The line MN is rotated about M through $45^{\circ}$ in anticlockwise direction. The equation of the line MN in the new position is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\mathrm{y}=-\sqrt{2}$
- **(B)** $\mathrm{y}=2$
- **(C)** $x=-2$
- **(D)** $x=2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(D) $x=2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of line MN is $x - y - 2 = 0$.

To find the coordinates of M, substitute $y = 0$ in the equation of line MN.

$x - 0 - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 2$

The coordinates of M are $(2, 0)$.

The slope of line MN is $m = 1$.

Let $\theta$ be the angle of inclination of line MN.

$\tan \theta = 1 \Rightarrow \theta = 45^{\circ}$

The line MN is rotated about M through $45^{\circ}$ in the anticlockwise direction.

The new angle of inclination of the line is $\theta' = 45^{\circ} + 45^{\circ} = 90^{\circ}$.

The new line passes through M $(2, 0)$ and is perpendicular to the X-axis.

The equation of the new line is $x = 2$.

Answer: $x=2$

</details>

### Q3. MHT CET 2024 (16 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `2`

The abscissa of the point on the curve $y=\mathrm{a}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{x}{a}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x}{a}}\right)$ where the tangent is parallel to the X -axis is

**Options:**

- **(A)** 0
- **(B)** a
- **(C)** 2 a
- **(D)** -2 a

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 0**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given the equation of the curve:

$y = a\left(e^{\dfrac{x}{a}} + e^{-\dfrac{x}{a}}\right)$

Differentiating with respect to $x$, we get:

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = a\left(e^{\dfrac{x}{a}} \cdot \dfrac{1}{a} + e^{-\dfrac{x}{a}} \cdot \left(-\dfrac{1}{a}\right)\right)$

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = e^{\dfrac{x}{a}} - e^{-\dfrac{x}{a}}$

Since the tangent is parallel to the X-axis, its slope is zero. Therefore, $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$.

$e^{\dfrac{x}{a}} - e^{-\dfrac{x}{a}} = 0$

$e^{\dfrac{x}{a}} = e^{-\dfrac{x}{a}}$

$e^{\dfrac{2x}{a}} = 1$

$\dfrac{2x}{a} = 0$

$x = 0$

The abscissa of the point is $0$.

Answer: 0

</details>

### Q4. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `3`

A line $4 x+y=1$ passes through the point $\mathrm{A}(2,-7)$ meets the line BC whose equation is $3 x-4 y+1=0$ at the point B . The equation of the line $A C$ so that $A B=A C$ is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $52 x+89 y+519=0$
- **(B)** $52 x+89 y-727=0$
- **(C)** $52 x-89 y+519=0$
- **(D)** $52 x-89 y-727=0$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $52 x+89 y+519=0$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The slope of the line AB is $m_1 = -4$.

The slope of the line BC is $m_2 = \dfrac{3}{4}$.

Since $AB = AC$, $\triangle ABC$ is an isosceles triangle, which means the angle between AB and BC is equal to the angle between AC and BC.

Let the angle between AB and BC be $\theta$. Then,

$\tan \theta = \left| \dfrac{m_1 - m_2}{1 + m_1 m_2} \right| = \left| \dfrac{-4 - \dfrac{3}{4}}{1 + (-4)\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)} \right| = \left| \dfrac{-\dfrac{19}{4}}{-2} \right| = \dfrac{19}{8}$

Let the slope of the line AC be $m$. The angle between AC and BC is also $\theta$, so:

$\left| \dfrac{m - \dfrac{3}{4}}{1 + \dfrac{3m}{4}} \right| = \dfrac{19}{8}$

$\dfrac{4m - 3}{4 + 3m} = \pm \dfrac{19}{8}$

Taking the positive sign yields $m = -4$, which is the slope of the line AB. Taking the negative sign gives the slope of AC:

$\dfrac{4m - 3}{4 + 3m} = -\dfrac{19}{8}$

$32m - 24 = -76 - 57m$

$89m = -52 \Rightarrow m = -\dfrac{52}{89}$

The equation of the line AC passing through $A(2, -7)$ with slope $m = -\dfrac{52}{89}$ is:

$y - (-7) = -\dfrac{52}{89}(x - 2)$

$89(y + 7) = -52(x - 2)$

$89y + 623 = -52x + 104$

$52x + 89y + 519 = 0$

Answer: $52 x+89 y+519=0$

</details>

### Q5. MHT CET 2024 (09 May Shift 1)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The straight line, $2 x-3 y+17=0$ is perpendicular to the line passing through the points $(7,17)$ and $(15, \beta)$, then $\beta$ equals

**Options:**

- **(A)** 5
- **(B)** $\dfrac{35}{3}$
- **(C)** $-\dfrac{35}{3}$
- **(D)** -5

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) 5**

**Detailed Solution:**

The slope of the given line $2x - 3y + 17 = 0$ is $m_1 = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

The slope of the line passing through the points $(7, 17)$ and $(15, \beta)$ is $m_2 = \dfrac{\beta - 17}{15 - 7} = \dfrac{\beta - 17}{8}$.

Since the two lines are perpendicular, the product of their slopes is $-1$.

$m_1 \times m_2 = -1$

$\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{\beta - 17}{8} = -1$

$\dfrac{\beta - 17}{12} = -1$

$\beta - 17 = -12$

$\beta = 5$

Answer: 5

</details>

### Q6. MHT CET 2024 (02 May Shift 2)

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

The diagonals of a parallelogram ABCD are along the lines $x+3 y=4$ and $6 x-2 y=7$. Then ABCD must be a

**Options:**

- **(A)** rectangle.
- **(B)** square.
- **(C)** rhombus.
- **(D)** cyclic quadrilateral

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(C) rhombus.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equations of the diagonals are $x + 3y = 4$ and $6x - 2y = 7$.

The slope of the first diagonal is $m_1 = -\dfrac{1}{3}$.

The slope of the second diagonal is $m_2 = \dfrac{6}{2} = 3$.

The product of their slopes is $m_1 m_2 = \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right)(3) = -1$.

This implies that the diagonals are perpendicular to each other.

A parallelogram whose diagonals intersect at right angles is a rhombus.

Answer: rhombus.

</details>
