The equation of the line $\sqrt{3}x - y + 4 = 0$ in the normal form is _____

**Options:**

- **(A)** $x\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} + y\sin\dfrac{\pi}{3} = 2$
- **(B)** $x\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + y\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 2$
- **(C)** $x\cos\dfrac{\pi}{6} + y\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = 2$
- **(D)** $x\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4} + y\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4} = 2$

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $x\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + y\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 2$**

**Detailed Solution:**

The given equation of the line is $\sqrt{3}x - y + 4 = 0$.

Rewriting the equation so that the constant term is positive, we get:

$-\sqrt{3}x + y = 4$

Dividing the entire equation by $\sqrt{(-\sqrt{3})^2 + (1)^2} = 2$, we obtain:

$-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}x + \dfrac{1}{2}y = 2$

Comparing this with the normal form $x\cos\alpha + y\sin\alpha = p$, we have:

$\cos\alpha = -\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\sin\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and $p = 2$

Since $\cos\alpha < 0$ and $\sin\alpha > 0$, $\alpha$ lies in the second quadrant.

$\alpha = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$

Therefore, the equation of the line in normal form is:

$x\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + y\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 2$

Answer: $x\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + y\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6} = 2$

</details>

A straight line passes through the point $(3, 3)$ and intersects the positive $x$ and $y$ axes at the points $A(h, 0)$ and $B(0, k)$ respectively. If O is the origin, then the minimum area of $\triangle OAB$ is:

**Options:**

- **(A)** $9$ square units.
- **(B)** $18$ square units.
- **(C)** $27$ square units.
- **(D)** $36$ square units.

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(B) $18$ square units.**

**Detailed Solution:**

The equation of the line with $x$-intercept $h$ and $y$-intercept $k$ is given by $\dfrac{x}{h} + \dfrac{y}{k} = 1$.

Since the line passes through the point $(3, 3)$, we have:

$\dfrac{3}{h} + \dfrac{3}{k} = 1$

The area of $\triangle OAB$ is given by $\Delta = \dfrac{1}{2}hk$.

Since the line intersects the positive axes, $h > 0$ and $k > 0$. Applying the AM-GM inequality to the positive numbers $\dfrac{3}{h}$ and $\dfrac{3}{k}$:

$\dfrac{\dfrac{3}{h} + \dfrac{3}{k}}{2} \ge \sqrt{\dfrac{3}{h} \times \dfrac{3}{k}}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} \ge \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{hk}}$

$\sqrt{hk} \ge 6$

$hk \ge 36$

Therefore, the area of the triangle is:

$\Delta = \dfrac{1}{2}hk \ge \dfrac{1}{2}(36) = 18$

The minimum area of $\triangle OAB$ is $18$ square units.

Answer: $18$ square units.

### Q38. MHT CET 2012

*Type:* `singleCorrect` | *Level:* `1`

If $2 a+b+3 c=0$, then the line $a x+b y+c=0$ passes through the fixed point that is

**Options:**

- **(A)** $\left(\frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3}\right)$
- **(B)** $(0,1)$
- **(C)** $\left(\frac{2}{3}, 0\right)$
- **(D)** None of these

<details>
<summary><b>View Answer & Solution</b></summary>

**Correct Option:** **(A) $\left(\frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3}\right)$**

**Detailed Solution:**

Given, $2 a+b+3 c+0$...(i)
and line, $\quad a x+b y+c=0$
$\Rightarrow \quad 3 a x+3 b y+3 c=0$...(ii)
On subtracting Eq. (i) from Eq. (ii), we get $(3 x-2) a+(3 y-1) b=0 \cdot a+0 \cdot b$
On comparing both sides, we get
$$
3 x-2=0 \Rightarrow x=\frac{2}{3}
$$
and $\quad 3 y-1=0 \Rightarrow y=\frac{1}{3}$
$\therefore$ Line, $a x+b y+c=0$ passes through the fixed point $\left(\frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3}\right)$.

</details>
